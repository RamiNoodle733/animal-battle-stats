# Whale Shark (*Rhincodon typus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Whale shark
- **Scientific name:** *Rhincodon typus* Smith, 1828
- **Exact animal represented:** the single living whale shark species, the largest fish alive. It is a filter-feeding carpet shark, not a whale.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult female, **12.0 m** total length and **15,000 kg**, representing a large adult rather than the 18.8 m record animal.
- **Sex choice:** females are the larger sex. Growth modelling from Ningaloo Reef (Meekan et al. 2020) found that males grow quickly and plateau at an average adult length of about 8–9 m, while females grow more slowly but reach an average adult length of about 14 m. Males mature at about 8–9 m (Marine Megafauna Foundation). Neither sex fights the other in any documented way, so the larger mature female is the representative body.
- **Population notes:** most animals seen at coastal aggregation sites (Ningaloo, Mozambique, Belize, Qatar, La Paz) are juvenile or young males of roughly 4–8 m. Large mature females are seen less often and spend more time offshore, so "typical sighted" size and "mature adult" size are very different.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- Georgia Aquarium: whale sharks typically reach about 12 m and weigh up to about 20 tons (search-result summary of the Whale Sharks 101 page).
- The largest specimen regarded as accurately weighed, caught near Karachi in 1947, was 12.65 m long and weighed more than 21.5 t (Wikipedia, tertiary).
- Reported species maxima reach about 20 m and 34 t (FishBase-style figures seen in search results; not verified on the page).
- **Canonical:** **15,000 kg** for a 12.0 m mature female. This is a conservative placement below the 20 t "up to" figure and the 21.5 t weighed specimen of similar length.
- **Confidence:** Low-moderate. No direct weighing series or volumetric mass study for mature free-swimming females was retrieved in this run.

### Length
- Largest reliably recorded individual about 18.8 m (Wikipedia, tertiary).
- Average adult length about 14 m for females and 8–9 m for males (Meekan et al. 2020 growth modelling).
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 1200.0** total length for a mature female.
- **Confidence:** Moderate.

### Height
- Standing height is not meaningful for a fully aquatic fish. **Canonical `height_cm`: 0.0.**

### Speed
- Popular and aquarium summaries describe routine cruising of about 5 km/h (about 1.4 m/s) or "just 3 mph". A Journal of Experimental Biology study (2020) used tags to show that whale sharks increase swimming effort while filter feeding, but no instrumented top speed was retrieved.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0**, meaning no defensible measured maximum, not immobility. The whale shark is a slow cruiser with no documented high-speed burst.

### Lifespan
- Ong et al. (2020) validated annual vertebral growth bands with bomb radiocarbon and aged whale sharks up to **50 years**; one 50-year-old animal had been born in 1962.
- Western Australian government material and other summaries estimate potential lifespans of 80–130 years from growth-band and growth-rate work; maturity is thought to come at about 30 years.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 70.0**, a moderate placement above the 50 years validated directly and below the higher modelled estimates.
- **Confidence:** Low-moderate.

### Bite and dentition
- The mouth holds more than 300 rows of tiny teeth and about 20 filter pads (search-result summary of ADW/Britannica-type accounts). The teeth are not used to feed; food is trapped in the filter pads.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** The whale shark does not bite prey, and no measurement exists.

### Defensive structure
- Skin is reported at about 10 cm thick, and up to about 15 cm in places, very hard and rough, and covered in dermal denticles (tertiary sources; no primary thickness series retrieved).
- Tomita et al. (2020, PLOS One) found that the eyeball is covered with dermal denticles (one eye can carry more than 3,000), with a shape suited to abrasion resistance, and that the whale shark can pull the eyeball back into the socket by about half the eye's diameter.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The whale shark has no functional offensive weapon. Its hundreds of rows of tiny teeth play no part in feeding, it has no spines, no venom and no cutting jaw, and it is not known to use its tail or body against other animals. Its only real offensive asset is **mass**: a 15-tonne body moving through the water carries enormous momentum, and a tail sweep from an animal this size would be dangerous to anything caught by it. That is incidental force, not a weapon it deploys.

### Defense and durability
Defense is the whale shark's strongest combat quality. Its skin is among the thickest of any animal, reported at around 10 cm and up to about 15 cm in places, hard and armored with dermal denticles. Even its eyes are protected: Tomita et al. (2020) described thousands of tooth-like denticles on each eyeball and a strong ability to retract the eye into its socket. Sheer tissue depth makes a mature adult very hard for almost any single predator to disable.

That protection has limits. A 2024 Frontiers in Marine Science study documented four killer whale predation events on whale sharks in the southern Gulf of California between 2018 and 2024. The orcas hunted cooperatively, deliberately targeted the pelvic region, and the sharks bled out, giving the orcas access to the lipid-rich liver. The victims appear to have been juveniles or sub-adults rather than full-size females, but the events show that a coordinated, intelligent predator can find the weak point.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Whale sharks are slow, economical swimmers. They cruise at walking pace, feed by swimming open-mouthed at the surface or by hanging vertically and gulping, and have no documented high-speed burst. A 12 m body gives a wide turning circle. Agility is therefore very low for an active fight, although the animal can dive steeply and change depth.

### Endurance
Endurance is a major strength. Satellite tags have recorded one pregnant-looking female travelling about 2,644 km in 37 days, crossing five national maritime boundaries (Robinson et al. 2017, PLOS One), and the longest recorded track exceeded 20,000 km (Mongabay report, 2018). Whale sharks also dive deeply: a Yucatán shark reached 1,928 m, and a 2025 Coral Sea record extended the known range to at least 1,978 m and possibly about 2,527 m. Most time is spent near the surface, with deep dives as occasional excursions.

### Senses
Whale sharks have the standard shark sensory suite of smell, lateral line and (as sharks) electroreception, plus small but protected eyes. They locate plankton blooms, coral spawning and fish-spawn events with notable timing, but species-specific sensory thresholds were not retrieved.

### Intelligence and tactics
Whale sharks return to seasonal feeding sites such as Ningaloo after coral spawning and gather around offshore platforms in Qatar to feed on fish spawn. This shows reliable navigation and timing, not tactical flexibility in a fight. There is no evidence of problem-solving or combat tactics.

### Hunting behavior
Filter feeding only: plankton, krill, fish eggs, small schooling fish and squid are strained from the water by ram feeding (swimming with the mouth open) or by active suction while stationary or vertical.

### Intraspecific fighting and predator defense
No intraspecific fighting is documented. The main defense is passive: thick skin, armored eyes, mass and the ability to dive away. Scars from boat strikes and predators are common on photo-identified animals.

### Aggression
Among the most docile large animals in the sea. Whale sharks routinely tolerate swimmers and divers around them.

### Environmental strengths
- Open ocean at any depth from the surface to around 2,000 m.
- Long-distance travel between feeding areas.
- Situations where an opponent must bite through very thick skin.

### Weaknesses
- No weapon and no attacking behavior.
- Very slow, with no documented burst speed.
- Pelvic and ventral regions are vulnerable to a coordinated predator.
- Docile temperament: it does not fight back.
- Shallow water and stranding are dangerous for an animal this size.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** small or medium predators that cannot get through its skin; opponents that must win by damage in a short time.
- **Mixed:** a single large predatory shark, which can wound but not quickly kill a mature adult.
- **Bad:** cooperative packs such as orcas, and any opponent that simply needs the whale shark to fail to fight back.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Armored Retracting Eye** — each eyeball carries thousands of tooth-like dermal denticles and can be pulled back into the socket by about half its own diameter. Against a biting or raking opponent, the whale shark's most vulnerable sense organ is shielded and can be withdrawn.
2. **Abyssal Plunge** — tags have recorded dives to 1,928 m and, in the Coral Sea, to at least 1,978 m. A whale shark under pressure can leave the surface fight entirely for depths few opponents can follow.

### Unique traits
1. **Largest Living Fish** — no other fish comes close in size; mature females average around 14 m at full growth and the largest reliably recorded individual was about 18.8 m.
2. **Filter-Pad Giant** — more than 300 rows of tiny teeth go unused while about 20 filter pads strain plankton, fish eggs and small fish from the water.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Whale sharks live in tropical and warm-temperate seas worldwide, both far offshore and at predictable coastal feeding hotspots such as Ningaloo Reef, the Yucatán Peninsula, La Paz, Mozambique, the Philippines and the Arabian Gulf. They move between surface waters and the deep sea, occasionally diving to about 2,000 m.

### Diet and ecology
The whale shark is a filter feeder. It eats plankton, krill, fish eggs, small schooling fish and squid, either by swimming slowly open-mouthed or by hanging vertically and sucking water in. It follows seasonal food pulses: coral spawning at Ningaloo, fish-spawn events around offshore platforms in Qatar and plankton blooms in the Gulf of California.

### Social structure
Whale sharks are solitary but gather in large numbers where food is concentrated. These aggregations are feeding gatherings, not social groups, and are often dominated by juvenile males.

### Reproduction and life history
Whale sharks give birth to live young; little else about their breeding is known, and mating has rarely been observed. Males mature at around 8–9 m; female maturity size is less certain. Vertebral banding validated with bomb radiocarbon confirms ages of at least 50 years, and maturity may take around 30 years, making populations very slow to recover.

### Conservation
The whale shark is listed as **Endangered** on the IUCN Red List (Pierce and Norman 2016), with estimated declines of more than half over three generations in the Indo-Pacific, driven by fisheries, bycatch and ship strikes. The Atlantic subpopulation was assessed separately as Vulnerable. It is listed on the Convention on Migratory Species appendices.

### Special features
- Skin around 10 cm thick, armored with dermal denticles.
- Denticle-covered eyeballs that can be retracted into the socket.
- More than 300 rows of tiny unused teeth and about 20 filter pads.
- Fish-record dive depth of about 2,000 m.
- Unique pale spot pattern used by researchers to identify individuals.

### Human interaction
Whale shark tourism supports local economies at many aggregation sites. The main threats from people are targeted fisheries (historically), bycatch in nets and collisions with ships. Encounters with swimmers are safe for the swimmer when codes of conduct are followed.

### Genuine fun facts
- A single whale shark eyeball can carry more than 3,000 tiny tooth-like denticles.
- The whale shark can pull its eye back into its head by about half the eye's width.
- Bomb radiocarbon from Cold War nuclear tests was used to confirm that one whale shark was 50 years old.
- One tagged whale shark swam about 2,644 km in 37 days, and the longest recorded track topped 20,000 km.
- A whale shark in the Coral Sea dived to at least 1,978 m, deeper than the old fish record of 1,928 m.
- Killer whales in the Gulf of California have been filmed hunting whale sharks by targeting the pelvic area.
- Female whale sharks keep growing after the males stop and end up far longer.

### Concise site-ready summary
The whale shark is the largest fish alive, a 12-metre, 15-tonne filter feeder protected by skin around 10 cm thick and even armored eyes. It is almost impossible for a lone predator to wound seriously, but it has no weapons, swims at walking pace and never fights back, so its battles are tests of endurance rather than attack.

### Rich narrative profile
The whale shark is a giant built for patience. A mature female stretches around 12 metres and weighs roughly 15 tonnes, yet she feeds on some of the smallest animals in the sea, cruising open-mouthed through plankton or hanging vertically to gulp fish eggs. Her hundreds of rows of tiny teeth are never used for eating; the real work is done by the filter pads behind them.

In a battle, that makes the whale shark a pure defender. Its skin is among the thickest of any animal, covered in denticles, and its eyes are armored with thousands of tiny tooth-like scales and can be pulled back into the head. A lone shark or seal-hunter would struggle to do more than scratch it. When things go badly, it can dive: tags show whale sharks plunging to around 2,000 metres, deeper than almost any opponent could follow.

What the whale shark cannot do is hurt anything on purpose. It has no bite, no spines and no fighting behavior, and it swims so slowly that a determined attacker can stay with it indefinitely. Orcas in the Gulf of California have learned exactly that, working together to open the soft pelvic region. Against a single predator the whale shark simply outlasts the attack; against a coordinated pack, its armor eventually runs out.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** no direct weighing of mature females was retrieved; 15,000 kg is a conservative estimate for a 12 m female and may be low.
- **Skin thickness:** the 10–15 cm figures come from tertiary sources; no primary histology series was retrieved.
- **Speed:** only popular cruising figures exist; `speed_mps` stays 0.0.
- **Lifespan:** only 50 years is directly validated; 70.0 is an editorial middle value.
- **Status:** the species is Endangered globally; the Atlantic subpopulation was assessed Vulnerable.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Blue Whale (120 t; A78 D91 Ag44 St87 I69 Sp76):** the blue whale is eight times heavier and keeps Size and Raw Power higher. Its Attack of 78.0 reflects huge lunging mass; the whale shark's 48.0 is much lower because it is slower, weaker per kilogram and never strikes. Defense 82.0 sits below the blue whale's 91.0 despite thicker skin, because of the smaller body. Stamina is close (88.0 vs 87.0).
- **Versus Manta Ray (1.4 t; A42 D57 Ag72 St89 I76 Sp70):** both are docile filter-feeding elasmobranchs. The whale shark is ten times heavier and much better protected, so Attack (48.0 vs 42.0) and Defense (82.0 vs 57.0) are higher, but the manta's agile "underwater flight" and large brain keep it far ahead in Agility (72.0 vs 32.0) and Intelligence (76.0 vs 45.0).
- **Versus Great White Shark (900 kg; A88 D68 Ag73 St91 I70 Sp87):** the great white is a true weapon-bearing predator and stays far above in Attack, Agility and Intelligence. The whale shark's mass and skin put its Defense (82.0) well above the great white's 68.0.
- **Versus Humpback Whale (30 t; A72 D85):** the humpback is heavier, faster and actively mobs orcas with its flippers, so it keeps a much higher Attack. Whale shark Defense 82.0 sits just below.
- **Versus Orca (5 t; A94 D78 Ag84 I99):** orcas are documented predators of whale sharks. The ratings match that: the whale shark is heavier and has higher Defense, but is outclassed in Attack, Agility and Intelligence.
- **Anti-inflation check:** Size is near the ceiling but Attack stays below 50 because body mass alone is not a weapon. Speed 22.0 and Agility 32.0 reflect the lack of any documented burst.
