# Bearded Vulture (*Gypaetus barbatus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Bearded Vulture (also called lammergeier or ossifrage, "bone breaker")
- **Scientific name:** *Gypaetus barbatus* (Linnaeus, 1758), family Accipitridae
- **Exact animal represented:** the bearded vulture of the mountains of southern Europe, Africa, the Middle East and Central Asia, treated as one species including the African subspecies *G. b. meridionalis*.
- **Living / extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult female, **6.2 kg**, **110.0 cm** total length, wingspan about **2.7 m**.
- **Sex choice:** females are slightly larger than males, a reversed size difference common in raptors. The adult female is used as the canonical specimen.
- **Population notes:** the nominate Eurasian race averages about **6.21 kg** and the African *G. b. meridionalis* about **5.7 kg**; birds around the Himalayas tend to be slightly larger than those of other ranges. The canonical 6.2 kg reflects a Eurasian adult female near the nominate average, well below the 7.0 kg top of the species range.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The bearded vulture is a bone specialist, not a fighter. Its hooked beak handles and swallows bones and its feet can carry heavy loads, but its talons are weak compared with an eagle's. Its most dangerous tool is height: it carries bones and live prey into the air and drops them onto rocks. Tortoises, often nearly as heavy as the vulture itself, are a favored live prey where common, and rock hyraxes, hares, marmots and, in one case, a 62 cm monitor lizard have been reported seized and dropped. Popular accounts also claim it sometimes knocks large animals off ledges with its wings; this is anecdotal and not scored.

### Feeding mechanics
Bones make up about **70–90%** of its diet. It swallows bones whole or bites through brittle ones up to the size of a lamb's femur, and bones too large to swallow are dropped from height onto favored rocky sites (ossuaries) until they shatter. Its highly acidic stomach then dissolves them.

### Defense and durability
It relies on size, flight and remote cliff nests. There is no armor, and like other large soaring birds it is vulnerable on the ground.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Long, narrow, pointed wings and a long wedge-shaped tail make it a graceful, controlled soarer that hugs mountain slopes and ridges. It is more agile in the air than most large vultures, but it is still a soaring bird built for efficiency, not rapid aerial combat.

### Endurance
Excellent low-cost endurance. Pyrenean GPS data show daily movements of about 24–46 km depending on social status, and non-territorial birds range over areas of around 10,000 km², mostly by soaring on slope and thermal lift.

### Senses
Keen long-range vision to search mountain slopes for carcasses and bones. No species-specific acuity measurement was located.

### Intelligence and tactics
Bone dropping is a learned, skilled behavior: birds choose rocky drop sites, adjust height and repeat drops until a bone breaks. It also shows a rare cosmetic behavior, bathing in iron-rich mud or springs to stain its naturally white underparts rusty orange. Researchers still debate whether this signals status or has a sanitary or medicinal function.

### Hunting behavior
Mostly a scavenger of bones from mountain ungulates and livestock carcasses. It occasionally takes live prey such as tortoises and small mammals by lifting and dropping them.

### Intraspecific fighting
Territorial pairs defend nesting areas with aerial displays and chases. Serious fights are rarely described.

### Predator defense
Few predators as an adult. It nests on inaccessible cliff ledges and caves.

### Aggression and ferocity
Low toward large animals; it avoids fights and rarely attacks anything that can resist.

### Environmental strengths
- High mountains with cliffs, updrafts and rocky slopes for bone dropping.
- Terrain where soaring lets it cover huge areas cheaply.

### Weaknesses
- Weak talons and a beak built for bones rather than killing.
- No armor; grounded, it is clumsy.
- Depends on mountain updrafts and on carcasses left by other animals.
- Low willingness to fight.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** small, slow animals it can lift and drop, and any ground-bound opponent it can avoid by flying away.
- **Mixed:** other large soaring birds in aerial disputes.
- **Bad:** eagles and other true raptors with killing talons, and any mammal predator that catches it on the ground.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Bone Bomber** — carries bones and live prey such as tortoises high into the air and drops them onto rocks at chosen sites, repeating until they shatter.
2. **Acid Gut** — a stomach with pH below 1 dissolves swallowed bones up to about 30 cm long within about a day, letting it live on food no other animal can use.

### Unique traits
1. **Bone Eater** — bones make up about 70–90% of its diet, described as the only animal whose diet is based mainly on bone.
2. **Rust-Stained Plumage** — bathes in iron-rich mud and springs to dye its white underparts rusty orange, a rare cosmetic behavior in birds.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Lives in high mountains and gorges: the Pyrenees, Alps (through reintroduction), Corsica, Crete, the Atlas, Ethiopian Highlands and eastern and southern Africa's mountains, the Caucasus, Middle East, and across Central Asia to the Himalayas and Tibetan Plateau. It needs cliffs for nesting and open slopes with wild or domestic grazing animals.

### Diet and ecology
A bone specialist: about 70–90% of its diet is bone, supplemented by meat, skin and occasional live prey such as tortoises and small mammals. It feeds after other scavengers have stripped a carcass, filling a niche no other bird uses. Bones too large to swallow are dropped from height onto rocky ossuaries to break them.

### Social structure
Lives in territorial pairs, sometimes with a helper, that defend large mountain territories. Non-breeding young birds wander widely over thousands of square kilometres.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeds in winter, laying one or two eggs in a cliff nest; usually only one chick is raised. It is long-lived, with individuals reported to live over 30 years in the wild and about 45 years in captivity.

### Conservation
Listed as **Near Threatened** on the IUCN Red List since 2014, with a decreasing global population estimated at roughly 1,675–6,700 birds. It was wiped out from most of Europe by persecution in the 19th and 20th centuries, surviving mainly in the Pyrenees, and has returned to the Alps and other ranges through reintroduction. Threats include poisoning (including veterinary drugs), power-line collisions, disturbance and falling food supply.

### Special features
- Stomach acid with pH below 1 that dissolves bone.
- Stretchy esophagus that swallows bones up to about 30 cm long.
- Long, narrow wings and a wedge-shaped tail for agile slope soaring.
- Black "beard" of bristles under the bill.
- Rust-orange cosmetic staining from iron-oxide bathing.

### Human interaction
Once feared and persecuted in Europe as a supposed killer of lambs and even children, it is now the focus of major reintroduction projects in the Alps and other mountains, coordinated by groups such as the Vulture Conservation Foundation. GPS tracking of released and wild birds guides conservation planning.

### Genuine fun facts
- Bones make up about 70–90% of its diet.
- Its stomach acid is below pH 1 and can dissolve bone within about a day.
- It can swallow bones up to about 30 cm long whole.
- It drops bones onto rocks from height to smash them, often at favorite sites.
- Its orange color is cosmetic: it stains its white feathers by bathing in iron-rich mud.
- One old name, "ossifrage," means "bone breaker."
- Young non-breeding birds can roam over about 10,000 km² of mountains.

### Concise site-ready summary
The bearded vulture is a 6 kg mountain vulture with a 2.7 m wingspan that lives mostly on bones, dropping them onto rocks to smash them and dissolving the pieces in stomach acid below pH 1. It is a graceful, far-ranging flier but a poor fighter, with weak talons and little appetite for conflict.

### Rich narrative profile
The bearded vulture is described as the only animal whose diet is based mainly on bone. Where other scavengers leave off, it arrives, swallowing ribs, vertebrae and leg bones whole. Anything too big it carries into the air and drops onto rocks, often at favorite smashing sites, until the bone shatters. Its stomach acid, below pH 1, dissolves bone within about a day, turning an almost useless food into a steady supply of fat-rich marrow and minerals.

It is also one of the most striking birds in the mountains. Adults have long, narrow wings spanning about 2.7 m, a diamond-shaped tail, a black "beard" under the bill and rusty orange underparts. That orange is not natural color: the birds bathe in iron-rich mud and springs to stain their white feathers, a rare cosmetic habit that researchers still debate.

As a fighter, the bearded vulture has little to offer. Its talons are weak, its beak is built for bones, and it avoids conflict. Its combat value lies in agile, low-cost soaring, excellent eyesight, and a clever drop technique that can kill small prey such as tortoises. Persecution once wiped it from most of Europe; today reintroduction and GPS tracking are bringing it back to the Alps and other ranges.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Lifespan:** 30 years is a reported wild upper figure; no measured wild average was found.
- **Speed and height:** no reliable measurements; both left at 0.0.
- **Stomach pH:** the pH below 1 figure is widely repeated; the underlying measurement was not reviewed in this run.
- **Wing-battering attacks on large animals:** anecdotal and not scored.
- **Cosmetic staining:** its function (status signal, sanitary or medicinal) is still debated.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Condor (10 kg; A34 D22 Ag70 St82 I69 Sp74):** the condor is heavier with a stronger flesh-tearing beak, so it stays above the bearded vulture in Attack and Defense. The bearded vulture's narrower wings and slope-hugging flight earn slightly higher Agility, and its bone-dropping and bone-dissolving stomach earn a slightly higher Special. Stamina and Intelligence are close.
- **Versus Vulture (legacy roster entry, *Gyps africanus*; A32 D11.6 Ag36.8 St81.3 I62.4 Sp62.4):** that entry has not been re-researched, so its numbers are context only. A griffon-type vulture's beak is a better flesh-tearing tool, which is why the bearded vulture's Attack is not set higher; its agility and specialization justify higher Agility and Special.
- **Versus Golden Eagle (5 kg; A40 D21 Ag88):** the eagle is lighter but has true killing talons, so it stays well above in Attack and Agility.
- **Versus Steller's Sea Eagle (9 kg; A43 St76):** a heavier true raptor with far stronger feet; the bearded vulture is below in Attack but above in Stamina because of its soaring range.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the bone-dissolving stomach is counted only in Special and Abilities, not in Attack or Toughness, and the anecdotal ledge-pushing stories are not scored.
