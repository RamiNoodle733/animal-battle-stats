# Indian Rhinoceros (*Rhinoceros unicornis*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Indian rhinoceros (greater one-horned rhinoceros, Indian rhino)
- **Scientific name:** *Rhinoceros unicornis* Linnaeus, 1758
- **Exact animal represented:** the greater one-horned rhinoceros of the Brahmaputra floodplains and Terai grasslands of India and Nepal, not the smaller Javan rhinoceros, which also has one horn.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature bull of about **2,100 kg**, **175 cm** at the shoulder and **370 cm** head-body length, with fully grown lower incisor tusks and neck folds. This is a prime bull, not a record animal.
- **Sex choice:** bulls are larger, carry heavy neck folds and are the sex that fights. Standard summaries give bulls about 2,070–2,200 kg and cows about 1,600 kg. Zoo records show males on average about 11% heavier than females.
- **Population variation:** no wild Indian rhino has ever been weighed. Published weights come from zoo animals, and Dinerstein's field work suggested that wild dimorphism is smaller than zoo records imply. Reported maxima up to 4,000 kg are not used as a baseline.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- Wikipedia summary of standard references: bulls **2,070–2,200 kg**, cows about **1,600 kg**; largest reported up to 4,000 kg.

- International Rhino Foundation: **1,800–2,700 kg**.
- *Zoo Biology* body-mass review: a zoo survey averaged **2,031 kg** for males (1,441–2,500 kg) and **1,711 kg** for females; no actual weighings of wild animals appear to exist.

- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 15.3 m/s**, low-moderate confidence, a short-burst figure only.

### Lifespan
- ADW: expected maximum about **40 years** in the wild; several captive animals have passed 40, the oldest reaching 47.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 35.0**, below the stated wild maximum.
- **Confidence:** Low-moderate; no wild survival study was located.

### Horn, tusks and bite
- **Horn:** a single keratin nasal horn in both sexes; IRF gives **20–61 cm**, and Wikipedia notes most adults carry about **25 cm**. The horn base is about 18.5 by 12 cm.
- **Tusks:** the lower incisors form sharp tusks. The Asian rhinos are the only living rhinos with tusk-like incisors; Indian rhinos use them as their main fighting weapon, slashing to inflict wounds, and use the horn less often than African rhinos do.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No bite-force measurement exists, and tusk-slashing is not a pressure bite that a PSI figure could describe.
- **Tusk length:** no standardized measurement located.

### Defensive structure
- Thick grey-brown skin in large folds at the neck, shoulders and hindquarters, with wart-like bumps on the upper legs and shoulders; bulls have huge neck folds. IRF describes the look as grey plates of armor with rivets. It is thick hide, not true armor: it does not stop biting flies, leeches and ticks.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The Indian rhino fights differently from the African species. Its main weapons are the sharp lower incisor tusks, driven by a two-tonne body in slashing bites that open deep wounds. The single horn is shorter than an African rhino's front horn and is used less in fights. Charging, shoving and trampling add damage. Fights between dominant bulls are reported as the most common natural cause of death in the species, which shows how damaging the tusks are against an equally armored opponent.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Heavy and short-legged, but quick in a short burst (up to 55 km/h reported) and an excellent swimmer that crosses rivers and wallows daily. Mass limits sharp turning. Tall floodplain grass lets it approach unseen.

### Endurance
Indian rhinos graze floodplain grasslands, wetlands and riverine forest and wallow often; heat pushes them into water and mud. Bulls chase receptive cows over distances of 1–2 km. They are not long-distance travellers: home ranges are about 2–8 km².

### Senses
Excellent hearing and smell, relatively poor eyesight. A Kaziranga study of disturbance distances found rhinos reacted from farther away when the wind blew from the rhino toward the observer or when there was no wind, consistent with strong hearing and scent detection. Communal dung heaps (middens) and pedal scent glands carry scent information.

### Intelligence and tactics
More than 10 distinct vocalizations have been recorded. Rhinos greet each other with head bobbing and nuzzling in aggregations, spar playfully, and young bulls cluster on the edges of dominant bulls' ranges, apparently for safety. Tactically, the response to threat is a charge or a retreat into tall grass or water.

### Sociality and hunting
Largely solitary, with cows accompanied by calves for up to four years and short-term gatherings of up to 10 animals at wallows in the monsoon. A grazer: mainly grasses, plus leaves, shrubs, fruit and aquatic plants.

### Fighting and aggression
Adult bulls start most fights; dominant bulls tolerate others passing through except in the breeding season, when dangerous fights break out. Bulls are also very aggressive toward cows during courtship. Rhinos attack people in and around Nepal's parks: over 2000–2020, rhinos killed an average of 3.3 people a year in Nepal, fewer than leopards, elephants and tigers, but a steady toll.

### Predator defense
Adults are rarely preyed on. Tigers kill unguarded calves, and an estimated 10% of calves die before maturity, mainly to tigers. In 2017 a group of four tigers killed a 20-year-old bull in Dudhwa Tiger Reserve, a rare event. Mothers guard calves closely.

### Environmental strengths
- Tall-grass floodplains, where it can charge from cover.
- Rivers, wallows and swamps, which it swims and uses to cool off.
- Soft, wet ground that slows lighter or less sure-footed attackers.

### Weaknesses
- Poor eyesight.
- Thick hide is not true armor; flanks and belly can be wounded.
- Heat drives it into water and limits long exertion.
- Tusks require very close contact; the horn is short.
- Mass limits turning.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** big cats and other large predators forced into close contact; large herbivores with less mass and thinner skin.
- **Mixed:** other rhinos and hippos of similar mass; young elephants.
- **Bad:** adult elephants with a large mass advantage; aerial or aquatic opponents it cannot reach; fast, evasive animals that avoid close range.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Tusk Slash** — bulls fight mainly with the sharp lower incisor tusks, slashing deep wounds while the horn plays a secondary role; bull fights are the most common natural cause of death.
2. **Floodplain Charge** — a two-tonne rush from tall grass at up to 55 km/h for short bursts, backed by strong swimming that lets it use rivers and wallows as cover.

### Unique traits
1. **Riveted Hide** — deep skin folds and wart-like bumps give the look that the International Rhino Foundation likens to grey armor plates with rivets.
2. **Comeback Rhino** — reduced to about 100 animals in the early 1900s and now over 4,000, one of Asia's major conservation recoveries.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Alluvial floodplain grasslands, swamps and adjoining riverine forest of the Brahmaputra basin and the Terai of northern India and southern Nepal, usually close to water. It survives in 12 protected areas, including Kaziranga, Orang, Pobitora, Manas, Jaldapara, Gorumara and Dudhwa in India and Chitwan, Bardia, Parsa and Shuklaphanta in Nepal.

### Diet and ecology
Mainly a grazer of tall and short grasses, also eating twigs, leaves, shrubs, flowers, fruit and aquatic plants. By grazing and wallowing it keeps floodplain grasslands open. Mynahs and egrets feed on insects around it.

### Social structure
Largely solitary. Cows keep calves close for up to four years; subadults form stable groups; groups of up to 10 rhinos gather at wallows. Bulls hold overlapping home ranges of about 2–8 km² and become intolerant of rivals during breeding.

### Reproduction and life history
Gestation is about 15.7 months, producing one calf of 40–81 kg; birth intervals are 34–51 months. Wild females usually start breeding at about six years and bulls dominate only when much older and larger. In captivity, four rhinos lived past 40 years, the oldest to 47.

### Conservation
Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. From about 100 animals in the early 1900s and roughly 1,870–1,895 in the early 1990s, the population grew to an estimated 4,075 in March 2025 (3,323 in India and 752 in Nepal). More than 70% live in Kaziranga National Park, so a single disaster could hit most of the species. Poaching for horn remains the main threat, along with invasive plants, shrinking grasslands and silting wetlands.

### Special features
- Sharp lower incisor tusks used as the main weapon.
- Single keratin horn, typically about 25 cm, up to about 61 cm.
- Thick hide in deep folds with wart-like bumps.
- Excellent swimmer.
- More than 10 distinct vocalizations and communal dung middens.

### Human interaction
Rhinos raid crops next to protected areas and attack people, killing an average of 3.3 people a year in Nepal over two decades. They are also a flagship of Asian conservation, protected by armed patrols in Chitwan and Kaziranga.

### Genuine fun facts
- Indian rhinos fight mainly with their lower tusks, not their horn.
- The Asian rhinos are the only living rhinos with tusk-like incisors.
- They are excellent swimmers.
- Bulls can spray urine 3–4 m behind them when disturbed.
- Over 10 different calls have been recorded, from honks and bleats to roars and squeak-pants.
- The population rose from about 100 in the early 1900s to more than 4,000 today.
- No wild Indian rhino has ever been weighed; all standard weights come from zoo animals.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Indian rhinoceros is a two-tonne floodplain giant wrapped in thick, deeply folded hide. Unlike African rhinos it fights mainly with sharp lower tusks rather than its single horn, and bull fights can be deadly, making it one of the roster's most heavily built and dangerous herbivores.

### Rich narrative profile
A prime Indian rhino bull looks like a tank built from grey leather: great folds of hide at the neck, shoulders and rump, studded with wart-like bumps, over roughly two tonnes of muscle and bone. A single horn rises from the snout, usually about 25 cm long. The real weapons are hidden until the mouth opens: sharp lower incisor tusks that bulls use to slash one another in fights that are reported as the leading natural cause of death in the species.

Most of the time Indian rhinos graze the tall grasses of the Brahmaputra and Terai floodplains, wallow in pools, swim rivers and keep to home ranges of a few square kilometres. They hear and smell well but see poorly, and a disturbed rhino either crashes away through the grass or charges. People living beside Nepal's parks lose crops and, every year, a few lives to rhinos.

On the ABS scale the Indian rhino sits beside the white rhinoceros at the top of the rhino group. The white rhino keeps a small edge in mass, raw power and horn reach, while the Indian rhino matches it in defense through its heavier folds and adds a slashing tusk system that no African rhino has. Both remain below the common hippopotamus in Attack, and far below elephants in size.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** IRF's 1,800–2,700 kg, ADW's 2,200 kg mean and a zoo-survey male mean of 2,031 kg all rest on captive animals; wild dimorphism may be smaller. 2,100 kg is a middle value.
- **Speed:** the 55 km/h figure comes from a secondary book citation; kept at low-moderate confidence.
- **Horn length:** typical adult about 25 cm vs maxima near 60 cm; ADW's 529 mm average appears to describe long horns and is not used.
- **Lifespan:** only a stated wild maximum and captive records exist.

- **Versus Rhinoceros (white rhino, 2,300 kg; A86 D82):** the white rhino keeps slightly higher Size, Raw Power (88.0 vs 86.0), Weaponry (85.0 vs 84.0) and Attack (86.0 vs 84.0) for its extra mass and much longer horn. - **Versus Black Rhinoceros (1,200 kg; A82 D76):** almost twice the mass, so higher Attack, Defense and Raw Power; the black rhino keeps higher Agility (59.0 vs 54.0).
- **Versus Hippopotamus (1,800 kg; A90 D84):** the hippo keeps higher Attack for its huge canines and gape and slightly higher Defense; the Indian rhino has a faster charge and higher Agility (54.0 vs 48.0).
- **Versus Bengal Tiger (A85):** Attack is nearly level (84.0 vs 85.0), but the rhino's Defense (83.0 vs 61.5) reflects that tigers take calves and only very rarely, in groups, kill adults.
