# Southern Ground Hornbill (*Bucorvus leadbeateri*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Southern Ground Hornbill (also southern ground-hornbill)
- **Scientific name:** *Bucorvus leadbeateri* (Vigors, 1825)
- **Exact animal represented:** the living African species *Bucorvus leadbeateri*, the largest hornbill in the world, a mostly ground-foraging carnivorous bird of southern and eastern African savanna and grassland. The separate Abyssinian ground hornbill (*B. abyssinicus*) of northern Africa is not included.
- **Status:** Living; Vulnerable globally, Endangered in South Africa, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult male, **4.2 kg**, **102.0 cm** total length, about **1.5 m** wingspan. Males are the larger sex with a larger casque (the ridge on top of the bill).
- **Sexual dimorphism:** eight males averaged **4.2 kg** and eight females **3.34 kg**. Published ranges are 3.5–6.2 kg for males and 2.2–4.6 kg for females. Adult males have an all-red face and throat; females have a violet-blue patch on the throat.
- **Population notes:** juveniles lack the red throat until about six years old. Groups of about 2–11 birds share a territory, with one breeding pair and helpers.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The long, heavy bill is its only real weapon. It seizes prey, digs it out of the ground and dung heaps, and handles large, awkward animals. Recorded vertebrate prey includes snakes, lizards, chameleons, tortoises, frogs, mongooses, squirrels, young hares and nestling birds; invertebrates such as grasshoppers and beetles make up much of the daily food. Mammals up to the size of hares are taken. It may also snatch food from birds of prey. No quantified description of how it kills large prey was located, so weapon scoring is based on bill size and the prey list.

### Offensive mechanics
Groups forage together on foot across grassland and open woodland. When one bird finds prey it can call the others with a low bark. The birds walk slowly, scanning and probing, and strike with the bill. There is no stoop, pounce or talon strike; the whole attack is delivered at bill length.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
A walker first. It spends about 70% of the day walking, and it can fly strongly, for example when neighboring groups chase each other in the air or when it flies up to trees. It is not built for quick turning or bursts of speed on the ground.

### Endurance
Birds have been tracked walking up to **10.6 km in a day**. Kruger GPS data (37,060 locations from six groups) showed that groups shift home-range size with the seasons and travel farther between foraging patches in the early wet season. Stamina is scored strong for sustained walking.

### Senses
It hunts by sight in grass, and in experiments it chose the correct baited container using visual cues but not sound cues. Hearing plays a role in group contact calls that carry up to 3 km.

### Intelligence and tactics
Captive-group experiments show that ground hornbills learn new foraging techniques from group members (all birds shown a demonstration opened a puzzle box, while all untrained controls failed), learn and reverse spatial and color associations, and chose correctly when both cups were shown in an exclusion task, with weaker results when only the empty cup was revealed. Breeding requires years of experience as a helper. These results support solid, socially based problem-solving, below the specialist corvids and parrots.

### Intraspecific behavior and aggression
Groups defend territories of up to about 100 km² with loud dawn choruses and aerial chases. In a 46-year database of captive mortalities, fatal aggression was most often aimed at unrelated adult birds during the breeding season. Wild males attack their own reflections in windows, sometimes breaking them.

### Environmental strengths
- Open grassland and savanna woodland with a sparse understory, where prey can be found on foot.
- Large territories with big old trees for nest holes.
- Group foraging, where several birds scan the ground together.

### Weaknesses
- Only about 4.2 kg in absolute mass.
- No talons or claws for gripping; the bill is the single weapon.
- Slow on the ground compared with runners and pouncers.
- No armor; bill strikes require close contact.
- Slow breeding makes populations fragile.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** snakes, lizards, tortoises, frogs, small mammals and young hares on open ground.
- **Mixed:** mongooses and similar small carnivores that fight back; similar-sized ground birds.
- **Bad:** medium and large carnivores, large raptors, ungulates, and any faster animal that can attack from outside bill range.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Walking Hunt Party** — groups of hornbills comb the ground together, and a bird that finds prey can call the others with a low bark. Several heavy bills can then work on snakes, tortoises and small mammals.
2. **Hammer-Bill Strike** — a 17–22 cm bill is used to seize and dig out prey, letting a 4 kg bird handle snakes, tortoises, mongooses and young hares.

### Unique traits
1. **World's Largest Hornbill** — at about 4 kg and 1 m long, it is the heaviest member of the hornbill order, and one of the few birds with prominent eyelashes.
2. **Helper-Run Family** — every breeding pair needs at least two helpers, and birds without years of helper experience fail to breed successfully. Young stay dependent for one to two years, longer than any other bird.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The southern ground hornbill lives from Kenya, Burundi and the southern Democratic Republic of the Congo south through Angola, Zimbabwe, Botswana, Mozambique and northern Namibia to northern and eastern South Africa, where it is widespread but scarce. It prefers grassland and savanna woodland with a sparse understory, from montane grassland to mopane and teak woodland. In South Africa it is now largely confined to reserves and national parks such as Kruger, where GPS-tracked groups favored grassland, open woodland and dense thicket for foraging.

### Diet and ecology
It is carnivorous, eating mainly insects such as grasshoppers and beetles plus frogs, snakes, lizards, chameleons, tortoises, mongooses, squirrels, young hares and nestling birds. It rarely drinks. As a large ground predator it is treated as an apex predator of the savanna ground layer, and its huge territories mean that protecting one group also protects many other species.

### Social structure
Groups of about 2–11 birds share a territory, with one dominant breeding pair and helpers that are usually adult males or young from earlier seasons. Groups keep contact with loud chorus calls heard up to 3 km away and defend territories of up to about 100 km².

### Reproduction and life history
Nests are almost always deep holes in very old trees. One or two eggs are laid; the second chick is 3–14 days younger and usually starves within weeks, so a group rarely raises more than one chick. Incubation is 40–45 days and fledging about 85 days, and the young depend on the group for one to two years. Groups therefore breed successfully only about every third year. Breeding begins around 10 years of age.

### Conservation
The species is listed as **Vulnerable** globally and Endangered in South Africa, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini. Threats include loss of big nest trees and habitat, poisoning, persecution (including for breaking windows), hunting and use in traditional medicine. Conservation programs rear the redundant second chick in captivity and reintroduce groups into former range. Hot, dry weather is linked with worse breeding results.

### Special features
- Massive 17–22 cm bill with a casque.
- Long eyelashes.
- Inflatable red throat pouch in adult males.
- Group chorus audible up to 3 km.
- Obligate cooperative breeding with helpers.

### Human interaction
It is culturally important across southern Africa; in several cultures killing one is taboo. Some birds are killed after breaking windows while attacking their reflections. Kruger National Park lists it as one of its "Big Six" birds, and reintroduction projects work with landowners to restore groups.

### Genuine fun facts
- It is the largest hornbill in the world.
- It is one of the few birds with long eyelashes.
- Its group chorus can be heard up to 3 km away.
- Young birds depend on their family for one to two years, longer than any other bird.
- A group usually raises only one chick about every three years.
- Hornbills that watched a trained group member open a puzzle box all learned to open it, while untrained birds all failed.
- Captive ground hornbills have lived to about 70 years.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Southern Ground Hornbill is the world's largest hornbill, a 4 kg ground-walking predator that hunts snakes, tortoises, small mammals and insects in family groups across African savanna. Its heavy bill is its only weapon, so it lacks the talons, kicks and speed of true raptors, but group foraging and long daily walks make it a persistent hunter.

### Rich narrative profile
The southern ground hornbill hunts on foot. A family group of up to about a dozen birds walks slowly across the grass, spending most of the day on the move and sometimes covering more than 10 km. When one bird finds something, a snake, a tortoise, a nest of chicks or a hiding mongoose, it can call the others with a low bark, and the group's heavy bills do the rest.

Its life is built around the group. Every breeding pair depends on helpers, and birds without years of experience as helpers fail to raise young. Chicks stay dependent for up to two years, and a group raises only about one chick every three years. In experiments, ground hornbills learned new foraging tricks by watching group members and adjusted to changing reward rules, evidence of solid, socially based learning.

In an absolute matchup, the hornbill is a 4 kg bird whose weapon is its bill. It has no talons, no kicking attack like the secretary bird, and no burst speed. It does well against snakes, reptiles and small mammals on open ground, but faster predators and anything larger than a mongoose can simply stay out of bill range.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** ADW's 2.2–4.2 kg range is lower than Wikipedia's male range of 3.5–6.2 kg. The canonical 4.2 kg is the male sample mean.
- **Lifespan:** 50–60 years is an estimate; the 30-year expected life of a fledged bird is used as the more typical wild value.
- **Kill method:** the bill is clearly the weapon, but no study describing how large prey is killed was located.
- **Speed:** no measured running or flight speed; field left at 0.0.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Secretary Bird (4.0 kg; A36.0 D27.0 Ag83.0 St84.0 Sp88.0):** almost the same mass and both kill snakes on foot, but the secretary bird's fast, powerful kicks and long-legged agility put it well above on Attack, Agility and Special. The hornbill's bill and group foraging give it better Intelligence (64.0 vs 61.0).
- **Versus Toucan:** both have oversized bills, but the toucan is under 1 kg, mainly eats fruit and has a light bill. The ground hornbill is several times heavier and fully predatory, so it should sit far above the toucan on Attack and Size.
- **Versus Peacock (5.0 kg; A29.0 Ag70.0):** similar mass; the hornbill's heavier bill and predatory habits earn slightly higher Attack, while the peacock's quicker ground movement keeps it above on Agility.
- **Versus Martial Eagle (4.8 kg; A41.5):** a bird of the same savannas and similar mass, but its talons and aerial strike put it far above in Attack.
- **Anti-inflation check:** group foraging is scored in Tactics and Special, not added to solo Attack.
