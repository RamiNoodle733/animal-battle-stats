# False Killer Whale (*Pseudorca crassidens*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** False killer whale
- **Scientific name:** *Pseudorca crassidens* (Owen, 1846)
- **Exact animal represented:** the false killer whale species, a large oceanic dolphin (family Delphinidae) and the only member of its genus. The name comes from a skull similar to the orca's; *crassidens* means "thick-toothed" (Wikipedia).
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime adult **male**, **550 cm** long and **1,300 kg**.
- **Sex choice:** males are larger, reaching almost 20 ft (6.1 m) against about 16 ft (4.9 m) in females (NOAA Fisheries), and Wikipedia's summary gives males about 10–15% larger with maxima of 6 m and 2,200 kg. The larger male is used.
- **Population notes:** false killer whales live in tropical and subtropical oceans worldwide, generally offshore. Around Hawaiʻi three genetically distinct populations overlap; the main Hawaiian Islands insular population (about 140 animals) is endangered (NOAA). Size varies around the world (Wikipedia).

### Defensive structure
- Blubber and a muscular, streamlined body; no armor. The male's head overhangs the lower jaw more than the female's and flattens with age (NOAA).

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The false killer whale is a large toothed predator with thick, conical teeth. It hunts big, fast fish such as mahi-mahi, wahoo and tuna (Wikipedia), and has been seen shaking prey until the head and guts come off and peeling fish with its teeth (ADW). It also attacks marine mammals: it takes smaller dolphins, has attacked sperm whales in the Galápagos (Palacios & Mate 1996), attacked a humpback calf, and in 2025 was reported hunting two humpback whales with a group of more than 50 (Wikipedia). NOAA notes it often leaps clear of the water when attacking prey.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
False killer whales are fast and agile for their size. Trained animals swam at 8.0 m/s, the same as common dolphins and nearly as fast as bottlenose dolphins (Rohr et al. 2002). They chase prey at high speed at depths of about 300–500 m (NOAA) and leap completely out of the water.

### Endurance
Satellite and time-depth tags on 21 Hawaiian false killer whales showed they spend most time near the surface but make dives beyond 1,000 m, with a record of **1,424 m**; larger individuals tended to dive deeper (Kratofil et al. 2026). Dives can last about 18 minutes (NOAA). Groups range over tens of kilometers (NOAA).

### Senses
Like other toothed whales, the false killer whale echolocates. Experiments with a trained animal showed it hears its own loud clicks about 40 dB quieter than the same sounds played from in front of it, and actively adjusts its hearing so faint echoes stay audible, a kind of automatic gain control (Nachtigall & Supin 2008). Researchers can hear its piercing whistles from about 200 m (ADW).

### Intelligence and tactics
False killer whales are highly social and form strong bonds. They spread out in small subgroups across clusters tens of kilometers wide, which probably helps them find prey; when one captures prey, others converge and the prey may be shared among several animals (NOAA). Females live well beyond their reproductive years (menopause at about 44–55; NOAA), as in orcas and pilot whales. Group attacks on sperm whales and humpbacks show real coordination.

### Sociality and hunting
Clusters in Hawaiʻi may include 40 to 50 animals, and larger groups occur elsewhere (NOAA); groups of hundreds are known (ADW). They associate peacefully with bottlenose dolphins and several other dolphin species, and mass strandings, such as over 800 animals in Argentina in 1946, reflect how tightly groups stay together (Wikipedia).

### Fighting and aggression
They are aggressive predators and, according to Wikipedia's summary, aggressive toward other dolphins in captivity. In fisheries they take fish and bait off lines, which leads to hooking and entanglement (NOAA). Killer whales prey on false killer whales (Wikipedia).

### Environmental strengths
- Deep offshore water where group hunting and deep diving work best.
- Fast chases after large fish.
- Group attacks on larger marine mammals.

### Weaknesses
- No armor; blubber and muscle only.
- A single animal is much smaller than the orca it resembles.
- Much of its power comes from the group, which is absent in a one-on-one matchup.
- Prone to mass strandings and heavily affected by fishing gear.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** large fish, smaller dolphins and marine animals it can outmaneuver and outlast.
- **Mixed:** big sharks and large whales, which it can harass but not easily kill alone.
- **Bad:** orcas, which prey on it, and much larger toothed giants such as the sperm whale when it lacks its group.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Gain-Control Sonar** — the false killer whale echolocates and actively turns down its hearing of its own loud clicks while keeping faint echoes audible, so it can track prey in dark, deep water. In a battle it can find and follow an opponent without light.
2. **Thick-Tooth Tear** — rows of thick conical teeth let it grip, shake and tear large, fast fish and bite at the bodies of whales. It is the main damage tool of a large, fast predator.

### Unique traits
1. **Prey-Sharing Clusters** — false killer whales spread out in small groups across tens of kilometers, then converge when one catches prey, which is often shared among several animals.
2. **Deep-Water Sprinter** — tagged Hawaiian animals dove to 1,424 m, and trained animals sprinted at 8 m/s, combining depth and speed in one large dolphin.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
False killer whales live in tropical and subtropical oceans worldwide, generally in deep offshore waters over 3,300 feet (1,000 m) deep (NOAA). Some populations, such as the main Hawaiian Islands insular population, stay close to islands, rarely more than 70 km from shore.

### Diet and ecology
They are top predators of large fish and squid, including mahi-mahi, wahoo and tuna (Wikipedia; NOAA), and sometimes attack other marine mammals, from smaller dolphins to sperm whales and humpbacks (Palacios & Mate 1996; Wikipedia). Around Hawaiʻi they dive mostly within the upper ocean but regularly go below 1,000 m, diving deeper by day and at full moon (Kratofil et al. 2026).

### Social structure
False killer whales are gregarious and form strong, long-term social bonds. They travel in small subgroups linked into larger clusters and often share captured prey (NOAA). Some individuals stay together for at least 15 years, and groups appear to be centered on females (Wikipedia).

### Reproduction and life history
Females mature at 8 to 11 years and males 8 to 10 years later. Gestation lasts 11 to 16 months and nursing 1.5 to 2 years; births are about 7 years apart. Females enter menopause at about 44 to 55 years (NOAA).

### Conservation
The species is listed as **Near Threatened** on the IUCN Red List (2018 assessment, as summarized by Wikipedia). In the US, only the main Hawaiian Islands insular population is listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act; it numbers about 140 animals and has declined about 3.5% a year for at least a decade, with fishery interactions a leading threat (NOAA).

### Special features
- Echolocation with active control of hearing during clicks.
- Thick conical teeth.
- Long, S-shaped flippers with a distinct hump on the leading edge (NOAA).
- Strong social bonds, prey sharing and post-reproductive females.
- Deep dives beyond 1,000 m.

### Human interaction
False killer whales take fish and bait from fishing lines, and hooking and entanglement are among the main threats to them (NOAA). They are also taken in drive hunts in some Japanese villages and have been kept in aquariums (Wikipedia).

### Genuine fun facts
- The name comes from a skull that looks like an orca's, but it is a large dolphin.
- *Crassidens* means "thick-toothed".
- When one false killer whale catches a fish, others often gather round and share it.
- A tagged false killer whale dove to 1,424 m.
- Females can live decades after they stop reproducing, as in orcas.
- Over 800 false killer whales stranded together in Argentina in 1946.

### Concise site-ready summary
The false killer whale is a 1.3-tonne, thick-toothed oceanic dolphin that hunts tuna and mahi-mahi at speed, shares its catch and has attacked sperm whales and humpbacks in groups. Fast, deep-diving and armed with biosonar, it is dangerous in a pack, but one animal is far smaller than the orca it resembles.

### Rich narrative profile
The false killer whale looks like a slimmer, all-dark orca, and it hunts like a smaller one. A big male is about 5.5 m long and 1.3 tonnes, with thick conical teeth and a streamlined body that has been clocked at 8 m/s. It chases large, fast fish such as tuna and mahi-mahi, often leaping clear of the water at the moment of attack, and tears them apart with its teeth.

What makes it formidable is teamwork. False killer whales spread out in small groups across tens of kilometers of ocean, then converge when one finds prey, and the catch is often shared. Groups have attacked sperm whales in the Galápagos and have been reported hunting humpbacks. They echolocate, and experiments show they actively adjust their hearing while clicking so that faint echoes stay clear.

In a one-on-one battle the picture is more modest. A single false killer whale has no armor and is a fraction of an orca's size, and orcas prey on it. Its ratings therefore sit well above most marine mammals of its size in Attack, Agility and Intelligence, but clearly below the orca in every category.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** NOAA (up to ~1,360 kg), ADW (up to 1,842 kg) and Wikipedia (up to 2,200 kg) differ, likely by region and method. The canonical 1,300 kg is a conservative large-male figure.
- **Lifespan:** only maximum estimated ages are published; the canonical 58 years is a maximum, not a mean.
- **Attacks on large whales:** the sperm whale attack is documented in a paper whose abstract was not accessible here; the 2025 humpback reports come from Wikipedia's summary of news accounts.
- **Bite force:** no measurement exists; none is invented.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Orca (5 t; A94 D78 Ag84 St88 I99 Sp94):** the false killer whale has a quarter of the mass, smaller teeth, less documented tactical range and is itself orca prey, so every headline sits below the orca: Attack 72.0, Defense 57.0, Agility 83.0, Stamina 82.0, Intelligence 88.0, Special 87.0.
- **Versus Bottlenose Dolphin (260 kg; A55 D50 Ag88 St76 I94 Sp92):** the false killer whale is five times heavier with heavier teeth and attacks whales, so Attack (72.0) and Defense (57.0) are clearly higher; the smaller, better-studied bottlenose keeps higher Agility, Intelligence and Special.
- **Versus Leopard Seal (420 kg; A70 D55 Ag78):** both are strong-toothed marine predators; the false killer whale is heavier, faster and a group hunter, so slightly higher Attack and Agility.
- **Versus Narwhal (1.2 t; A61 D59) and Beluga (1.5 t; A61 D66):** similar mass, but the false killer whale is a far more active predator of large prey, so Attack is higher while Defense is similar or lower.
- **Anti-inflation check:** group hunting is credited in Tactics and Intelligence, not in the single-animal Attack score. No bite force is assumed.
