# Caucasian Shepherd Dog (*Canis familiaris*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Caucasian Shepherd Dog (Caucasian Ovcharka, Kavkazskaya Ovcharka, Caucasian Mountain Dog)
- **Scientific name:** *Canis familiaris* Linnaeus, 1758 (domestic dog; Caucasian Shepherd Dog breed, FCI standard No. 328)
- **Exact animal represented:** the FCI-standard Caucasian Shepherd Dog, the breed standardized in the Soviet Union from Caucasus mountain and southern Russian steppe guardian dogs, not the separate Georgian Shepherd or Armenian Gampr landraces.
- **Living / extinct:** Living (domestic breed).
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult male, **60.0 kg**, **72.0 cm** at the withers.
- **Sex choice:** the FCI standard sets a male minimum of 68 cm and 50 kg, with a desirable height of 72–75 cm, and says males are "more massive, bigger and often shorter in body than females". Females: minimum 64 cm and 45 kg.
- **Population notes:** the breed was standardized in the Soviet Union from about the 1920s and accepted by the FCI in 1984 under Soviet patronage; Russia is its patron country today. Regional types differ: the UKC and Wikipedia describe more massive dogs in the trans-Caucasus regions and leggier, often short-coated dogs in the steppe; Wikipedia notes that Azerbaijani dogs are taller and lighter, Dagestani dogs smaller, and dogs of Ingushetia and Chechnya very large. Three coat lengths are accepted by the UKC.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- FCI: male minimum **50 kg**.
- Wikipedia (tertiary): usually **45–70 kg**.
- AKC (tertiary): **99–170 lb (44.9–77.1 kg)**, sexes combined.
- **Canonical:** **60.0 kg**, a well-conditioned male in the middle of the usual range, well above the FCI minimum but below the heaviest dogs.
- **Confidence:** Moderate; no field weight survey was located.

### Height
- FCI: males desirable **72–75 cm**, minimum 68 cm. UKC: male minimum 25 in (63.5 cm).
- **Canonical:** **72.0 cm**.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Length
- FCI: body length exceeds withers height by **3–8%**.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 76.0 cm**, a derived trunk length for the 72 cm male at about 5% longer than tall. This is **not** head-body length.
- **Confidence:** Low-moderate; derived from the standard's ratio.

### Speed
- No instrumented speed measurement was located.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0** (no reliable measurement).

### Lifespan
- Wikipedia summarizes a 2020 source giving **10–11 years**, and McMillan et al. (2024) finding a median of **5.4 years**, the lowest of the UK breeds studied, but from only 22 dogs.
- AKC (tertiary): **10–12 years**.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 10.0**; the 5.4 year figure is flagged but not used because of its tiny sample.
- **Confidence:** Low-moderate.

### Bite force
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No measured bite force for the breed was located. Popular claims circulate without a traceable study.
- Measured dog bite data are forces: Lindner et al. (1995) 13–1,394 N (mean 256 N) in 22 pet dogs up to 55 kg; Ellis et al. (2008) 147–3,417 N in anesthetized dogs. Neither includes this breed or supports a PSI value.

### Coat and defensive structure
- FCI: straight, coarse, stand-off coat with a well-developed undercoat; guard coat and undercoat each **at least 5 cm** long; longer coat forms a mane around the neck, "brushes" on the ears and "trousers" on the thighs.
- FCI: skin "thick, sufficiently elastic, without any folds and wrinkles".
- No armor, but the long mane and thick skin give the best natural padding over the neck among the dogs in this batch.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary weapon and offensive mechanics
The weapon is the bite, delivered from a large, massive, broad-cheeked head (FCI) on a 60 kg frame. Like other mountain guardians, it confronts intruders with warning, posture and a charge before biting. Its mass lets it push and hold, but it has no second weapon system.

### Guarding role
The FCI states that Caucasian Shepherd Dogs were historically used to guard herds, flocks and dwellings from predators, and lists its use today as a guard and watchdog. Wikipedia adds that they defended sheep mainly from wolves and sheep thieves, were used as bear-hunting dogs, guarded factories and government sites in the Soviet Union, and still work as prison guard dogs in Russia. No field study measuring its effect on wolf predation was located. A US field study of other large guardian breeds (Kangal, Karakachan, Transmontano and "white dogs") found similar responses to wolves across breeds, which argues against rating any one guardian breed far above the others on reputation.

### Defense and toughness
Its main defensive assets are mass, heavy bone, thick elastic skin and a long double coat with a neck mane. These cushion bites to the neck and shoulders better than the short coats of working shepherds and bull breeds. There is still no armor.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
The UKC calls it powerful and athletic for its size, and steppe types are leggier, but it is a heavy mountain-type molossoid (FCI Group 2, Section 2.2). It is slower to turn and accelerate than the Kangal and far below wild canids.

### Endurance
The FCI credits the breed with the endurance to work "in all kinds of climatic conditions including the most severe ones". Its coat suits long cold nights on guard. No measured endurance data were located.

### Senses
The FCI lists "sharply developed hearing" and "good sight" among the qualities kept by breeders, alongside normal canid smell.

### Intelligence and tactics
An independent guardian that decides for itself when to warn and when to engage (FCI: steady, active, self-confident, fearless and independent). It is not a cooperative hunter.

### Intraspecific fighting
Males are strongly territorial toward strange dogs; no quantitative data were located.

### Predator defense
Bred to face wolves and sheep thieves in the Caucasus. Anecdotes of dogs killing wolves circulate but were not traced to documented studies and are not used.

### Aggression and ferocity
A serious guardian: the AKC says it "should never be taken lightly", and the FCI calls it fearless. It is more willing to press a confrontation than the deterrence-first Kangal, but less athletic.

### Environmental strengths
- Cold mountains and steppe, helped by the long double coat.
- Guarding a fixed flock, yard or compound.

### Weaknesses
- Heavy and slow to turn; the least agile dog in this batch.
- No armor; one bite weapon.
- Heat is a poor fit for the heavy coat.
- Possibly short-lived, although lifespan data are thin.
- Far outmatched by bears, big cats and large ungulates.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** coyotes, jackals, dingoes and other light canids; medium unarmored mammals; single intruders that can be faced head-on.
- **Mixed:** a single wolf, hyenas, leopards, other heavy guardian breeds.
- **Bad:** bears, lions, tigers, large ungulates, crocodilians, armored or venomous opponents.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Maned Neck Guard** — a mane of long, coarse coat over thick, elastic skin covers the neck and shoulders, the targets wolves and other dogs aim for. It blunts bites there without being true armor.
2. **Frontline Charge** — meets intruders head-on with warning, posture and a heavy charge, the style shepherds kept it for against wolves and thieves. Its 60 kg mass makes the charge hard for lighter animals to stand.

### Unique traits
1. **Bear-Like Mountain Guardian** — a massive, broad-headed dog whose stand-off coat on the cheeks and backskull gives it the bear-like look the UKC standard describes.
2. **Soviet-Standardized Caucasus Dog** — shaped in the 20th-century Soviet Union from the flock guardians of Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Dagestan and the southern Russian steppe, and accepted by the FCI in 1984.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Native to the Caucasus Mountains and the steppe of southern Russia, especially Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Dagestan. It is now kept in Russia, Europe and North America as a guardian and companion.

### Diet
Fed by its owners on meat, dog food and household scraps. It does not hunt for its living.

### Ecology
In its working role it guards flocks and villages against wolves and thieves. In the Soviet Union and Russia it has also been used to guard factories, government sites and prisons.

### Social structure
Bonds closely with its family and flock; suspicious of strangers and strange dogs. It works independently rather than under constant command.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeding is managed by people. No breed-specific growth or maturity data were located; the FCI standard notes that males are more massive and often shorter in body than females.

### Conservation
Not applicable as a wild species; it is a **Domesticated** breed.

### Special features
- Large, massive, broad-cheeked head.
- Coarse stand-off coat with undercoat, each at least 5 cm, forming a neck mane and "trousers".
- Thick, elastic skin without folds.
- Body 3–8% longer than tall; heavy bone.
- Fearless, independent guarding temperament.

### Human interaction
A serious guardian that needs experienced handling. The AKC warns that it "should never be taken lightly". It is prized in Russia and the Caucasus as a flock and property guard.

### Genuine fun facts
- The FCI accepted the breed in 1984 under the patronage of the Soviet Union; Russia is its patron country today.
- The breed standard requires its guard coat and undercoat to be at least 5 cm long.
- Soviet state kennels used Caucasian Shepherd Dogs to guard factories and government facilities.
- They still work as prison guard dogs in Russia.
- Dogs from different parts of the Caucasus look quite different: tall and light in Azerbaijan, compact in Dagestan, very large in Chechnya and Ingushetia.
- A 2024 UK study found a median lifespan of just 5.4 years, but it included only 22 dogs.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Caucasian Shepherd Dog is a massive 60 kg mountain guardian from the Caucasus, with a long maned coat and thick skin. It fights by standing its ground and charging head-on, which makes it one of the strongest and best-padded dogs on the roster, but it is slow to turn, has no armor and is still far below bears and big cats.

### Rich narrative profile
For centuries shepherds in the Caucasus Mountains kept big, heavy-coated dogs to defend their flocks from wolves and thieves. In the 20th century Soviet breeders gathered these regional dogs, from Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Dagestan and the southern Russian steppe, into a single breed, the Caucasian Ovcharka, which the FCI accepted in 1984. A mature male weighs around 60 kg and stands about 72 cm, with a massive broad head and a coarse coat that forms a mane around its neck.

Its approach to a fight is to hold its ground. It warns, postures and then charges, relying on mass and a heavy bite. The mane and thick skin over its neck blunt the bites that decide most dog and wolf fights, and its coat lets it work through the harshest winters. Soviet state kennels used it to guard factories and government sites, and it still guards prisons in Russia.

That makes it the heaviest and best-protected dog in this batch, rated slightly above the Kangal and Tibetan Mastiff in Attack and Defense. But mass comes at a cost: it is the slowest dog here to turn and accelerate, and it has no armor. Against wolves and hyenas it is an even contest; against bears, big cats and large hoofed animals it is outmatched.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Kangal (50 kg; A53 D44 Ag59 St68 I63 Sp47):** 10 kg heavier with a longer maned coat and thicker skin, so slightly higher Attack and clearly higher Defense; less agile and slightly lower Stamina and Intelligence.
- **Versus Tibetan Mastiff (55 kg; A51 D47 Ag52 St70 I62 Sp56):** heavier and more willing to engage, so higher Attack and Defense; the mastiff keeps higher Special for its documented high-altitude physiology.
- **Versus Gray Wolf (50 kg; A55 D41 Ag66 St89 I78 Sp64):** heavier and better padded, so higher Defense and Raw Power; one point lower in Attack because the wolf is a proven large-prey killer, and far lower in Agility, Stamina and Intelligence.
- **Versus Rottweiler (50 kg; A52 D43):** heavier with a protective coat, so higher Attack and Defense; less agile.
