# Great Horned Owl research report

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Roster name:** Great Horned Owl
- **Exact taxon:** *Bubo virginianus* (J. F. Gmelin, 1788), family Strigidae.
- **Living:** Yes.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy fully mature **female**, **1.7 kg**, **55.0 cm body length**, representing the larger sex without using a record individual.
- **Why female:** reverse sexual size dimorphism is well established. Animal Diversity Web (ADW) gives mean mass about 1.7 kg for females versus 1.3 kg for males. Binder Park Zoo gives female 1.0-2.5 kg versus male 0.68-1.45 kg.
- **Population variation:** this is a geographically variable species. ADW gives 45.7-63.5 cm overall length and notes larger bodies/wingspans at northern latitudes, with marked subspecific plumage and size variation. The canonical animal is therefore a representative large adult female, not the 2.5 kg upper extreme.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
ADW reports mean female mass **1.7 kg**, male **1.3 kg**. Broader institutional ranges extend to about **2.5 kg**. **Canonical `weight_kg = 1.7`.** Confidence: high-moderate.

### Body length and wingspan
ADW reports **45.7-63.5 cm** body length and **127-152.4 cm** wingspan. Other institutional summaries give broadly similar body-length ranges. **Canonical `length_cm = 55.0`**, a representative adult-female body length rather than maximum. Representative wingspan for profile prose: roughly **140 cm**, not a schema field. Confidence: high-moderate.

### Height
A standardized standing-height measurement is not consistently reported and would be posture-dependent. **Canonical `height_cm = 0.0`** rather than inventing a value.

### Flight speed
Squam Lakes Natural Science Center reports flight speed about **20-40 mph**. This is an institutional husbandry/natural-history figure, not a controlled physiological maximum. For a top-speed schema field, **40 mph = 17.9 m/s** is retained with **moderate-low confidence** and explicitly separated from Agility. **Canonical `speed_mps = 17.9`.** Future telemetry should supersede this if a species-specific instrumented maximum is found.

### Lifespan
ADW reports average wild life expectancy around **13 years** and a wild record around **28 years**. Northwest Territories government material reports longevity **21-28 years** as an upper-longevity context, while older USFS material reports a much lower historical banding record. **Canonical `lifespan_years = 13.0`**, representing typical wild longevity rather than record survival. Confidence: moderate.

### Bite force
No defensible species-specific beak-bite PSI measurement was found. The beak is useful for tearing and processing prey, but the feet/talons are the principal killing tools. Popular web claims for owl grip pressure are not promoted because pressure requires defensible force and contact-area measurement. **Canonical `bite_force_psi = 0.0`.** Confidence: high that unsupported PSI should not be used.

### Natural weapons and defenses
- **Feet/talons:** primary capture and killing system. Four strongly curved talons per foot seize and puncture prey. No fabricated talon-force PSI is used.
- **Hooked beak:** tears flesh and assists prey processing after the feet secure the target.
- **Broad wings:** enable low-speed control, short gliding attacks and aerial repositioning.
- **Plumage:** soft owl flight feathers reduce flight noise and aid approach; feathers provide insulation but little meaningful armor against trauma.
- **Facial disc / auditory system:** channels sound toward the ears and supports nocturnal localization.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary offense
The great horned owl is an aerial ambush predator. It typically watches from a perch, makes a short quiet approach, extends the feet and uses the talons to seize and puncture prey. The beak is secondary to the feet in initial capture. USFS diet synthesis documents prey including rabbits, rodents, skunks, ducks, geese, pheasants, grouse, reptiles and other vertebrates. A North Dakota study recorded **2,900 prey items at 22 nesting areas**, with wetland-dependent prey making up 76% of prey biomass and birds 84% of biomass in that local breeding sample. This is strong evidence for dietary breadth, not evidence that every owl routinely kills the largest listed prey.

### Secondary offense
The hooked beak tears tissue after restraint. Wing-assisted balance and rapid foot presentation make the owl dangerous during a brief contact window. Against similarly sized birds and small mammals, the combination of surprise, puncture weapons and control is more important than raw muscular force.

### Defense and durability
Defense is primarily avoidance. Flight, concealment, nocturnality, camouflage and threat displays reduce contact. Feathers and a light avian skeleton are not armor. A grounded owl that cannot disengage is physically vulnerable to larger mammalian predators or crushing attacks.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Broad rounded wings support controlled low-speed flight, short glides, braking and attack-angle changes in cluttered habitat. Feet are effective for perching and prey handling but terrestrial running is not a combat strength. The species deserves high Agility for three-dimensional control and precision, not merely because an institutional source lists 40 mph flight.

### Endurance and stamina
Great horned owls are sit-and-wait or short-flight hunters rather than long-distance pursuit specialists. Resident territorial life can demand repeated nightly movements, but there is no basis for treating them like migratory endurance flyers. Stamina is therefore moderate rather than elite.

### Senses
Forward-facing eyes provide binocular vision. ADW cites visual-acuity research and describes strong night navigation and prey detection. The facial disc and auditory system provide strong acoustic localization. The combined nocturnal sensory package is among the species' most battle-relevant advantages.

### Intelligence and tactics
The owl flexibly exploits many habitats and prey types. It chooses perches, approaches from concealment, times short attacks, and can switch among terrestrial, avian, aquatic-edge and arboreal prey. Territorial disputes can be severe and ADW notes that they may be fatal. This supports meaningful tactical competence without equating predatory specialization with primate-level cognition.

### Fighting and predation behavior
Great horned owls are mainly solitary outside breeding and strongly territorial. Hooting advertises territory and may prevent physical conflict, but disputes can escalate. Their predatory niche includes other raptors and owls as well as mammals. A peer-reviewed Mexican report documents predation on hooded skunk, reinforcing that chemically defended prey can still be taken.

### Environmental strengths
- Darkness and broken woodland favor acoustic localization and quiet approach.
- Perches provide gravity-assisted attack angles and reconnaissance.
- Forest edges, wetlands, farmland and urban mosaics support the species' generalist hunting style.
- Three-dimensional space lets the owl disengage from many ground opponents.

### Environmental weaknesses
- Open daylight reduces concealment and surprise.
- Grounded combat removes much of the owl's positional advantage.
- Large mammals and heavily armored animals exceed the talons' practical control scale.
- Wings are large exposed structures that can be damaged during grappling.
- High speed alone does not compensate for low body mass against much larger roster animals.

### Matchup archetypes
**Best:** rodents, rabbits, small birds, other small vertebrates, and opponents vulnerable to surprise talon strikes.

**Worst:** large mammals, heavily armored reptiles, large constrictors if contact becomes a grapple, and durable opponents too massive for the feet to control.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Silent Talon Drop** - soft flight plumage and controlled short-glide attacks reduce warning time before a talon-first strike.
2. **Night-Sound Lock** - facial-disc-assisted hearing and forward sensory focus allow precise prey localization in darkness and clutter.

### Unique traits
1. **Tiger-Owl Generalist** - an unusually broad vertebrate prey spectrum lets the owl switch tactics across mammals, birds, reptiles and wetland-edge prey.
2. **Horned Territorial Sentinel** - resident pairs use conspicuous hooting and persistent territorial defense, with disputes capable of escalating to lethal contact.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
*B. virginianus* occupies an enormous American range and uses forests, woodland/open-field mosaics, deserts, swamps, marshes, mangroves, farmland and urban environments. ADW reports occurrence from sea level to about 3,353 m elevation. Most populations are resident, although severe northern winters can produce movements.

### Diet and ecology
This is a highly generalist carnivore. Mammals often dominate, but local ecology can shift the diet dramatically. USFS lists rabbits, rodents, skunks, waterfowl, pheasants, grouse, reptiles and other prey. The North Dakota breeding study is especially useful because 2,900 identified prey items showed that local wetland birds could dominate biomass.

### Social structure
Adults are generally solitary outside nesting and maintain territories. Pairs communicate by hooting and defend breeding space. Social coordination exists in pair and parental contexts, but there is no basis for pack-hunting bonuses.

### Reproduction and life history
Great horned owls often use existing nests, cavities, ledges or other structures rather than building elaborate nests. Clutches are commonly small. Females incubate while males provision food. Government material indicates many females are not sexually active until about two years old.

### Conservation
The species is broadly considered **Least Concern** and remains widespread, but local mortality can arise from vehicles, poisoning, electrocution and other human pressures. Conservation status does not imply that all regional populations are equally secure.

### Major adaptations
Broad wings, soft flight feathers, forward-facing eyes, strong hearing, a sound-directing facial disc, grasping talons, a hooked beak, camouflage and flexible habitat use combine into a coherent nocturnal ambush system.

### Human interaction
Great horned owls readily occupy human-modified landscapes where trees and prey remain available. Rodenticides can indirectly poison raptors that consume contaminated prey. Their territorial and predatory capability warrants respectful distance around nests, but they are not normally a human-directed predator.

### Genuine fun facts
- The visible "horns" are feather tufts, not the owl's ears.
- Females are larger than males, reversing the common mammalian pattern of male-biased size.
- The species can prey on skunks despite their chemical defense.
- A North Dakota study identified 2,900 prey items from only 22 nesting areas.
- Great horned owls commonly appropriate nests built by other large birds.
- Wild longevity can greatly exceed the roughly 13-year typical figure, with records approaching three decades.

### Concise site summary
A large, highly adaptable American owl, the Great Horned Owl combines precise talons, quiet low-light flight and exceptional nocturnal targeting. Its absolute power is limited by a roughly 1.7 kg body, but surprise, aerial control and sensory specialization make it extremely dangerous to prey near its own scale.

### Rich narrative profile
The Great Horned Owl is not a miniature eagle that wins through brute force. Its advantage is a tightly integrated ambush system. A female can wait motionless above a clearing, detect movement with strong hearing and forward vision, descend with little warning, brake precisely and present both feet at the moment of contact. Against rabbit-sized prey or another bird, the result can be a fast puncture-and-restraint event rather than a prolonged wrestling match.

That specialization must still obey ABS absolute scaling. A representative female weighs about 1.7 kg. Feathers are not armor, hollow/light avian construction is vulnerable to crushing trauma, and no reliable beak or talon PSI value was found. The owl therefore belongs far below wolves, great apes and megafauna in Raw Power, Attack and Defense. Its elite scores instead belong in Maneuverability, Senses and Special, where nocturnal localization, quiet flight and three-dimensional positioning genuinely compete with the roster's best specialists.

## 11. Confidence, conflicts and cross-animal normalization

### Confidence summary
- **Taxon:** high.
- **Female mass / body length:** high-moderate.
- **Flight speed:** moderate-low because the available institutional range is not an instrumented maximum.
- **Typical lifespan:** moderate; record longevity is much higher and kept separate.
- **Bite/talon PSI:** unresolved and deliberately not fabricated.
- **Diet/hunting:** high, with government and peer-reviewed support.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- Body size varies strongly by geography and sex. The 1.7 kg female mean is preferable to treating a 2.5 kg upper extreme as normal.
- Longevity sources differ because some report average expectancy while others report banding records or upper longevity. The canonical field uses 13.0 years as typical wild life, not the record.
- Flight-speed pages repeat 20-40 mph, but controlled maximum-speed evidence was not found. The 17.9 m/s field is therefore explicitly lower confidence and should be replaced if telemetry/biomechanics provides a stronger maximum.
- Popular 300-500 PSI talon claims were excluded because the source chain did not establish a defensible species-specific pressure experiment.

### Cross-animal normalization
- **Golden Eagle:** Great Horned Owl should be lower in absolute Attack and Raw Power because the canonical eagle is much heavier and more specialized for large diurnal prey; the owl can exceed it in nocturnal sensory specialization but not brute force.
- **Goose:** the owl has much stronger weaponry and predatory tactics despite being lighter; Goose retains stronger migration-oriented endurance.
- **Crow:** the owl is physically more dangerous and better armed, while corvid cognition should remain higher.
- **Gray Wolf / Gorilla:** these provide critical absolute-scale controls. Owl proportional killing performance must not leak into mammal-scale Raw Power, Attack or Defense.
- **Goliath Birdeater / Emperor Scorpion:** the owl's aerial control and vertebrate-scale talons put it far above large arthropods in absolute attack, while specialized venomous arthropods can still outrank it in narrow Special mechanisms.
