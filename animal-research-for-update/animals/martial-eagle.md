# Martial Eagle (*Polemaetus bellicosus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Martial Eagle
- **Scientific name:** *Polemaetus bellicosus* (Daudin, 1800)
- **Exact animal represented:** the living African species *Polemaetus bellicosus*, the only member of its genus, a large booted eagle (feathered tarsi) of open and lightly wooded savanna south of the Sahara.
- **Status:** Living; Endangered.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult female, **4.8 kg**, **88.0 cm** total length, about **2.1 m** wingspan. Females are the heavier, stronger-footed sex and take the largest prey.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** females average about 10% larger in linear size but roughly 36% heavier. Published female samples average 4.67 kg (7 birds, Maasai Mara) and 4.95 kg (7 birds, southern Africa), within a 4.45–6.5 kg female range. Males weigh about 2.2–3.8 kg, with samples averaging 3.17–3.45 kg. Female tarsi are longer (114–130 mm versus 97–118 mm) and the feet more robust.
- **Population notes:** unsexed samples average about 3.9–4.2 kg, so the canonical female sits above the species mean but well below the 6.5 kg female maximum. Adult plumage is reached only around the seventh year; adults take larger and more dangerous prey than immatures.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- Wikipedia (summarizing field samples): females 4.45–6.5 kg, with means of 4.67 kg and 4.95 kg in two samples; males 2.2–3.8 kg; unsexed means 3.93–4.23 kg.
- The Peregrine Fund: **3–6.2 kg** for the species. Animal Diversity Web (ADW): **3.1–6.2 kg**.
- **Canonical:** **4.8 kg** mature female, between the two published female means.
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high.

### Length and wingspan
- Total length **78–96 cm**, averaging about 85.5 cm; the short tail keeps total length modest for the wingspan.
- Wingspan **188–240 cm** (ADW gives up to 260 cm, which Wikipedia treats as possibly unsubstantiated). Ten wild birds measured averaged **211.9 cm**, placing the species about fourth in average wingspan among living eagles.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 88.0 cm** for a female (about 10% larger than males in linear size).
- **Confidence:** High for range; moderate for the sex-specific canonical.

### Height
- No standardized standing-height measurement was found. **Canonical `height_cm`: 0.0.**

### Speed
- The species hunts mainly by a long, shallow stoop from a high soar, but no instrumented stoop or flight speed was located. **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0.**

### Lifespan
- ADW: average wild life expectancy about **14 years**; one bird was recaptured **25 years** after banding.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 14.0.**
- **Confidence:** Moderate.

### Talons, legs and bill
- Hallux (rear killing claw) samples: unsexed mean **51.1 mm** (Tsavo East); **47.4 mm** in 6 males and **56.2 mm** in 7 females. These are the largest talons of any booted eagle except the crowned eagle, and similar to the golden eagle's 51.7 mm mean.
- The inner front claw is unusually large and can approach the hallux in size.
- The tarsus is the longest of any booted eagle, useful for striking prey in long grass.
- Mean culmen (bill) length 43.7 mm, comparable to the golden eagle; gape is relatively wide, suited to swallowing large pieces.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No bill-force or grip-force measurement exists.

### Defensive structure
- No armor. Protection is feathers, wariness, and distance: it hunts from high soar and avoids people.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The feet are the weapon. Large, curved talons (female hallux about 5.6 cm), an oversized inner claw and the longest tarsus of any booted eagle let it strike and hold prey on the ground, often in grass. The bill tears food after the kill. Most studies place mean prey mass between 1 and 5 kg: about 2.26 kg in the largest dietary study (Cape Province, dominated by 2.1 kg Cape hares), 2.31 kg in Tsavo East, and in the Maasai Mara 1.98 kg for males and 3.74 kg for females. Its prey range spans roughly 0.2 kg to about 7.5 kg, and ADW reports kills up to 35 kg, which should be treated as an exceptional upper claim.

The strongest evidence of weapon power is what it chooses to attack. Wikipedia's account lists monitor lizards, venomous snakes, jackals and medium-sized wild cats among its prey. A 2024 paper documented seven records of martial eagles killing or attempting to kill lion cubs in Kenya's greater Mara region, nine cubs in total, most at least partly eaten. The Peregrine Fund notes that it has taken spotted eagle-owls and white-headed vultures.

### Offensive mechanics
Its signature attack starts from a high soar. After spotting prey, it descends in a long, shallow stoop, or parachutes down steeply when the target is in a more enclosed space, and hits before the prey sees it coming. It may hover briefly, which is unusual for an eagle this large, and sometimes hunts from a perch. Vervet monkeys give a specific eagle alarm call chiefly to martial eagles and respond by looking up and diving into cover.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
It is a master soarer with long, broad wings, but ADW describes it as poor at maneuvering, which helps explain why it avoids dense forest. Agility is scored high for open-country stoops and controlled strikes, not for tight twisting pursuit through cover.

### Endurance
It spends an exceptional share of the day on the wing, soaring so high that binoculars are often needed to see it. GPS-tagged territorial adults in Kruger National Park held home ranges averaging about **108 km²**, and non-territorial "floater" adults ranged over about **44,000 km²**. Stamina is scored strong for soaring endurance.

### Senses
Vision is its main sense. It has been reported to detect prey from as far as 5–6 km. That figure comes from secondary summaries rather than a controlled acuity test, so senses are scored elite but just below animals with instrumented vision records.

### Intelligence and tactics
It switches between shallow stoops, steep drops, hovering and perch hunting depending on terrain and prey, and adults take dangerous prey more often than immatures, which suggests skill improves with experience. It is shy and evasive around people, which helps it survive. Tactics are scored solid for a raptor.

### Intraspecific behavior and aggression
Adults hold large territories, and some pairs have kept the same territory for years (The Peregrine Fund). Its name means "war-like", referring to its bold hunting. It commits to prey that can fight back, such as venomous snakes, monitor lizards and small carnivores.

### Environmental strengths
- Open savanna, wooded grassland and thornveld, with open sky above for soaring and spotting.
- Areas near rivers with higher tree cover for nests and perches (Kruger GPS preference model).
- Surprise attacks on prey in the open that cannot see the eagle at the distance the eagle sees them.

### Weaknesses
- About 4.8 kg in absolute mass.
- No armor; light avian skeleton.
- Less maneuverable in dense forest and cluttered cover.
- Loses most of its advantage once grounded or pinned by a heavier grappler.
- Relies on surprise; a prepared, alert opponent removes its best attack.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** hares, hyraxes, game birds, monitor lizards, snakes, small carnivores surprised in the open.
- **Mixed:** similar-sized raptors, medium carnivores such as jackals and small cats, where the first strike decides the fight.
- **Bad:** large carnivores, great apes, armored animals, large ungulates, and any heavy grappler that survives the first strike.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **High-Soar Stoop** — the eagle circles high enough to be hard to see, then comes down in a long, shallow stoop or a steep parachute drop and hits prey before it can react. In open terrain this almost guarantees the first strike.
2. **Snake-and-Carnivore Takedown** — large talons, an oversized inner claw and the longest tarsus of any booted eagle let it strike from arm's length at dangerous prey such as venomous snakes, monitor lizards, jackals and lion cubs.

### Unique traits
1. **Africa's Widest-Winged Eagle** — ten wild-measured birds averaged about 2.12 m in wingspan, about fourth among living eagles and the largest in Africa.
2. **The Vervet's Eagle Alarm** — vervet monkeys give a distinct eagle alarm call mainly to martial eagles, and young monkeys learn to aim it at the right predator.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The martial eagle lives across sub-Saharan Africa in open and semi-open savanna, thornveld and wooded grassland with scattered large trees. It avoids dense forest. It has been recorded up to about 3,000 m but residents rarely exceed 1,500 m. It is most common in large protected areas such as Kruger and Kgalagadi in South Africa and Etosha in Namibia. In Kruger, GPS-tracked eagles preferred areas near rivers with more tree cover.

### Diet and ecology
It is an opportunistic apex predator of mammals, birds and reptiles. Hares dominate in the Cape (about 53% of prey in the largest study), and rock hyraxes elsewhere. Other prey include larger birds (including spotted eagle-owls and white-headed vultures), monitor lizards, venomous snakes, striped polecats, jackals, medium-sized wild cats and, rarely, lion cubs. Mean prey mass is usually 1–5 kg. It rarely scavenges, but it is still killed by poisoned baits set for mammalian predators.

### Social structure
Adults live in territorial pairs spread at very low density. Territories in Kruger averaged about 108 km². Non-territorial adults (floaters) wander widely. No cooperative hunting is documented.

### Reproduction and life history
Pairs often breed only every second year and may alternate between nests. The single chick depends on its parents for months, full independence comes at 2–3 years, and breeding begins at about 4–5 years. GPS tracking in South Africa found productivity of about 0.46 young per pair per year, higher than the 0.22 found by field nest checks, but still low.

### Conservation
The species is listed as **Endangered** by BirdLife International for the IUCN Red List, with continuing declines in mature individuals and range. Threats include persecution by farmers over livestock and game losses (almost certainly exaggerated), poisoned baits, electrocution and collisions with power infrastructure, and habitat fragmentation. In Kruger, three of four wide-ranging floaters died from human-related causes, and declines are occurring even inside protected areas.

### Special features
- Hallux claw about 5.6 cm in females, with a very large inner front claw.
- Longest tarsus of any booted eagle.
- Very broad wingspan of about 2.1 m for soaring.
- Hunts mostly from a high soar with a long stoop, and can briefly hover.
- Very sharp long-range vision.

### Human interaction
Farmers and game managers have long shot and poisoned martial eagles for taking livestock and game, though its effect on livestock is almost certainly exaggerated. It is shy of people and prefers undisturbed land, so its future depends heavily on large protected areas and on reducing persecution in farmland.

### Genuine fun facts
- Its scientific name, *Polemaetus bellicosus*, roughly means "warlike war-eagle".
- Martial eagles have been recorded killing and eating lion cubs in Kenya's Mara region.
- It has been reported to spot prey from 5–6 km away.
- Vervet monkeys have a special alarm call for eagles, given mainly to martial eagles.
- Immature birds keep a pale plumage for years; full adult plumage comes around the seventh year.
- Kruger floater eagles ranged over about 44,000 km², far beyond the park.
- Unusually for such a large eagle, it can hover briefly while hunting.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Martial Eagle is Africa's largest eagle by wingspan. The canonical female weighs about 4.8 kg and hunts from a high soar, dropping in a long stoop onto hares, monitor lizards, snakes, small carnivores and even lion cubs. Large talons and very long legs make its strike dangerous, but its small absolute mass and lack of armor keep it far below large mammals in raw power and defense.

### Rich narrative profile
The martial eagle hunts from above. It spends much of the day circling so high over the savanna that it is hard to see, scanning ground where hares, hyraxes, game birds and monitor lizards move in the open. When it commits, it drops in a long, shallow stoop, or a steeper parachute into cover, and arrives before the prey knows it has been seen.

Its weapons match its choice of prey. Females carry hallux claws averaging about 5.6 cm, an inner claw almost as large, and the longest legs of any booted eagle, letting them strike at snakes and small carnivores without putting the body within reach. Records of lion cubs killed in Kenya and of venomous snakes, jackals and small cats in the diet show that it takes on prey that can hurt it. Vervet monkeys take the threat seriously enough to keep a separate alarm call for it.

In an absolute matchup, however, it is still a bird under 5 kg. Its wide wings suit soaring and open-country stoops, not twisting fights in dense cover, and it has no armor and a light skeleton. It wins by striking first from above. If the first strike does not end the fight, or a heavy grappler gets a grip on it, the martial eagle has little else to rely on.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** unsexed means of 3.9–4.2 kg hide strong sex differences; the canonical 4.8 kg female follows two female sample means.
- **Wingspan:** 260 cm claims are possibly unsubstantiated; the 211.9 cm wild-measured mean is preferred.
- **Maximum prey:** ADW's 35 kg is an exceptional or unverified claim. Weaponry is based on routine kills of 1–5 kg prey and documented dangerous prey.
- **Detection distance:** 5–6 km comes from secondary summaries, not a controlled acuity test.
- **Speed:** no instrumented stoop speed; field left at 0.0.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Crowned Eagle (4.0 kg; A41.0 Ag89.0 St57.0 Sp74.0):** similar talon size and also Africa's other top eagle. The martial eagle is heavier and far better at soaring (Stamina 80.0 vs 57.0) but less maneuverable in cover (Agility 84.0 vs 89.0). Attack 41.5 sits just above.
- **Versus Golden Eagle (5.0 kg; A40.0 Ag88.0 St79.0 Sp70.0):** near-identical mean mass and hallux size. The martial eagle's regular kills of dangerous prey (snakes, monitors, small carnivores) earn Attack 41.5, but ADW's note on poor maneuvering keeps Agility below the golden eagle.
- **Versus Harpy Eagle (8.0 kg; A44.0 Ag91.0 Sp79.0):** the harpy has much larger talons and nearly twice the mass, so it stays above on Attack, Weaponry and Size.
- **Versus Secretary Bird (4.0 kg; A36.0):** similar mass and both kill snakes, but the eagle's talons and aerial strike give it higher Attack.
- **Versus Caracal (17 kg; A44.0):** the caracal, about 3.5 times heavier, stays above on Attack and Defense despite the eagle's reach and strike from above.
- **Anti-inflation check:** lion-cub kills and the 35 kg claim are counted in Ferocity and Weaponry, not in Raw Power or Size.
