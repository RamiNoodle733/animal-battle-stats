# Pronghorn (*Antilocapra americana*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Common name:** Pronghorn
- **Scientific name:** *Antilocapra americana* (Ord, 1815), the only living species of family Antilocapridae.
- **Exact animal represented:** living North American pronghorn, not a true antelope and not a particular endangered subspecies such as Sonoran pronghorn.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime **adult male (buck)**. Males are heavier and carry the long, pronged horns used in territorial and rut fights, so they are the appropriate fighting sex.
- **Canonical mass:** **51.0 kg**, approximately the midpoint of the Yellowstone/NPS adult-male range of 100–125 lb (45.4–56.7 kg). Broader references can run higher, so this is deliberately representative rather than exceptional.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** NPS gives males at 100–125 lb and females at 90–110 lb. Male horns are typically 10–16 in (25.4–40.6 cm), curved and pronged; about 70% of females have much shorter 1–2 in horns. Adult males also carry conspicuous black cheek markings.
- **Population variation:** body mass and dimensions vary geographically and seasonally. The canonical animal is a fit mature buck in ordinary condition, not a record-sized northern individual.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass and dimensions
- **Adult male mass:** **45.4–56.7 kg** (100–125 lb) in Yellowstone and Golden Spike NPS summaries. Canonical: **51.0 kg**.
- **Adult female mass:** **40.8–49.9 kg** (90–110 lb) in the same NPS material.
- **Adult length:** **114–140 cm** (45–55 in) in NPS material. Canonical total/body length: **140.0 cm**, a representative large adult male rather than a record.
- **Shoulder height:** **88.9–101.6 cm** (35–40 in). Canonical: **95.0 cm**.
- **Male horns:** typically **25.4–40.6 cm** (10–16 in) in Yellowstone; Theodore Roosevelt NPS notes horns can reach 18 in (45.7 cm). Canonical weapon dimension: about **33.0 cm** horn length.
- **Horn construction:** permanent bony cores carry keratinous sheaths. Unusually among living horned ungulates, the sheaths are shed and regrown annually; males bear the characteristic forward prong.

### Speed
- NPS sources report sustained sprints around **45–50 mph** (72–80 km/h), while Theodore Roosevelt NPS reports maxima up to **55 mph** (88.5 km/h). National Geographic gives more than 53 mph and emphasizes the ability to run for miles at roughly half that speed.
- **Canonical `speed_mps = 24.6`** (55 mph / 3.6), treated as a defensible upper running speed from a government source rather than the less consistently sourced 60 mph internet figure.
- Top speed is not used as a substitute for Agility. The exceptional sustained-speed physiology instead contributes primarily to Stamina and escape mechanics.

### Lifespan
- Yellowstone NPS reports an average **7–10 years**; Theodore Roosevelt NPS says a fortunate wild animal can survive up to about 10 years. ADW similarly describes a short 7–10 year life span.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years = 9.0`**, a representative wild-adult value rather than a captive maximum.

### Bite force and natural weapons
- No reliable species-specific bite-pressure measurement was located. Pronghorn are herbivorous ruminants and the bite is not their primary fighting weapon.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi = 0.0`** rather than fabricating or importing a generic ungulate value.
- The primary weapons are the male's paired pronged horns, driven by neck and body force during close contests. Hooves can kick but are secondary to the horn-and-shove contest system.

### Defenses
Pronghorn lack armor, thick hide, tusks or heavy skeletal protection. Their principal defense is detection plus escape: very large eyes, broad visual coverage, explosive acceleration, high top speed and unusually strong aerobic running capacity. The white rump patch can be erected as an alarm signal.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offensive mechanics
A mature buck fights with paired, backward-curving horns bearing forward prongs. Yellowstone describes rivals approaching until the horns meet, then twisting and shoving until the weaker male retreats. ADW reports that fights average only about **two minutes** but can cause serious injury. Fatalities are uncommon in Yellowstone's account, which constrains Attack below heavily armed, much larger ungulates such as oryx and large bovids.

The prongs can catch and redirect an opponent's horn/head position while the curved main shafts transmit pushing and twisting force. The system is optimized for contests with another pronghorn, not for killing large predators. Kicks remain a plausible emergency weapon but are not treated as a separate high-damage system.

### Defense and toughness
There is little passive protection. A roughly 51 kg male has useful mammalian mass and a sturdy cursorial skeleton, but no armor or exceptionally thick hide. The species survives primarily by avoiding contact. Against an opponent that closes and secures a hold, its physical Defense is modest.

### Locomotion and agility
Pronghorn are extreme open-country cursors. Their long distal limbs, streamlined build and high-speed gait support extraordinary forward acceleration and sustained speed. They can reposition rapidly across open ground and maintain useful control at speed, but their specialty is not tight obstacle negotiation, climbing or sharp confined-space turning. This supports elite Agility while keeping it below a reflex/maneuverability ceiling.

### Endurance and physiology
This is the species' defining combat-relevant strength. NPS describes sustained 45–50 mph sprints, and National Geographic notes that pronghorn can travel for miles at about half their maximum speed. A comparative physiology study explicitly calls *A. americana* one of the most athletic mammals and relates its blood rheology to very high aerobic capacity. High sustained speed is therefore not merely folklore or a top-speed statistic.

### Senses
Large laterally placed eyes are adapted for open-country surveillance. Theodore Roosevelt NPS reports an estimated **320-degree field of visibility** and the ability to detect moving objects at great distance. Hearing and smell supplement this, while the conspicuous rump patch provides a visual alarm signal to conspecifics. Vision is the dominant battle-relevant sense.

### Intelligence and tactics
Pronghorn use territorial systems, seasonal grouping, migration, alarm communication and flexible mate-defense behavior. Adult males defend territories and harems, assess rivals and often resolve contests through signaling before escalation. These behaviors support competent Tactics but do not justify primate/corvid/cetacean-level Intelligence.

### Fighting behavior
Adult males defend territories through much of the warm season and become especially combative during the rut. Yellowstone describes snorts and wheezing coughs before physical engagement. If escalation occurs, opponents interlock horns, twist and shove. ADW reports short fights that can nevertheless produce serious injury. High-ranking males are more likely to hold territories, and research on male dominance found associations among body size, horn/prong size and territorial status.

### Predation and predator defense
Adults rely overwhelmingly on early detection and outrunning threats. Fawns are vulnerable to coyotes, bobcats, wolves, bears and golden eagles. An adult that cannot flee can use horns and hooves, but its anatomy is optimized to make such a last stand unnecessary.

### Environmental strengths
- Open prairie, sagebrush and semi-desert with long sight lines.
- Firm, unobstructed terrain where sustained running can create separation.
- Large arenas where acceleration and endurance can be fully expressed.
- Migratory landscapes in which route memory and broad-scale movement matter.

### Environmental weaknesses
- Dense forest, steep broken ground or cramped enclosures reduce the value of extreme speed.
- Fences are a major real-world obstacle because pronghorn tend to crawl under rather than jump them.
- No armor and only moderate body mass if caught.
- Horns are dangerous in frontal contests but do not match the reach/mass of large bovids, rhinoceroses or megafauna.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** similarly sized terrestrial opponents that cannot match its speed, endurance or visual detection; slower herbivores that can be disengaged from repeatedly.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized horned ungulates, medium canids/felids if sufficient open space exists, and fast opponents that can force a close exchange.
- **Bad:** ambush predators that begin at contact range, large grappling carnivores, heavily armored opponents, and much larger horned/tusked ungulates in confined terrain.

## 7. Abilities and traits

### Special abilities
1. **Prairie Overdrive** - exceptional aerobic machinery lets a pronghorn combine near-elite terrestrial speed with sustained fast running, allowing it to open distance long after many burst-speed opponents would fatigue.
2. **Panoramic Sentinel** - very large, laterally placed eyes provide exceptionally broad open-country surveillance, improving early threat detection and route selection before contact.

### Unique traits
1. **Shedding Pronged Horns** - the pronghorn is unique among living horned ungulates in annually shedding its keratin horn sheaths, while mature males also carry the namesake forward prong.
2. **Flash-Rump Alarm** - erectile white rump hairs create a conspicuous long-distance visual alarm signal that coordinates vigilance and escape in open habitat.

## 8. Expanded profile content

### Habitat and geographic range
Pronghorn inhabit open grasslands, sagebrush steppe, plains and semi-desert across western and central North America, from southern Canada through the western United States into northern Mexico. Open sight lines are central to their predator-detection strategy.

### Diet and ecology
They are herbivorous ruminants that browse sagebrush, rabbitbrush, forbs and other vegetation, with grasses generally a smaller component. Yellowstone notes that they can consume plants toxic to some other ungulates and suggests their proportionally large liver may assist detoxification.

### Social structure
Winter brings mixed-sex and mixed-age herds. In spring and summer, sexes segregate more strongly: females form nursery groups, younger males may form bachelor groups, and mature males defend territories. During the rut, successful males defend access to females.

### Reproduction and life history
The species is polygynous. Gestation is about **252 days**. Females usually bear one or two fawns, with twins common after the first reproductive year. Fawns use hiding behavior and low scent during the earliest vulnerable stage. Sexual maturity occurs around two years in ADW's synthesis.

### Conservation
The species overall is currently treated as **Least Concern**, although local populations and subspecies can be much more vulnerable. Fences, roads, habitat fragmentation, severe winter and disruption of migration corridors remain major management concerns.

### Major adaptations
Long cursorial limbs, very high aerobic capacity, large eyes, broad visual field, annually renewed horn sheaths, alarm rump patch and seasonal migratory behavior define the species. These adaptations favor detection and prolonged escape rather than close-range durability.

### Human interaction
Pronghorn are managed as game animals through much of their range. Roads and conventional livestock fencing fragment habitat and migration routes. Conservation programs increasingly emphasize wildlife-friendly fencing and protection of migration corridors.

### Fun facts
- The pronghorn is the **only living member of Antilocapridae**.
- Its horn sheaths are **shed annually**, unlike the retained horn sheaths of typical bovids.
- NPS reports adult males commonly carrying **10–16 in (25–41 cm)** pronged horns.
- It can reach roughly **55 mph (88.5 km/h)** and also sustain remarkably high speeds over distance.
- Its large eyes provide an estimated **~320-degree field of visibility** in open country.
- Rut fights can cause serious injury even though Yellowstone reports fatalities as rare.
- The white rump hairs can be erected into a conspicuous visual alarm patch.

### Concise site-ready summary
The pronghorn is North America's extreme endurance sprinter: a roughly 51 kg adult buck armed with pronged horns, enormous open-country visual awareness and the aerobic machinery to sustain speeds that exhaust most terrestrial pursuers. Its passive durability is modest, but on open ground its combination of detection, acceleration and stamina makes forcing a clean engagement exceptionally difficult.

### Narrative profile
The pronghorn's battle identity begins before contact. Its huge eyes scan an enormous arc of prairie, and once danger is recognized the animal's best weapon is distance. A healthy mature buck can accelerate into a run approaching 25 m/s, yet unlike many speed specialists it is also built to keep moving fast. Government field summaries describe sustained 45–50 mph running, and comparative physiology places the species among the most athletic mammals. That combination is why Stamina, Agility and Senses dominate its ABS profile rather than raw physical power.

When escape is impossible, the male is not defenseless. Its paired keratin-sheathed horns can exceed 30 cm and include forward prongs used during rut contests. Rivals threaten, approach, lock horns, twist and shove; short bouts can cause serious injury. Still, a 51 kg pronghorn is not an oryx, bison or rhinoceros. Its weapons evolved primarily for contests with other pronghorn, and its thinly protected cursorial body is poorly suited to absorbing a predator's bite or grapple.

The result is a highly asymmetric fighter. In open terrain it can dictate whether many encounters happen at all. In cramped terrain, against an ambush attacker already inside its acceleration envelope, much of its greatest advantage disappears. ABS therefore keeps Attack and Defense moderate while allowing the evidence-backed locomotor and sensory specializations to approach the elite end of the roster.

### Evidence conflicts and uncertainty
- Maximum-speed figures vary from 45–50 mph sustained to 53–55 mph maxima, with some tertiary sources claiming 60 mph. The canonical **24.6 m/s (55 mph)** uses the stronger NPS maximum and does not promote 60 mph.
- Adult-male mass can be reported above the Yellowstone 100–125 lb range in broader references. **51.0 kg** intentionally represents a normal mature buck rather than the upper geographic/record tail.
- No reliable species-specific bite-pressure value was found; **0.0 PSI** is an unknown/unsupported placeholder, not a claim that the animal produces zero bite force.
- The 320-degree visual-field figure is useful agency guidance but should not be presented as a laboratory measurement unless a primary optics source is later recovered.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes
- **Versus Ostrich (130 kg; Attack 58.0 / Agility 83.0 / Stamina 89.0):** pronghorn is much lighter and less damaging, so Attack 47.0 stays well below Ostrich. Its better sourced sustained high-speed running justifies higher Agility/Stamina.
- **Versus Oryx (230 kg; Attack 60.0 / Defense 49.0 / Stamina 82.0):** pronghorn horns are serious but shorter and attached to less than one-quarter the mass, so Attack/Defense remain clearly lower. Pronghorn wins locomotor endurance/speed.
- **Versus Polar Bear (500 kg; Attack 78.0 / Defense 72.0):** there is intentionally a huge damage/durability gap. The pronghorn's advantage is avoidance, not force.
- **Versus Peregrine Falcon (Agility 96.0):** Pronghorn Agility 94.0 recognizes extraordinary terrestrial locomotion but remains just below the falcon's three-dimensional aerial maneuvering ceiling.
- **Versus Proboscis Monkey (21.2 kg; Agility 85.0):** the monkey is more terrain-versatile, but pronghorn has much higher open-ground acceleration and speed. The 94.0 score reflects that without treating top speed alone as Agility.
- **Inflation check:** no physical-damage substat is elevated merely because pronghorn performance is extraordinary pound-for-pound. Its near-elite values are confined to directly supported speed, endurance, maneuverability and senses.
