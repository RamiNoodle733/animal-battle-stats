# Wolverine (*Gulo gulo*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Wolverine (also glutton, carcajou, "skunk bear")
- **Scientific name:** *Gulo gulo* (Linnaeus, 1758)
- **Exact animal represented:** the wolverine species across its Holarctic range, Eurasian and North American populations together. The site's live photo shows an adult wolverine standing in open ground.
- **Status:** Living. Least Concern globally; the contiguous United States population is listed as threatened under the U.S. Endangered Species Act (2023).
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult male, **16.0 kg**, **42.0 cm** at the shoulder and **90.0 cm** head-body length, tail excluded.
- **Sex choice:** males are 10–15% larger than females in linear measurements and 30–40% heavier (Wikipedia; ADW gives about 10% and 30%), and males range far more widely, so a male is used.
- **Population variation:** Wikipedia gives males **11–18 kg** and females **8–12 kg**, with some sources claiming Eurasian animals up to 20 kg. Soviet-literature reports of 32 kg males are considered improbable. The USDA Fire Effects Information System (FEIS) compiles adult male averages of **10.9–27.5 kg** across studies, a wider spread than most field summaries. The canonical 16.0 kg is a robust male, not an outlier.

- ADW: "a very powerful bite, with which they crush bone"; large prey are killed by biting the back or front of the neck.
- Claws are semi-retractable and crampon-like on large five-toed, near-plantigrade paws that spread weight on snow and grip rock and trees.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No species-specific bite-force measurement was found.

### Defensive structure
- Thick, dense fur and a thick hide over a compact, muscular body. No armor. Potent anal scent glands.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The wolverine's main weapon is a heavy mustelid bite able to crack bone and frozen meat, backed by strong forelimbs and long, hooked claws. It kills small and medium prey outright and takes large ungulates, including reindeer, caribou, deer and even adult moose and elk, when they are weakened by winter or floundering in deep snow, usually with a bite to the neck. It also takes over carcasses from other predators; ADW notes it can drive bears, cougars and even wolf packs from kills. In captive reindeer herds held in deep snow, wolverines have killed many animals at once.

### Defense and durability
Defense comes from a compact, muscular body under thick fur and hide, a willingness to fight back and a pungent scent. Physical protection is still limited for a 16 kg animal. Wolves are its most important natural predator, with many recorded wolverine deaths from wolves in North America and Eurasia; black bears, brown bears and cougars also kill adults, and golden eagles take young animals.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Short legs and a loping gait make the wolverine slow in a straight race, but its large paws act like snowshoes and its claws like crampons. It climbs trees quickly, scales cliffs and snow-covered peaks, and swims well. Agility is terrain-based: excellent in snow and rock, ordinary on flat, firm ground.

### Endurance
Endurance is exceptional for its size. ADW reports travel of 10–15 km without rest and up to 45 km in a day. FEIS compiles an average minimum of **20.8 km per day** over 42 days in one study, males easily covering 30 km per day, and dispersal movements of up to **378 km**. One Wyoming male, M-56, travelled about 800 km south into Colorado and later more than 1,300 km in total before reaching North Dakota. Male home ranges exceed 620 km² (Wikipedia), with adult males in Idaho averaging 1,522 km² (FEIS).

### Senses
Wolverines find carcasses and cached food across large areas, including under snow, and follow wolf and lynx trails to scavenge. Precise sensory thresholds were not verified in this pass, so no detection distances are claimed.

### Intelligence and tactics
A 2025 movement-model study showed that wolverines use spatial memory to plan routes through rugged mountains, most often to destinations **5.3–9.8 km** ahead, saving about **19.3 kcal per 135 minutes** of movement. The authors suggest adding the wolverine to the short, primate-dominated list of species with complex spatial memory. Wolverines also cache food, scent-mark caches to deter other scavengers, and shift diet toward wolf-killed moose where wolves are present.

### Social structure and fighting
Solitary and territorial. A male's range overlaps those of several females. Territories are marked with anal-gland secretions (one analysis found 123 compounds across six animals) and urine.

### Predator defense
Wolverines usually avoid wolves and grizzly bears, and in some areas abandon ground when wolves arrive. When cornered or defending food they fight hard, and they sometimes displace much larger predators from carcasses, but they also lose such contests fatally.

### Environmental strengths
- Deep snow, where large prey flounder and the wolverine's paws keep it on top.
- Cold alpine and boreal terrain, cliffs and trees.
- Long-distance travel through rugged mountains using remembered routes.

### Weaknesses
- Small absolute size; wolves, bears and cougars can kill it.
- Slow straight-line speed on firm ground.
- Relies on persistent spring snow for dens; lower snowpack threatens southern populations.
- No armor; its bite is its main damaging weapon.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** smaller carnivores, prey animals stuck in deep snow, similar-size animals without heavy weapons, and scavenging contests against hesitant rivals.
- **Mixed:** lynx, coyotes and badgers of similar mass, where ferocity and bite have to overcome speed or claws.
- **Bad:** wolves, bears, big cats, large healthy ungulates on firm ground, and armored or venomous opponents.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Frozen-Carcass Crusher** — a heavy bite and robust teeth crack bone and frozen meat, and a neck bite kills prey many times the wolverine's own weight once the prey is stuck in snow.
2. **Deep-Snow Hunter** — large, wide paws spread its weight and crampon-like claws grip ice and rock, so the wolverine stays on top of snow where hoofed prey sinks and cannot escape.

### Unique traits
1. **Mountain Route Planner** — GPS tracks show wolverines plan routes to places 5.3–9.8 km ahead from memory, saving energy in rugged terrain.
2. **Largest Land Weasel** — the largest land-dwelling member of the weasel family, stocky and bear-like, with potent scent glands that earned it the name "skunk bear."

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Boreal forest, taiga, alpine tundra and Arctic tundra across northern Eurasia and North America, from Scandinavia and Russia to Alaska, Canada and the northern Rocky Mountains. In the contiguous United States it survives mainly in high mountains, and wolverines are returning to areas such as Mount Rainier, with a reintroduction program planned in Colorado. Year-round occupation is limited to areas where snow persists into late spring.

### Diet and ecology
An opportunistic predator and scavenger. In Norway, diet in tundra was 40% reindeer, 39% moose and 9% rodents; in forest with wolves it shifted to 76% moose, 18% reindeer and 5% rodents, because wolf kills supplied moose carcasses. Prey ranges from voles, lemmings, hares, marmots, beavers and porcupines to caribou, deer, moose and livestock. Vegetation, including whitebark pine nuts in Yellowstone, is eaten occasionally.

### Social structure
Solitary. Males hold large territories overlapping several females. Densities are very low: 0.71–6.74 wolverines per 1,000 km² across Alberta landscapes, for an estimated 955 animals in the province.

### Reproduction and life history
Females give birth in dens dug into deep snow, often in alternate years, to litters averaging about three kits born between January and April. Young stay in the mother's territory until they can hunt; FEIS records juveniles and subadults dispersing 30–378 km.

### Conservation
Least Concern on the IUCN Red List because of its wide range and large northern populations. In 2023 the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service listed the North American wolverine in the contiguous United States as threatened. Main concerns are loss of spring snowpack for denning, human development, trapping and low genetic connectivity. In the Arctic, reduced snow and sea ice, changing prey and increased human access are research priorities.

### Special features
- Snowshoe-like five-toed paws with semi-retractable, crampon-like claws.
- Heavy jaws and teeth able to crush bone and frozen meat.
- Dense, thick fur over a thick hide.
- Potent anal scent glands used to mark territory and caches.
- Spatial memory used to plan efficient mountain routes.

### Human interaction
Trapped for fur and persecuted in reindeer-herding and livestock areas, especially in Fennoscandia, where it takes semi-domestic reindeer. They are rarely seen, and climate change models predict shrinking spring snowpack in much of the western United States by 2050.

### Genuine fun facts
- A young male wolverine tracked from near Grand Teton National Park travelled about 800 km south, and his journey eventually exceeded 1,300 km.
- Wolverines plan routes from memory, often to places 5.3–9.8 km away.
- Wolverine scent-gland secretions contained 123 different compounds in one six-animal study.
- Females give birth in dens tunnelled into deep spring snow.
- Where wolves returned in Norway, wolverine diet switched to about 76% moose from wolf kills.
- Wolverines follow wolf and lynx trails to scavenge what those hunters leave behind.
- It is the largest land-dwelling member of the weasel family.

### Concise site-ready summary
The wolverine is a 16 kg snow-country powerhouse with bone-crushing jaws, crampon claws and snowshoe paws. It travels enormous distances, plans routes from memory and will face down larger predators over a carcass, but wolves and bears can still kill it and it is slow on open ground.

### Rich narrative profile
The wolverine looks like a small bear and behaves like a much larger animal. A mature male weighs about 16 kg, yet it hunts reindeer and moose trapped in deep snow, cracks frozen bones that other scavengers leave, and sometimes drives much bigger predators off their kills. Its paws are broad and furred, its claws hooked like climbing spikes, and its coat thick enough for Arctic winters.

That reputation needs context on the absolute scale. Wolves are its main natural killer, bears and cougars also kill wolverines, and a clean fight against a 50 kg wolf or a big cat is a losing one. What makes the wolverine formidable is the combination of a heavy bite, raw toughness for its size and a readiness to fight when food or life is at stake, not raw mass.

Its most remarkable features are endurance and mind. Wolverines cover 20 to 45 km in a day, disperse hundreds of kilometres, and one tracked male travelled more than 1,300 km. A 2025 study showed they plan routes through rugged mountains from memory, aiming at places several kilometres out of sight. Those abilities, together with its snow specialization, give the wolverine high Stamina, Intelligence and Special ratings while its Attack and Defense stay in line with other mid-sized carnivores.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** field summaries (11–18 kg males) are narrower than ADW's 9–30 kg and FEIS's compiled 10.9–27.5 kg averages; 16.0 kg is a robust but ordinary male.
- **Speed:** ADW contains both a ≤15 km/h statement and a >48 km/h attack claim; neither is instrumented, so canonical speed is 0.0.
- **Tail length:** Wikipedia gives two ranges (17–26 cm and 25–35 cm); the tail is kept out of `length_cm`.
- **Lifespan:** 5–7 years (ADW) vs 8–10 years (Wikipedia) for wild animals; 8.0 years is used.
- **Legend vs record:** stories of wolverines routinely driving off bears are reported, but so are fatal losses to bears and wolves; ratings follow the documented predator record.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Honey Badger (14 kg; Attack 42.0, Defense 45.0, Special 80.0):** the wolverine is slightly heavier and kills large ungulates in snow, so Attack is higher (46.0). The honey badger keeps higher Defense for its loose, tough skin and higher Special for its toughness against stings and venom. The wolverine is far ahead in Stamina (83.0 vs 70.0).
- **Versus Gray Wolf (50 kg; Attack 55.0, Defense 41.0, Stamina 89.0):** wolves kill wolverines, so the wolf stays above in Attack and in Stamina; Defense is set one point below the wolf (40.0) because the wolverine's hide and fur partly offset its much smaller size.
- **Versus Badger (13 kg; Attack 38.0, Defense 39.0):** a similar mustelid build; the wolverine's larger size, bigger prey and greater endurance put it above the badger in every headline except a near-equal Defense.
- **Versus Coyote (15 kg; Attack 38.0, Agility 82.0):** similar mass; the wolverine has the stronger bite and higher Attack, while the coyote is far faster and more agile.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the old live placeholders (Attack 1.6, Toughness 98, Ferocity 98) are not used. A 16 kg carnivore does not have near-ceiling toughness, and it is far more dangerous than Attack 1.6.
