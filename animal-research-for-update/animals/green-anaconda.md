# Green Anaconda research report

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Roster name:** Green Anaconda
- **Resolved taxon:** *Eunectes murinus* (Linnaeus, 1758), family Boidae.
- **Living/extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature **adult female**, because female-biased sexual size dimorphism is extreme in green anacondas and the female is the substantially larger fighting form.
- **Canonical body:** **50.0 kg**, **450.0 cm total length**. This represents a large mature female without using an exceptional record animal.
- **Roster duplication warning:** ABS also contains `Anaconda`. Its staging report independently resolved that generic label to the green-anaconda complex and *E. murinus*. Until the roster decides whether those entries should represent different taxa, Green Anaconda must not be given materially different combat scores merely because its label is more specific.

### Taxonomic uncertainty

Rivas et al. (2024) proposed a northern green-anaconda species, *Eunectes akayima*, based on genetic divergence and geographic structure, while retaining *E. murinus* for the southern lineage. Current taxonomic treatments are not fully settled, and Wikispecies presently lists *E. akayima* as a synonym of *E. murinus*. ABS should therefore keep the site-facing taxon *E. murinus* while preserving this conflict rather than silently treating the 2024 split as universally accepted.

### Sexual dimorphism and population variation

Female-biased size dimorphism is one of the defining facts of the species. Smithsonian describes females as substantially larger than males; ADW likewise reports males around 3 m and females around 6 m in older pooled literature. Broad zoo and reference ranges extend far beyond typical field animals and include disputed giant claims. A long-running Venezuelan field program is repeatedly cited for a largest well-documented female near **5.21 m and 97.5 kg**, illustrating why 7-12 m internet claims should not be used as a normal baseline. The 50 kg / 4.5 m ABS female is intentionally representative rather than record-sized.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- **Canonical:** **50.0 kg**.
- SeaWorld gives a broad average figure of 148.5 kg, while Franklin Park Zoo gives 350-550 lb and ADW repeats historical maxima to 250 kg. Those institutional values are useful for demonstrating the species' potential size but are poorly suited to a representative wild-female baseline because they pool contexts and upper-end animals.
- Field-based summaries of the Venezuelan work are substantially more conservative, with many adult females in the 4-5 m class and the largest documented field female near 97.5 kg.
- **Confidence:** moderate. Body mass changes strongly with length, reproductive state and recent feeding.

### Length
- **Canonical total length:** **450.0 cm**.
- Smithsonian and ADW summarize much larger upper values, but verified field animals are more conservative than legendary giant claims.
- **Confidence:** moderate-high for a large representative adult female; low for extreme historical maxima.

### Height
- **Canonical `height_cm`: 0.0.** Standing height is not a meaningful snake measurement.

### Speed
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0.** Strong sources consistently describe the species as an excellent swimmer and cumbersome on land, but this run did not recover a defensible species-specific instrumented maximum speed. Tertiary values such as 16 km/h were rejected.

### Lifespan
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 10.0.** Smithsonian and National Geographic give about 10 years in the wild; managed-care longevity can extend well beyond 20 years and some zoo sources cite roughly 30 years.

### Bite force
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No defensible standardized species-specific PSI measurement was found. The bite is primarily a prey-seizing and anchoring mechanism that enables coiling. Popular constriction-pressure figures were also rejected because they were not traced to a primary species-specific experiment.

### Natural weapons and defenses
- Multiple recurved teeth anchor struggling prey.
- The primary weapon is the long muscular trunk, which wraps and compresses prey after seizure.
- Large female girth creates major grappling leverage and makes established coils difficult for similarly sized opponents to peel away.
- Dorsally positioned eyes and nostrils permit surveillance while most of the body remains submerged.
- Labial heat-sensitive pits, tongue-flicking and the vomeronasal system contribute to prey detection.
- Smooth scales and thick musculature provide abrasion resistance and structural robustness but are not rigid armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary weapon: whole-body constriction

The green anaconda is a nonvenomous ambush constrictor. Smithsonian describes the attack sequence as seizure with the jaws followed by wrapping the muscular body around prey. Constriction is best treated as a positional circulatory/compressive weapon rather than a mythical fixed PSI number. Once several coils are established around a vulnerable torso, a similarly sized opponent can lose effective movement quickly. Before that point, the snake must still land and maintain a close-range hold.

### Secondary weapon: anchoring bite

The mouth is not the primary damage engine. Recurved teeth make backward withdrawal difficult and keep prey attached while the trunk establishes coils. The bite can lacerate, but its greatest combat value is the transition it creates into grappling control.

### Offensive mechanics

A typical high-value sequence is concealment, short strike, tooth hold, rapid looping, repeated tightening and positional adjustment. The system is powerful against targets that cannot keep distance or attack the head effectively. It is much less reliable against much larger animals, heavily armored opponents, or fast cutters that can punish the snake before coils become secure.

### Defense and toughness

A 50 kg female has a large amount of muscular tissue distributed over several metres, so superficial injury away from the head may not immediately end a grapple. Coiling can shield the head and create a compact defensive posture. However, the snake has no shell or osteoderm armor. Deep tearing, crushing, trampling, severe puncture and head trauma remain major vulnerabilities.

### Locomotion and maneuverability

This species is built for wetlands. Water supports its heavy body, improves concealment and allows more efficient repositioning than open dry ground. On land, a large female is comparatively cumbersome. Agility therefore depends heavily on environment and should not be inferred from a dubious top-speed claim.

### Endurance

Green anacondas are low-metabolism ambush predators capable of long fasting intervals. Smithsonian notes that, outside reproductive females, feeding may occur only every few weeks, and large meals can support much longer gaps. This is ecological energy efficiency, not evidence of elite high-output combat endurance. Sustained coiling is useful, but repeated explosive pursuit is not the species' strategy.

### Senses

Smithsonian documents advanced chemosensation, vibration detection and heat-sensitive pit organs on the upper lip. Dorsal eyes and nostrils are well suited to waterline ambush. These senses strongly support target acquisition at close range, especially in murky habitat, without implying mammalian-style long-range vision or cognition.

### Intelligence and tactics

The green anaconda is a competent specialist rather than a flexible strategic generalist. Its conceal-seize-coil sequence is highly effective, and individuals must make prey-risk decisions, but there is no evidence for the broad problem solving, manipulation, coalition tactics or learned hunting traditions seen in the roster's high-intelligence mammals and birds.

### Predation behavior

Smithsonian describes adults taking deer, capybaras, caimans and large birds, with juveniles focused on smaller fish, birds and mammals. Large prey is dangerous, and prey choice therefore reflects opportunity and risk rather than an ability to overpower anything the snake can physically encircle.

### Fighting and reproductive behavior

Green anacondas are usually solitary outside breeding. Multiple males can form prolonged breeding balls around one female, sometimes for weeks. Females can cannibalize males, a striking consequence of the size disparity. This does not make the species generally social in combat.

### Environmental advantages
- Shallow swamps, marshes, slow rivers and flooded forest.
- Murky water and vegetation that conceal the body.
- Water deep enough to compromise a terrestrial opponent's footing.
- Close-range encounters where an opponent cannot disengage before coils form.

### Environmental disadvantages
- Open dry ground with no concealment.
- Cold conditions that suppress ectothermic performance.
- Opponents able to maintain distance.
- Very large targets too massive to control with a practical coil.
- Cutting, crushing or tearing weapons that can reach the head during the grapple.

### Weaknesses
- No rigid armor.
- Critical dependence on securing first contact.
- Head remains a decisive target.
- Limited pursuit and ranged threat.
- Long body exposes substantial surface area during failed grappling attempts.
- Temperature and terrain can materially affect performance.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Favorable:** similarly sized or smaller vertebrates with weak anti-grapple tools, especially in water or dense wetland cover.
- **Competitive:** medium predators where the first secure bite and coil can decide the encounter, but failure exposes the snake to teeth or claws.
- **Unfavorable:** large crocodilians, heavy clawing predators, armored animals, large hoofed animals able to trample, and highly mobile opponents on open dry ground.
- **Extremely unfavorable:** megafauna too massive to encircle/control effectively.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and exactly two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Circulatory Coil Lock** - a successful tooth hold allows the snake to wrap multiple muscular loops around the opponent and convert close contact into sustained circumferential compression and positional control.
2. **Submerged Heat Ambush** - dorsal eyes/nostrils, labial heat sensitivity, chemosensation and aquatic camouflage let the snake detect and strike from a mostly concealed waterline position.

### Unique traits
1. **Female Heavyweight Form** - extreme female-biased size dimorphism makes the mature female dramatically larger and more powerful than the male, reversing the common male-fighter assumption.
2. **Water-Supported Giant** - the semi-aquatic body plan lets a very heavy snake move and conceal itself more effectively in water than its terrestrial bulk would suggest.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and geographic range
Green anacondas inhabit tropical South American freshwater systems, especially swamps, marshes, slow rivers, flooded forest and vegetated banks. Traditional range descriptions span the Amazon and Orinoco systems and the Guianas, though exact boundaries depend on how the recent *E. akayima* proposal is ultimately treated.

### Diet and ecology
They are opportunistic carnivores. Smithsonian lists fish, birds and small mammals for juveniles and much larger vertebrates including deer, capybaras, caimans and large birds for adults. Their ecological role is that of a large semi-aquatic ambush predator rather than an active long-distance pursuit hunter.

### Social structure
Normally solitary. Temporary aggregations occur during breeding, when numerous males can gather around a female in a breeding ball.

### Reproduction and life history
Sexual maturity is commonly placed around three to four years. Females bear live young after a long gestation and often reproduce only every other year because of the energetic cost. Smithsonian gives typical litters of roughly 20-40 young and notes an exceptional record of 82.

### Conservation
Smithsonian describes green anacondas as Least Concern and notes habitat loss, persecution and trade as threats. International trade is regulated under CITES Appendix II. Taxonomic splitting could alter conservation assessments because a broad old range may contain more than one lineage.

### Major adaptations
- Extreme female body size and girth.
- Recurved prey-holding teeth.
- Whole-body constriction.
- Dorsal eyes and nostrils.
- Labial heat sensitivity.
- Forked-tongue/vomeronasal chemosensation.
- Aquatic camouflage and wetland locomotion.
- Low-frequency feeding and ability to process very large meals.

### Human interaction
Popular media routinely exaggerate anaconda size and human predation. Smithsonian states there are no recorded instances of a green anaconda consuming a human. Large individuals are nevertheless powerful wild constrictors and should not be handled casually. Humans kill anacondas out of fear or livestock concerns, and animals also enter the international pet and skin trades.

### Genuine fun facts
- Green anacondas are generally considered the heaviest living snakes even though reticulated pythons can grow longer.
- Females are dramatically larger than males.
- The eyes and nostrils sit high on the head so the snake can remain mostly submerged while monitoring the surface.
- A breeding ball can contain many males around one female and persist for weeks.
- Females have been documented cannibalizing smaller males.
- After a large meal, an anaconda can go weeks or months before feeding again.
- Giant 9-12 m stories are much less secure than the famous reputation suggests; representative ABS values should stay anchored to documented animals.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Green Anaconda is a heavyweight wetland grappler whose entire body becomes a weapon after one successful bite. A large adult female combines recurved prey-holding teeth, immense trunk musculature, heat and chemical sensing, and submerged ambush tactics. It is devastating once coils are established, but lacks rigid armor and loses much of its mobility advantage on open dry ground.

### Rich narrative profile
A green anaconda does not win by trading strikes. It wins by changing the geometry of the fight. From shallow water, only the upper head may be obvious while metres of muscular body remain concealed. A short bite is the entry point. Recurved teeth hold the target long enough for the trunk to loop, and each secure coil reduces the opponent's freedom to use its own weapons.

That makes the animal unusually nonlinear in a matchup. Before contact, it can be outmaneuvered. After a good coil, an opponent near its size may suddenly have almost no useful space. The same specialization also creates its limits. A 4.5 m snake looks enormous but the canonical animal is only about 50 kg, so length must not be mistaken for megafaunal mass. A failed wrap exposes a long unarmored body, and powerful claws, jaws, horns or trampling can punish the attempt.

Water is therefore central rather than cosmetic. It supports the body, hides the approach, and can compromise the footing of terrestrial prey. On dry open ground the snake's tactical profile becomes much more ordinary. ABS should preserve that environment dependence instead of converting sensational size stories into universal Attack, Defense or Agility inflation.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus staged Anaconda:** both roster entries currently resolve to essentially the same biological animal. Green Anaconda therefore stays nearly identical to Anaconda's 70.0 Attack / 51.0 Defense / 45.0 Agility / 48.0 Stamina / 42.0 Intelligence / 73.0 Special. The one-point differences here reflect the fresh sensory and ecology reassessment, not a claim that the duplicate label is a stronger species. A future roster cleanup should either merge them or explicitly assign distinct taxa before allowing meaningful score separation.
- **Versus Gray Wolf:** the anaconda has greater grappling lethality after positional control but much worse pursuit, terrestrial maneuverability, endurance and behavioral flexibility. It should not inherit the wolf's 89.0 Stamina or 78.0 Intelligence.
- **Versus Great White Shark:** both are ambush predators, but the shark's 900 kg canonical mass and immediate bite damage put it far above the 50 kg snake in absolute Attack and physical scale.
- **Versus Gorilla:** the anaconda's specialized coil can be matchup-changing, but its absolute muscular force and trauma resistance remain below a 195 kg silverback's. Proportional snake strength is not converted into ape-scale Raw Power.
- **Anti-double-counting:** constriction primarily drives Weaponry/Attack and Abilities/Special. It does not also create artificial armor, elite endurance or elite intelligence.
- **Speed/agility control:** no unsupported swim-speed number is used, and aquatic competence is not treated as universally high Agility.
