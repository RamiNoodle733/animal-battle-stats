# King Cobra

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** King Cobra
- **Scientific name:** *Ophiophagus hannah* (Cantor, 1836), used here in the **2024 restricted sense**: the Northern King Cobra.
- **Taxonomic warning:** the historical *O. hannah* complex was revised in 2024 into four species. Das et al. restrict *O. hannah* sensu stricto to eastern Pakistan, northern/eastern India, the Andaman Islands, Indo-Burma and Indo-China south to central Thailand; *O. kaalinga* is Western Ghats, *O. bungarus* Sundaic, and *O. salvatana* Luzon. Older whole-complex measurements and photos therefore require locality scrutiny.
- **Status:** living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy fully mature **adult male Northern King Cobra, 8.0 kg and 360.0 cm total length**. Males are generally the larger sex and engage in ritual male-male wrestling. This is a representative large adult, not the famous 5.4-5.85 m exceptional maximum.
- **Dimorphism:** males generally attain greater length and have proportionally longer tails. Females perform nest construction/guarding and can be especially defensive during reproduction. The canonical fighter uses the larger male while documenting female-specific nesting behavior separately.
- **Population variation:** substantial geographic variation formerly hidden under the king-cobra species complex is now taxonomic variation. Values from Malaysia, Indonesia, the Western Ghats or Luzon should not automatically be treated as measurements of *O. hannah* sensu stricto.

## 3. Weapons, defenses and combat biology

### Primary weapon: envenomating bite
The king cobra's decisive weapon is a rapid front-fanged bite that injects a complex venom dominated by neurotoxic effects. A peer-reviewed proteomic/toxicology study found route-dependent mouse LD50 values and demonstrated rapid mortality in untreated experimental envenomation. The practical battle advantage is not jaw pressure but biochemical disruption after successful fang penetration. Venom potency and quantity must not be conflated: a large venomous snake can be exceptionally dangerous without having the lowest LD50 among snakes.

### Secondary weapon: reach and elevated strike geometry
At 3.6 m canonical length, the king cobra has unusually long reach for a venomous snake. It can elevate the anterior body, spread its hood and remain mobile. This changes engagement geometry by placing the head and fangs well above ground level, improving threat display and allowing strikes at higher targets. The hood itself is not armor and is not scored as protection.

### Defense and durability
Scales provide abrasion resistance but not meaningful armor against large claws, crushing bites, hooves or talons. The snake's long flexible body can absorb ordinary substrate contact and negotiate clutter, but severe vertebral or cranial trauma is dangerous. Defense therefore comes mainly from deterrence, threat posture, reach, concealment and avoidance rather than structural protection.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
King cobras are capable terrestrial snakes, can climb, and are associated with forest, bamboo, agricultural mosaics and stream environments. Smithsonian notes substantial use of trees or bushes. The body can reverse direction and exploit narrow cover efficiently, but a 3.6 m snake has less instantaneous open-ground turning freedom than tiny serpents. No unverified top speed is allowed to inflate Agility.

### Endurance
The species is an active snake predator rather than a sedentary ambush-only specialist. It searches for elongate prey across complex habitat, but reptilian physiology does not justify mammalian pursuit-endurance assumptions. Stamina is moderate: useful sustained movement and prolonged alert/guarding behavior, but not kangaroo, hyena or migratory-bird aerobic output.

### Senses
King cobras combine vision, chemoreception and substrate/environmental cues. Smithsonian states their eyesight is better than that of many snakes and can detect a moving person at roughly 100 m. Tongue-flicking and the vomeronasal system provide chemical tracking typical of snakes. These senses are effective for locating other snakes and monitoring threats but do not justify invented thermal-pit sensing, which king cobras do not possess.

### Intelligence and tactics
The species shows more behavioral complexity than a simple strike reflex. Smithsonian describes ritualized male wrestling in which rivals attempt to press the opponent's head down. Recent field work documents structured male-male combat, while nesting studies document construction and active guarding. These behaviors support useful tactical flexibility but do not warrant primate/corvid-level Intelligence.

### Hunting and predation
*Ophiophagus* literally reflects snake-eating specialization. Smithsonian describes a diet centered on other snakes, including rat snakes, pythons, Indian cobras, kraits and occasionally smaller king cobras. A king cobra must locate, approach, bite and safely manage elongate prey that can itself constrict or envenomate. Venom immobilization reduces the danger of prolonged physical restraint.

### Intraspecific fighting
Male-male combat is ritualized wrestling rather than routine venomous biting. Smithsonian describes males attempting to force a rival's head to the ground, and a 2025 open-access Ecology and Evolution report documents ritualistic male-male combat in Northern King Cobras in Thailand. This is strong evidence for species-specific combat behavior without implying that males normally attempt to kill one another.

### Reproduction and nest defense
King cobras are remarkable among snakes for constructing and guarding nests. A 2021 Bhutan study monitored a female guarding 37 eggs. A 2025 Yunnan study documented three nests, including 30 eggs at one site and an exceptional 72-egg nest associated with multiple guarding individuals, while cautioning that the latter may represent unusual or group nesting. Nest defense can increase female aggression, but the canonical combat specimen remains the larger adult male.

## 4. Environment, weaknesses and matchup archetypes

**Strong environments:** forest floor, bamboo, brush, stream margins and structurally complex terrain that permits concealment and approach routes.

**Advantages:** long striking platform; chemically assisted prey tracking; venom that can transform one successful penetrating bite into a delayed systemic win condition; ability to rear and threaten; familiarity with dangerous snake prey.

**Weaknesses:** low body mass relative to medium/large mammals; no armor; long vulnerable body; limited crushing/grappling force; venom is not instantaneous; fangs must penetrate; large armored or thick-skinned targets can reduce delivery effectiveness; aerial attackers can bypass much of the ground-control advantage.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** unarmored small-to-medium vertebrates vulnerable to envenomation, especially opponents that must enter striking range.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized venomous snakes or agile predators where first clean contact matters and both sides possess dangerous weapons.
- **Bad:** heavily armored animals, large thick-skinned mammals, very large hoofed animals, powerful birds attacking from above, and opponents capable of inflicting immediate catastrophic trauma before venom acts.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities
1. **Neurotoxic Venom Delivery** - fixed front fangs inject venom containing neuroactive components that can disrupt neuromuscular function after a successful bite. This creates a delayed systemic win condition that is distinct from the snake's low absolute raw power.
2. **Elevated Hood Strike** - elongated cervical ribs spread the hood while the cobra raises and controls a substantial anterior section of its body, increasing threat presence and allowing strikes from an unusually elevated platform for a snake.

## 8. Exactly two unique traits
1. **Snake-Hunter Specialist** - king cobras specialize on other snakes, including potentially dangerous venomous and constricting species, giving their prey-handling ecology unusual relevance to one-on-one combat analysis.
2. **Nest-Building Elapid** - females actively construct and guard vegetation nests, an exceptional reproductive behavior among snakes that is now supported by field observations including guarded clutches in Bhutan and Yunnan.

## 9. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Under the 2024 revision, Northern King Cobra *O. hannah* sensu stricto occupies the northern/eastern South Asian through Indo-Burmese and Indochinese portion of the old complex, extending south to central Thailand. Historical sources that describe the former complex from India through Indonesia and the Philippines should not be copied wholesale into the exact-taxon profile. Habitat includes forest, bamboo, stream-associated areas and some agricultural mosaics.

### Diet and ecology
The diet is strongly ophiophagous. Smithsonian lists rat snakes, dhamans, pythons, Indian cobras, kraits and smaller king cobras among prey. This specialization makes the king cobra a predator of animals that are themselves often formidable hunters.

### Social and life history
Adults are generally solitary outside reproductive interactions. Males engage in ritual wrestling. Females build and guard nests. Smithsonian reports 21-40 eggs in its general account, while peer-reviewed field reports show local variation and exceptional cases. Lifespan is about 20 years.

### Conservation
The king-cobra complex has long been treated as threatened by habitat destruction and persecution. Modern conservation assessment now has to catch up with the four-species taxonomy because each lineage has a smaller range than the historical single-species concept. This report therefore avoids treating old range-wide abundance statements as automatically valid for *O. hannah* sensu stricto.

### Adaptations
- fixed front-fang venom delivery
- expandable hood supported by elongated cervical ribs
- strong chemical tracking of snake prey
- unusually long body for a venomous snake
- controlled anterior-body elevation
- specialized snake predation
- nest construction and guarding

### Human interaction
King cobras are medically significant venomous snakes, but sensational descriptions should not be confused with normal behavior. Smithsonian emphasizes warning displays and defensive behavior rather than indiscriminate attack. Envenomation is a medical emergency requiring professional treatment and appropriate antivenom; ABS does not turn human fatality anecdotes into fabricated combat measurements.

### Fun facts
- King cobras are the world's longest living venomous snakes under the traditional common-name concept.
- The genus name *Ophiophagus* refers to snake eating.
- Their low-frequency hiss is unusually deep compared with many snakes.
- Male combat is often wrestling rather than biting.
- Females build vegetation nests, rare behavior among snakes.
- A 2024 taxonomic revision showed that the animal historically called one king-cobra species actually represents four species.
- A 2025 Yunnan report documented multi-individual nest guarding and an exceptional 72-egg nest, while explicitly discussing alternative explanations for that unusually large clutch.

### Concise site summary
The Northern King Cobra is a long, active snake specialist whose danger comes from reach, controlled strike posture and neurotoxic venom rather than brute force. A representative 3.6 m male is physically light compared with mammalian predators, but one clean envenomating bite can radically change a matchup. Its snake-hunting ecology, ritual male wrestling, strong chemoreception and unusual nest-building behavior make it one of the roster's strongest biological specialists without justifying inflated Defense or Raw Power.

### Narrative profile
A king cobra wins by controlling the terms of contact. Its long body gives it reach and access through clutter, while chemical sensing helps it track prey that may be hidden from sight. When threatened it can elevate the front of the body and spread the hood, turning a ground-level snake into a mobile striking platform. Yet the display should not be mistaken for armor. The animal remains an 8 kg vertebrate with a long vulnerable body, and a large predator that lands a decisive bite, stomp or talon strike can end the fight before venom becomes decisive.

Its unusual power is therefore asymmetric. Raw muscular force is low on the full ABS roster, while Special is elite. The fangs do not need to crush bone; they need to penetrate and deliver venom. This distinction is exactly why pound-for-pound reasoning is avoided. Against an unarmored opponent near its own size, venom can dominate the matchup. Against a hippopotamus, elephant or heavily armored animal, the same venom system faces major delivery and time-to-effect constraints while the cobra remains catastrophically vulnerable to one direct hit.

### Key conflicts and uncertainty
- **Taxonomy:** older literature uses *O. hannah* for four modern species. Locality is checked before importing claims.
- **Maximum length:** famous 5+ m records can derive from regions no longer assigned to *O. hannah* sensu stricto. Canonical 3.6 m uses Smithsonian's representative range instead.
- **Mass:** exact modern sex-specific mass distributions for Northern King Cobra are weak. 8.0 kg is moderate-confidence and should be replaced if a strong population morphometric dataset is recovered.
- **Venom yield:** popular sources quote very large milligram yields, but this report does not canonize a yield number without a clearly traceable primary measurement tied to the modern taxon.
- **Speed:** no strong measured species-specific maximum recovered; `speed_mps` stays 0.0.
- **Bite force:** no reliable PSI measurement; `bite_force_psi` stays 0.0.

## 12. Cross-animal normalization notes
- **Versus Huntsman Spider:** King Cobra is vastly larger and can deliver much more consequential vertebrate envenomation, so Attack and absolute Weaponry are far higher; it should not inherit the spider's tiny-animal raw-power compression problem.
- **Versus Honey Badger:** the cobra has much higher Special but much lower Defense/Toughness and lower direct physical power. A venomous bite can change the matchup, but the badger's mammalian robustness cannot be erased by a Special score.
- **Versus Jaguar:** Jaguar remains clearly superior in immediate physical Attack, Defense, power and contact control. King Cobra's advantage is biochemical, delayed and delivery-dependent.
- **Versus Kangaroo:** Kangaroo has much higher Raw Power, Defense and Stamina; King Cobra has far higher Special and a more lethal small-contact weapon.
- **Versus Hippopotamus:** the mass gap is enormous. Venom does not justify rating an 8 kg cobra anywhere near an 1,800 kg hippo in Raw Power or Defense.
- **Inflation check:** Attack 59.0 is intentionally far below the old site's near-ceiling Weaponry/Abilities placeholders. Special 94.0 carries the venom specialization; the same mechanism is not duplicated into elite Defense, Stamina or Raw Power.
