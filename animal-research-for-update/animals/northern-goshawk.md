# Northern Goshawk (*Astur gentilis*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Northern Goshawk (now often called the Eurasian goshawk)
- **Scientific name:** *Astur gentilis* (Linnaeus, 1758), long known as *Accipiter gentilis*
- **Exact animal represented:** the living Eurasian species *Astur gentilis*, found across the forests of Europe and northern Asia. Since 2023, most authorities treat North American goshawks as a separate species, the American goshawk (*Astur atricapillus*). That form is similar in build and habits but is not the canonical animal here.
- **Status:** Living; Least Concern.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult female of a northern or central European population, **1.3 kg**, **62.0 cm** total length, about **1.15 m** wingspan. Females are much larger than males and take the largest prey.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** very strong. Across races, males average about 762 g (357–1,200 g) and females about 1,150 g (758–2,200 g). In central Fennoscandia averages are 865 g for males and 1,414 g for females. Females are 58–69 cm long with a 108–127 cm wingspan, against 46–63 cm and 89–122 cm for males. ADW gives females 860–1,360 g and 61 cm average length.
- **Population notes:** size increases toward the north and east. The largest females can weigh up to four times the smallest adult males, which is exceptional. Some populations now nest in city woods in Berlin, Hamburg and Tokyo.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The feet are the weapon. It kills by driving its talons into prey and squeezing, holding its head back to avoid flailing limbs, then kneading until the prey stops struggling. It takes a wide range of birds and mammals: corvids, pigeons, grouse, pheasants, thrushes and woodpeckers, and squirrels, rabbits and hares. Males have taken rabbits up to 1.6 kg, about 2.2 times their weight. Females in Fennoscandia have killed adult mountain hares of 2.7–3.6 kg, up to 2.4 times their own weight, and adult females have been reported killing cock capercaillie in winter. It sometimes preys on ravens, which are about its own size and fight back.

### Offensive mechanics
It is a surprise-attack specialist. Hunting flights are short and hidden, averaging about 83 seconds for males and 94 seconds for females, and it may abandon a chase if the prey spots it too early. A goshawk wearing a head camera intercepted moving prey by holding it at a constant visual bearing, then switched to direct pursuit for the final strike. Only sharp sideways turns by the prey broke its visual lock. When determined, it can chase a squirrel or hare for up to 15 minutes, and pairs occasionally hunt agile prey together.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Short, broad wings and a long tail make it one of the best fliers through dense woodland. It twists through branches, accelerates hard from a perch and turns sharply at speed. It hunts mostly inside large mature forest patches, but also over farmland and in towns.

### Endurance
It is built for short bursts and repeated perch-hunting rather than long soaring, though it can soar on migration. GPS-tracked males in Białowieża Forest regularly flew up to 20 km from forest nests to hunt feral pigeons in a town. Stamina is scored moderate-strong.

### Senses
It hunts by sight in cluttered forest. Head-camera data show it keeps visual fixation on fleeing prey better horizontally than vertically, and prey that jink sideways can break its lock. No acuity measurement was located.

### Intelligence and tactics
Individuals use different foraging strategies: some hunt deep in old-growth forest, others make regular trips to farmland, villages and towns. It uses cover to stay hidden, adjusts pursuit strategy to stationary or moving targets, caches prey during nesting, and changes diet with season and prey abundance. Younger breeders travel farther than older, more experienced birds.

### Intraspecific behavior and aggression
Nest territories of 600–4,000 ha are strongly defended, each sex mainly against its own sex, using display flights and threat postures. Goshawks are famous for fiercely attacking intruders near the nest, people included (ADW). Females sometimes kill males during courtship. In one case, a goshawk ambushed by a fox slashed the fox's windpipe, and the fox died soon after.

### Environmental strengths
- Mature forest with enough open space below the canopy for fast flight.
- Forest edges, farmland woods and city parks with dense pigeon, corvid or rabbit populations.
- Short-range surprise attacks from cover.

### Weaknesses
- Only about 1.3 kg in absolute mass.
- No armor; light skeleton.
- Burst hunter; long open-country chases are not its strength.
- Prey that turns sharply sideways can break its visual lock.
- Vulnerable to eagle-owls, large eagles, foxes and martens when on the ground or moulting.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** pigeons, corvids, grouse, squirrels, rabbits and hares, other small and medium animals surprised in cover.
- **Mixed:** ravens, similar-sized raptors, small carnivores, animals able to fight back or dodge sideways.
- **Bad:** eagle-owls, large eagles, foxes and larger carnivores, and anything heavy enough to overpower it once it is grounded.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Interception Pursuit** — it holds a fleeing target at a constant visual bearing to cut it off, then switches to direct pursuit for the final strike. Through dense woodland this lets it catch fast, dodging birds and squirrels at close range.
2. **Crush-and-Knead Grip** — powerful gripping muscles drive the talons in and squeeze, while the head is held back from kicks. Females have killed mountain hares weighing up to 2.4 times their own weight.

### Unique traits
1. **Ghost of the Forest** — short, hidden hunting flights averaging only 83–94 seconds, from perch to strike, make it one of the most feared woodland predators of birds.
2. **Extreme Size Gap Between Sexes** — females can be more than twice the weight of males in some populations, so the pair hunts different prey sizes.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The goshawk breeds across the forests of Europe, North Africa and northern Asia to Japan. It prefers large patches of mature forest with open space beneath the canopy, but also uses farmland woods, forest edges and, increasingly since the 20th century, wooded areas in cities such as Berlin, Hamburg and Tokyo. Northern birds may move south in winter.

### Diet and ecology
It is a generalist predator of medium-sized birds and mammals. Corvids, pigeons, grouse, pheasants, thrushes and woodpeckers dominate the bird prey; squirrels, rabbits and hares dominate the mammal prey. One goshawk needs about 120–150 g of food a day, and a kill can last one to three days. Hunting success is roughly 15–30% overall, higher on some prey. In Białowieża Forest, some males hunted in the forest while others commuted up to 20 km to hunt pigeons in town.

### Social structure
Adults live as territorial pairs outside the breeding season. Each sex defends the territory mainly against its own sex. No regular group hunting is documented, though pairs occasionally hunt agile prey together.

### Reproduction and life history
Clutches average around three eggs; the male provides almost all the food while the female incubates and guards the nest. A brood of three needs roughly 700–950 g of food a day. Nest success is often 80–95%, with 2–3 fledglings per successful nest. Breeding typically starts at about 2 years.

### Conservation
The species is listed as **Least Concern**. It was heavily persecuted in the past and has recovered in many areas. In the UK, BTO atlas data show a clear increase in occupied 10-km squares, linked to reduced persecution and better access to prey. Illegal killing and loss of mature forest remain local threats.

### Special features
- Short, broad wings and a long tail for flying through dense woodland.
- Large gripping muscles and strong talons.
- Very strong size difference between the sexes.
- Interception-based pursuit steering.
- Short, hidden attack flights from perches.

### Human interaction
Goshawks have been flown in falconry for centuries. They are also persecuted where they take game birds, racing pigeons or poultry, and nesting goshawks may attack people who come too close to the nest.

### Genuine fun facts
- A goshawk fitted with a head camera helped scientists work out how it steers to intercept fleeing prey.
- Prey that jink sharply sideways are the ones most likely to break a goshawk's visual lock.
- Female goshawks have killed mountain hares up to 2.4 times their own weight.
- Some Białowieża Forest goshawks commuted up to 20 km to hunt pigeons in a town.
- Siberian jays have a special mobbing response to goshawks, their main predator, and young jays that witness it survive better.
- A goshawk killed by a fox once slashed the fox's windpipe, and the fox died soon afterwards.
- Goshawks live wild in the Imperial Palace gardens in central Tokyo.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Northern Goshawk is a powerful forest hawk. The canonical female weighs about 1.3 kg but kills hares and grouse up to twice her weight. Short wings and a long tail let it chase prey through dense trees with elite agility, and it defends its nest fiercely. Its small size and lack of armor keep it far below large predators in raw power and defense.

### Rich narrative profile
The goshawk is the forest's surprise attacker. It waits on a perch, then launches a short, hidden flight, usually under a minute and a half, through gaps in the trees. When it has been seen, it can still turn a chase into a sprint through the branches, holding its target at a fixed bearing to cut it off before switching to a direct strike.

Its power is unusual for its size. Females weighing about 1.3 kg kill mountain hares of up to 3.6 kg, and adult females have been reported taking cock capercaillie. It kills by squeezing, and the grip is backed by large gripping muscles like those of other predatory hawks. Nest defense matches this: goshawks attack intruders, people included, and one goshawk killed by a fox fatally wounded the fox in return.

In an absolute matchup, though, it is a bird of little more than a kilogram with no armor. Its best fights happen in woodland, where it can use cover and turn faster than prey can follow. In the open, against a heavier animal ready for it, or once pinned on the ground, its advantages disappear quickly.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Taxonomy:** older sources, including ADW, mix Eurasian and North American birds under *Accipiter gentilis*. Canonical values here follow Eurasian data.
- **Mass:** strong regional variation (Spanish females median about 1,050 g; Fennoscandian females 1,414 g). The canonical 1.3 kg is a northern or central European female.
- **Lifespan:** BTO's 7 years applies to birds that reach breeding age; ADW's 11 years is a broader average.
- **Maximum prey:** hares up to 2.4 times body mass and cock capercaillie are exceptional upper records, mostly by Fennoscandian females.
- **Speed:** no instrumented top speed; field left at 0.0.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Red-tailed Hawk (1.2 kg; A35.0 Ag90.0 St74.0):** similar mass. Wikipedia notes goshawks have greater agility and endurance in squirrel chases and higher success than red-tailed hawks, so the goshawk sits above on Attack and Agility. The red-tail keeps higher Stamina from open-country soaring.
- **Versus Peregrine Falcon (1.0 kg; A34.0 Ag96.0 Sp93.0):** the peregrine's instrumented stoop keeps it well above on Speed and Special. The goshawk hits harder with a stronger grip on heavier prey, so it sits slightly above on Attack.
- **Versus Gyrfalcon (1.7 kg; A37.0 St84.0):** the heavier gyrfalcon stays just above on Attack and well above on Stamina; the goshawk is more agile in cover.
- **Versus Golden Eagle (5.0 kg; A40.0):** the eagle's much greater mass and larger talons keep it clearly above.
- **Versus Eurasian Eagle-Owl (3.0 kg; A39.5):** eagle-owls prey on goshawks and keep goshawks from nesting near them; with more than twice the mass they stay above on Attack.
- **Anti-inflation check:** kills of prey up to 2.4 times body mass are counted in Weaponry and Ferocity, not in Raw Power or Size.
