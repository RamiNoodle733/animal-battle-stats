# Tapir (*Tapirus terrestris*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Tapir, represented by the lowland tapir (South American or Brazilian tapir, *anta*)
- **Scientific name:** *Tapirus terrestris* (Linnaeus, 1758)
- **Exact animal represented:** the lowland tapir of tropical South America shown in the site photo, not the Malayan, Baird's or mountain tapir. The disputed "kabomani" tapir, which the Tapir Specialist Group does not recognise as a separate species and which genetic evidence suggests is at most a subspecies, is not used.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult female, **230.0 kg**, about **95 cm** at the shoulder and **200 cm** head-body. Females are the larger sex in this species.
- **Sex choice:** Animal Diversity Web lists sexual dimorphism as "female larger", with body length reaching 221 cm in females against 204 cm in the other sex.
- **Population variation:** Wikipedia gives an average adult weight of about 225 kg and a range of 150–320 kg; ADW gives 150–250 kg. 230 kg is a well-grown adult female, not the heaviest records.

### Defensive structure
- Wikipedia (tapir genus): the name comes from Tupi and roughly means "thick", referring to the thick hide; the thick skin on the back of the neck helps protect against jaguars, crocodilians and anacondas. SDZWA: adults deter predators with their tough hide and by snapping and biting.
- A low, erect crest and short mane run from the crown down the back of the neck.

### Proboscis
- The nose and upper lip form a short, flexible, prehensile trunk made almost entirely of soft tissue (ADW). SDZWA notes it works as a snorkel underwater and can explore a circle of ground about 30 cm across without the head moving.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The tapir is a browsing herbivore with one weapon: a strong bite backed by a 230 kg body. It uses it when cornered. In 2005 a farmer in south-eastern Brazil surprised and stabbed a lowland tapir in his corn plantation; the tapir inflicted deep bites to his thighs, neck and cervical region and he died of haemorrhage, the first recorded fatal tapir attack (Haddad et al. 2005). Wikipedia adds that the tapir, a female of about 180 kg, also died of its wounds. Males competing for a receptive female bite each other on the feet (ADW). The tapir also has shoving mass, but no horns, tusks or claws.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Despite its bulky, pig-like shape, the lowland tapir is an excellent swimmer and diver, moves quickly on land and climbs steep terrain (Wikipedia; ADW). Its teardrop-shaped body, narrow at the front, is built for pushing through dense undergrowth (SDZWA). When threatened it bolts into thick cover or water. It is not a fast pursuit runner or an agile fighter in the open.

### Endurance
Tapirs forage over large home ranges at night; GPS tracking of a lowland tapir in the Brazilian Cerrado showed range size responding to how abundant and predictable food was (Mezzini et al. 2025), and DNA diet work shows animals feeding on different plant species across an extensive home range rather than locally common plants (Lautenschlager et al. 2026). SDZWA reports tapirs eating up to 34 kg of food per day. This supports good ordinary endurance for a large herbivore.

### Senses
Vision is poor, but sensitive ears and a strong sense of smell compensate (Wikipedia). The mobile proboscis constantly samples scents, and tapirs use the flehmen response to read scent marks.

### Intelligence and tactics
Behaviour is simple and effective: stay in dense forest near water, feed at night, flee into thickets or water when threatened, and bite if cornered. Camera-trap work in Colombia found occupancy highest in dense forest and near water and lowest in pastures (Alviz et al. 2025). There is no evidence of complex problem solving.

### Fighting and aggression
Normally shy and non-aggressive; Wikipedia notes that tapirs usually avoid confrontation by running, hiding or submerging. Serious attacks on people are rare and usually follow provocation or cornering, including zoo incidents and a bite suffered by a lost Costa Rican environment minister in 2006 (Wikipedia). A Brazilian review of animal-bite lesions lists the lowland tapir among wild animals capable of dangerous bites (Haddad 2022).

### Predator defense
Jaguars and cougars attack tapirs, often at night on riverbanks; black caimans, Orinoco crocodiles and green anacondas also take them (Wikipedia). Defense relies on escape into water, thick neck skin and biting back.

### Environmental strengths and limitations
- Rivers and swamps: it can swim, dive and snorkel away from land predators.
- Dense forest: its shape and mass let it crash through undergrowth that slows pursuers.
- Open pasture: low occupancy and reduced escape options.

### Weaknesses
- Only one weapon, the bite, with no reach.
- Poor eyesight.
- No armor beyond thick skin.
- Prefers flight to fighting; low willingness to press an attack.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** smaller predators and animals that cannot follow it into water or dense cover.
- **Mixed:** similar-mass herbivores and medium predators where its bite and mass can deter an attacker.
- **Bad:** jaguars, large crocodilians, big constrictors and heavy megafauna.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Snorkel Escape** — flees into rivers and swamps, swims and dives well, and can breathe through its raised trunk while the body stays submerged, leaving land predators on the bank.
2. **Cornered Bite** — when trapped, bites hard and repeatedly with chisel-like incisors; a provoked lowland tapir caused fatal bite wounds to a farmer in Brazil.

### Unique traits
1. **Prehensile Proboscis** — a short, flexible trunk formed from the nose and upper lip, made of soft tissue, used to grab branches, pluck fruit and sample scents.
2. **Amazon Heavyweight** — the largest native land mammal of the Amazon, with females outweighing the other sex and adults averaging around 225 kg.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Tropical forests, swamps and river margins of South America east of the Andes, from Venezuela, Colombia and the Guianas south through Brazil to Paraguay, Bolivia and northern Argentina, and west to Peru and Ecuador. It prefers dense forest near water and avoids open pasture. ADW records the species from sea level to high elevations and describes it as an adept climber. In 2024 it was seen in Rio de Janeiro state for the first time since 1914.

### Diet and ecology
A generalist browser of leaves, buds, shoots, small branches, fruit, grasses and aquatic plants, using its trunk to pull food to its mouth. DNA metabarcoding of dung from 31 latrines in Brazil's Atlantic Forest found 61 plant species from 46 families, chosen across a wide home range rather than from the most common local plants (Lautenschlager et al. 2026). Tapirs swallow and spread large numbers of seeds, making them important seed dispersers.

### Social structure
Largely solitary and mainly nocturnal. Mothers and calves stay together, and pairs meet to breed. Social behaviour in the wild is still poorly studied.

### Reproduction and life history
Mating peaks in April to June. Gestation lasts about 13 months (390–395 days), and females usually have one calf every two years. Newborns weigh about 6.8 kg, carry white spots and stripes that fade after about seven months, and are weaned at around six months. Sexual maturity comes in the third year.

### Conservation
Listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. Habitat loss and fragmentation, hunting, road deaths and fire are the main threats. Pantanal megafires in recent years affected large areas of habitat.

### Special features
- Short, flexible, prehensile proboscis used as a snorkel.
- Thick hide, especially on the back of the neck.
- Low crest and short mane along the neck.
- Teardrop-shaped body for pushing through dense undergrowth.
- Four toes on the front feet and three on the hind feet.

### Human interaction
Tapirs are hunted for meat across their range. Serious attacks on people are rare and follow cornering or injury, such as the fatal 2005 case in Brazil. They sometimes feed in smallholder crops; in Colombia, occupancy was positively linked to crop areas used for household food (Alviz et al. 2025).

### Genuine fun facts
- A tapir's trunk is made from its nose and upper lip and works as a snorkel.
- It can explore a circle of ground about 30 cm across with its trunk without moving its head.
- Tapir calves are born with white spots and stripes, like a watermelon.
- Pregnancy lasts about 13 months, one of the longest among land mammals of its size.
- Competing tapirs bite at each other's feet.
- The name "tapir" comes from a Tupi word linked to "thick", after its tough hide.
- In 2024 a lowland tapir was recorded in Rio de Janeiro state for the first time in 110 years.

### Concise site-ready summary
The lowland tapir is a 230-kilogram forest browser that prefers running to fighting. It crashes through undergrowth, swims and dives well and can breathe through its trunk while hiding underwater. When cornered its bite can be deadly, as a fatal 2005 attack in Brazil showed, but it has no horns, tusks or claws and jaguars can still bring it down.

### Rich narrative profile
The lowland tapir is the largest native land mammal of the Amazon, a heavy, rounded browser with a short trunk made from its nose and upper lip. It looks slow, but it moves quickly through dense forest, climbs steep ground and is an excellent swimmer and diver. Its body tapers at the front, letting it push through tangled undergrowth that slows pursuers.

Its strategy against predators is to leave. At the first sign of danger a tapir bolts into thick cover or plunges into water, where it can stay submerged with just the tip of its trunk above the surface. Thick skin on the back of the neck gives some protection against a jaguar's bite, the classic attack on a tapir resting on a riverbank at night.

When there is nowhere to run, the tapir fights with its teeth. In 2005 a Brazilian farmer who stabbed a tapir in his cornfield was bitten repeatedly on the legs and neck and died. That shows real defensive danger, but the tapir remains a single-weapon herbivore: no horns, tusks or claws, poor eyesight and a strong preference for avoiding conflict. On the absolute scale it sits in the moderate middle, with Defense a little above Attack.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** ADW's 150–250 kg and Wikipedia's 150–320 kg differ at the top end; 230 kg sits inside both.
- **Fatal attack details:** the peer-reviewed case report does not give the tapir's weight or sex; Wikipedia's "400 lb female" is secondary and unverified.
- **Lifespan:** Wikipedia's 25–30 years has no stated context; SDZWA's 19.5-year median covers zoo tapirs in general.
- **Speed:** no measurement; "moves quickly" is qualitative.
- **Elevation:** ADW's high-elevation records may mix in mountain-tapir data and are not used in the ratings.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Pygmy Hippopotamus (250 kg; A50 D52 Ag42 St48 Sp50):** similar mass and a similar defensive-bite role. The pygmy hippo's ever-growing canines and thick dermis keep it slightly ahead in Attack and Defense, as its report predicted. The tapir is more mobile on land and in hills and forages more widely, so it gets higher Agility (52.0), Stamina (60.0) and Special (56.0).
- **Versus Okapi (275 kg; A53 D44 Ag61 St67 I56):** a slightly heavier forest browser with hooves and ossicones. The okapi keeps higher Attack, Agility and Intelligence; the tapir gets higher Defense for its thick neck hide.
- **Versus Capybara (54 kg; A31 D29 Ag65 Sp63):** the other big South American water-escape herbivore. The tapir is four times heavier and far better at hurting an attacker, so Attack and Defense are much higher; the capybara keeps higher Agility and a similar water-escape Special.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the old live placeholders (Attack 2.3, Defense 72.4, Agility 17.4) are not used. A 230 kg animal with a proven lethal bite is nowhere near the Attack floor, and thick skin without armor does not justify a 72.4 Defense.
