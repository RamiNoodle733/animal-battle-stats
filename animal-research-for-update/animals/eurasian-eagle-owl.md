# Eurasian Eagle-Owl (*Bubo bubo*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Eurasian Eagle-Owl
- **Scientific name:** *Bubo bubo* (Linnaeus, 1758)
- **Exact animal represented:** the living species *Bubo bubo*, family Strigidae, a widespread owl of Europe and Asia, not other eagle-owl species such as Verreaux's or Blakiston's.
- **Status:** Living; Least Concern.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult female, **3.0 kg**, **70.0 cm** total length and about **1.8 m** wingspan. She is the heavier sex and therefore the stronger fighting form.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Wikipedia summarizes females at 1.75–4.2 kg and the smaller sex at 1.5–3 kg. Guinness World Records gives 71 cm length, 190 cm wingspan and 4.2 kg for the largest females.
- **Population notes:** body size varies widely across the huge range and between subspecies. The 3.0 kg canonical value is a representative large female, not a record bird.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The feet and talons seize and kill; the bill tears apart prey. Everyday prey are mostly voles and rats, but the species also takes hares, rabbits, hedgehogs, ducks, grouse, crows, seabirds and other birds of prey, including other owls. Exceptional records include foxes, marmots and young deer; Wikipedia summarizes prey up to about 17 kg when taken by surprise, but these are rare extremes, not the norm.

The species also shows that a heavy predator does not always take the heaviest prey available. In a rabbit size-selection study, eagle-owls positively selected rabbits under about 900 g, and this preference was weaker in winter when rabbits were scarce.

### Offensive mechanics
Eagle-owls are nocturnal, active from dusk through the night, and spend the day roosting high in trees. They attack from ambush in the dark, a time when many diurnal competitors and prey are inactive. Large prey (over about 3.5 kg) is eaten on the ground, which can expose the owl to losing its kill or to attack by predators such as foxes.

### Intraguild predation
The eagle-owl is a well-documented killer of other birds of prey. European studies have examined raptors in its diet in relation to rabbit availability, and a 2026 study asked whether expanding eagle-owl populations contribute to declines of Northern Goshawks, measuring effects on goshawk and Common Buzzard breeding success, territories and density. This "raptor killer" role is a distinct matchup feature.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
A large, broad-winged owl that hunts over woodland edges and open country at night. It is heavier and less nimble than smaller owls, so Maneuverability is set a little below the Great Horned Owl.

### Endurance
The species is a perch-based nocturnal hunter, not a long-distance migrant or pursuit specialist. Stamina is scored as moderate.

### Senses
As a nocturnal owl it depends on hunting in darkness. No species-specific sensory measurement was retrieved in this run, so Senses is set just below other large owls on the roster (Great Horned Owl 94.0, Snowy Owl 91.0) until measured evidence is added.

### Intelligence and tactics
Evidence of flexible behavior includes selective prey choice (small rabbits when available), a very broad diet across habitats, and removal of competing raptors. Territories are advertised with loud calls, and the owl is heard far more often than seen. This supports good raptor-level tactics.

### Intraspecific behavior and aggression
Adults are solitary outside mating and fiercely defend their territories against other owls, overlapping only slightly when food is scarce. Young leave or are driven out between September and November. Ferocity is high because of territorial defense and its habit of killing other predators.

### Environmental strengths
- Night conditions, when its nocturnal ambush is most effective.
- Woodland edges and mixed landscapes with high roosting perches.
- Areas with many medium prey, such as rabbits, hedgehogs and rats.

### Weaknesses
- Only about 3 kg in absolute mass.
- No armor; light avian skeleton.
- Eating large prey on the ground exposes it to foxes and theft.
- Less effective in daylight open-country contests with fast diurnal raptors.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** rodents, rabbits, hedgehogs, waterbirds, smaller owls and raptors, and any animal caught unaware at night.
- **Mixed:** similar-sized raptors in daylight; foxes, which are prey for the owl but also a threat when it is feeding on the ground.
- **Bad:** medium and large mammals that survive the first strike, armored animals, and large grapplers once the owl is grounded.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Dusk Ambush** — active from dusk through the night and roosting high in trees by day, the eagle-owl attacks in darkness when many diurnal competitors and prey cannot respond.
2. **Raptor Killer** — it kills other birds of prey, including buzzards, goshawks and other owls, and European studies have examined its effect on competing raptors. Against other birds it is often the predator, not the rival.

### Unique traits
1. **Giant Among Owls** — Guinness World Records lists it with Blakiston's fish owl as the largest owls, with the largest females about 71 cm long, 190 cm across the wings and 4.2 kg.
2. **Spine-Proof Palate** — hedgehogs, which few other predators take, are part of its diet.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The Eurasian eagle-owl is found across much of Europe and Asia. The studies cited here come from southwestern Europe, Romania, goshawk and buzzard study areas in Europe, Israel's Judean region and Pakistan, which shows how widely it is distributed. It needs high perches for daytime roosting and open or edge habitat where it can hunt at night.

### Diet and ecology
It is a top nocturnal predator with one of the broadest diets of any owl. Voles and rats are the most common prey; hares, rabbits, hedgehogs, ducks, grouse, crows, seabirds and other raptors are also taken, with rare records of foxes, marmots and young deer. Where rabbits are common they can dominate the diet, and the owl prefers smaller rabbits. In Judea, Israel, breeding success did not depend on the local prey base.

### Social structure
Adults are solitary outside mating and strongly territorial, defending territories against other owls and overlapping only slightly when food is scarce. They call loudly to mark territory and are heard far more often than seen.

### Reproduction and life history
ADW reports a breeding season from December to April. Young leave the nest area or are driven out by the adults between September and November. Ring recoveries show wild individuals can exceed 20 years of age.

### Conservation
The species is listed as **Least Concern**, with a total population often summarized at about 100,000–500,000 individuals and a decreasing trend. Local declines and recoveries vary widely; in parts of Europe, expanding eagle-owl populations are now studied for their effects on other raptors.

### Special features
- Among the largest owls in the world.
- Nocturnal activity with daytime roosting high in trees.
- Powerful talons able to kill hares, hedgehogs and other raptors.
- Loud territorial calls.
- Extremely broad, flexible diet.

### Human interaction
Eagle-owls can live in mixed landscapes near people, where rats and rabbits are available. As populations expand in parts of Europe, conservationists study their effect on other protected raptors such as goshawks. Rehabilitated birds are sometimes shown to the public at raptor events.

### Genuine fun facts
- Guinness World Records ranks it with Blakiston's fish owl as the largest owls.
- It is heard far more often than seen, thanks to loud territorial calls.
- It kills other birds of prey, including other owls.
- Hedgehogs, which few predators tackle, are among its prey.
- In a rabbit study it preferred rabbits under about 900 g, even though it can kill much larger prey.
- Exceptional prey records include foxes and young deer.
- Ring recoveries show wild eagle-owls can live more than 20 years.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Eurasian Eagle-Owl is one of the world's largest owls, about 3 kg in the canonical female with a wingspan near 1.8 m. It hunts at night from high perches, taking rats, rabbits and hedgehogs and even killing other birds of prey. It is powerful for a bird, but its low absolute mass and lack of armor keep it far below mammals in raw power and defense.

### Rich narrative profile
The Eurasian eagle-owl is the night-time counterpart of the great eagles. Females reach around 3–4 kg with wings spanning well over 1.5 m, and the species is ranked with Blakiston's fish owl as the largest owl alive. It roosts high in trees through the day, and from dusk onward it hunts across woodland edges and open country.

Its diet is extremely broad. Voles and rats make up much of it, but the owl also takes hares, rabbits, hedgehogs, waterbirds and crows, and it has a reputation among raptor researchers as a killer of other birds of prey, from buzzards and goshawks to smaller owls. Studies even show it choosing smaller rabbits when it can, a sign that it hunts with efficiency, not just raw ambition.

In an absolute matchup its strengths are timing and surprise. A 3 kg owl striking at night can kill animals much larger than most birds could handle. But it has no armor and a light skeleton, and when it has to eat large prey on the ground, foxes and other predators can steal the kill or attack the owl. In daylight open-country contests or against large, tough opponents, its advantages fall away.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Largest owl:** Blakiston's fish owl is slightly heavier on average and the great grey owl slightly longer; "largest owl" is shared, not exclusive.
- **Maximum prey:** 10–17 kg prey figures are exceptional records of surprise attacks and are not used as the typical kill.
- **Lifespan:** 20 years is an upper-range ringing figure; mean wild survival is unmeasured here; 60-year captive claims are not used.
- **Senses:** no species-specific sensory measurement was retrieved, so the Senses score is calibrated against other owls and flagged for review.
- **Length:** a representative female mean was not retrieved; 70.0 cm is near the upper range.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Great Horned Owl (1.7 kg; A35.0 D18.0 Ag89.0 St57.0 I66.0 Sp80.0):** the eagle-owl is almost twice as heavy and routinely kills raptors and hedgehogs, so Attack (39.5), Weaponry and Defense are higher. Agility is lower because of the heavier build. Special stays slightly below, as the Great Horned Owl score rests on better-documented sensory evidence.
- **Versus Snowy Owl (2.15 kg; A39.0 St83.0):** the eagle-owl should clearly exceed the snowy owl on Attack given its greater mass and larger prey. The current Snowy Owl 39.0 leaves only a 0.5 gap, so **the Snowy Owl Attack looks slightly high** and should be checked in the roster audit. Snowy Owl keeps far higher Stamina because of its migratory, open-tundra life.
- **Versus Barn Owl (0.35 kg; A13.0):** a large gap on every physical rating is correct.
- **Versus Golden Eagle (5.0 kg; A40.0):** both kill foxes and raptors, but the eagle is heavier with stronger feet, so the owl sits just below on Attack.
- **Versus Red Fox (7.2 kg; A28.0 D25.0):** foxes are occasional prey and also a threat to a feeding owl. The owl's higher Attack reflects its strike from ambush, while the fox keeps higher Defense and much higher Raw Power relative to the owl's size.
- **Anti-inflation check:** "raptor killer" is counted in Special and Ferocity, not added again to Raw Power.
