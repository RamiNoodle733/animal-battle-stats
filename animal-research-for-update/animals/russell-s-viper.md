# Russell's Viper (*Daboia russelii*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Russell's Viper (chain viper, Indian Russell's viper, seven pacer)
- **Scientific name:** *Daboia russelii* (Shaw & Nodder, 1797), family Viperidae, subfamily Viperinae
- **Exact animal represented:** the Russell's viper of the Indian subcontinent (India, Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, possibly Bhutan). Populations in South-East Asia are now treated as a separate species, the eastern Russell's viper *Daboia siamensis*, and are not used here.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult, **1.3 kg** and **120 cm** total length, sex unspecified. Wild venom-yield work found no significant difference between males and females, and this pass found no reliable size difference between the sexes, so no sex-based modifier is applied.
- **Population notes:** mainland adults average about 120 cm; island animals are slightly shorter on average. Venom composition and potency vary between Indian regions, and antivenom works poorly against the semi-arid north Indian population (Senji Laxme et al. 2021). Sri Lankan and some south Indian bites can also cause paralysis, which is rarer elsewhere.

### Bite, fangs and venom
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No bite-pressure measurement; the weapon is venom through hinged front fangs.
- **Fangs:** about **16.5 mm** in an average specimen, with up to five or six pairs of replacement fangs behind the active pair.
- **Yield:** wild Indian snakes averaged **106.6 mg** dry venom (median 86 mg, range 0.5–749 mg, n = 115); adults had a median of about 96 mg (Gond et al. 2025). Older reports of adult yields range 130–268 mg.
- **Potency:** mouse LD50 about **0.31 mg/kg** in the Gond et al. comparison; other values 0.133 mg/kg intravenous, 0.40 mg/kg intraperitoneal and about 0.75 mg/kg subcutaneous. The estimated lethal dose for most adult humans is 40–70 mg, within one bite's yield.
- **Composition and action:** a venom rich in enzymes, including a factor X activator and a factor V activator that trigger runaway clotting, plus phospholipase A2s. Bites cause severe pain, swelling, blistering, consumption coagulopathy, bleeding from gums and urine, falling blood pressure and heart rate, kidney failure in about 25–30% of untreated bites, and in some survivors lasting pituitary damage. The clotting activator is used in the dilute Russell's viper venom time (dRVVT) laboratory test.

### Defensive structure
- Strongly keeled dorsal scales on a stout, round body. No real armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary and secondary weapons
The primary weapon is a powerful strike through hinged fangs about 16.5 mm long, injecting a large dose of venom that activates clotting factors X and V, uses up the blood's clotting capacity and damages kidneys and blood vessels. The secondary weapon is intimidation: the snake forms S-loops, raises the front third of its body and produces a hiss often described as louder than any other snake's.

### Offensive mechanics
Adults usually sit still and strike only when provoked, but the strike is fast and forceful; large individuals can lift off the ground with the effort. The bite may be a quick snap or the snake may hang on for many seconds, increasing the dose. With a typical adult yield near 100 mg and a lethal human dose estimated at 40–70 mg, one bite can deliver several lethal doses for a person.

### Defensive features and durability
A 1.3 kg stout-bodied viper with keeled scales but no armor. It relies on cryptic chain-like patterning, the loud hiss and the strike.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Adults are slow and sluggish when moving, while juveniles are more nervous. Close-range strike control is good; overall mobility is limited.

### Endurance
A terrestrial, mainly nocturnal forager that becomes more active by day in cool weather. Ectothermic and not built for sustained exertion.

### Senses
It lacks the heat pits of pit vipers, but experiments show it reacts to thermal cues, and nerve endings in the supranasal sac resemble those of other heat-sensitive organs. It also has normal viper chemoreception and large eyes.

### Intelligence and tactics
Defense is graded: S-loops, raised posture and hiss before striking. It settles where rodents are common, including farmland and village edges. No evidence of flexible problem solving.

### Sociality and hunting
Solitary. Adults specialize in rodents; they also eat small reptiles, land crabs, scorpions and other arthropods. Juveniles forage actively for lizards at dusk.

### Fighting and aggression
Adults generally do not attack unless provoked, but they react violently when handled or cornered. The long, lifting strike has fed the myth that Russell's vipers "chase" people.

### Environmental strengths
- Open grassland, scrub, plantations and farmland with rodents.
- Night encounters, when its patterning and stillness hide it.
- Close range, where its strike and large venom dose work best.

### Weaknesses
- Slow-moving adults with limited escape ability.
- No armor; vulnerable to crushing and to snake-eating predators.
- Venom damage, though severe, builds over minutes to hours.
- Needs close contact to bite.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** rodents and small to medium unarmored mammals; opponents that step or reach within striking range.
- **Mixed:** other venomous snakes of similar size.
- **Bad:** snake-eating raptors and mongooses, large hoofed animals that can trample it, armored animals and fast opponents that attack from outside strike range.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Clotting-Factor Hijack** — the venom contains activators of clotting factors X and V, setting off runaway clotting that uses up the blood's clotting capacity and leads to bleeding and kidney failure. A typical adult carries around 100 mg, above the estimated 40–70 mg lethal dose for an adult human.
2. **Pressure-Cooker Hiss** — when threatened it forms S-loops, lifts the front third of its body and gives a hiss reputed to be the loudest of any snake, then strikes with enough force that large individuals can lift off the ground.

### Unique traits
1. **Chain-Link Camouflage** — three rows of dark, black-ringed spots edged in white or yellow run down a tan or brown body, breaking up its outline in grass and leaf litter; the harmless rough-scaled sand boa has a similar pattern.
2. **Mass Live Birth** — it gives birth to live young, with litters of 20–40 common and up to 75 recorded, each 21–26 cm long at birth.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Russell's viper lives in India, Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh, and possibly Bhutan. It avoids dense forest and very humid places such as swamps and rainforest, preferring open grassy or bushy areas, scrub, plantations and farmland in plains, coastal lowlands and hills. It is common in parts of Punjab, the west coast and southern India, and is often found near settlements because of the rodents that live around people.

### Diet and ecology
Adults eat mainly rodents; juveniles eat lizards and forage actively. Other prey include small reptiles, land crabs, scorpions and other arthropods. Radio-tracking in a rural Indian community found people within 50 m of a tagged viper in about 17% of 2,066 locations, showing how closely the species lives alongside humans.

### Social structure
Solitary.

### Reproduction and life history
Live-bearing. Mating usually occurs early in the year; gestation lasts more than six months, and young are born from May to November, mostly in June and July. Litters of 20–40 are common and up to 75 has been recorded. Sexual maturity is reached in about 2–3 years; the smallest gravid females are about 100 cm long.

### Conservation
Listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List (2021 assessment, as reported by Wikipedia). It is common across much of its range.

### Special features
- Hinged fangs about 16.5 mm long with replacement fangs behind.
- Large venom yield with clotting-factor activators.
- Loud defensive hiss and S-loop posture.
- Thermal-cue sensitivity without pit organs.
- Chain-like blotched camouflage.

### Human interaction
Russell's viper is one of India's "big four" snakes and is thought to cause the majority of clinically severe envenomings in a country with about 58,000 snakebite deaths a year (Senji Laxme et al. 2021; Suraweera et al. 2020). The Reptile Database describes it as possibly the most common dangerously venomous snake in southern Asia. Plantation workers were the most at-risk group in the radio-tracking study. Survivors can suffer long-term pituitary damage. Its venom's clotting activator is used worldwide in the dRVVT test for lupus anticoagulant.

### Genuine fun facts
- Its hiss is often said to be the loudest of any snake.
- Large individuals can lift off the ground with the force of a strike.
- A single litter of 75 young has been recorded.
- A typical adult carries around 100 mg of venom, more than the estimated lethal dose for an adult human.
- Hospitals use its venom in a blood test called the dilute Russell's viper venom time.
- In one study, about 29% of bite survivors had pituitary damage.
- The genus name *Daboia* is thought to come from a Hindi word meaning "the lurker".

### Concise site-ready summary
Russell's viper is a stout, chain-patterned South Asian viper about 1.2 m long, with 16.5 mm fangs and a venom that sets off runaway clotting. It warns with a very loud hiss and strikes hard, and it is thought to cause most severe snakebites in India. Its body is still small and slow, so its danger lies almost entirely in the bite.

### Rich narrative profile
Russell's viper is built around one devastating weapon. At about 1.2 m and 1.3 kg it is a mid-sized viper, slow when it moves and usually content to lie still in grass or farmland. But its hinged fangs are around 16.5 mm long, and a typical adult carries about 100 mg of venom that activates clotting factors X and V, draining the blood of its ability to clot and damaging kidneys and blood vessels.

When disturbed it does not simply flee. It pulls its body into S-loops, raises the front third of its body and produces a hiss often called the loudest of any snake. If the threat continues, it strikes with enough force that a large snake can lift off the ground, sometimes holding on for several seconds. This forceful strike is behind the myth that it chases people.

In battle terms, Russell's viper has one of the strongest venom weapons among the roster's vipers, stronger in delivery than a rattlesnake of similar size. Everything else is modest: little strength, no armor, slow movement and an ectotherm's limited stamina. It wins fights that come to it, and loses fights where the opponent can stay out of reach or crush it first.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** only one aggregated figure; the canonical 1.3 kg is low-moderate confidence.
- **Venom yield:** older reports (130–268 mg) run higher than the 2025 wild median (86 mg); large captive animals probably explain the gap.
- **LD50:** values range from 0.133 to 0.75 mg/kg depending on route of injection and population.
- **Hiss:** "loudest of any snake" is a traditional claim, not a measured comparison.
- **Death share:** no species-level share of Indian deaths was verified in this pass; the "majority of severe envenomings" statement comes from Senji Laxme et al.
- **Lifespan:** 15 years is a captive maximum.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Gaboon Viper (7 kg):** the Gaboon viper has much longer fangs (about 5 cm), a larger body and extreme camouflage, so it keeps higher Weaponry and Special. Russell's viper gets higher Attack because its venom is far more potent per milligram and its strike is more forceful and readily used, and higher Agility.
- **Versus Rattlesnake (2.5 kg):** similar size class. The rattlesnake has heat pits and a rattle, so it keeps higher Senses, Defense and Agility. Russell's viper gets higher Attack and Weaponry for a more potent venom and larger doses, while Special stays one point lower because it lacks true pit organs.
- **Versus Indian Cobra (2 kg):** a fellow big four snake. The cobra is more agile and more flexible in behavior; Russell's viper gets higher Attack and Weaponry for longer hinged fangs and larger venom yields.
- **Versus Saw-scaled Viper (0.15 kg):** both are big four vipers with clot-destroying venoms. Russell's viper is almost nine times heavier with roughly 30 times the venom yield of southern saw-scaled vipers, so it is well above in Attack, Weaponry, Defense and Special; the saw-scaled viper is more agile.
- **Anti-inflation check:** its central role in India's snakebite burden reflects how often it meets people in farmland, not extra combat power, and is not counted in Attack. Physical ratings stay low for a 1.3 kg unarmored snake.
