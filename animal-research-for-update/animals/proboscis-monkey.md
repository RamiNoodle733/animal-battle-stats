# Proboscis Monkey (*Nasalis larvatus*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Common name:** Proboscis Monkey
- **Scientific name:** *Nasalis larvatus* (Wurmb, 1787), family Cercopithecidae, colobine Old World monkey.
- **Exact animal represented:** the living Borneo-endemic proboscis monkey, not another odd-nosed colobine.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime adult **male**, because this is one of the most sexually dimorphic colobines and adult males average about twice female mass.
- **Canonical mass:** **21.2 kg**. The 2015 *Mammalian Species* synthesis reports mean mass 21.2 kg for 13 males versus 10.0 kg for 14 females. Other reputable summaries give roughly 16–22 kg adult males.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** pronounced. Adult males are substantially larger, possess the dramatically enlarged pendulous nose, and serve as resident males in one-male social units. Females are roughly 7–12 kg. This makes the adult male the biologically appropriate ABS fighter.
- **Population variation:** reported adult dimensions vary somewhat among references and field populations. The canonical values below favor measured means and central adult-male values, not record individuals.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass and dimensions
- **Male mass:** mean **21.2 kg** (n=13) in the species synthesis; ADW gives 16–22 kg males. Canonical: **21.2 kg**.
- **Female mass:** mean **10.0 kg** (n=14); approximately 7–12 kg in broader summaries.
- **Male head-body length:** about **73–76 cm**, mean about **75.5 cm** in the Wisconsin Primate Info Net synthesis. ADW gives about 70 cm in its shorter summary.
- **Male tail:** commonly about **66–75 cm** depending source/population. Tail is approximately body length and functions in balance rather than as a prehensile weapon.
- **Canonical total length:** **142.0 cm**, representing a 75.5 cm head-body animal plus about 66.5 cm tail. Because the site has one `length_cm` field, total nose-to-tail length is more battle-useful than head-body length alone.
- **Canonical height:** **75.5 cm** entered as the best-supported adult-male head-body linear dimension. This is not a standing shoulder height and must be labeled as such in future schema work.
- **Male nose:** adult male nose can exceed **10 cm** and published historical measurements reach **17.5 cm**. It is display/acoustic anatomy, not a striking weapon.
- **Hands/feet:** large; digits have partial webbing associated with unusually capable swimming.

### Speed
No defensible peer-reviewed species-specific maximum running or swimming speed was located. Popular web values such as 24–36 km/h are not sufficiently sourced for a canonical physiological maximum. **Canonical `speed_mps = 0.0`** pending defensible measurement.

### Lifespan
Wild longevity is poorly constrained. Wisconsin Primate Info Net reports **>25 years in captivity**. Some zoological summaries give 15–20 years, but these are not strong enough to treat as a measured wild mean. **Canonical `lifespan_years = 20.0`**, moderate/low confidence as a representative profile value, with >25 years documented as captive potential.

### Bite force and weapons
- No reliable species-specific bite force or bite-pressure study was located. **Canonical `bite_force_psi = 0.0`** rather than recycling the live site's unsupported 100 PSI placeholder.
- Natural offense consists of primate jaws/canines, grasping hands, pulling and striking at close range. No defensible canine-length measurement suitable for canonical use was found.
- The 2015 species account reports **very low observed aggression**, with many agonistic events being chases, threats or displacements rather than physical contact. This strongly limits Attack/Ferocity inflation.

### Defensive structures
There is no armor, shell or unusually thick hide. Defense is behavioral: arboreal escape, powerful leaps, group vigilance, river crossing and underwater swimming. Large body size for a colobine helps against small opponents but does not make this a durable roster-wide tank.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offensive mechanics
The proboscis monkey is a 20+ kg primate with grasping hands, mobile limbs and a toothed jaw, so it can bite, grapple, shove and strike at close range. However, field behavior argues against treating it like a baboon or macaque specialized for frequent violent contest. Long observational records describe agonism as uncommon and often non-contact. Resident males can chase intruders and may confront threats while group members retreat, but intimidation and displacement are more typical than sustained damaging combat.

### Defense and toughness
Physical protection is ordinary mammalian skin, fur, skeleton and muscle. Its main defensive advantages are escape options. It can move through canopy, leap into water, swim submerged and choose narrow river crossings. The species account records underwater travel up to about **20 m** in predator avoidance and reports groups leaping from **5–15 m** into rivers. Crocodiles remain a serious counter: modern field work explicitly notes monkeys avoiding river crossing where saltwater crocodiles were abundant.

### Locomotion and agility
Proboscis monkeys combine arboreal walking, suspensory movement, leaping, quadrupedal movement and strong swimming. Their unusually long hindlimb proportions and large hands/feet support controlled canopy and river-edge movement. Most activity in one field study occurred 10–17 m above ground, with animals climbing to 21 m. This versatility supports high Agility, but no invented top speed is used.

### Endurance
Daily travel is not extreme by mammalian standards but is sustained in difficult three-dimensional habitat. Published field summaries report about **500 m to 1.81 km/day**, with averages near **0.9 km/day** in multiple studies. River crossings and repeated climbing add energetic complexity. Stamina is therefore good but not elite endurance-predator level.

### Senses and communication
Vision, hearing and primate spatial awareness are useful in canopy travel. The enlarged adult-male nose has a documented role in sexual display and amplification/resonance of calls. Four common call types include shrieks, honks, roars and brays; high-frequency alarm/aggression calls may aid contact and predator avoidance in dense vegetation.

### Intelligence and tactics
This is a socially organized primate with flexible one-male units, all-male groups and larger aggregations. It selects narrower river crossings, shifts sleeping sites and uses riverside sleeping positions in ways interpreted as predator avoidance. These are meaningful tactical behaviors, but low aggression and relatively limited direct social interaction argue against assigning ape-like or near-human combat intelligence.

### Fighting, predation and predator defense
The species is primarily a folivore/frugivore, not a predator. Adult males may confront intruders while others depart. Intraspecific physical violence is uncommon in the best behavioral synthesis. Predator avoidance emphasizes vigilance, canopy escape and water use. Crocodiles are a particularly important aquatic hazard, so swimming is a situational escape advantage rather than universal safety.

### Environmental strengths
- Riverine/mangrove forest with trees over or near water.
- Branch networks that permit climbing and leaping.
- Water crossings where crocodile pressure is low enough to make swimming useful.
- Dense vegetation where vocal contact and arboreal escape matter.

### Environmental weaknesses
- Open terrestrial ground removes much of its canopy advantage.
- Deep water with large crocodilians turns its signature swimming route into danger.
- No armor or high-damage specialized weapon.
- Large fermenting gut and folivorous specialization are not combat advantages.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** smaller terrestrial animals unable to follow into canopy/water; similarly sized animals with poor climbing and weak grappling reach.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized agile mammals or primates with stronger weaponry.
- **Bad:** baboon-like weapon specialists, medium/large carnivores, constrictors able to secure a hold, crocodilians at crossings, and any large predator that removes the escape route.

- **Agility: 85.0** - excellent canopy control plus leaping and swimming gives unusually broad maneuvering options.
- **Stamina: 69.0** - sustained daily travel and mixed climbing/swimming support a strong but non-elite score.
- **Intelligence: 74.0** - flexible social and predator-avoidance behavior, route choice and primate learning, kept below great apes and the strongest cognitive roster anchors.
- **Special: 84.0** - unusually aquatic primate anatomy/behavior plus the male's enlarged acoustic/display nose are genuinely distinctive matchup-relevant biology.

## 7. Abilities and traits

### Special abilities
1. **River Escape** - partial digital webbing, large hands/feet and strong swimming let the monkey enter water, cross channels and travel submerged for short distances, creating an escape route unavailable to most arboreal primates.
2. **Resonant Alarm** - the mature male's enlarged pendulous nose modifies/amplifies vocal output, supporting loud communication and threat/alarm signaling in dense riverine forest.

### Unique traits
1. **Pendulous Resonator** - mature males possess an extraordinary nose that can exceed 10 cm and historically has been measured to 17.5 cm, tied to sexual display and acoustic function.
2. **Foregut Fermenter** - as a colobine, it relies on a complex microbial foregut that supports a leaf-heavy diet; ecologically important but not scored as extra physical offense.

## 8. Expanded profile content

### Habitat and range
Endemic to **Borneo**, including parts of Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei. Strongly associated with mangroves, riverine forest, swamp forest and lowland forest near water.

### Diet and ecology
Primarily leaves, seeds and unripe fruit, with diet varying seasonally. Foregut fermentation allows use of fibrous plant material. Fruit availability influences daily path length. It is not a meaningful predatory species.

### Social structure
One-male groups commonly contain an adult male, several females and offspring; all-male groups also occur, and units may aggregate near rivers/sleeping sites. Observed within-group aggression is low. Resident-male changes can occur without the severe fighting seen in some other primates.

### Life history
Gestation is reported around 166–200 days. Captive longevity exceeds 25 years. Wild longevity is less securely quantified.

### Conservation
The species is **Endangered** and CITES Appendix I. Habitat destruction/fragmentation and hunting have driven major declines. ESABII's IUCN-derived summary reports a decline greater than 50% over approximately three generations.

### Adaptations
Large hands and feet, partial webbing, strong swimming, long limbs for arboreal/suspensory locomotion, enlarged male nose, flexible social grouping and colobine foregut fermentation.

### Human interaction
River traffic can alter behavior. A controlled field study found approaching motor boats induce stress-related behavior in riparian proboscis monkeys. Habitat conversion, hunting and fragmentation remain much larger conservation threats.

### Fun facts
- It is the **largest living colobine** in the 2015 species synthesis.
- Adult males average about **twice female mass**.
- Proboscis monkeys have been documented traveling **up to about 20 m underwater** to avoid threats.
- Groups may leap **5–15 m** from trees into rivers when crossing.
- The mature male nose can reach extraordinary lengths, historically reported up to **17.5 cm**.
- Despite the spectacular male appearance, field studies describe **low levels of aggression** and many agonistic interactions without physical contact.

### Concise site-ready summary
A large, sexually dimorphic Bornean colobine, the proboscis monkey combines a 21 kg male body with remarkable arboreal and aquatic mobility. Its partially webbed digits and strong swimming create unusual escape options, while the male's pendulous nose amplifies its distinctive calls. It is agile and tactically flexible, but low observed aggression and the absence of specialized killing weapons keep its absolute Attack modest.

### Narrative profile
The proboscis monkey is built around the river margin. In the canopy it walks, suspends and leaps; at the water's edge it can commit to a route most monkeys would avoid, diving from branches and swimming across channels. That versatility matters more in a matchup than the animal's famous nose. A healthy adult male is large for a colobine at roughly 21 kg, but it is not a primate bruiser on the scale of a baboon, chimpanzee or gorilla. Field observations repeatedly show low rates of physical aggression. Its best battle plan is positional: use height, branches, water and social awareness to avoid a bad engagement, then bite or grapple only if cornered.

The enormous male nose is biologically important without being a weapon. It functions in display and vocal resonance, contributing to the deep honks and other calls used in dense habitat. The same male may act as a defensive focal point for a one-male unit, confronting an intruder while others retreat. Yet the monkey's strongest ABS identity remains mobility. Few roster primates can combine canopy movement with confident river crossing and short underwater travel.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- Male body mass converges around ~20–21 kg, with reputable ranges generally 16–22+ kg. The 21.2 kg measured mean is preferred.
- Head-body length ranges differ by source, but ~73–76 cm is repeatedly reported for adult males.
- Tail values vary around 66–75 cm; total-length canonical value is therefore moderate-confidence.
- Popular top-speed claims are rejected because primary measurement support was not located.
- Wild lifespan is not well established. The 20.0-year canonical value is a profile compromise and should be replaced if a robust wild survivorship/longevity dataset is found.
- No bite PSI is inferred from anatomy or generic primate values.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes
- At **21.2 kg**, this monkey must remain dramatically below the 120 kg Black Bear and 500 kg Polar Bear in Raw Power, Attack and Defense.
- It is heavier than the 12 kg North American River Otter, but the otter's carnivorous dentition and predatory behavior justify the otter retaining comparable or greater practical Attack despite lower mass.
- Agility can legitimately exceed many much larger animals because this category measures control/repositioning rather than force. The combination of arboreal movement and swimming supports an **85.0** without importing top-speed claims.
- Intelligence is raised by flexible primate behavior but held well below Orca's 99.0 and below great-ape expectations.
- Special is high because aquatic proficiency is unusually developed for a primate and the male nasal-acoustic system is distinctive, but neither adaptation is allowed to inflate Raw Power or Defense.
- Low observed aggression prevents the old site's generic primate reputation from anchoring Ferocity/Tactics. The new ratings are independently researched.
