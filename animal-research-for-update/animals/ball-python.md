# Ball Python (*Python regius*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Ball Python (royal python)
- **Scientific name:** *Python regius* (Shaw, 1802), family Pythonidae
- **Exact animal represented:** the wild ball python of West and Central African savanna and open woodland, from Senegal to Sudan and Uganda. Captive "morphs" are bred from wild stock and are not used as the combat form. This is not the much larger reticulated or Burmese python covered on the roster as Python.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult **female**, **1.3 kg** and **125.0 cm total length**, representing a well-grown wild female rather than a record or captive-fattened animal.
- **Sex choice:** females are the larger sex. A study of 1,250 field-caught ball pythons in Togo found that adult females were larger, in better body condition and had relatively longer jaws than males, and that females mature at about 95 cm snout-vent length. The larger, heavier-jawed female is therefore the stronger specimen, as with the roster's other pythons.
- **Population variation:** Wikipedia's summary of the Togo data gives average snout-vent lengths of 116.2 cm for females and 111.3 cm for males, with the heaviest females at 1.635 kg and the heaviest males at 1.561 kg. Captive animals often grow heavier.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- Aubret et al. (2005) as summarised on Wikipedia: heaviest wild females **1.635 kg**, heaviest males **1.561 kg** in the Togo sample.
- Breeding data (Wikipedia, citing McCurley 2005): wild females breed at weights as low as 0.8 kg, most commonly at **1.2 kg or more**.
- AnAge lists an "adult weight" of 8,858 g, which is far above every field figure and is rejected as an error or captive outlier.
- **Canonical:** **1.3 kg**, a typical breeding-size wild female below the heaviest recorded field females.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The ball python is an ambush constrictor. It strikes, holds with its recurved teeth and wraps coils around the prey. Its prey is small: rats, mice, shrews, bats, squirrels, bushbabies and birds such as young grey parrots. Juveniles strike as fast as much larger snakes relative to their size, but a three-year study found strike performance fell steeply during the first 18 months of life. At 1.3 kg its constriction is only a threat to small animals.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Stocky and short-tailed, it moves slowly on the ground and spends much of its time in or near burrows. Males climb more than females. It is not a fast snake.

### Endurance
Like other pythons it runs on low energy, aestivating in burrows during the dry season and going long periods between meals. High-output effort is brief.

### Senses
Heat-sensing pits along the lips detect warm-bodied prey. Imaging of living ball python pit organs found them much more absorbent of infrared radiation than the surrounding skin. It also relies on tongue-flicking chemical senses and moderate vision.

### Intelligence and tactics
Hunting is ambush from cover or burrows, targeted by heat and scent. Behavioural flexibility beyond that is limited.

### Sociality and hunting
Solitary. Active at night and around dusk and dawn. Diet shifts with size: snakes under 70 cm mainly eat small birds, while those over 100 cm mainly eat small mammals. Males take more birds and females more mammals.

### Fighting and aggression
The species is famous for not fighting back. When threatened it rolls into a tight ball with its head tucked in the middle, rather than biting, which is a major reason it became a popular pet.

### Environmental strengths
- Savanna, grassland and farmland with rodent burrows.
- Night-time ambush of small mammals.
- Hot, dry seasons, which it waits out underground.

### Weaknesses
- Small absolute size for a python.
- Slow on the ground.
- Rarely counterattacks; the ball defense can be pried open by large predators.
- No armor.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** rodents, shrews, small birds and other prey it can wrap.
- **Mixed:** similar-sized snakes and lizards.
- **Bad:** mongooses, birds of prey, larger snakes and almost any medium or large predator.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Heat-Pit Strike** — pits along the lips sense the body heat of prey, guiding a fast strike in darkness that is followed by constriction. Against warm-blooded prey at night it can find and hit targets it cannot see.
2. **Ball Guard** — when threatened it coils into a tight ball with the head hidden in the centre, protecting its most vulnerable part instead of biting back.

### Unique traits
1. **Burrow Lodger** — it spends much of its time in mammal burrows and other underground refuges, where it also waits out the dry season.
2. **Virgin Birth** — a wild-caught pet female with no contact with a male laid eggs containing embryos that genetic tests showed came from her alone, demonstrating parthenogenesis in the species.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The ball python lives in grassland, savanna, open woodland and farmland across West and Central Africa, from Senegal through Cameroon to Sudan and Uganda. It favours mammal burrows and other underground hiding places, and it tolerates land cleared for farming.

### Diet and ecology
It eats mainly small mammals, including Gambian pouched rats, soft-furred and striped grass mice, bats, forest giant squirrels and Demidoff's bushbabies, plus birds such as young grey parrots. Young snakes under 70 cm feed mostly on small birds, and snakes over 100 cm mostly on mammals.

### Social structure
Solitary. It is nocturnal or crepuscular. Males are more often found climbing and females on the ground.

### Reproduction and life history
Females lay 3–11 large, leathery eggs and coil around them; the eggs hatch after about 55–60 days, and the young fend for themselves. Males mature at 11–18 months and females at 20–36 months, with body weight as important as age. Parthenogenesis has been confirmed in a pet female.

### Conservation
The species is listed as **Near Threatened** on the IUCN Red List (2022) and is on CITES Appendix II. Wild populations are believed to be declining in most of West Africa, mainly because of collection for the international pet trade, along with hunting for skin, meat and traditional medicine and habitat loss. In southern Togo, 58 interviewed hunters collected 3,000 live ball pythons and 5,000 eggs in 2019 alone for sale to snake ranches.

### Special features
- Infrared-sensing lip pits.
- Coiling ball defense with the head hidden.
- Pelvic spurs on both sexes, larger in males and used to grip during mating.
- Stocky body with a short tail and small head.
- Recurved teeth for holding prey.

### Human interaction
The ball python is the most popular pet snake. Breeders have produced more than 7,500 colour and pattern morphs; the "spider" morph is linked to malformations of the inner ear that cause head wobbling, and one herpetological society banned its sale at events. The Igbo people of southeastern Nigeria revere the ball python, and in many communities an accidentally killed python is given a funeral.

### Genuine fun facts
- It gets its name from rolling into a ball with its head tucked in the middle when scared.
- A ball python at the Saint Louis Zoo lived to 62, the oldest known snake in captivity.
- Breeders have produced more than 7,500 different morphs.
- Its lip pits can sense the body heat of prey in darkness.
- A pet female produced embryos without ever meeting a male.
- In Togo, hunters collected 3,000 live ball pythons and 5,000 eggs in a single year.

### Concise site-ready summary
The ball python is a small, stocky constrictor from the savannas and farmland of West and Central Africa. A 1.3 kg adult female hunts rats and other small animals at night, finding them with heat-sensing pits and killing them by constriction. When threatened it would rather roll into a ball with its head hidden than bite, which makes it a gentle pet and a weak fighter.

### Rich narrative profile
In the West African savanna, a ball python spends its day underground, often in a burrow dug by a rodent. At night it waits near a trail or burrow entrance. The pits along its lips pick up the heat of a passing rat, and the snake strikes, grips with rows of curved teeth and throws its coils around the prey. It is an efficient hunter of small mammals and birds, and not much more.

Its defense is what made it famous. When a predator or a person grabs it, the ball python usually does not bite. It rolls into a tight ball with its head buried in the middle, protecting its skull and neck. That makes it easy to handle, and it is now the most popular pet snake in the world, bred into thousands of colour morphs. The same pet trade weighs on the wild population: the species is Near Threatened, and hunters in Togo alone collected thousands of snakes and eggs in a single year.

In ABS terms the ball python is a small constrictor with modest Attack, low Defense and middling Agility and Stamina. Its heat pits and constriction give it a solid Special score, but at about 1.3 kg it is far below the roster's big pythons, and its reluctance to bite means it rarely presses a fight.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Boa Constrictor (12 kg; 58.0 / 35.0 / 53.0 / 49.0 / 44.0 / 67.0):** the boa is about nine times heavier with measured circulatory-arrest constriction, so it is far higher in Attack and higher in Defense. The ball python's heat pits keep its Special close to the boa's.
- **Versus Python (50 kg; 72.0 / 47.0 / 52.0 / 52.0 / 44.0 / 82.0) and Anaconda (50 kg; Attack 70.0):** the giant constrictors are about 40 times heavier. The ball python's Attack is a third of theirs, and its Special is lower because its constriction only matters against small prey.
- **Versus Rattlesnake (2.5 kg; Attack 58.0, Special 94.0):** similar size, but venom lifts the rattlesnake far above the ball python in Attack and Special.
- **Versus Corn Snake (0.5 kg; 18.0 / 12.0 / 58.0):** the ball python is heavier and stronger, but the corn snake climbs and moves faster, so it has higher Agility.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the python name and constriction do not carry big-python scores to a 1.3 kg snake; Ferocity stays low because it usually refuses to bite.
