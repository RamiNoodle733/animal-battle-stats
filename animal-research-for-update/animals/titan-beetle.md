# Titan Beetle (*Titanus giganteus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Titan beetle
- **Scientific name:** *Titanus giganteus* (Linnaeus, 1771), family Cerambycidae (longhorn beetles), subfamily Prioninae
- **Exact animal represented:** the South American titan beetle, one of the largest beetles in the world by length.
- **Living/extinct:** Living
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult male, **15.5 cm** long and **37.5 g**, matching the wild male collected in French Guiana in January 2019 and measured in the first comprehensive physiological study of the species (Insects, 2020). This is a single measured individual, used because no population averages exist.
- **Sex choice:** the measured specimen is male, and males are the sex usually collected at lights. Sexual size differences were not quantified in the sources located.
- **Population notes:** the largest reliably measured specimen is **16.7 cm**. Tertiary pages claim masses above 50 g, but no measurement supports that, and it is not used.

### Length
- Insects (2020): 155 mm for the measured male. Largest reliable measured specimen: 16.7 cm (Wikipedia, summarising collection records). The Smithsonian notes adults up to about 6.5 in (16.5 cm) and holds a record specimen in the National Entomological Collection.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 15.5**.
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high.

### Speed
- Adults can fly; the measured male came to a 250 W light at about 1:30 am. No flight or running speed has been measured.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0** (no reliable measurement).

### Lifespan
- Larvae have never been found; they are thought to feed inside wood and may take several years to reach full size. The adult stage is short: tertiary sources give about two to five weeks, and the 2020 study found adults cannot refuel (see below).
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 0.0**, meaning unresolved: total life length is unknown because the larval stage has never been observed.

### Bite and mandibles
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No bite-force measurement exists.
- **Mandibles:** short, curved and sharp. Wikipedia reports they can snap pencils in half and cut into human flesh; this is a widely repeated claim without a located primary measurement, so it is used only as qualitative evidence of a painful defensive bite.

### Defensive structure
- A large, hard, heavily sclerotised body with thick elytra. Threatened adults produce an audible hiss by stridulation (rubbing body parts together).

### Physiology of the adult
- The 2020 study found the adult gut degenerated and the fat body almost absent. Adults rely on reserves built up as larvae; storage lipids in the flight muscles contained 13 fatty acids, with oleic acid about 60% of the total. Adults do not eat.
- The antennae carry fields of coeloconic sensilla and many sensilla trichoidea, fitting a male that searches for mates by scent.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary and secondary weapons
The primary weapon is a pair of short, curved, sharp mandibles, used defensively; they are reported to cut human skin. The secondary defense is a loud hiss that warns off predators. It has no venom, sting, horn or chemical spray.

### Offensive mechanics
The titan beetle never hunts; adults do not eat at all. Its bite is a defensive response when it is grabbed or cornered. Against other insects or a small animal's fingers or snout, a cutting bite can draw blood; against anything large it is a nuisance.

### Defensive features and durability
Its large, hard body is one of the most robust insect exoskeletons on the roster, and its size puts it beyond many small predators. The hiss adds a warning. Its weakness is that it cannot repair or refuel: with no working gut, an adult runs down its reserves over a few weeks.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Heavy and slow on the ground, able to climb tree trunks and to fly, mostly at night. Flight is brief and appears mainly for dispersal and mate searching. It is not an agile animal.

### Endurance
Low. Adults run on stored fat and cannot eat, so every flight and every struggle draws down a fixed supply.

### Senses
Antennae with dense sensilla detect scent, useful for finding mates. Vision and other senses were not studied in the sources located.

### Intelligence and tactics
Simple and stereotyped: hiss, bite, fly away. No evidence of learning or flexible tactics.

### Sociality and hunting
Solitary. Adults do not feed; larvae are presumed wood borers. Males search the forest at night and are drawn to lights.

### Fighting and aggression
Defensive only. It bites and hisses when handled but does not start fights. No observations of male-male combat were located.

### Environmental strengths
- Old-growth lowland rainforest with large dead trees.
- Night-time forest understory where its size deters small predators.
- Close-quarters defensive fights against small animals that try to grab it.

### Weaknesses
- Slow and clumsy on the ground.
- Cannot feed as an adult, so it tires and cannot recover.
- No venom or chemical defense.
- Tiny in absolute terms despite being a giant among insects.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** other insects, spiders and small predators that try to seize it.
- **Mixed:** small birds and small mammals, which may be put off by the bite and hiss.
- **Bad:** anything larger than a rat, anything with armoured jaws or beaks, and any fight that lasts long enough to drain its reserves.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Pencil-Snapping Mandibles** — short, curved, sharp jaws that are reported to snap pencils and cut into human flesh, used to defend itself when grabbed.
2. **Warning Hiss** — when threatened, the adult rubs body parts together to make a loud hiss that warns predators before it bites.

### Unique traits
1. **Giant of the Insect World** — one of the largest beetles known, with a reliable record of 16.7 cm and a measured wild male of 15.5 cm and 37.5 g.
2. **Adult That Never Eats** — the adult gut is degenerated and it lives entirely on reserves stored during its hidden larval life; its larvae have never been found.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Lowland tropical rainforest of northern South America: Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, the Guianas and north-central Brazil. Its prime habitat is old-growth Amazon and Guiana forest with large trees and dead wood.

### Diet and ecology
Adults do not feed. Larvae are thought to feed inside dead wood, possibly for several years, but have never been found, so their diet and development are inferred rather than observed. Adults are active at night, and males are drawn to lights.

### Social structure
Solitary.

### Reproduction and life history
Adults emerge, search for mates at night and live only a few weeks on stored reserves. Males track females by scent using dense antennal sensilla. Nothing is known directly about the larvae or pupae.

### Conservation
Not Evaluated: no IUCN Red List assessment was located in this pass. The species depends on old-growth rainforest with large dead trees, and specimens are prized by collectors.

### Special features
- One of the longest beetles in the world, up to 16.7 cm.
- Sharp, curved mandibles.
- Defensive hissing by stridulation.
- Non-feeding adult with a degenerated gut.
- Flight muscles stocked with storage fat rich in oleic acid.
- Antennae packed with scent-detecting sensilla.

### Human interaction
Handling a titan beetle can earn a painful, skin-cutting bite. Specimens are valuable to collectors, and night-time light traps are the main way scientists obtain adults. The 2020 study was one of the first to examine the species' biochemistry and anatomy in detail.

### Genuine fun facts
- The largest reliably measured titan beetle was 16.7 cm long.
- A wild male from French Guiana weighed 37.5 g, about as much as a large mouse.
- Its larvae have never been found.
- Adults do not eat; their gut is degenerated and they live on stored fat.
- Its jaws are said to snap pencils in half.
- It hisses when threatened.
- The scientists who studied the 2019 male caught it when it flew to a light at 1:30 in the morning.

### Concise site-ready summary
The titan beetle of the Amazon is one of the largest beetles on Earth, reaching almost 17 cm. It defends itself with a loud hiss and sharp mandibles said to snap pencils, but adults cannot eat and live only a few weeks on stored reserves. Huge for an insect, it is still tiny and short on stamina in any battle with vertebrates.

### Rich narrative profile
The titan beetle is a giant among insects. A wild male measured in 2019 was 15.5 cm long and weighed 37.5 g, and the largest reliable specimen reached 16.7 cm. It lives in the lowland rainforests of northern South America, where adults fly at night through old-growth forest and are sometimes drawn to lights.

It is also a mystery. Its larvae have never been found, though they are thought to grow inside dead wood for years. The adult stage is a short final chapter: the gut is degenerated, the fat body almost gone, and the beetle lives on reserves stored in its flight muscles. When threatened it hisses and bites with short, curved mandibles that can cut skin.

On the absolute battle scale, the titan beetle gets the highest Attack and Defense of the beetles in this batch because of its sharp jaws and heavy armour, but those scores remain very low next to vertebrates. Its biggest weakness is stamina: an animal that cannot eat cannot keep fighting, and anything larger than a small mammal can overpower it.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** only one measured individual (37.5 g); tertiary claims above 50 g are unsupported.
- **Adult lifespan:** tertiary figures range from about 2 weeks to 5 weeks; no field study located.
- **Total lifespan:** unknown because the larval stage has never been found.
- **Mandible strength:** the pencil-snapping claim is widely repeated but not measured; bite force stays at 0.0.
- **Range:** listed from secondary sources; distribution records outside the Guianas and the Amazon basin should be checked.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Hercules Beetle (A 7.0, D 12.0, Ag 44.0, St 32.0, I 20.0, Sp 60.0):** almost the same mass (37.5 g versus 34 g). The titan's cutting mandibles injure better than the Hercules beetle's gripping horns, so Attack is slightly higher (11.0), and its larger armoured body gives slightly higher Defense (14.0). The Hercules beetle is the better climber and flier with a feeding adult, so it keeps higher Agility and Stamina. Its horn clamp is a rarer mechanism than the titan's hiss, so the Hercules keeps the higher Special.
- **Versus Hornet (A 10.0, Sp 76.0):** similar Attack (the titan's jaws against the hornet's small venom dose), but the hornet's flight and venom give far higher Agility and Special.
- **Versus Bombardier Beetle (A 5.0, D 16.0, Sp 85.0):** the titan is nearly two thousand times heavier and hits harder, but the bombardier's chemical spray makes it the better defender and gives it a much higher Special.
- **Versus Stag Beetle (live values are legacy placeholders, no research report):** a large male stag beetle and the titan are both big-jawed beetles; the titan is heavier and should stay at or above the stag beetle in Size and Protection when the stag beetle is re-researched.
- **Anti-inflation check:** "largest beetle" status drives Size and Protection only. It does not lift Attack out of the insect band, and no pound-for-pound strength claims are used.
