# Huntsman Spider research report

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Roster label:** Huntsman Spider
- **Resolved taxon:** Giant huntsman spider, *Heteropoda maxima* Jäger, 2001
- **Family:** Sparassidae
- **Living/extinct:** Living
- **Known distribution:** Laos. The World Spider Catalog accepts *H. maxima* and gives Laos as its distribution; Bayer & Jäger (2009) document it from limestone caves in Khammouan Province.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature **female**, using a representative adult body length of **3.8 cm** rather than the 4.85 cm upper measured female. Females are materially larger-bodied: Bayer & Jäger measured adult females at 28.5-48.5 mm body length versus 24.0-30.0 mm in males.
- **Dimorphism:** females have the larger body-size range; males are slimmer and, as in sparassids generally, long-legged males can retain strong cursorial performance. The canonical choice follows the larger fighting-body standard, while not assuming that the female owns every locomotor maximum.
- **Population note:** the species is unusually range-restricted in the formal literature and associated with Lao limestone caves. Do not generalize Australian huntsman ecology or clinical bite data to this species without labeling it family/genus context.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Body size
Bayer & Jäger's redescription is the strongest quantitative source. Adult males measured **24.0-30.0 mm** body length. Adult females measured **28.5-48.5 mm**. Female prosoma length was 13.0-19.0 mm and opisthosoma length 15.5-25.7 mm; males had prosoma 12.9-15.0 mm and opisthosoma 11.2-14.4 mm.

The famous **30 cm leg span** is a documented species maximum/record-scale value, not a suitable normal body-length field. Guinness recognizes *H. maxima* as the largest arachnid by leg span at up to 30 cm. The 2009 taxonomic treatment gives male leg-II summed segment lengths of 106.6-130.6 mm for one leg, illustrating the extreme limb elongation directly.

### Mass
No defensible species-specific live adult mass distribution was recovered. The research does **not** use popular unsourced ounce/gram figures. `weight_kg` therefore remains 0.0 pending a measured *H. maxima* dataset.

### Height
A standing-height metric is not standardized or useful for this laterigrade spider. `height_cm` remains 0.0.

### Speed
No measured maximum running speed for *H. maxima* was recovered. Sparassids are cursorial spiders, and a 2021 comparative biomechanics study measured sparassid/lycosid running across animals from 0.0054 to 3.01 g, finding body-size scaling and no strong hydraulic-extension limitation. Another 2021 study on *Delena cancerides* found maturity/sex-linked morphology affects running energetics. Those studies support the **mechanism**, not a species-specific top speed for *H. maxima*. `speed_mps` remains 0.0.

### Lifespan
The Australian Museum states most huntsman species live about two years or more, but this is family-level context and is not a measured *H. maxima* longevity estimate. `lifespan_years` remains 0.0 rather than laundering a family generalization into a species fact.

### Bite force and weapons
No credible *H. maxima* bite force in Newtons or PSI was found. `bite_force_psi` remains 0.0. The taxonomic redescription documents three promarginal cheliceral teeth and usually four retromarginal teeth, occasionally five, plus denticles near the promarginal teeth. The chelicerae are therefore real grasping/puncturing weapons, but no pressure value is assigned.

Heteropoda spiders are active hunters rather than prey-capture-web users. Published genus-level work describes strong chelicerae gripping prey followed by venom injection. That is appropriate behavioral context, but species-specific venom potency for *H. maxima* was not recovered.

### Defensive structures
The main defenses are evasive locomotion, a low flattened profile, long laterigrade legs, camouflage and the arthropod exoskeleton. This is not heavy armor. The species should not receive tarantula urticating-hair defenses: those belong to Theraphosidae and are not a huntsman adaptation.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary offense
The giant huntsman is a cursorial capture predator. Its combat sequence is best modeled as rapid approach/reorientation, leg-assisted control, cheliceral grip and venom delivery. Heteropoda literature supports strong chelicerae and venom-assisted prey capture, but there is no basis for treating *H. maxima* venom as a roster-level vertebrate killer.

### Defensive biology and toughness
Its defense is avoidance-heavy. Laterigrade legs provide a broad stance and multidirectional movement potential. Comparative work on other laterigrade flat spiders shows how splayed legs can generate fast translation and rotation toward stimuli, a useful biomechanical analogue but not a direct *H. maxima* performance measurement. The spider remains a small, lightly protected arthropod in absolute terms and is vulnerable to crushing, birds, reptiles and specialized arthropod predators.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Huntsmans are active, non-web capture hunters. Long legs, a flattened body and sideways-capable stance are well suited to rock faces, crevices and complex surfaces. *H. maxima* retains normal eyes despite its cave association. Sparassidae are among spider groups whose visual systems are associated with active predation. Tactile/vibrational input through the legs is also central to spider-scale close-range control, though this report does not claim a unique quantified *H. maxima* threshold.

### Endurance
Evidence is insufficient for a species-specific sustained-output estimate. Cursorial hunting implies repeated locomotor bouts, but the animal is not calibrated as an endurance specialist. Hydraulic leg extension also means injury, dehydration and physiological state can sharply constrain performance.

### Intelligence and tactics
This is a flexible active predator rather than a passive trap. It can orient, pursue and select capture movements, but there is no evidence for vertebrate-like planning or complex social combat. Intelligence stays low on the full 225-animal absolute scale while tactics are somewhat higher because the hunting system is behaviorally directed.

### Fighting and predation behavior
Direct species-specific field observations of *H. maxima* prey choice and intraspecific combat are sparse. Family-level huntsman evidence supports insect/other-invertebrate predation and active capture. Females of several huntsman taxa guard egg sacs aggressively, but this should be treated as Sparassidae context rather than asserted as a measured *H. maxima* maternal-care program.

### Environmental strengths
- Rock walls, caves, crevices and other structurally complex surfaces favor its low profile and long legs.
- Darkness and clutter reduce the value of long-range vertebrate vision while preserving close-range tactile/vibrational hunting.
- Narrow spaces can deny access to much larger opponents.

### Environmental weaknesses
- Open terrain removes much of its crevice advantage.
- Water immersion, severe desiccation and cold can impair a terrestrial ectothermic arthropod.
- Its absolute mass and exoskeletal protection are tiny relative to mammalian, avian and large-reptile opponents.

### Matchup archetypes
**Best:** small insects and other small arthropods that can be seized by the legs/chelicerae; opponents dependent on slow turning in tight microterrain.

**Worst:** birds, lizards, medium/large mammals, armored prey too large for cheliceral control, and any opponent capable of one-step crushing or pecking at range. Its large apparent span should never be confused with large absolute mass.

## 6. Six headline ABS ratings

- **Attack: 10.0** - chelicerae and venom can rapidly subdue small arthropods, but absolute damage is extremely low across this roster.
- **Defense: 7.0** - evasion helps avoid contact, but physical protection is minimal against larger animals.
- **Agility: 86.0** - this is the standout category: long laterigrade limbs and cursorial hunting support excellent reorientation and surface control. This is not a disguised top-speed score.
- **Stamina: 35.0** - active-hunter capacity without evidence for exceptional sustained exertion.
- **Intelligence: 34.0** - directed active predation and sensorimotor choice, but no evidence for high-order tactical cognition.
- **Special: 61.0** - venom-assisted capture plus extreme laterigrade reach and cave/crevice locomotor geometry create matchup effects without making the spider physically powerful.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and exactly two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Laterigrade Blitz** - the crab-like, laterally oriented leg geometry gives rapid multidirectional repositioning and a very wide capture envelope on walls and irregular surfaces.
2. **Cheliceral Venom Lock** - prey is physically controlled by the chelicerae and venom-assisted bite, making small-arthropod captures much more decisive than raw body mass suggests.

### Unique traits
1. **Thirty-Centimeter Reach** - *H. maxima* holds the documented arachnid leg-span record at up to about 30 cm, an extreme reach-to-body-size configuration.
2. **Lao Karst Giant** - the accepted species is known from Laos and documented in limestone-cave systems, pairing giant sparassid proportions with a crevice/cave-associated ecology.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Accepted records place *H. maxima* in Laos, particularly Khammouan limestone-cave localities. The narrow documented range warrants caution: absence of records elsewhere is not proof of absolute absence, but the site should not portray it as a pan-Asian species.

### Diet and ecology
Direct species-specific diet quantification is lacking. Heteropoda and huntsman spiders are active predators of insects and other invertebrates and do not depend on a prey-capture web. The ecological role is therefore best described as a cursorial arthropod predator, with exact prey composition left unresolved.

### Social structure and life history
No robust *H. maxima*-specific social or reproductive field dataset was recovered. Huntsman family references document egg-sac guarding and, in some taxa, prolonged maternal attendance. Those are useful hypotheses for future *H. maxima* work, not facts to copy into the canonical profile.

### Conservation
No IUCN Red List assessment for *H. maxima* was recovered in this run. Its apparently restricted cave/karst distribution makes habitat specificity relevant, but assigning a threatened category without an assessment would be inappropriate.

### Major adaptations
The defining package is extreme leg elongation, laterigrade stance, flattened cursorial body plan, cheliceral/venom prey capture and sensory systems suited to active hunting in complex surfaces.

### Human interaction
The species is famous because of its leg-span record, but species-specific clinical bite evidence is sparse. Clinical findings from Australian huntsman species must not be represented as *H. maxima* trials. Avoid sensational claims that it is either harmless or medically dangerous without species-specific evidence.

### Fun facts
- It was formally described only in **2001**.
- Guinness recognizes it as the arachnid with the largest documented leg span, up to **30 cm**.
- Bayer & Jäger later measured **20 females and 7 males** in their redescription, providing unusually useful sex-specific morphology.
- Females can reach **48.5 mm body length**, while the measured male range was **24.0-30.0 mm**.
- The World Spider Catalog still lists the accepted species distribution simply as **Laos**.
- Its fame comes from span, not mass: the Goliath birdeater remains the much heavier kind of giant spider.

### Concise site summary
The giant huntsman spider is a Lao cave-associated sparassid and the world-record spider for leg span. Its battle value comes from extreme reach, crab-like multidirectional movement, active hunting and venom-assisted chelicerae, not from absolute strength or armor.

### Rich narrative profile
*Heteropoda maxima* is a useful test of ABS's absolute-scale discipline. At spider scale it is extraordinary: a body only a few centimeters long can command a leg span approaching 30 cm, giving the animal a huge sensory and capture perimeter. Its laterigrade legs spread sideways rather than behaving like miniature mammal limbs, fitting the active huntsman lifestyle on complex surfaces. It does not need a prey web to make contact. It closes distance, controls prey with the legs and chelicerae, and uses venom as part of the capture sequence.

That spectacular geometry does not turn it into a heavyweight. Against vertebrate-sized opponents, the same spider is fragile and extremely low-mass. Its best ABS categories should therefore be Agility, Senses and Special, while Raw Power, Protection, Attack and Defense stay near the roster floor. This preserves what is biologically exceptional without converting proportional spider feats into fictional absolute strength.

### Main uncertainties and conflicts
- **Mass:** no species-specific live adult dataset recovered. Do not repeat popular unsourced mass figures.
- **Speed:** no *H. maxima* maximum measured. Family speed studies are context only.
- **Lifespan:** huntsman family estimate exists, species-specific value does not.
- **Venom:** presence/use is biologically grounded; species-specific potency and human clinical severity are not well quantified.
- **Diet:** active invertebrate predation is well supported at family/genus level; exact *H. maxima* prey frequencies are not.
- **Image:** explicit adult female source is too low resolution; higher-res exact-species sources found remain below preferred threshold.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Hercules Beetle:** Huntsman receives higher Agility and sensory/tactical flexibility, but both remain extremely low in absolute Raw Power and Defense. Neither gets mammal-like physical scores from spectacular proportional feats.
- **Versus Hornet:** Hornet's flight and sting create a different delivery system; huntsman has stronger surface control and a broader physical capture envelope but should not automatically outrank a flying venom-delivery specialist in Special.
- **Versus Goliath Birdeater:** *H. maxima* wins documented leg span, while the Goliath birdeater is substantially heavier. Span is not mass and should not inflate Size/Raw Power.
- **Versus Hummingbird:** both can be exceptionally maneuverable while physically tiny. Agility is allowed to score very high because the category measures control, while Attack/Defense remain low.
- **Inflation check:** Attack 10.0 and Defense 7.0 preserve the huge absolute gap to Honey Badger, large cats, bears, hippos and megafauna. Agility 86.0 is not derived from an invented top speed.
