# Giant Centipede

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Giant Centipede / Amazonian giant centipede
- **Scientific name:** *Scolopendra gigantea* Linnaeus, 1758
- **Family:** Scolopendridae
- **Living/extinct:** Living
- **Exact ABS animal:** The live schema explicitly identifies Giant Centipede as *Scolopendra gigantea*.
- **Canonical specimen:** Healthy mature adult of unknown sex, **30.0 cm total length**. Sex is not used as a combat selector because reliable external sexing is difficult and this run found no strong quantitative sex-specific size series.
- **Taxonomic/geographic caution:** Shelley & Kiser's neotype revision stabilizes *S. gigantea* around northern Venezuela and documents its core range in northern Colombia/Venezuela plus Trinidad, Margarita, Curaçao and Aruba. Some older Pacific South American material belongs to *S. galapagoensis*, so broad “Peruvian giant centipede” claims must not be imported uncritically.

## 2. Physical measurements and functional anatomy

### Length and mass

Animal Diversity Web reports a normal maximum around **12 in / 30 cm**, while Guinness gives **26 cm** for the largest-centipede record entry. Australian Museum independently describes *S. gigantea* as a 30 cm South American centipede. The disagreement is best treated as source/population variation rather than false precision. A representative large mature specimen is therefore set at **30.0 cm total length**.

No strong primary or institutional adult body-mass dataset was found in this run. The live site's 0.05 kg value is therefore **not retained as verified**. `weight_kg` is set to **0.0 pending defensible measurement**, rather than anchoring to a placeholder.

### Height

A standardized standing height is not biologically useful for a dorsoventrally flattened centipede and no defensible adult value was found. `height_cm = 0.0`.

### Speed

The animal is a rapid terrestrial predator, but no instrumented species-specific maximum speed for *S. gigantea* was verified. The old 3.6 km/h value is rejected as unsupported. `speed_mps = 0.0`.

### Lifespan

Animal Diversity Web reports a normal lifespan of about **10 years**. Husbandry sources vary, so **10.0 years** is retained with moderate confidence rather than extending it to poorly supported 15-year maxima.

### Forcipules, legs and venom delivery

The first trunk appendages are modified into powerful venom-delivering **forcipules** under the head. They pierce and hold prey while venom is injected. The terminal legs are elongated and spiny and can assist anchoring, defense and prey control. The species has the many-segmented, low-profile body typical of scolopendromorph centipedes and usually 21 or 23 leg-bearing segments in secondary descriptions.

There is no defensible bite-pressure PSI measurement. Calling the forcipule strike a “50 PSI bite” confuses venom-delivery mechanics with jaw pressure. `bite_force_psi = 0.0`.

### Exoskeleton and water balance

The sclerotized exoskeleton provides meaningful protection against tiny arthropod prey, but on the 225-animal absolute scale it is thin and cannot function like vertebrate armor. ADW notes spiracular water loss and dehydration risk, making humid refuges behaviorally important.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary and secondary weapons

The primary weapon is the paired venomous forcipules. They combine penetration, gripping and chemical incapacitation. Mandibles process food after capture. Numerous legs stabilize the body and allow rapid traction around irregular substrate, while the terminal legs contribute to anchoring and grappling.

### Offensive mechanics

*S. gigantea* is not merely an insect eater. The peer-reviewed Venezuelan cave observations documented giant centipedes climbing cave ceilings, capturing bats and subduing prey substantially heavier than themselves. The predator can hang with only part of its body anchored while restraining prey with other appendages. This is unusually strong evidence for practical grip, climbing and venom-assisted prey control.

That feat is impressive proportionally but must not be converted into mammal-level absolute Raw Power. Its success comes from leverage, many contact points, penetration and venom, not large absolute muscle force.

### Venom and medical significance

A published human neonatal envenomation caused intense local pain, edema, local hyperthermia and clotting at punctures. The clinical literature establishes medically significant venom but does not justify a fabricated universal LD50 or a direct PSI-equivalent damage number. Human effects and prey immobilization are therefore treated qualitatively.

### Defense and durability

Defense is mostly avoidance, concealment, threat display, rapid movement and painful counterattack. The exoskeleton is useful at arthropod scale but provides little absolute protection against a bird, mammal or large reptile. Crushing, pecking or tearing attacks from much larger opponents are severe threats.

### Locomotion and maneuverability

The long, low body with many independently articulated legs gives excellent traction, reversal and obstacle-following in leaf litter, rock crevices and cave surfaces. It climbs extremely well, as directly demonstrated by ceiling-hunting behavior. Its agility is therefore high for terrain control even though no top-speed number is assigned.

### Endurance

It is an active nocturnal predator capable of climbing and prolonged searching, but there is no evidence for exceptional sustained aerobic output. Water loss through spiracles is an important environmental constraint. Stamina is rated moderate-low on the whole-roster scale.

### Senses

Long antennae provide close-range tactile and chemical information, while simple eyes offer limited visual resolution. It is well suited to dark cluttered environments but lacks the long-range sensory reach of visually or acoustically specialized vertebrate predators.

### Intelligence and tactics

Behavior is effective but largely stereotyped predatory sequencing: detect, approach, seize, anchor, envenomate and hold. Ceiling ambush on bats demonstrates ecological exploitation and body-position control, but there is no evidence for mammal- or corvid-like flexible cognition.

### Fighting, predation and predator defense

The species eats large invertebrates and small vertebrates. Published Venezuelan observations include predation on three bat species and show that centipedes can kill prey heavier than themselves. Against predators it can flee into narrow refuges, coil or orient its venomous anterior end toward the threat, and use terminal legs to maintain purchase.

### Environmental strengths

- Humid tropical forest floor, caves, bark, rock crevices and cluttered three-dimensional substrate
- Tight spaces where a long flexible body and many contact points matter
- Small opponents susceptible to penetration and venom
- Ambush positions where anchoring permits the rest of the body to grapple prey

### Environmental weaknesses

- High dehydration risk in dry exposed environments
- Very low absolute mass and tissue depth
- Exoskeleton offers little protection from much larger vertebrate attacks
- Venom delivery requires close contact
- Open terrain removes much of its concealment and climbing advantage

### Matchup archetypes

**Favorable:** insects, arachnids and other small arthropods; small amphibians/reptiles; tiny mammals or birds that can be seized and envenomated.

**Unfavorable:** armored or thick-skinned animals that cannot be penetrated effectively; large birds able to strike from reach; mammals many orders of magnitude heavier; dry exposed arenas where water loss and lack of cover matter.

## 7. Abilities and traits

### Proposed special abilities, exactly 2

1. **Forcipule Injection** — Modified front limbs pierce and hold prey while delivering medically significant venom, turning a low-mass strike into a disproportionately effective small-prey weapon.
2. **Ceiling Grapple** — Many contact points and powerful terminal anchoring allow the centipede to suspend itself from cave ceilings while seizing bats, a directly observed predatory tactic.

### Proposed unique traits, exactly 2

1. **Multi-Point Traction** — More than twenty pairs of walking legs distribute grip across irregular terrain, allowing rapid control through leaf litter, crevices and steep surfaces.
2. **Segmented Low Profile** — A long, flexible, flattened body permits access to narrow refuges and lets the animal wrap its contact points around complex substrate while keeping the venomous head mobile.

## 8. Expanded profile content

### Habitat and range

The taxonomically revised core range is northern South America and the southern Caribbean, especially northern Colombia and Venezuela, Trinidad, Margarita, Curaçao and Aruba, with later literature extending verified distribution westward to Panama and eastward toward the Guianas. It occupies tropical/subtropical forest and dry-forest habitats but relies on dark humid refuges such as leaf litter, soil, rocks, logs and caves.

### Diet and ecology

It is a generalist carnivorous predator of large invertebrates and small vertebrates. ADW lists crickets, worms, snails and roaches plus lizards, toads and mice. The strongest field evidence is the Venezuelan cave study documenting predation on *Mormoops megalophylla*, *Pteronotus davyi* and *Leptonycteris curasoae* bats.

### Social structure and life history

Adults are primarily solitary predators. Reproduction is indirect: the male deposits a spermatophore/sperm package and the female receives it. Females brood eggs and remain with the early young, an important form of parental care for an arthropod predator.

### Conservation

No robust IUCN global assessment was located for *S. gigantea* in this run. Conservation status should therefore be recorded as **not evaluated / no verified assessment found**, not assumed from abundance in the pet trade.

### Adaptations

- Venom-delivering forcipules
- Many-legged traction and rapid low-profile locomotion
- Elongated terminal legs used in anchoring and defense
- Flexible segmented trunk
- Antennal close-range sensing
- Strong climbing ability
- Maternal egg brooding

### Human interaction

Bites can be intensely painful and medically significant. A peer-reviewed neonatal case documented severe local symptoms. Human risk should not be exaggerated into routine lethality, but handling is inappropriate because venom delivery is real and defensive strikes are possible.

### Fun facts

- *S. gigantea* is among the world's largest living centipedes, reaching roughly 30 cm in strong references.
- The famous Venezuelan cave observations show it can hang from ceilings and capture bats in flight or at roost.
- The bat study explicitly reports prey substantially heavier than the centipedes themselves.
- Its “fangs” are not true jaws: they are the first pair of trunk limbs modified into venomous forcipules.
- Females brood their eggs rather than abandoning them immediately.
- Taxonomic revision showed that some famous “giant centipede” records from western South America belong to *S. galapagoensis*, illustrating why exact-species sourcing matters.

### Concise site-ready summary

The Amazonian giant centipede is a roughly 30 cm venomous predator built around close-range control. Its absolute strength and durability are tiny on the full ABS roster, but venomous forcipules, dozens of gripping legs and exceptional climbing let it kill small vertebrates and even capture bats from cave ceilings. Its profile is therefore low in raw power and defense but very high in agility and biological specialization.

### Detailed narrative profile

*Scolopendra gigantea* is a useful calibration test because its most dramatic feats are real but easy to mis-score. A giant centipede can subdue prey heavier than itself, yet that does not make its Raw Power comparable with a fox, wolf or large reptile. The correct explanation is a package of penetration, venom, leverage and many-point anchoring operating against relatively small prey.

Its cave-bat predation is particularly informative. Rather than chasing bats through open air, the centipede exploits the cave ceiling as an anchor, leaving enough of its body free to seize and envenomate a passing or perched bat. This supports very high maneuverability and Special without inventing high sprint speed or vertebrate-scale strength.

Defense remains poor in absolute terms. The exoskeleton is meaningful against arthropod-scale attacks but offers little against crushing or pecking by much larger animals. Humidity dependence also creates an environmental weakness. The result is a polarized but biologically grounded fighter: extremely specialized at close-range predation on small targets, yet physically fragile against most of the roster.

### Conflicts and uncertainty

- **Length:** strong sources range from 26 to roughly 30 cm. Canonical 30.0 cm represents a large mature adult, not an asserted population mean.
- **Mass:** no defensible representative adult mass dataset was found. The live 0.05 kg placeholder is not treated as fact.
- **Speed:** no measured species-specific maximum verified. Legacy 3.6 km/h rejected.
- **Bite force:** no PSI measurement verified. Legacy 50 PSI rejected.
- **Venom potency:** clinical and prey evidence establish meaningful venom, but no species-specific universal LD50 or simple human lethality number is asserted.
- **Sex:** reliable external sexing is difficult and quantitative sex-specific combat-size data were not found, so sex is left unknown rather than guessed.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Bullet Ant:** Giant Centipede is much larger and has far greater individual prey-handling capacity, so Attack and Raw Power should be higher, while both remain tiny in absolute roster terms.
- **Versus Emperor Scorpion:** Both are venomous arthropods, but *S. gigantea* has stronger documented vertebrate predation and extraordinary climbing/grappling. Neither should approach medium-mammal Defense or Raw Power.
- **Versus Gecko:** Gecko remains comparable or superior in fine adhesive maneuverability; Giant Centipede has the much more dangerous chemical offense. This prevents “small animal agility” from leaking into Attack.
- **Versus Fennec Fox:** Even a 1.5 kg fox massively exceeds the centipede in absolute body force and durability. The centipede's high Special does not erase that physical gap.
- **Anti-inflation check:** Attack 18.0 reflects venom-assisted small-prey lethality, not proportional strength. Defense 7.0 reflects its true vulnerability to vertebrate-scale trauma. Special 94.0 is the deliberately high category because venom plus ceiling grappling is the genuine matchup-changing specialization.
