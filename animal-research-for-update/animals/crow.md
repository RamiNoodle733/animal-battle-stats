# Crow

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Crow / American Crow
- **Scientific name:** *Corvus brachyrhynchos* Brehm, 1822
- **Family:** Corvidae
- **Living/extinct:** Living
- **Exact ABS animal:** The live schema identifies Crow as *Corvus brachyrhynchos*, the American crow.
- **Canonical specimen:** Healthy mature adult male, **0.50 kg**, **48.0 cm total length**, approximately **95.0 cm wingspan**. Males tend to be larger, but the sexes overlap heavily and are externally similar.
- **Variation:** Cornell gives 40–53 cm length, 316–620 g mass, and 85–100 cm wingspan for both sexes. Western birds are slightly smaller than eastern birds, Florida birds are small with relatively large feet, and Pacific Northwest birds formerly treated as Northwestern Crows were merged with American Crow in 2021.

## 2. Physical measurements and functional anatomy

### Mass, length, wingspan

Cornell reports **316–620 g**, **40–53 cm** total length and **85–100 cm** wingspan. A 500 g male is a representative robust adult without using the 620 g upper extreme. Total length includes the substantial tail, so it must not be treated as standing height.

### Height

No authoritative standardized standing-height field was found. A perched crow's apparent height depends strongly on posture. `height_cm` is therefore **0.0** rather than an invented conversion from total length.

### Speed

Many web pages repeat crow speeds around 40–60 km/h, but this run did not find a strong species-specific instrumented maximum for *C. brachyrhynchos*. `speed_mps` is therefore **0.0** pending defensible measurement. Flight still supports high maneuverability independently of an unsourced top-speed number.

### Lifespan

USGS Bird Banding Laboratory records a verified female American Crow alive at a minimum age of **18 years 4 months**. That is a longevity record, not an average. Because a defensible representative adult lifespan/expectancy value was not found in equally strong primary evidence, canonical `lifespan_years` is conservatively **0.0** pending a demographic survival estimate. The longevity record is retained as context.

### Bill, feet and weapons

American Crows have a heavy straight bill, strong legs and feet, and broad rounded wings. The bill is effective for seizing and killing small prey, opening accessible foods, pulling apart soft material, pecking and defensive striking. Cornell specifically notes that despite the large bill, crows cannot break through even gray-squirrel skin when scavenging. This is an important ceiling on weapon exaggeration.

No defensible species-specific bite pressure was found. `bite_force_psi` is **0.0**. No force value is converted to PSI without contact area.

### Defensive structures

There is no armor, shell, thick hide or exceptional trauma tolerance. Feathers provide insulation and limited superficial protection. The crow's real defense is avoidance: flight, rapid repositioning, vigilance, alarm communication and group mobbing.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offensive mechanics

The bill is the primary weapon, backed by feet for bracing and grappling small targets. Crows kill insects, small vertebrates, eggs and nestlings, but are not raptorial birds: their feet lack eagle-like killing talons and their bill lacks the power and specialization of a large raptor. Against similar-sized opponents they can peck, jab, pull feathers, chase and harass. Against much larger animals, damage potential is low unless sensitive tissues such as eyes are exposed.

### Defense and durability

Absolute durability is low. A half-kilogram bird has little tissue depth and no hard protection. Defense comes from not being caught. Flight allows vertical escape, and social mobbing can impose repeated harassment on predators without requiring close sustained grappling.

### Locomotion and maneuverability

American Crows walk and hop confidently on the ground and fly with broad, rounded wings and a short squared or rounded tail. Cornell describes characteristic rowing wingbeats with some gliding. The combination supports strong aerial control, takeoff flexibility and obstacle navigation, although it is not specialized for the extreme pursuit maneuverability of falcons or the hovering precision of hummingbirds.

### Endurance

Crows routinely commute between feeding grounds, territories and communal roosts, and northern populations can migrate. This supports good general endurance. However, no evidence found here justifies treating them as extreme long-duration flight specialists like albatrosses.

### Senses

Vision is the standout combat-relevant sense. Field experiments show crows attend to human gaze, recognize specific dangerous human faces, retain threat associations for years and use visual identity in risk decisions. Hearing and vocal communication are also important for alarms, recruitment and social information.

### Intelligence and tactics

This is the species' strongest category. In a five-site experiment, wild American Crows learned a dangerous human face individually and socially. Knowledge spread horizontally to uncaptured birds and vertically to offspring, while scolding spread at least **1.2 km over five years**. Brain-imaging work showed distinct neural responses to threatening versus caring familiar human faces. In a laboratory string-pulling study of **27 wild-caught American Crows**, birds learned a novel food-access task and successful birds could refine technique using social information. These are species-specific results, not cognition borrowed from New Caledonian Crows.

### Fighting, predation and predator defense

American Crows prey on invertebrates, eggs, nestlings, young turtles, fish, mice and other small animals. They are also frequent nest predators. Against threats they recruit others and mob hawks, owls, herons and dangerous humans. Mobbing is strategically important but should not be mistaken for individual raw combat power.

### Aggression / ferocity

They will aggressively chase much larger birds and defend nests or social groups. This supports moderate ferocity relative to their size, but a crow generally relies on harassment, numbers and escape rather than committing to lethal close combat with a larger opponent.

### Environmental strengths

- Airspace, trees, rooftops and complex urban structures that permit repeated repositioning
- Environments where visual surveillance and social alarm information matter
- Matchups against slower terrestrial animals that cannot reach a flying opponent
- Group contexts where mobbing and information sharing are allowed

### Environmental weaknesses

- Forced enclosed ground combat removes the main defensive advantage
- Very low absolute mass and tissue depth
- No raptorial talons, armor, venom or heavy killing bite
- Aerial mobility does not guarantee meaningful damage against armored or large opponents

### Matchup archetypes

**Favorable:** tiny invertebrates and small vertebrates it can seize or peck; slow opponents unable to reach it; threat-deterrence scenarios where repeated mobbing works.

**Unfavorable:** raptors that can catch it in air, similarly agile predators with superior weapons, cats and mustelids in confined spaces, and virtually any large armored animal in a forced finish-to-incapacitation contest.

## 7. Abilities and traits

### Proposed special abilities, exactly 2

1. **Threat Ledger** — American Crows learn and retain the identity of dangerous individual humans, discriminate threatening from neutral faces, and spread that information socially. In a matchup context this represents unusually durable opponent recognition and risk updating.
2. **Mobbing Network** — Alarm calls recruit other crows to harass larger predators. The mechanism converts social information into coordinated deterrence, while remaining distinct from individual physical power.

### Proposed unique traits, exactly 2

1. **Generational Warning** — Experimental work shows threat knowledge can pass horizontally among adults and vertically from parents to young, allowing local danger information to persist and spread.
2. **Urban Generalist Mind** — The species combines flexible omnivory, human-gaze sensitivity, novel-problem learning and exploitation of human-modified environments without requiring a specialized anatomical weapon.

## 8. Expanded profile content

### Habitat and range

American Crows occupy most of North America and thrive in open or semi-open landscapes with trees. Cornell lists farmland, pasture, landfills, city parks, golf courses, cemeteries, yards, vacant lots, roadsides, river and marsh edges, and beaches. They generally avoid extensive unbroken forest and deserts. Northern birds may migrate while many populations remain resident.

### Diet and ecology

They are broad omnivores. Foods include grains, seeds, nuts, fruits, berries, insects, earthworms, fish, crayfish, mussels, young turtles, mice, eggs, nestlings, carrion and human refuse. Carrion is only part of the diet, and Cornell notes that the bill cannot open even gray-squirrel skin, so scavenging often depends on already accessible carcasses.

### Social structure

Family groups may persist year-round and include the breeding pair plus offspring from previous years. Young can help raise later broods. Outside breeding, very large communal roosts form. Sociality supports information transfer, predator mobbing and flexible exploitation of patchy foods.

### Reproduction and life history

Young American Crows generally do not breed before age two, and many wait until four or older. Cooperative family structure means offspring can remain with parents and help rear siblings. USGS banding records prove at least one female survived to 18 years 4 months in the wild.

### Conservation

The species is currently widespread and classified as Least Concern in major bird-conservation summaries. Cornell describes it as numerous, though long-term survey trends have varied and West Nile virus has caused important local mortality. The report does not convert abundance into combat capability.

### Adaptations

- Broad, rounded wings and strong terrestrial walking ability
- Heavy general-purpose bill
- Excellent visual discrimination and long-term associative memory
- Complex alarm and social communication
- Flexible omnivory
- Cooperative family structure and communal roosting
- Behavioral tolerance of urban and agricultural landscapes

### Human interaction

American Crows are among the clearest examples of wild animals learning about individual humans. They exploit agriculture, refuse and urban food sources, but may also be persecuted as crop or roost nuisances. Experiments show they adjust escape behavior to direct human gaze and can retain specific threat associations for years.

### Fun facts

- Cornell's adult range spans **316–620 g**, nearly a twofold mass difference.
- The USGS longevity database contains a female American Crow verified alive at **18 years 4 months**.
- Experimental threat knowledge spread at least **1.2 km over five years** from one study site.
- Crows can learn a dangerous human face without personally being trapped by observing other crows.
- Young crows can learn whom to scold from their parents.
- American Crow families may include offspring from multiple previous years that help raise younger siblings.
- Despite their reputation as scavengers, Cornell notes carrion is only a small part of their diet and their bill cannot tear through intact gray-squirrel skin.

### Concise site-ready summary

The American Crow is a half-kilogram generalist whose physical weapons are modest but whose cognition is elite. Its bill handles small prey, while flight, vigilance and mobbing keep it out of reach of larger enemies. Species-specific experiments show long-lasting recognition of dangerous humans, social transmission of threat information and flexible problem solving, making intelligence and tactics its defining battle advantages rather than raw force.

### Detailed narrative profile

The crow is an important ABS anti-inflation test because its strongest abilities are real but mostly informational. A 0.5 kg bird should never receive wolf-like Attack or mammal-like Defense simply because it is clever. Its bill can kill small prey and its feet help manipulate targets, yet Cornell's observation that the bill cannot even open intact gray-squirrel skin demonstrates the physical ceiling clearly.

Its defensive strategy is almost the opposite of armor. The crow survives by seeing danger early, leaving the attack line, gaining altitude and recruiting allies. That makes Agility strong while Defense stays very low. Mobbing is not magical damage: it is repeated, coordinated harassment that can make a predator abandon an area or expose itself to additional attackers.

The cognitive case is much stronger than generic claims that corvids are smart. American Crow experiments directly show individual recognition of dangerous human faces, memory lasting years, sensitivity to human gaze, horizontal and vertical social learning, and flexible acquisition of a novel string-pulling solution. These justify an elite Intelligence rating and unusually high Tactics on the full roster.

This produces a deliberately polarized profile: tiny absolute power and protection, excellent aerial maneuverability, solid general endurance, and near-top-tier behavioral intelligence. That separation preserves real biology instead of letting cognition leak into every score.

## 11. Cross-animal calibration notes

- **Versus Cockatoo:** Crow is slightly smaller than the canonical 0.8 kg cockatoo and has less destructive bill force, so Attack 14.0 stays below Cockatoo 21.0. Crow cognition is comparably exceptional but grounded in different evidence, so Intelligence 96.0 versus Cockatoo 94.0 is plausible without implying greater physical power.
- **Versus Condor:** Condor is about twenty times heavier and has a larger hooked beak, so Crow must remain far below Condor Attack 34.0 and Defense 22.0. Crow is substantially more maneuverable and has stronger species-specific problem-solving evidence.
- **Versus Coyote:** Coyote at 15 kg overwhelmingly exceeds Crow in raw damage and durability. Crow's advantages are flight and cognition, not a compressed physical score.
- **Versus Chameleon:** Both illustrate specialization without physical inflation. Crow's Intelligence and Agility can be high while its 0.5 kg body keeps Attack and Defense near the low end.
- **Versus future large raptors:** Eagles and falcons should decisively exceed Crow in Weaponry and Attack because they possess specialized talons and predatory strike mechanics. Crow can remain competitive or superior in some social-learning/tactical dimensions.
- **Inflation check:** Intelligence 96.0 is intentionally elite because it is supported by multiple species-specific experiments. That evidence is not reused to inflate Attack, Defense or raw power.
