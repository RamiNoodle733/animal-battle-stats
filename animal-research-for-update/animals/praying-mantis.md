# Praying Mantis / Chinese Mantis (*Tenodera sinensis*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** Praying Mantis
- **Resolved taxon:** Chinese mantis, *Tenodera sinensis* (Saussure, 1871), family Mantidae. The generic roster label is resolved to this large, well-studied species rather than treating all Mantodea as one animal.
- **Living/extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime **adult female**, **0.006 kg (6 g)** and **10.0 cm body length**. Females are the larger, heavier sex and are the appropriate absolute-combat representative.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** NC State Extension reports males about 3 in long and females about 4 3/8 in (11.1 cm); adult females are flightless while males retain much greater flight capability. Female mass is not well standardized in primary literature, so 6 g is a moderate-confidence representative within a secondary 3-7 g species range, not a record specimen.
- **Population/taxonomy note:** older North American literature frequently uses *Tenodera aridifolia sinensis*. Current field-guide usage recognizes *T. sinensis* as the species. Size varies with sex, nutrition and population.

## 3. Weapons, defenses and combat mechanics
### Raptorial capture system
The mantis attacks with paired grasping forelegs rather than impact force. Spined femora and tibiae close around prey and create multiple contact points that resist escape. Preliminary 3D high-speed work on *T. sinensis* measured about 0.746 m/s maximum distal-tarsus velocity and 60.7 m/s² peak acceleration in two strikes, suggesting direct muscular actuation rather than an extreme spring-loaded power amplifier. The sample is explicitly preliminary, so it is evidence for fast controlled capture, not a hard maximum.

### Mandibles and kill mechanics
Once prey is immobilized, the mantis uses chewing mandibles to consume tissue. Against insects and other tiny animals this combination is highly effective. Against the 225-animal roster as a whole, however, a 6 g animal has negligible absolute force and reach. Published bird-predation records demonstrate that large mantids can occasionally subdue small vertebrates, including hummingbirds, but those rare feats do not justify mammal-scale Attack.

### Defense
The exoskeleton provides modest protection at insect scale but is fragile under large absolute loads. Cryptic green/brown coloration reduces detection. Threatened mantids can posture and expose apparent size, but intimidation is not armor. The female's reduced flight performance also limits escape compared with the lighter male.

## 4. Locomotion, endurance, senses and tactics
### Locomotion and agility
Chinese mantises climb stems and foliage with precise multi-legged control, pivot the head independently, and lunge over short distances. Adult females are effectively flightless according to NC State, so canonical Agility is based on close-range positioning, balance and reflexive foreleg control rather than aerial speed.

### Vision and targeting
Mantises are the only insects currently demonstrated to use stereoscopic vision. Current Biology experiments showed a motion-based form of stereopsis that differs from human static-image matching and is well suited to answering whether moving prey is at striking distance. New 2025 work on *T. sinensis* further shows that mantids alter head and thorax saccade frequency/duration as target speed changes, supporting active gaze stabilization and tracking.

### Hunger-state tactics
Peer-reviewed experiments on *T. sinensis* found that satiety changes prey attention, detection range, pursuit and hunting strategy. As mantises feed, long-range pursuit declines while opportunistic close-range attacks persist. This is real context-sensitive behavioral flexibility, but it is not evidence for vertebrate-like planning.

### Endurance
The canonical female is a sit-and-wait predator optimized for economical ambush, not prolonged high-output pursuit. Adults can remain active for months, but this should not be confused with high combat endurance. Stamina therefore stays modest on the roster scale.

## 5. Fighting, predation and ecology
- **Predation:** broad generalist predator of insects and other arthropods, with documented opportunistic predation on small vertebrates.
- **Bird predation:** a 2017 global review compiled 147 mantid captures of small birds across 12 mantid species. More than 70% of observations came from the USA, and 78% of captured birds were killed/eaten. *Tenodera* was among implicated genera. This establishes capability, not frequency for every individual Chinese mantis.
- **Intraspecific conflict:** cannibalism is common enough to be a major husbandry and reproductive risk; females can consume males and conspecifics can prey on one another.
- **Predator defense:** camouflage, stillness, rapid grasping response and threat display. These work best against similarly scaled threats.
- **Environment:** vegetation is ideal because stems provide ambush perches and concealment. Open flat terrain removes much of the camouflage and approach advantage.

### Major weaknesses
- Tiny absolute mass and very low impact tolerance.
- No venom, toxin injection, armor plate or long-range weapon.
- Adult female flight is lost or severely reduced.
- Foreleg capture is devastating only when the opponent is small enough to restrain.
- Molting stages are vulnerable, though the canonical specimen is fully adult.
- Cold conditions terminate seasonal adult activity in temperate introduced populations.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Best:** insects and other tiny arthropods that fit within the foreleg capture envelope.
- **Good:** some similarly tiny soft-bodied vertebrates when ambush permits immediate restraint.
- **Mixed:** other armed predatory arthropods where first contact and positioning decide the fight.
- **Bad:** armored arthropods too large to pin, or fast attackers that bypass the forelegs.
- **Very bad:** essentially all medium and large vertebrates. Proportional predatory excellence does not overcome the 6 g mass ceiling.

## 8. Exactly two special abilities
1. **Raptorial Vice** - paired spined forelegs snap around prey and maintain multi-point restraint while the mandibles begin feeding; high-speed work supports a rapid, precisely coordinated muscular strike.
2. **Motion Stereo Lock** - mantis stereopsis is tuned to temporal change and moving targets, allowing robust depth judgments for prey inside the strike zone even when static visual detail is poor.

## 9. Exactly two unique traits
1. **Swiveling Ambush Head** - the mobile triangular head can rotate roughly 180 degrees, letting the mantis track targets while minimizing whole-body movement that could reveal its position.
2. **Hunger-Switched Hunt Mode** - feeding state changes detection distance, pursuit and attention, while close opportunistic attacks persist after longer-range hunting motivation declines.

## 10. Expanded profile
### Habitat and range
*T. sinensis* is native to East Asia and is now widespread in much of eastern and central North America. It occupies meadows, gardens, old fields, woodland edges and other vegetation-rich habitats that provide perches and abundant arthropod prey.

### Diet and ecology
The Chinese mantis is a generalist predator. Field experiments show it can alter arthropod communities and even trigger trophic cascades by reducing herbivore abundance. Typical prey are insects and other arthropods; unusually large individuals can take small amphibians, reptiles and birds.

### Social structure and life history
Adults are solitary predators rather than cooperative hunters. Eggs overwinter inside a protective ootheca and nymphs emerge in spring. NC State reports roughly 50-600 eggs per ootheca; nymphs molt six or seven times before adulthood. Cannibalism can occur among nymphs and adults. In temperate North America adults die with cold weather after reproducing.

### Conservation and human interaction
The species is not a conservation-priority native animal in North America. It is introduced there and can consume native insects and hummingbirds, so describing it simply as a beneficial pest-control species is misleading. Humans also keep it in captivity and historically distributed mantis egg cases for garden biological control.

### Adaptations
- Long prothorax and mobile head expand the visual targeting envelope.
- Forward-facing compound eyes support stereoscopic prey ranging.
- Raptorial forelegs combine reach, spines and rapid closure.
- Green/brown polymorphism improves background matching.
- Still ambush posture reduces energetic cost and visual detection.

### Genuine fun facts
- Mantises are the only insects demonstrated to possess stereoscopic vision.
- Their stereopsis relies strongly on changes over time rather than matching detailed static pictures the way humans do.
- Chinese-mantis hunting behavior changes measurably as the animal becomes satiated.
- Adult females are much heavier-bodied and effectively flightless, while males are slimmer and better fliers.
- A global review documented mantids killing and eating small birds, especially hummingbirds at feeders in North America.
- Chinese mantises can influence whole old-field arthropod communities, not merely individual prey.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Chinese mantis is a large ambush mantis whose spined grasping forelegs, chewing mandibles and motion-based 3D vision make it formidable against tiny prey. Its combat system is exceptionally precise, but at only a few grams its absolute Attack and Defense remain near the bottom of the full ABS roster.

### Rich narrative profile
The praying mantis is a classic test of ABS absolute scaling. At insect scale, *Tenodera sinensis* is a highly capable predator. It can remain nearly motionless, rotate its head toward a target, estimate strike distance stereoscopically and fire both raptorial forelegs into a moving animal. Once those spined limbs close, the prey is held against escape while the mandibles begin cutting. Rare vertebrate captures prove that the system can exceed ordinary insect prey size.

None of that turns a 6 g female into a miniature tiger. Its muscles, exoskeleton and weapons operate over centimeters and fractions of a newton-scale body. Against a mammal or large bird, the mantis cannot produce meaningful trauma and is itself extremely vulnerable to crushing. Attack therefore remains low despite impressive proportional feats.

The more defensible elite scores are Agility, Senses and Special. Mantis stereo vision is genuinely unusual, and experiments show that its visual system is tuned to movement in a way that supports rapid prey-distance judgments. *T. sinensis* also changes its pursuit strategy with satiety, demonstrating useful state-dependent behavioral control. These capabilities make the animal sophisticated without inflating Raw Power, Defense or Intelligence beyond the evidence.

## 13. Confidence, conflicts and cross-animal normalization
- **Mass is the weakest canonical factual field.** Strong institutional sources retrieved emphasize length rather than adult mass. 6 g is retained as a moderate-confidence representative female value, not a measured population mean.
- **Length conflict:** institutional sources span roughly 65-85 mm general adults, ~111 mm female from NC State, and up to 127 mm from Missouri DOC. The 100 mm canonical female intentionally sits below the documented maximum.
- **Speed:** no whole-animal top speed is promoted. Foreleg strike velocity is not locomotion.
- **Bite:** 0.0 PSI is an evidence-quality choice, not a claim that mandibles exert zero force.
- **Absolute scaling:** Attack 12.0 remains far below the 1 kg Peregrine Falcon (34.0), 1.2 kg Piranha (37.0), 10.5 kg Porcupine (35.0), 130 kg Ostrich (58.0), and 500 kg Polar Bear (78.0). This prevents proportional-strength leakage.
- **Agility vs speed:** Agility 80.0 reflects close-range control, interception and reflexes, not an invented sprint speed. It is near the Monarch Butterfly's maneuverability class but achieved through a different locomotor system.
- **Special:** 84.0 is below animals with extreme toxins/venoms or exceptional multimodal systems such as Golden Poison Frog 99.0 and Platypus 94.0, but high because motion stereopsis plus raptorial capture is genuinely unusual.
- **Defense:** camouflage is not armor. Defense stays 9.0 despite the mantis's excellent ambush concealment.
