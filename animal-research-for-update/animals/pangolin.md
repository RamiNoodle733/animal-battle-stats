# Pangolin — Sunda Pangolin (*Manis javanica*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** Pangolin.
- **Resolved taxon:** Sunda/Malayan pangolin, *Manis javanica* Desmarest, 1822. “Pangolin” spans eight living species, so a species resolution is mandatory; *M. javanica* is used as the representative medium-sized Asian pangolin and is independently recognized by USFWS.
- **Living/extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime mature **male**, **7.0 kg**, **110.0 cm total length**. Males are larger than females. ADW gives 75–121 cm total length and up to 10 kg; World Land Trust gives 40–65 cm head-body, 35–57 cm tail, males up to 10 kg. Seven kilograms is a large representative adult male without using the maximum.
- **Variation:** sources differ substantially because some report head-body length while others report total length, and body mass varies by sex/condition. Do not mix Sunda measurements with Chinese, Indian, Philippine or African pangolins.

## 2. Physical measurements
### Size
- Total length: **75–121 cm** in ADW; head-body **40–65 cm** plus tail **35–57 cm** in World Land Trust.
- Mass: adult males can reach **10 kg**; 4–7 kg is commonly reported for adults. Canonical: **7.0 kg**.
- Standard shoulder height was not recovered, so `height_cm` remains 0.0.

### Locomotion and speed
Sunda pangolins are terrestrial, fossorial and capable climbers. ADW records quadrupedal movement, faster hind-limb movement when threatened, a prehensile tail used in climbing, swimming, strong digging, and estimated daily travel of roughly 0.7–1.8 km. No defensible instrumented maximum speed was recovered. `speed_mps` remains **0.0** rather than promoting unsourced web speed claims.

### Lifespan
Long-term wild lifespan is poorly known. ADW explicitly says no long-term published Sunda-pangolin lifespan study is available and cites a congeneric Indian pangolin that lived nearly 20 years. Because a species-specific representative lifespan is not defensible, `lifespan_years` remains **0.0**.

### Weapons and armor
- Forefeet have large, powerful digging claws used to tear into ant/termite nests. These can scratch or hook at close range but are not evolved as pursuit-predator killing claws.
- The body and tail carry overlapping keratinized scales. Histological work on *M. javanica* directly supports specialized keratinization of this scale armor.
- The ventral surface is comparatively soft and unscaled. Rolling into a ball hides the vulnerable underside and presents the scales outward.
- The tail can be used defensively; sharp scale edges make a tail swing hazardous at close range.
- The species is edentulous. No meaningful bite-pressure field exists: `bite_force_psi = 0.0`.
- The tongue can extend about **25 cm** according to ADW, but it is a feeding adaptation, not a combat weapon.

## 4. Combat biology
### Offense
Absolute offense is low. A 7 kg pangolin has strong forelimbs and formidable excavation claws, but its normal strategy is digging and defense, not seizing vertebrate prey. It cannot bite. Tail swings and clawing can punish a close attacker, yet reach and damage potential remain far below similarly sized carnivores.

### Defense
Defense is the defining combat adaptation. Overlapping keratin scales cover most of the dorsal body and tail. When threatened, the pangolin curls tightly, protecting the soft belly and head while presenting scale armor. This is excellent protection against many small-to-medium biting/scratching opponents but not invulnerability to crushing, very large jaws, horns, fire, drowning or human tools.

### Locomotion and agility
The animal can dig, climb and swim and uses a prehensile tail as a climbing aid. These are versatile abilities, but it is not a high-speed evasive specialist. Its large foreclaws constrain ordinary forefoot posture. Agility is therefore moderate rather than inflated by climbing skill.

### Endurance
Field evidence is limited. ADW reports estimated movement of 0.7–1.8 km/day. A radio-tracked female in Singapore had a mean daily active period of **127 ± 13.1 minutes**, peaking between 03:00 and 06:00. This supports routine nocturnal foraging but not exceptional sustained combat endurance.

### Senses and intelligence
Smell is central to locating social and prey cues, while reduced external ears and small eyes fit a nocturnal insect specialist. Behavioral flexibility includes burrow construction, climbing, swimming and switching between terrestrial and arboreal foraging. Evidence does not justify primate/carnivore-level tactical intelligence.

### Fighting, predation and tactics
Sunda pangolins are mainly solitary, nocturnal myrmecophages. Peer-reviewed diet work and captive nutrition research confirm specialization on ants and termites. The canonical defensive sequence is avoidance, curling and armor presentation rather than aggressive pursuit. Claws and tail become relevant if escape or rolling fails.

### Environmental strengths
Forest structure, hollow trees, burrows and climbable vegetation favor escape and concealment. Strong claws make soil and termite-mound terrain useful. The prehensile tail improves arboreal stability.

### Weaknesses
The soft ventral surface is vulnerable when uncurled. The animal has no bite, limited offensive reach, low absolute mass, modest open-ground speed and no ranged weapon. Rolling sacrifices mobility and initiative. Human capture exploits this defensive behavior directly.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Favored defensively:** small clawing/biting attackers unable to penetrate or pry open the scale ball.
- **Competitive:** similarly sized generalists where armor can frustrate offense and create escape time.
- **Poor:** large crushing predators, large constrictors, heavy ungulates, megafauna, and opponents capable of attacking exposed areas or overwhelming the curl mechanically.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and exactly two unique traits
### Special abilities
1. **Scale-Lock Fortress** — curls into a tight ball that hides the soft underside and presents overlapping keratinized scales, sharply reducing access for many biting and clawing opponents.
2. **Excavator Claws** — enlarged foreclaws breach compact insect nests and soil; in forced close contact they provide the animal's strongest direct natural weapon.

### Unique traits
1. **Prehensile Armored Tail** — a scale-covered tail provides climbing support and can contribute to close-range defensive sweeping.
2. **Toothless Myrmecophage** — an edentulous skull, elongated sticky tongue and specialized digestive biology commit the animal to ants and termites rather than vertebrate predation.

## 8. Expanded profile
### Habitat and range
Sunda pangolins occur across Southeast Asia, including Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam, Peninsular Malaysia, Singapore and major Sunda islands. They use primary/secondary forest, scrub and some agricultural landscapes.

### Diet and ecology
They are specialized myrmecophages. A Thailand Natural History Museum study identified multiple ant taxa plus termites in fecal material, while a Zoo Biology experiment on adult *M. javanica* confirms the importance of ant/termite-associated chitin and soil in digestive management. Digging also contributes to soil disturbance/aeration.

### Social and life history
Mostly solitary and nocturnal. A Singapore radio-telemetry study of a female and young documented one offspring, approximately 3–4 months of maternal care, three natal dens and a 95% home range of 5.63 ha. A 2021 captive-breeding study produced 49 offspring across three generations and found captive-born females could mature at 7–9 months and one male successfully fertilized a female at nine months, showing that older generalized two-year maturity claims are not universal.

### Conservation and human interaction
The Sunda pangolin is **Critically Endangered** and protected from international commercial trade under CITES Appendix I. Poaching for scales/meat and habitat loss are major threats. Its instinct to curl defensively, highly effective against natural attackers, makes an individual easy for a human to pick up and is therefore an ecological defense with an anthropogenic downside.

### Adaptations
Overlapping keratin scales, strong foreclaws, long sticky tongue, sealable/reduced ear and nasal openings, prehensile tail, burrowing and climbing form an integrated ant-and-termite specialist package.

### Genuine fun facts
- Pangolins are the only living mammals with this extensive armor of large overlapping keratin scales.
- Sunda pangolins have no teeth.
- ADW reports the tongue can extend about 25 cm.
- They can climb and swim as well as dig.
- A field-tracked female used three different natal dens while caring for one young.
- Controlled breeding research has produced three captive generations of *M. javanica*.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Sunda pangolin is a 7 kg armored insect specialist whose battle value is almost entirely defensive. Its digging claws can injure at close range, but its defining move is to curl into a scale-covered ball that hides the vulnerable belly. It is far less dangerous offensively than similar-sized carnivores, yet unusually difficult for many small predators to damage.

### Rich narrative profile
The pangolin is a useful test of absolute ABS scaling because its most spectacular trait is not raw power. A mature male weighs only a few kilograms and lacks teeth, so its Attack must remain low beside cats, canids, apes and megafauna. Its forequarters are powerful because they excavate hardened insect nests, but proportional digging strength is not converted into imaginary large-animal striking force.

Defense is different. The overlapping keratinized scales and tight curl genuinely change how an opponent can attack. A predator that relies on shallow bites or claws may be unable to reach vulnerable tissue at all, while a much larger crushing or prying opponent can overwhelm the same system. That is why Defense and Special can be high without inflating Raw Power or Attack. The result is a specialist profile: difficult to hurt for its size, but poorly equipped to finish a serious large opponent.

## 11. Confidence, conflicts and cross-animal normalization
- **Taxon confidence:** high for the chosen Sunda-pangolin interpretation, but the roster's bare “Pangolin” name is inherently ambiguous across eight species.
- **Mass/length:** moderate-high. Source conventions differ between total length and head-body plus tail; canonical 7 kg/110 cm is representative rather than maximal.
- **Speed:** unresolved; 0.0 is preferred to unsupported internet maxima.
- **Lifespan:** unresolved specifically for wild Sunda pangolins; 0.0 avoids importing a congener's record.
- **Bite force:** 0.0 is biologically appropriate because the species is toothless.
- **Normalization:** Attack 28.0 stays below the 12 kg Otter (35.0) because the pangolin lacks a carnivore bite and pursuit offense. Defense 66.0 is much higher than Otter (29.0) because real scale armor and curling directly prevent access to vulnerable tissue. Agility 59.0 stays far below Otter (91.0). Special 88.0 is high because the armor-curl mechanism is rare and matchup-changing, but below Opossum's 90.0 control only narrowly and without translating armor into inflated raw power. No proportional-strength leakage is used.
