# Aardwolf (*Proteles cristatus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Aardwolf (Afrikaans for "earth wolf"; also maanhaarjakkals, "mane-jackal")
- **Scientific name:** *Proteles cristatus* (Sparrman, 1783); also written *Proteles cristata*
- **Exact animal represented:** the living aardwolf of eastern and southern Africa, the termite-eating member of the hyena family. It is not a canid and not an aardvark.
- **Living / extinct:** Living.
- **Status:** Living; Least Concern on the IUCN Red List.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult of about **10.0 kg**, head-body about **70 cm**, shoulder height about **47.5 cm**. Males are used as the nominal specimen because they scent mark more and fight other males in the mating season, but there is little size difference between the sexes.
- **Sex choice:** the IUCN/SSC Hyaena Action Plan reports little variation in body mass between sexes, so the canonical mass is the representative adult rather than a male-specific figure.
- **Population variation:** southern African aardwolves average about 8–10 kg and lose up to 20% of their weight in winter when termites are scarce; East African animals are larger, reaching about 14 kg. A mid-range 10 kg adult is used.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- IUCN/SSC Hyaena Action Plan (Anderson 1994, in Mills & Hofer 1998): southern Africa about **8–10 kg**, little sex difference; up to 14 kg in East Africa (Kingdon 1977).
- ADW: **8–14 kg**.
- Wikipedia summary: 7–10 kg, sometimes 15 kg; southern animals about 10 kg, eastern about 14 kg.
- **Canonical:** **10.0 kg**.
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high; mass varies with season.

### Height
- IUCN action plan: **0.475 m (0.45–0.50 m)** at the shoulder. Wikipedia gives 40–50 cm.
- **Canonical:** **47.5 cm**.
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high.

### Length
- IUCN action plan: **0.95 m (0.85–1.05 m) head to tail**. ADW: total length 850–1050 mm, of which the tail is 200–300 mm. Wikipedia: 55–80 cm excluding the tail.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 70.0 cm** head-body (about 95 cm total minus a 25 cm tail).
- **Confidence:** Moderate.

### Speed
- Wikipedia describes aardwolves as "not fast runners". While foraging they cover about 1 km per hour. No maximum speed was found.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0** (no reliable measurement).

### Lifespan
- ADW: no average wild lifespan has been recorded; the maximum documented was a 20-year-old captive female. Wikipedia reports the oldest captive aardwolf at Frankfurt Zoo at 18 years 11 months.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 0.0** (no reliable typical wild value; captive maxima of about 19–20 years are not used).

### Bite and dentition
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No measured bite exists. Comparative bite-force quotient tables (summarised on Wikipedia) give the aardwolf about 77, far below the spotted hyena (124) and brown hyena (123).
- **Teeth:** the cheek teeth are reduced to small, widely spaced pegs suited to termites, and some are lost with age without affecting feeding. The canines are kept and are used mainly for fighting and defense. A 2026 craniometric study found the aardwolf skull is the smallest and proportionally narrowest of the four hyaenids.
- **Claws:** five digits on the front feet (other hyaenids have four), used for digging dens.

### Defensive structure
- No armor. Its defenses are a long erectile mane along the back, which makes it look much larger, a foul-smelling anal-gland secretion, and burrows to retreat into.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The aardwolf is a termite specialist, not a predator of vertebrates. Its only real weapons are its canines, which are kept for fights with other aardwolves and for defense, backed by a light jaw with a very low bite-force quotient. It does not dig into termite mounds; it licks harvester termites off the soil surface with a long, broad, sticky tongue.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Long, slender legs and a long neck give it a light, jackal-like frame with a less sloping back than other hyenas. It is not a fast runner and is not adept at fighting off predators. It shelters in burrows, often old springhare or aardvark holes, and can dig its own.

### Endurance
It forages for much of the night at about 1 km per hour, covering 8–12 km per summer night and 3–8 km per winter night. This is steady, low-intensity work, not sustained high-effort exercise, and it loses up to 20% of its body mass during winter food shortages.

### Senses
It locates termites by sound and by the smell of the soldier termites' defence secretions, using large ears and a keen nose. ADW notes keen sight and hearing when searching at night. A 2021 genome study found an expanded set of smell-receptor genes across the hyena family.

### Intelligence and tactics
It follows the activity of its prey, switching from nocturnal foraging on Trinervitermes to earlier, afternoon foraging on daytime-active Hodotermes in cold weather. It harvests termite patches without destroying colonies, returning to them over time. Social tactics are limited to territorial scent marking, chasing intruders and guarding the den.

### Sociality and hunting
Socially monogamous: a pair shares a territory of about 1–4 km² with its latest young, but each adult forages alone. Both sexes paste scent from anal glands on grass stalks, more than twice per 100 m moved and about 200 times per night, concentrated along territory edges. Mating is promiscuous, with dominant males often mating with neighbouring females.

### Fighting and aggression
When an intruder enters, the resident raises its mane and chases it to the border; intruders are seldom caught. Fights mostly occur between males in the mating season and can be highly aggressive, with deep roars and bites to the neck and rump, and fatal fights have been recorded. Against predators it relies on bluff, scent and burrows.

### Environmental strengths
- Open grassland and savanna with dense surface-foraging termites, including heavily grazed farmland.
- Night, when it forages.
- Areas with burrows to retreat into.

### Weaknesses
- Only about 10 kg, lightly built.
- Weak jaw and peg-like cheek teeth.
- Slow runner with no armor.
- Seasonal weight loss and dependence on one food type.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** very small animals it can bluff or bite; insects.
- **Mixed:** jackals and similar small carnivores, which prey on its cubs but are chased off by den-guarding males.
- **Bad:** almost every medium or large predator, armored animals and anything able to press through its bluff.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Termite Vacuum Tongue** — a long, broad, sticky tongue laps up to about 250,000–300,000 termites in a night, and it is the only African termite-eater known to tolerate the terpene defence secretions of Trinervitermes soldiers.
2. **Mane Bluff** — when threatened it raises the long mane along its back to look much larger and can release a foul-smelling liquid from its anal glands, then retreat into a burrow.

### Unique traits
1. **Peg-Toothed Hyena** — a member of the hyena family whose cheek teeth are reduced to small pegs; it keeps its canines for fighting and swallows sand with its termites.
2. **Den-Guarding Father** — pairs share a territory, and males may guard the den for up to six hours a night against jackals; females with a guarding male raise about three times as many cubs.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Two separate populations: one in East and Northeast Africa and one in southern Africa, split by wetter woodlands in Zambia and southern Tanzania. It prefers open, grassy plains and savanna with 100–800 mm of rain a year, avoids forest and pure desert, and does not need to drink. It thrives in heavily grazed grassland and farmland where harvester termites are common.

### Diet and ecology
Feeds almost entirely on one or a few local species of nasute harvester termites (Trinervitermes), licked from the soil surface as they forage in dense patches. In southern African winters it switches to the pigmented harvester termite Hodotermes, and in the East African rains to other termites. It does not destroy mounds, so colonies keep supplying food. In the Serengeti aardwolves and aardvarks overlap heavily in space and time, and aardwolves appear to benefit from aardvark activity.

### Social structure
Socially monogamous pairs with their latest offspring share a territory of about 1–4 km², marked with anal-gland paste. Young stay about a year and leave around the time the next litter is born.

### Reproduction and life history
In South Africa mating is in early July and cubs are born from October to December after a gestation of about 91 days. Litters average 2.5 cubs (1–4). Cubs first leave the den at about one month, forage near it at about nine weeks, and are weaned at about four months.

### Conservation
Least Concern on the IUCN Red List. Most southern African aardwolves live on farmland, where the main threat is indirect poisoning from pesticide spraying against locusts; other threats are persecution by people who mistake it for a livestock predator, dogs trained to hunt jackals, roads and drought. Camera trapping in southern Tanzania estimated 9.2–13.3 aardwolves per 100 km².

### Special features
- Long, broad, sticky tongue.
- Peg-like cheek teeth and retained canines.
- Five toes on the front feet.
- Erectile mane along the back.
- Anal-gland scent paste used about 200 times a night.

### Human interaction
Harmless to livestock and a useful consumer of harvester termites on grazing land, but sometimes killed by farmers who mistake it for a jackal or hyena that kills stock. It suffers indirectly from locust poisoning campaigns.

### Genuine fun facts
- One aardwolf can lap up about 250,000–300,000 termites in a single night.
- It is the only African termite-eater known to tolerate the chemical defences of Trinervitermes soldier termites.
- Its cheek teeth are tiny pegs, yet it belongs to the hyena family.
- It has five toes on its front feet, while other hyenas have four.
- A pair may leave about 200 scent marks in one night.
- Fathers can guard the den for up to six hours a night.

### Concise site-ready summary
The aardwolf is a slender, 10 kg member of the hyena family that eats termites instead of meat, licking up hundreds of thousands in a night. It can raise a mane and release a foul scent to bluff predators, but with a weak jaw, peg-like teeth and no speed, it is one of the least dangerous fighters of its size.

### Rich narrative profile
The aardwolf looks like a small, thin striped hyena with a long mane, big ears and slender legs. A typical adult weighs about 10 kg. Despite its name and its family, it is a specialist insect-eater: its cheek teeth are reduced to little pegs, and it feeds by licking harvester termites off the ground with a long, broad, sticky tongue, taking hundreds of thousands in a night.

Its life is tied to those termites. It forages alone through the night at about 1 km per hour, finding termite columns by sound and smell, and in cold southern winters it can lose a fifth of its weight. Pairs share small territories of 1–4 km², mark them with hundreds of scent pastes a night and chase out intruders, and fathers stand guard at the den against jackals.

In a battle context the aardwolf is weak. Its canines are its only real weapons, its bite-force quotient is a fraction of the other hyenas', and it is neither fast nor armored. Its survival relies on bluff: a raised mane that makes it look far larger, a foul anal secretion and a burrow to dive into. It sits near the bottom of the medium-small mammals in Attack and Defense, with its Special score coming from its unusual feeding specialization and defensive display.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Termite intake:** Wikipedia cites both "up to 250,000" and "as many as 300,000" termites per night; ADW gives about 300,000. A range is reported.
- **Teeth:** ADW describes the canines as reduced, while Wikipedia and the IUCN action plan stress that the canines are kept and used in fights. Both agree the cheek teeth are greatly reduced.
- **Lifespan:** no wild value exists, so the field is 0.0 rather than a captive maximum.
- **Mass:** strongly seasonal and regional; 10 kg is a mid-range adult.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Hyena (spotted, 75 kg; A61 D53):** the aardwolf is a small insect-eater with a bite-force quotient about 60% of the spotted hyena's, so it sits far below it in every combat rating despite sharing the family.
- **Versus Jackal (8.1 kg; A34 D27 Ag74 St66 I68):** similar mass, but the jackal is an active predator with a stronger bite and far better running and turning, so the jackal is well above the aardwolf in Attack and Agility. Jackals prey on aardwolf cubs, which fits this order.
- **Versus Aardvark (65 kg; A41 D40 Ag40 Sp58):** both are termite specialists and share burrows and habitat. The aardvark is more than six times heavier with powerful digging claws, so it is far above the aardwolf in Attack and Defense; the aardwolf is lighter and more agile and has a similar Special score for its feeding toolkit and bluff.
- **Versus Striped Hyena (38 kg; A50 D42):** the aardwolf resembles a small striped hyena but has about a quarter of its mass and none of its bone-cracking power.
- **Versus Skunk (4.5 kg; Sp93):** the aardwolf's anal-gland secretion is a deterrent, not a sprayed chemical weapon, so its Special stays far below the skunk's.
- **Anti-inflation check:** belonging to the hyena family does not raise its Attack; it is scored as the 10 kg termite-eater it is.
