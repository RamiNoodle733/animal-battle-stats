# Tasmanian Devil (*Sarcophilus harrisii*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Tasmanian devil
- **Scientific name:** *Sarcophilus harrisii* (Boitard, 1841), family Dasyuridae
- **Exact animal represented:** the living Tasmanian devil of Tasmania, the largest surviving carnivorous marsupial. The live site photo shows an adult at Healesville Sanctuary, Victoria, Australia.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult male, **9.0 kg**, about 65 cm head-body length plus a 25–26 cm tail and about 30 cm at the shoulder.
- **Sex choice:** males are larger. Wikipedia (citing Guiler's measurements) gives an average male of 8 kg with a 652 mm head-body and 258 mm tail, versus 6 kg, 570 mm and 244 mm for females. Animal Diversity Web gives 5.5–12 kg for males and 4.1–8.1 kg for females. The Tasmanian government (NRE Tasmania) says adult males can reach 14 kg. The 9.0 kg canonical male is a little above the average male and well below the exceptional maximum.
- **Population notes:** devils in western Tasmania tend to be smaller. Body size also varies with diet and habitat. Since the late 1990s devil facial tumour disease (DFTD) has reshaped populations; high-density, disease-free study sites are not typical of the whole island today. The species no longer occurs on mainland Australia, where it disappeared around 3,500 years ago.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- Wikipedia (Guiler data): average male 8 kg, average female 6 kg.
- ADW: males 5.5–12 kg, females 4.1–8.1 kg; species range 4–12 kg.
- NRE Tasmania: adult males can weigh up to 14 kg.
- **Canonical `weight_kg`: 9.0.**
- **Confidence:** High for the range; moderate for the exact representative value.

### Height
- NRE Tasmania and the Australian Museum: about **30 cm at the shoulder**. ADW: males may reach 30 cm at the shoulder.
- **Canonical `height_cm`: 30.0.**
- **Confidence:** High.

### Length
- Average male head-body 652 mm with a 258 mm tail (Wikipedia, Guiler). ADW gives total length 525–800 mm and tail 230–300 mm. The Australian Museum lists 55–65 cm with a 24–25 cm tail.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 65.0** as head-body length for a mature male; tail recorded separately at about 25–26 cm.
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high; sources differ on whether the tail is included.

### Speed
- Wikipedia: devils run up to about 13 km/h for short distances on typical terrain, and there are reports of 25 km/h for up to 1.5 km on flat roads. Pemberton reported averages near 10 km/h for extended periods on several nights a week.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 6.9** (25 km/h), with **low-moderate confidence**: it is a road-based report, not an instrumented trial. A sprint on natural ground is closer to 3.6 m/s (13 km/h).

### Lifespan
- ADW: most wild devils live to at most about 5 years; 7–8 years is possible. NRE Tasmania: up to six years in the wild. Wikipedia: few devils live longer than five years in the wild.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 5.0.**
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high.

### Bite and dentition
- Wroe, McHenry and Thomason (2005) estimated a canine bite force of **418 N** for a 12.0 kg devil specimen and a bite force quotient (BFQ, bite force relative to body mass) of **181**, the highest of any living mammalian carnivore in their sample. Wikipedia quotes 553 N at the canine with the same BFQ; the origin of that figure is less clear.
- The jaw opens to 75–80 degrees. Devils have 42 teeth; the teeth are not replaced and keep erupting slowly through life. A 2023 Proceedings B study measured canine over-eruption in live wild devils, with skull condylobasal lengths of 116–139 mm in its devil sample.
- Devils eat bone and fur and can bite through metal traps and thick wire.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** The bite is documented in newtons from skull-based modeling; pressure cannot be derived without a contact area.

### Defensive structure
- No armor. A stocky, muscular body, thick neck and large head. Devils regularly survive bite wounds from other devils, especially around the face and rump during feeding and mating disputes.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The devil's weapon is its jaw. A large head, heavy jaw muscles and robust teeth give it the highest bite force for its body size of any living mammalian carnivore measured by Wroe et al. The absolute figure, about 418 N at the canine for a 12 kg animal, is roughly what a medium dog produces. It lets a devil crush bone, consume whole carcasses including fur, and kill prey larger than itself. Andersen et al. (2020) note that hunting is rarely seen but that devils can kill Bennett's wallabies and bare-nosed wombats, using a crushing bite to the head or neck. Wikipedia describes attacks on wombats up to 30 kg.

Forelimbs are used for holding and digging; the claws are not the main weapon. With only a 9 kg body behind it, the bite can maim a medium animal but cannot overpower a large predator head-on.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Devils amble with a characteristic gait and gallop with both hind feet together. Their forelegs are slightly longer than their hind legs. Young devils climb trees well; adults can swim across rivers. They are not sprinters: they cannot run down a healthy wallaby or rabbit. Agility is therefore modest.

### Endurance
Devils are wide-ranging night travelers. NRE Tasmania and the Australian Museum report travel of up to 16 km in a night along well-used trails, while ADW gives a 3.2 km nightly average. Pemberton reported sustained travel near 10 km/h on several nights a week. Video collars on 13 devils showed that resting is still the most common behavior overall, so endurance is strong in travel rather than in continuous exertion.

### Senses
Devils forage at night and locate carcasses largely by smell and hearing. Long whiskers on the face and head help in close contact and in the dark. No quantitative sensory thresholds were located.

### Intelligence and tactics
Behavior is opportunistic and flexible: collared devils spent more time scavenging than hunting, used roads and fence lines as well as natural vegetation, and took whatever food was available. Disputes at carcasses are managed with an elaborate set of vocalizations, gapes and postures. Cognitive studies comparable to those of primates or corvids were not located, so Intelligence stays in the roster's middle-low band.

### Social behavior and fighting
Devils are solitary with overlapping home ranges. They meet at carcasses and during mating. Andersen et al. found that most interactions involved vocalizations and brief chases without physical contact, but biting does occur, particularly in the mating season. That biting is how DFTD tumour cells pass between devils, and a Proceedings B study linked tumour progression to changes in devil contact networks.

### Aggression
The famous open-mouthed gape, growls and screeches are mainly threat displays that help avoid real fights. Devils are nonetheless tenacious biters once engaged and can defend themselves against larger animals with their jaws and robust teeth (ADW).

### Environmental strengths
- Dense forest, coastal scrub and woodland at night.
- Scavenging contests where bite power and persistence matter.
- Terrain requiring long overnight travel.

### Weaknesses
- Small absolute mass (9 kg).
- Slow for a predator; poor at pursuing fast prey.
- No armor; relies on a single close-range weapon.
- Highly vulnerable to DFTD, a transmissible cancer.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** smaller mammals, birds and reptiles; opponents that allow a sustained bite; slow or cornered prey up to wombat size.
- **Mixed:** similar-mass carnivores such as badgers and honey badgers, where toughness, claws and bite placement decide the fight.
- **Bad:** large predators, fast agile opponents that avoid the jaws, armored animals and megafauna.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Bone-Crusher Bite** — a large head, heavy jaw muscles and 75–80 degree gape give the highest bite force relative to body mass of any living mammalian carnivore measured (BFQ 181). It lets the devil crush bone and deliver a killing bite to the head or neck of wallabies and wombats.
2. **Screech-and-Gape Standoff** — growls, screeches and the wide open-mouthed gape settle most carcass disputes without contact. In a matchup it can make a rival or scavenger hesitate, but it does not add damage.

### Unique traits
1. **Largest Carnivorous Marsupial** — the biggest surviving meat-eating marsupial, a stocky 6–12 kg animal found only in Tasmania.
2. **Whole-Carcass Scavenger** — eats meat, bone and fur, typically about 15% of its body weight a day and up to 40% in 30 minutes when food is plentiful.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Found only on the island of Tasmania, where it uses almost all habitats, including coastal heath, dry forest, wet forest and farmland edges. It disappeared from mainland Australia about 3,500 years ago. Insurance populations are kept in zoos and disease-free sites under the Save the Tasmanian Devil Program, and a small group has been returned to the Australian mainland for the first time in over 3,000 years (Wikipedia).

### Diet and ecology
A scavenger and hunter that eats whatever animal food is available. Favorite prey and carrion include wallabies, possums and wombats; small mammals and birds are eaten as prey or carrion, and seabird feathers and fur-seal fur have turned up in scats. Collared devils spent more time scavenging than hunting. By clearing carcasses quickly, devils help limit insects that could otherwise harm livestock (Wikipedia).

### Social structure
Solitary, with overlapping home ranges of about 2,500 ha at one study site; Wikipedia gives two-to-four-week home ranges of 4–27 km². Devils gather at large carcasses, where they compete with noise and posture, and meet during the mating season (recent studies place it between February and June), when biting is most frequent. Contact-network studies found all devils in one population linked into a single network.

### Reproduction and life history
Gestation lasts about 21 days. A female gives birth to 20–30 tiny young of about 0.2 g, but has only four nipples in the pouch, so no more than four can be raised. Young leave the pouch at about 100–105 days, weighing around 200 g, and are fully grown at about two years. In populations with DFTD, females often begin breeding at one year instead of two.

### Conservation
Endangered on the IUCN Red List (2008), under Tasmania's Threatened Species Protection Act and under Australia's EPBC Act. Devil facial tumour disease, a fatal, contagious cancer spread by biting, has reached about 95% of the species' range in two decades. The Save the Tasmanian Devil Program manages insurance populations and disease-free sites.

### Special features
- Highest bite force for body mass among living mammalian carnivores.
- Teeth that keep erupting throughout life.
- 75–80 degree jaw gape.
- Long whiskers from chin to the rear of the jaw.
- Forelegs slightly longer than hind legs.

### Human interaction
Believed to kill livestock, the devil was targeted by a bounty scheme as early as 1830; it is now a Tasmanian icon and a flagship conservation species. Roadkill is a significant local mortality cause because devils feed on carcasses along roads.

### Genuine fun facts
- Pound for pound, the devil has the strongest bite of any living mammalian carnivore measured, with a bite force quotient of 181.
- A newborn devil weighs about 0.2 g, roughly the weight of a raisin.
- A female gives birth to 20–30 young but has only four nipples in her pouch.
- Devils can eat up to 40% of their body weight in 30 minutes.
- Their teeth are never replaced but keep erupting slowly through life.
- DFTD is one of only a handful of contagious cancers known in nature.
- Devils can travel up to 16 km in a single night.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Tasmanian devil is a stocky 6–12 kg marsupial scavenger with the strongest bite for its size of any living mammalian carnivore. It crushes bone, eats whole carcasses and can kill wallabies and wombats, but its small body, slow gallop and lack of armor keep it far below the big predators in a straight fight.

### Rich narrative profile
The Tasmanian devil is a jaw on legs. A mature male weighs about 9 kg, yet its massive head and jaw muscles produce the highest bite force relative to body size of any living mammalian carnivore that has been measured. That bite lets it crush bones, swallow fur and hide, and clean a carcass so thoroughly that little remains. When it hunts, it kills with a crushing bite to the head or neck and can take animals heavier than itself, including wallabies and wombats.

The rest of the body supports that weapon rather than adding new ones. Devils amble and gallop instead of sprinting, so they rarely catch healthy fast prey. Their strength is endurance: they patrol trails for up to 16 km in a night, find carcasses by smell and hearing, and settle disputes with a chorus of growls, screeches and wide-mouthed gapes. Most encounters end without contact, but when devils do fight, especially during mating, they bite each other hard, which is how devil facial tumour disease spreads.

In a matchup the devil is dangerous to anything its own size or smaller that lets it close in. Its bite is far stronger than its mass suggests, and it tolerates wounds. Against large predators, armored animals or fast opponents, its small body, limited speed and single weapon leave it outmatched. On the absolute roster scale it sits among small carnivores such as badgers and honey badgers, with a notable Special score for its bone-crushing specialization.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Bite force:** 418 N (Wroe et al. 2005, 12 kg specimen) versus 553 N (Wikipedia, source unclear). Both are force estimates; no PSI is derived.
- **Maximum mass:** 12 kg (ADW) versus 14 kg (NRE Tasmania). The canonical 9.0 kg avoids both maxima.
- **Speed:** 25 km/h comes from reports on flat roads; 13 km/h is the typical short sprint on natural ground.
- **Travel:** 3.2 km average (ADW) versus up to 16 km (NRE, Australian Museum) reflects average versus maximum nights.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Quoll (A32.0 D22.0 Ag77.0 St64.0 I55.0 Sp60.0):** the devil is about two and a half times heavier, with a far stronger bite, so it sits above the quoll in Attack, Defense, Stamina and Special. The quoll keeps much higher Agility (a climbing hunter) and slightly higher Intelligence.
- **Versus Honey Badger (A42.0 D45.0 Ag61.0 St70.0 I64.0 Sp80.0):** a 14 kg honey badger has claws as well as a bite and a famously loose, thick skin, so it stays slightly higher in Attack and clearly higher in Defense, Agility and Intelligence. The devil's bite is stronger for its size, which keeps Attack close.
- **Versus Badger (A38.0 D39.0 Ag52.0 St59.0 I50.0 Sp46.0):** similar mass. The devil's bone-crushing bite earns slightly more Attack and much more Special; the badger's digging build and dense body earn more Defense.
- **Versus Hyena (A61.0 D53.0 Ag61.0 St83.0 I78.0 Sp72.0):** the devil shares the bone-crushing niche but at about one-eighth of the mass. Every physical rating stays far below the hyena, and Special stays slightly below because the hyena combines bone-cracking jaws with pack hunting.
- **Wolverine:** the live wolverine entry is still a legacy placeholder and is not used as an anchor.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the "devil" reputation and the high BFQ are relative measures; the 418 N absolute bite and 9 kg body keep Attack at 40.0 rather than a big-predator score.
