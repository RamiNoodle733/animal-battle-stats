# Rottweiler (*Canis familiaris*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Rottweiler (Rottweil butcher's dog, Rottweiler Metzgerhund)
- **Scientific name:** *Canis familiaris* Linnaeus, 1758 (domestic dog; Rottweiler breed, FCI standard No. 147)
- **Exact animal represented:** the German-type Rottweiler as described by the FCI and the breed's home club, the ADRK, not oversized "giant" or crossbred Rottweiler-type dogs.
- **Living / extinct:** Living (domestic breed).
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult male, **50.0 kg**, **65.0 cm** at the withers.
- **Sex choice:** the FCI standard gives males 61–68 cm (65–66 cm "large, correct height") and a weight of 50 kg; females 56–63 cm and about 42 kg. Males are larger and heavier.
- **Population notes:** Wikipedia gives males 50–60 kg; US registry dogs (AKC 95–135 lb, 43–61 kg) cover a wider range. The breed takes its name from the German town of Rottweil, where butchers kept these dogs to drive cattle and pull carts. The FCI standard states the Rottweiler was officially recognized as a police dog in 1910.

- Short, straight top coat with an undercoat that should not show through (FCI). No armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary weapon and offensive mechanics
The weapon is the canid bite on a broad, powerful head with full 42-tooth dentition. The Rottweiler is compact and heavily muscled, and when working cattle it uses its body and shoulders as well as its teeth. As a guard and police dog it is trained to grip and hold.

### Cattle-driving behavior
Wikipedia, drawing on herding descriptions, reports that a working Rottweiler "searches out the dominant animal and challenges it", uses "a very intimidating charge", and may push stock with its body and shoulders, so should be used on horned stock with caution. That is a real confrontational working style against much larger animals, but it is designed to move cattle, not to kill them.

### Defense and toughness
A 50 kg heavy-boned dog with a short coat and undercoat. Its mass and muscle give it real durability for a dog, but there is no armor and the short coat gives less padding than the dense guardian coats of the Kangal or the long-coated Caucasian Shepherd.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
The FCI standard calls it a trotting dog and says its build suggests "great strength, agility and endurance". In practice it is heavier and less nimble than the German Shepherd or Malinois.

### Endurance
Drove work meant long days moving cattle at the trot. No endurance measurements were located. Its short average life and a high rate of elbow dysplasia (38.1% in one North American study, via Wikipedia) weigh against sustained performance late in life.

### Senses
Ordinary domestic-dog smell and hearing, used in police and search-and-rescue work. No breed-specific data were located.

### Intelligence and tactics
The FCI describes it as obedient, biddable and eager to work. It is trained for police, guard and rescue work, and its cattle-driving style shows some tactical reading of a herd. It has no cooperative hunting behavior.

### Intraspecific fighting
A 2008 breed-club survey (via Wikipedia) found Rottweilers average in aggression toward owners and other dogs but more aggressive than average toward strangers.

### Predator defense
A property and person guard rather than a livestock guardian against wolves.

### Aggression and ferocity
Self-assured, steady and fearless according to the FCI standard, with protective behavior toward strangers. The standard disqualifies vicious or nervous dogs.

### Environmental strengths
- Farms, yards and towns where it guards a fixed area.
- Temperate climates.

### Weaknesses
- No armor; short coat.
- Heavier and slower to turn than working shepherds.
- Short average lifespan and common elbow and hip dysplasia.
- Far outmatched by bears, big cats and large ungulates.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** coyotes, jackals, foxes, dingoes and other light canids; unarmored medium mammals.
- **Mixed:** wolves, hyenas, heavy guardian breeds, leopards.
- **Bad:** bears, lions, tigers, large ungulates, crocodilians, armored or venomous opponents.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Dominant-Animal Challenge** — when driving cattle it singles out the dominant animal and challenges it with an intimidating charge. In a matchup it goes straight at the main threat instead of circling.
2. **Shoulder Drive** — pushes and moves stock with its body and shoulders as well as its teeth, using its compact 50 kg mass to shove an opponent off balance.

### Unique traits
1. **Rottweil Butcher's Dog** — named after the town of Rottweil, where butchers kept these dogs to drive cattle and pull meat carts to market.
2. **Black-and-Tan Powerhouse** — a compact, stalwart dog, black with sharply defined rich tan markings on the cheeks, muzzle, chest and legs, with a broad head and 42-tooth scissor bite.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
A domestic breed from southern Germany, now kept worldwide in homes, farms, police units and guard work.

### Diet
Fed by its owners on commercial dog food, meat and household scraps.

### Ecology
No wild ecological role. Working roles have included cattle droving, cart pulling, police work, guarding and search-and-rescue.

### Social structure
Devoted to its family and handler, calm at home, and watchful toward strangers.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeding is managed by breed clubs and owners; the ADRK, founded in 1921, is the breed's home club.

### Conservation
Not applicable as a wild species; it is a **Domesticated** breed.

### Special features
- Compact, heavy-boned, muscular build.
- Broad head with 42 teeth in a scissor bite.
- Black coat with rich tan markings.
- Efficient trotting gait.
- Confident, protective temperament.

### Human interaction
The Rottweiler is a popular guard, police and family dog. In a US study of 238 dog bite-related deaths from 1979 to 1998, pit bull-type dogs and Rottweilers were involved in more than half, though the authors warned that breed identification is unreliable and fatal attacks are rare. A later study of 2000–2009 deaths found preventable management factors in most cases and valid breed identification in few. Health problems include elbow and hip dysplasia and a short average life.

### Genuine fun facts
- Its German name, Rottweiler Metzgerhund, means "Rottweil butchers' dog".
- The FCI standard says Rottweilers were officially recognized as police dogs in 1910.
- The breed's home club, the ADRK, was formed in 1921 when several German Rottweiler clubs merged.
- A working Rottweiler moving cattle picks out the dominant animal and challenges it.
- The viral "328 PSI" bite figure is a television force measurement relabelled as pressure.
- The FCI standard describes the Rottweiler as a trotting dog and faults sluggish movement at the trot.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Rottweiler is a compact, heavily muscled 50 kg German drover and guard dog. It fights with a strong grip and shoulder-driving power and will challenge the dominant animal head-on, putting it close to the Kangal and Gray Wolf in attack, but it has no armor, a short coat and is heavily outclassed by bears and big cats.

### Rich narrative profile
The Rottweiler takes its name from Rottweil in southern Germany, where butchers used powerful black-and-tan dogs to drive cattle to market and pull carts of meat. When German police began testing breeds in the early 1900s, the Rottweiler proved so suitable that it was officially recognized as a police dog in 1910. A male weighs about 50 kg and stands about 65 cm tall, compact and heavy-boned with a broad head.

Its working style is direct. When moving cattle it picks out the dominant animal and challenges it with an intimidating charge, using its body and shoulders to push stock around. As a guard and police dog it is trained to grip and hold. That combination of mass, muscle and willingness to confront much larger animals gives it one of the stronger attack ratings among dog breeds.

It is still a 50 kg dog. It is heavier but less agile than working shepherds, lacks the long protective coat of mountain guardian breeds, and is prone to elbow and hip dysplasia and a short life. Against light wild canids it holds a clear edge; against a wolf or a heavy guardian dog it is an even contest; against bears, big cats and large hoofed animals it is simply too small.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Kangal (50 kg; A53 D44 Ag59 St68 I63 Sp47):** same mass. The Rottweiler is rated slightly higher in Raw Power for its compact musculature but one point lower in Attack and Defense because the Kangal is bred to confront wolves and carries a denser guardian coat; the Kangal also has better guarding endurance.
- **Versus Tibetan Mastiff (55 kg; A51 D47 Ag52):** slightly lighter but more athletic; higher Attack and Agility, lower Defense because it lacks the mastiff's heavy mane.
- **Versus Gray Wolf (50 kg; A55 D41 Ag66 St89 I78):** same mass. The Rottweiler has slightly higher Defense and Raw Power from heavier bone, but lower Attack, Agility, Stamina and Intelligence than a wild large-prey hunter.
- **Versus German Shepherd (35 kg; A48 D36 Ag66):** heavier and stronger, so higher Attack and Defense; less agile and less enduring.
