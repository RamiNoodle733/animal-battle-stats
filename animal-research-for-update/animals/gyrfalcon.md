# Gyrfalcon (*Falco rusticolus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Gyrfalcon
- **Scientific name:** *Falco rusticolus* Linnaeus, 1758, family Falconidae
- **Exact animal represented:** the circumpolar Arctic and sub-Arctic gyrfalcon, the largest falcon in the world, across all its color morphs (white, gray and dark).
- **Living / extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult female, **1.7 kg**, **58.0 cm** total length, wingspan about **125 cm**.
- **Sex choice:** the species shows reversed size dimorphism. Females weigh **1,130–2,150 g** against **769–1,450 g** for males, and females measure 51–65 cm against 48–61 cm for males. The female is the larger, stronger sex and is used as the canonical specimen, matching the approach used for the Peregrine Falcon.
- **Population notes:** plumage ranges from almost pure white in high-Arctic Greenland to dark gray-brown further south. The canonical mass sits in the middle of the female range, not at the 2.15 kg maximum.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The gyrfalcon kills with its feet and beak. It strikes or grabs prey with its talons, often binding to it and bringing it to the ground, then finishes it with a bite to the neck using its notched bill. Its prey is large for a falcon: ptarmigan make up the bulk of the diet in many areas, and it also takes ducks, seabirds, Arctic hares and lemmings. At 1.7 kg it is roughly 70% heavier than a female peregrine, which gives it more grip and impact against bird-sized and hare-sized prey.

### Hunting mechanics
Field observations describe low surprise attacks launched from a perch or while ridge-soaring. If the first strike misses, the gyrfalcon commonly switches to a long tail-chase, pursuing prey close to the ground until it tires. It also cruises low over terrain to flush birds. It rarely uses the high stoops that define the peregrine.

### Pursuit guidance
A study of captive-bred, hunting-naive gyrfalcons chasing maneuvering targets found that their attack paths follow the same guidance law as peregrine falcons, but with a lower guidance gain. In practice this means gyrfalcons steer toward their target in the same way as peregrines but correct their heading less sharply, matching a heavier bird built for sustained pursuit rather than split-second interception.

### Defense and durability
Evasion and height are its defenses. The body is unarmored and the wings are flight-critical, so a heavy blow can end its effectiveness.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
A powerful flier with broad-based pointed wings and a relatively long tail. It is fast in level flight and accelerates hard from a perch. Its heavier wing loading makes it less nimble than the peregrine in tight aerial turns.

### Endurance
Endurance is a standout feature. Its prolonged tail-chases wear down fast-flying ptarmigan and waterfowl. Satellite tracking of Greenland gyrfalcons showed some birds spending more than a month at a time over winter sea ice far from land, and one juvenile female covered nearly 3,000 miles (about 4,800 km) in roughly 200 days, more than half of it at sea between Greenland and Iceland.

### Senses
Vision is its primary sense, as in all falcons, used to spot prey at long range across open tundra and sea ice. No gyrfalcon-specific visual acuity measurement was located.

### Intelligence and tactics
It combines several hunting modes (perch ambush, ridge soaring, low flushing flights and tail-chases) and adjusts between them when the first attack fails. Pairs sometimes hunt near each other on breeding territories, but coordinated pair hunting was not verified in this run.

### Hunting behavior
A specialist predator of ptarmigan. In northeast Iceland, rock ptarmigan make up on average about **72%** of summer diet biomass (range 52–86%), and gyrfalcon breeding tracks ptarmigan numbers.

### Intraspecific fighting
Territorial during breeding, defending nest cliffs with aerial displays and chases.

### Predator defense
Few natural predators as adults. Nests on cliffs, and adults drive off intruders with aggressive aerial attacks.

### Aggression and ferocity
High and committed as a predator, attacking prey as large as geese and hares.

### Environmental strengths
- Open tundra, coastal cliffs and sea ice where it can see prey at a distance and chase it low over the ground.
- Extreme cold: it stays in the Arctic through winter.
- Long, low pursuits where endurance matters more than a single dive.

### Weaknesses
- Low absolute mass and no armor.
- Flight-critical wings; a grounded falcon is badly exposed.
- Less agile in tight turns than smaller falcons.
- Weapons suited to bird-sized and hare-sized prey, not large animals.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** medium birds and small mammals that can be chased down in open country; opponents that cannot threaten it in the air.
- **Mixed:** other raptors of similar size, where first strike and position decide.
- **Bad:** large eagles and owls, large mammals able to crush it on the ground, and any opponent that can reach it when it is grounded with prey.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Tundra Tail-Chase** — when an ambush misses, it keeps chasing low over the ground, wearing down fast ptarmigan and ducks with sustained pursuit rather than a single dive.
2. **Neck-Breaking Bite** — binds prey with its talons, then kills with a bite to the neck from its notched falcon bill.

### Unique traits
1. **Largest Falcon** — the biggest falcon in the world, with females reaching about 2.1 kg and a wingspan up to 130 cm.
2. **Sea-Ice Winterer** — some birds spend more than a month at a time over winter sea ice far from land, hunting seabirds and sea ducks around open water.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Breeds across the Arctic and sub-Arctic of North America, Greenland, Iceland, Scandinavia and Russia on cliffs and outcrops in tundra, coastal and mountain landscapes. Many adults stay near breeding areas all year, while some birds, especially juveniles, move south in winter or out onto sea ice.

### Diet and ecology
A bird specialist centered on rock and willow ptarmigan, which make up about 72% of summer diet biomass in northeast Iceland. It also takes waterfowl, seabirds, shorebirds, Arctic hares, ground squirrels and lemmings. In Iceland its breeding success rises and falls with ptarmigan numbers.

### Social structure
Solitary outside the breeding season; breeding pairs hold territories around cliff nest sites and often reuse old raven or rough-legged hawk nests, as they build no nest of their own.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeds from about 2–3 years of age and lays a small clutch on a cliff ledge. Icelandic studies estimate annual survival at about 83% for adults and 40% for juveniles. The oldest known wild bird was at least 15 years 9 months old.

### Conservation
Listed as **Least Concern** on the IUCN Red List since 2004 because of its huge range and a population estimated at about 50,000–70,000 birds. Partners in Flight estimates a global breeding population of about 70,000 and reports no evidence of long-term decline in North America. Warming that alters ptarmigan cycles and Arctic habitats is a concern for the future.

### Special features
- Largest falcon, with reversed size dimorphism.
- Notched (tomial) bill for killing bites.
- White, gray and dark color morphs.
- Dense plumage for Arctic winter survival.
- Strong flight muscles for sustained low pursuit.

### Human interaction
Prized in falconry for centuries, historically reserved for royalty in parts of Europe and Asia, and still bred in captivity for falconry. Wild populations are remote and little disturbed by people.

### Genuine fun facts
- It is the largest falcon in the world.
- Females can weigh nearly twice as much as males.
- In northeast Iceland, ptarmigan make up about 72% of its summer diet by weight.
- Some gyrfalcons spend weeks living over winter sea ice far from land.
- One tracked juvenile female traveled nearly 3,000 miles in about 200 days, much of it over the sea.
- It rarely uses the peregrine's high-speed stoop and prefers low, long chases.
- The oldest known wild gyrfalcon was at least 15 years 9 months old.

### Concise site-ready summary
The gyrfalcon is the world's largest falcon, a 1.7 kg Arctic hunter that chases ptarmigan and ducks low over the tundra until they tire. It is fast, tough in the cold and armed with sharp talons and a killing bite, but its light body means it can only overpower prey far smaller than the roster's heavy hitters.

### Rich narrative profile
The gyrfalcon is the heavyweight of the falcon world. A female, the larger sex, weighs about 1.7 kg and spans up to 130 cm, nearly twice the weight of a male. It lives where few raptors can, nesting on Arctic cliffs and staying through the polar winter, when some birds live for weeks over sea ice hunting seabirds and ducks around open water.

Its hunting style is different from the peregrine's famous dive. Gyrfalcons usually attack low, bursting from a perch or sweeping along a ridge to surprise ptarmigan and other birds. When that first strike misses, they settle into a long tail-chase just above the ground, relying on powerful flight muscles to wear their prey down. A study of young gyrfalcons found they steer toward targets using the same rule as peregrines, but correct their heading less sharply, a match for a bigger bird built for sustained pursuit.

In a battle, the gyrfalcon is a dangerous small predator. Its talons and notched bill kill prey as large as ducks and hares, and it presses attacks with real commitment. But at under 2 kg it has no armor, and a heavy hit to its wings can end the fight. It rates highly for Agility, Stamina and senses, and modestly for Attack and Defense.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Speed:** compiled figures of 145 km/h level and about 209 km/h in a dive could not be traced to a measurement, so `speed_mps` stays at 0.0.
- **Lifespan:** 8 years is derived from survival rates; the longevity record is 15.8 years.
- **Population:** estimates range from about 50,000 to 70,000 birds.
- **Pair hunting:** described anecdotally but not verified in this run, so it is not scored.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Peregrine Falcon (1.0 kg female; A34 D18 Ag96 St78 I70 Sp93):** the gyrfalcon is about 70% heavier with bigger prey, so Attack (37.0) and Defense (20.0) sit slightly above the peregrine. The peregrine keeps higher Agility because it steers more sharply and flies tighter, and a much higher Special for its instrumented stoop and fast vision. The gyrfalcon takes higher Stamina for its tail-chases and sea-ice winters.
- **Versus Snowy Owl (2.15 kg; A39 D20 Ag88 St83):** another Arctic bird predator of similar mass. The owl's heavier feet keep it slightly ahead in Attack; the two are close in Agility and Stamina.
- **Versus Golden Eagle (5 kg; A40 Ag88):** the eagle is three times heavier with far stronger feet, so it stays above the gyrfalcon in Attack and Raw Power despite similar Agility.
- **Versus Osprey (1.8 kg; A31 Ag87 St82):** similar mass; the gyrfalcon's bird-killing weapons and committed pursuit justify higher Attack and Ferocity.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the compiled speed claims are not used to raise Speed or Agility to peregrine level, and Arctic hardiness is counted in Special and Stamina only.
