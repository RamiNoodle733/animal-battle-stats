# Maned Wolf research report

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Common name:** Maned Wolf
- **Scientific name:** *Chrysocyon brachyurus* (Illiger, 1815)
- **Exact taxon represented:** the living maned wolf, sole living species of *Chrysocyon*, not a true *Canis* wolf or fox.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy fully mature **adult male**, represented at **23.0 kg**. Sexes are broadly similar in external appearance; males are somewhat more active in field summaries, but there is no wolf-like extreme male size dimorphism.
- **Population variation:** body condition and size vary geographically and seasonally. A representative animal is used rather than exceptional zoo or wild individuals.

## 2. Physical measurements
### Mass and dimensions
Smithsonian gives about **23 kg** and **90 cm shoulder height**. Animal Diversity Web reports **20-23 kg**, head-body **124.5-132.0 cm**, and tail **28.0-40.5 cm**. SeaWorld gives 20-26 kg, 74-90 cm shoulder height and 28-49 cm tail. These differences partly reflect measurement convention and population.

- **Canonical `weight_kg`: 23.0. Confidence: high.**
- **Canonical `height_cm`: 90.0. Confidence: high.** Smithsonian independently gives approximately 90 cm at the shoulder.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 130.0. Confidence: moderate-high.** This is representative head-body length, not head-to-tail-tip length.

### Speed and locomotion
No instrumented or otherwise strong species-specific maximum running-speed measurement was recovered. Popular speed figures are therefore not promoted.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0.** This means maximum speed is unresolved, not that the animal lacks locomotor ability.

The exceptionally long limbs are primarily an open-grassland adaptation, improving movement and visual access through tall vegetation. Maned wolves walk and trot extensively but are not built like gray wolves for cooperative pursuit of large prey.

### Lifespan
Wild longevity remains poorly constrained. Smithsonian's main species page gives a managed-care median of 6.5 years and maximum 12-15 years, while older Smithsonian releases describe wild median lifespan around 13 years. Zoo sources commonly report 12-15 years.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 13.0. Confidence: moderate.** The uncertainty and captive/wild conflict are retained explicitly.

### Bite force and dentition
A 2020 peer-reviewed canid jaw-muscle/FEA study predicted *C. brachyurus* bite forces of **246 N canine / 384 N carnassial at occlusion**, and **225 N / 355 N at wide gape**. These are modeled forces, not direct in-vivo pressure measurements. ADW describes reduced upper carnassials, weak upper incisors, and long slender canines, consistent with a diet dominated by small prey and plant foods rather than large-prey killing.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** Force is not converted to PSI without defensible tooth-contact area.

### Weapons and defenses
- **Primary weapon:** long slender canines and jaws suitable for seizing small vertebrates.
- **Secondary weapon:** forefeet used to flush prey, followed by a pounce; claws and body checks are secondary rather than specialized killing tools.
- **Defense:** tall stance, awareness, mobility and threat displays. No armor, thick hide, heavy skull or massive neck comparable with larger canids.

## 4. Combat biology
### Offensive mechanics
The maned wolf is a small-prey generalist, not a scaled-down gray wolf. Its narrow skull, reduced carnassials and slender canines suit rapid seizure of rodents, rabbits, birds, reptiles and invertebrates. Smithsonian describes a characteristic sequence in which the animal rotates its large ears to locate prey, taps the ground with a forefoot to flush it, then pounces or digs. Against a similarly sized opponent it can bite and use its reach to reposition, but its jaw architecture is not specialized for holding and dismantling large struggling prey.

### Defensive adaptations and toughness
At roughly 23 kg, the maned wolf has ordinary mammalian soft-tissue vulnerability. Long legs create reach and visibility but are relatively slender and are not defensive armor. Its best defense is early detection, distance control and avoidance. Smithsonian notes pumas and domestic dogs as reported killers, illustrating the limits of its direct defensive package.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
The species' very long legs are excellent for tall savanna grasses. Its narrow body and elevated stance give useful obstacle clearance and sensory access. It can pounce vertically and make precise short-range prey captures. Those features support high maneuverability, but top speed is unknown and must not be substituted for agility.

### Endurance
Wild maned wolves maintain large territories. ADW gives an average home range around **27 km²**; recent tracking of a healthy female found a **55.7 km²** home range and substantial year-round travel. This supports good routine locomotor endurance and patrol capacity. It does not establish gray-wolf-like long-distance cooperative pursuit performance.

### Senses
Large independently orientable ears are central to prey localization in grass. Olfaction is also highly developed and is used heavily in territorial scent marking; urine and feces are deposited at prominent points. Vision benefits from the elevated head position. These senses combine particularly well in open grassland.

### Intelligence and tactics
Maned wolves are behaviorally flexible omnivores and territorial canids. They coordinate spatially with a monogamous mate while usually hunting, traveling and resting alone. Territorial scent communication and prey-flushing behavior support good tactical competence, but there is little evidence for the cooperative pack hunting that elevates some *Canis* species.

### Fighting and aggression
Wild pairs share territories while remaining physically independent much of the time. ADW reports growling during agonistic encounters and captive same-sex pairs initially fighting before establishing dominance. The species generally relies on territorial signaling rather than frequent high-risk combat. Ferocity should therefore remain below more confrontational similarly sized carnivores.

### Hunting and predation
Diet is unusually omnivorous for a canid. Smithsonian reports fruits and vegetables can make up about half the diet, especially lobeira or wolf apple, alongside rodents, rabbits, insects and other small animal prey. Long legs and large ears are a grassland hunting system, not large-prey pursuit adaptations.

### Environmental strengths
- Tall grass and cerrado: excellent sensory height, obstacle clearance and prey localization.
- Mixed savanna/forest edge: broad omnivory and flexible foraging.
- Low-light hunting: strong hearing and olfaction complement crepuscular/nocturnal activity.

### Environmental limitations
- Close grappling: long slender limbs and modest body mass are liabilities against stockier carnivores.
- Large prey: dentition and jaw mechanics are not specialized for it.
- Pack combat: normally solitary hunting provides no individual cooperative multiplier.
- Deep water or vertical arboreal terrain: no special aquatic or climbing weapon system.

### Major weaknesses
1. Slender limbs are exposed in close combat.
2. No armor or unusually thick hide.
3. Modest absolute mass and bite force relative to large canids and cats.
4. Long slender canines and reduced carnassials reflect small-prey specialization.
5. Generally avoids costly direct conflict rather than pressing attacks.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Best against:** smaller unarmored animals that can be detected and pounced on in open vegetation.
- **Worst against:** stocky similarly sized carnivores, large cats, armored opponents, and much larger mammals capable of forcing close contact.

## 6. Proposed six headline ABS ratings
- **Attack: 35.0** - adequate canine weaponry and precise pouncing, but only 23 kg and anatomically biased toward small prey rather than large-prey killing.
- **Defense: 27.0** - mobility and awareness help avoid damage, but there is no armor and the long limbs are vulnerable at contact range.
- **Agility: 78.0** - excellent grassland control, pouncing and long-legged repositioning without pretending an unknown top speed is measured agility.
- **Stamina: 72.0** - extensive territorial ranging supports strong routine endurance, below specialized long-distance pursuit canids.
- **Intelligence: 67.0** - flexible omnivory, spatial territoriality and adaptive hunting behavior support solid canid cognition without pack-coordination inflation.
- **Special: 65.0** - the combination of extreme leg length, mobile ears and scent-based territorial awareness is matchup-relevant but not a venom/electric/armor-class mechanism.

## 7. Abilities and traits
### Special abilities, exactly two
1. **Grassline Radar** - very large mobile ears plus an unusually elevated head position help locate small prey hidden in tall vegetation before a targeted pounce.
2. **Flush-and-Pounce** - the wolf can tap vegetation/ground with a forefoot to disturb concealed prey, then exploit the movement with a rapid pounce or dig.

### Unique traits, exactly two
1. **Stilt-Legged Canid** - exceptionally long limbs raise the body above cerrado grasses and improve movement and visual access through dense herbaceous cover.
2. **Wolf-Apple Omnivore** - unusually heavy fruit use, especially lobeira, broadens food availability and reduces dependence on risky vertebrate kills.

## 8. Expanded profile
### Habitat and range
Maned wolves occupy central and eastern South America, especially Brazil's cerrado, with populations extending into Paraguay, Bolivia, northern Argentina and southern Peru. They use grassland, savanna, shrubland, wet fields, forest edges and open woodland.

### Diet and ecology
They are broad omnivores. Small mammals, birds, reptiles, insects and other animal foods are combined with substantial fruit and vegetable matter. Lobeira is particularly important. This mixed diet makes the species an important predator of small vertebrates and a seed disperser rather than a large-prey apex hunter.

### Social structure
A male and female can form a monogamous territorial pair sharing a large home range, but typically hunt, travel and rest alone. Scent marks and loud roar-barks maintain spacing. Physical association increases around breeding and parental care.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeding is seasonal. Smithsonian reports approximately 65-day gestation and a mean litter near 2.5, range 1-5. Pups begin transitioning to regurgitated food at about four weeks and generally disperse around one year. Both sexes can contribute parental care, although females provide most direct rearing.

### Conservation
The IUCN assessment cited by Smithsonian is **Near Threatened**. Major pressures include conversion and fragmentation of cerrado, road mortality, persecution, disease and conflict with domestic animals. Remaining populations are concentrated in Brazil.

### Human interaction
Maned wolves sometimes take poultry and are persecuted because of perceived livestock risk, although their natural diet is dominated by smaller prey and plant foods. Road strikes and habitat conversion are major human-caused mortality pressures. Zoo breeding programs maintain genetically managed populations and support reproductive research.

### Major adaptations
- Extremely elongated limbs for tall grass.
- Large mobile ears for acoustic prey localization.
- Strong olfactory scent-marking system.
- Flexible omnivory with heavy seasonal fruit use.
- Solitary hunting within pair-held territories.
- Erectable dark mane used in threat/display contexts.

### Genuine fun facts
- The maned wolf is the only living species in the genus *Chrysocyon*.
- Despite its name and fox-like face, it is neither a true wolf nor a fox.
- It stands around 90 cm at the shoulder while weighing only about 23 kg.
- Fruit can constitute roughly half of its diet, unusual for a large canid.
- It can rotate its large ears to locate prey concealed in grass.
- Pairs may share a territory while spending most hunting and resting time apart.

### Concise site-ready summary
The maned wolf is South America's tallest canid, a 23 kg grassland specialist built around reach, hearing and precise small-prey pounces rather than brute force. Its stilt-like legs and huge ears dominate tall-grass matchups, while its slender build, modest jaw mechanics and lack of armor keep direct combat power well below stockier wolves, hyenas and large cats.

### Rich narrative profile
The maned wolf's silhouette can make it look larger and more formidable than its mass really is. At roughly 90 cm at the shoulder it stands almost as tall as much heavier predators, yet a representative adult weighs only about 23 kg. That mismatch is the key to its ABS profile. The legs are ecological tools: they lift the animal over cerrado grasses, extend stride and give its eyes and enormous ears access to prey cues above dense vegetation. They do not turn the animal into a heavyweight fighter.

Its hunting sequence is correspondingly precise. Rather than running down deer-sized prey, the wolf listens, approaches, taps or disturbs concealed quarry, and pounces. Jaw anatomy supports that interpretation. Comparative modeling predicts a useful but modest bite, while anatomical summaries describe reduced carnassials and slender canines. The animal can certainly injure a peer, but a gray wolf, hyena or large cat brings a much heavier killing apparatus to direct contact.

The other half of the maned wolf is ecological flexibility. It patrols large territories, communicates intensely by scent and vocalization, and survives on a diet that can be half plant matter. That flexibility supports good stamina, senses and tactical ratings. Its best matchup is not a wrestling contest. It is an open-grass encounter in which early detection, reach, pouncing control and the option to disengage matter more than crushing force.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes
- **Gray Wolf / Arctic Wolf:** maned wolf Attack and Defense must remain much lower. Height does not compensate for the large gap in robusticity, jaw mechanics, large-prey specialization and cooperative combat ecology.
- **Jackal:** maned wolf is taller and somewhat heavier than a black-backed jackal, but its dentition is less carnivory-specialized; its advantage is reach/sensory height rather than proportionally inflated power.
- **Lynx:** the 24 kg Eurasian lynx is nearly equal in mass but has substantially more specialized grappling claws and killing anatomy, justifying Lynx Attack 48.0 versus Maned Wolf 35.0.
- **Mandrill:** the 32 kg male mandrill has larger absolute mass, serious canine weaponry and manipulative grappling hands, justifying Mandrill Attack 47.0 versus Maned Wolf 35.0.
- **Llama:** the 150 kg llama massively exceeds the maned wolf in absolute body force and durability despite lower agility.
- **Agility control:** 78.0 reflects maneuverability and pounce precision, not an unsupported sprint-speed number.
- **Special control:** sensory-height ecology is distinctive but is deliberately kept far below venom/electricity/extreme-armor mechanisms.
