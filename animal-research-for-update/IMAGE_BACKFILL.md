## 2026-09-23 Lobster legacy audit
Roster Lobster resolves to American lobster ***Homarus americanus***, with a healthy fully mature hard-shelled adult male preferred because male crusher-claw mechanics and agonistic role provide the strongest fighting-sex basis.

The existing report candidate was re-opened at its original **5040x3224** resolution:
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:American_lobster,_Homarus_americanus_in_Newfoundland,_Canada_(21184223265).jpg
- Original: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2b/American_lobster%2C_Homarus_americanus_in_Newfoundland%2C_Canada_%2821184223265%29.jpg
- **CC BY 2.0**, exact *H. americanus*, Derek Keats, Newfoundland.
- Direct visual inspection shows a lobster deep in a rock crevice. The two large chelae dominate the frame, but the abdomen/tail and multiple walking appendages are obscured. It fails the mandatory whole-animal silhouette standard and should not be treated as a primary full-body candidate.

Fresh broad search checked NOAA, Commons, Flickr/iNaturalist-derived Commons files and general image discovery. A second **5174x3441 CC BY 2.0** Derek Keats photograph was also opened at original resolution:
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:American_lobster,_Homarus_americanus_in_Newfoundland,_Canada_(20996211958).jpg
- Original: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cd/American_lobster%2C_Homarus_americanus_in_Newfoundland%2C_Canada_%2820996211958%29.jpg
- Direct inspection again shows a close frontal/crevice composition with the abdomen, tail fan and multiple legs hidden behind the claws/body.

Other high-resolution exact-species Commons files from the same Newfoundland series reach 5039x3370 and 5237x3496 and have compatible CC BY licensing, but metadata does not explicitly establish adulthood or sex, and the searched views did not establish a strict complete appendage-and-tail silhouette. NOAA has excellent exact-species imagery, but the inspected discovery results likewise did not establish the required adult male plus complete anatomy combination. Historical Freshwater and Marine Image Bank files explicitly labeled male/adult male are illustrations or low-resolution historical material and therefore fail the real-photograph standard.

**Result:** `REPLACEMENT SOURCE NEEDED`. The old crevice candidate is explicitly rejected as a full-body primary rather than grandfathered under historical weak wording. No PNG created. **Lynx is next practical unresolved legacy audit.**

## 2026-09-23 Saltwater Crocodile new-animal image audit
Roster Saltwater Crocodile resolves directly to ***Crocodylus porosus***. Canonical fighting specimen is a healthy prime adult male.

Best reusable discovery candidate inspected this run:
- Source page: https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/Crocodylus%20porosus
- Original: https://inaturalist-open-data.s3.amazonaws.com/photos/12290705/original.jpeg
- Exact *C. porosus*; discovery metadata reports **CC BY-SA 3.0**.
- Direct visual inspection shows one real crocodile on a riverbank with head, complete torso, all four limbs/feet and entire tail inside the frame. The framing is excellent for a future cutout.
- Mandatory blocker: adulthood is not explicitly established by the candidate metadata, and sex is unknown. Sex is not inferred from appearance.

Animal Diversity Web sources were also checked. Multiple *C. porosus* photographs explicitly identify the subjects as **adult/sexually mature**, including Phil Myers and Jerry Gingerich material, but the available evidence did not simultaneously establish superior complete-body framing, preferred sex, high resolution and reusable licensing. Some are restricted to educational fair use; the Phil Myers image is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 but is documented primarily for mouth/teeth/sunning context rather than strict whole-body cutout suitability.

**Result:** `SOURCE FOUND - FULL-BODY NOT VERIFIED`. Full visible anatomy is verified for the iNaturalist candidate, but mandatory adulthood is not. No PNG created.
