# Water Buffalo (*Bubalus arnee*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Water buffalo; the combat profile uses the wild water buffalo (also Asian or Asiatic wild buffalo, *arna*)
- **Scientific name:** *Bubalus arnee* (Kerr, 1792)
- **Exact animal represented:** the wild Asian water buffalo *Bubalus arnee*. The domestic water buffalo (*Bubalus bubalis*, river and swamp types) was bred from wild stock and is usually smaller and less aggressive; it is noted for context but is not the canonical combat form.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult male (bull) of about **900 kg** and **170 cm** shoulder height, with full-grown horns. This is a representative wild bull, not a record animal.
- **Sex choice:** bulls are larger and heavier-horned, and are reported to be more aggressive than cows, sometimes attacking without provocation. Cows with calves also defend boldly, including against tigers.
- **Population variation:** most surviving wild buffalo live in Assam, India (Kaziranga and nearby reserves). Many populations are mixed with feral or domestic buffalo, so "wild" measurements from mixed herds need care.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The wild buffalo's offense is a mass-backed charge with the head lowered and the horns swung to hook and toss. Because the horns sweep sideways and backward rather than forward, the most dangerous surfaces are the heavy horn bases and the curved outer sections, used in sideways hooks and upward tosses. Body checks, shoving and trampling add damage. The sheer width of the horns also makes a frontal approach awkward for a predator.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Buffalo are heavy-bodied and built for footing in mud, marsh and floodplain grassland. They move and turn adequately on land but there is no measured top speed, and a 900 kg frame limits fine evasion. In water and deep mud they cope far better than most large terrestrial mammals.

### Endurance
Water buffalo depend on water and mud: they spend long periods wallowing to shed heat and avoid biting insects, and they swim readily. This makes them resilient in hot, wet floodplains but limits sustained exertion in heat away from water.

### Senses
Buffalo use smell and hearing strongly in tall grass and reedbeds, with vigilant herd behavior. No quantitative sensory data were located.

### Intelligence and tactics
Herds bunch, face threats and counter-attack. Cows with calves confront tigers, and bulls may charge without obvious provocation. Behavior is effective group defense rather than advanced problem solving.

### Sociality and predator defense
Wild buffalo live in herds of cows and young, with bulls joining for breeding or living in small bachelor groups or alone. The tiger is the main predator. Kaziranga, which has the highest tiger density of any protected area, also holds the largest breeding population of wild buffalo. A classic study of tiger predatory behavior followed 26 kills of buffalo in full: the first contact was a bite to the throat or nape in 12, a near-simultaneous seizure with teeth and forepaws in 4, and a forepaw seizure before the bite in 10. Tigers are occasionally killed by water buffalo and gaur.

### Fighting and aggression
Wild bulls are described as more aggressive than cows and sometimes attack without provocation. Buffalo defending calves or herd-mates will charge tigers and people. A defensive charge or kick from an animal this size can break a big cat's bones or cause fatal internal injuries.

### Environmental strengths
- Floodplain grassland, marsh and river margins.
- Deep mud and water where heavier-footed or less aquatic opponents struggle.
- Tall grass where herd vigilance and close-range charges favor the buffalo.

### Weaknesses
- No true armor; flanks, hindquarters and belly are exposed.
- Backward-swept horns are less of a forward goring tool than hooked or straight horns.
- Heat and water dependence limit exertion on dry ground.
- No measured speed; heavy frame limits agility.
- Tigers can kill adults, especially lone animals.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** big cats and other predators forced into a frontal or wetland fight; similar-sized herbivores in mud or water.
- **Mixed:** other tonne-class bovids on firm ground.
- **Bad:** elephants, rhinoceroses and hippos with major mass advantages; aerial or fully aquatic opponents in deep water.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Crescent Horn Hook** — the widest-spreading horns of any bovid, heavy at the base, swung sideways and upward in a mass-backed charge to hook and toss attackers.
2. **Wallow and Swim** — spends long periods in water and mud and swims readily, letting it fight on wetland ground where many heavy land animals lose footing.

### Unique traits
1. **Record Horn Spread** — horns in both sexes can span about 2 m along the outer edges, with a record horn just under 2 m long.
2. **Wild Stock of a Working Giant** — the domestic water buffalo, one of Asia's key farm animals, was bred from this wild species; hybridisation with domestic buffalo now threatens the wild form.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Wild water buffalo live in floodplain grasslands, marshes, swamps and riverine forest. The species survives in India, Nepal, Bhutan, Thailand, Cambodia and Myanmar, with the great majority in Assam, especially Kaziranga National Park. Its former range was far wider across South and Southeast Asia.

### Diet and ecology
They graze grasses, reeds and sedges and eat aquatic and floodplain vegetation. Wallowing and grazing shape wetland vegetation, and buffalo are an important large prey for tigers where the two occur together.

### Social structure
Cows and young form herds that bunch and face threats together; bulls live in small bachelor groups or alone and join herds to breed. Cows with calves are strongly defensive.

### Reproduction and life history
Females usually bear a single calf. Calves are the main target of tigers and depend on herd defense. Specific gestation and longevity data for the wild form were not captured in this research pass.

### Conservation
Endangered on the IUCN Red List, a status held since 1986, with fewer than 4,000 mature individuals. The global population has been estimated at about 3,400, of which about 3,100 (91%) live in India, mostly in Assam. Threats are habitat loss and degradation, hunting, disease from livestock and hybridisation with domestic buffalo.

### Special features
- Widest horn spread of any living bovid.
- Horns in both sexes, heavy at the base and crescent-shaped.
- Heavy build suited to mud, marsh and water.
- Strong wallowing and swimming behavior.
- Aggressive group and calf defense against tigers.

### Human interaction
The domestic water buffalo, bred from wild stock, is one of the most important livestock animals in South and Southeast Asia, used for milk, meat and draught work. Domestic and feral buffalo mix with wild herds and spread disease, and genetic studies of the wild buffalo mitochondrial genome are used to assess hybridisation. Wild bulls are dangerous to people and can attack without warning.

### Genuine fun facts
- Wild water buffalo have the widest horn spread of any bovid, up to about 2 m along the outer edges.
- About 91% of the world's wild water buffalo live in India, mostly in Assam.
- Kaziranga holds both the world's highest tiger density and the largest wild buffalo population.
- Tigers are occasionally killed by water buffalo.
- Both bulls and cows carry horns.
- The world's domestic water buffalo were bred from this wild species.
- Cows with calves are known to face tigers head-on.

### Concise site-ready summary
The wild water buffalo is a heavy, wetland-dwelling Asian bovid armed with the widest-spreading horns of any living bovid. Bulls near a tonne charge readily, hook with crescent horns and hold their ground against tigers, and the species is most dangerous in the mud and water where it lives.

### Rich narrative profile
A wild water buffalo bull is built for the floodplain. Standing around 1.7 m at the shoulder and weighing close to a tonne, it spends much of its day wallowing in mud and water, grazing reeds and grasses along rivers and swamps. Its horns are its signature: thick at the base and sweeping out and back in a vast crescent, they can reach about 2 m along the outer edge, more than any other living bovid.

In a fight the buffalo relies on mass, horns and temper. Bulls are reported to be more aggressive than cows and sometimes attack without provocation, while cows with calves confront tigers directly. The backward sweep of the horns means the buffalo hooks and tosses rather than stabbing forward, but a charge from a 900 kg animal can break bones regardless. Tigers do kill buffalo, including adults, and a classic study documented in detail how they seize the throat or nape, yet tigers are occasionally killed in return.

The wild buffalo is now Endangered, with only a few thousand left, most of them in Assam. Domestic buffalo bred from it far outnumber the wild form and are one of Asia's key farm animals, and hybridisation with them is a direct threat to the wild form. For ABS purposes the wild buffalo sits alongside the Cape Buffalo and Bison: similar in raw power and attack, less agile than Bison, and with a real terrain advantage in wetlands that neither of the others has.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Cape Buffalo (750 kg):** the wild water buffalo is heavier with longer horns, but its horns sweep back and lack a fused boss, so Weaponry is slightly lower (73.0 vs 74.0) while Raw Power is slightly higher (77.0 vs 74.0). Attack lands one point above (77.0 vs 76.0). Ferocity is close (80.0 vs 82.0). Agility is lower (51.0 vs 55.0) for the heavier wetland frame, and Special higher (56.0 vs 52.0) for swimming and mud mobility.
- **Versus Bison (900 kg):** same canonical mass and similar Raw Power (77.0 vs 78.0). Buffalo gets more Attack (77.0 vs 72.0) for bigger horns and higher aggression; Bison keeps clearly higher Agility (64.0 vs 51.0) and Stamina (74.0 vs 68.0).
- **Versus Gaur (1,000 kg, same batch):** gaur is heavier and stronger (Raw Power 81.0) with forward-curving horns, so it stays one point above in Attack (78.0 vs 77.0) and Defense (72.0 vs 71.0); the buffalo is more aggressive and more capable in wetlands.
- **Versus Hippopotamus:** the hippo remains far above in Attack, Defense and Size; both are wetland heavyweights, but the hippo's bite and 1,800 kg mass put it in another tier.
- **Watch item:** Attack 77.0 depends partly on aggression reports from a park tourism page and generic sources; revisit when a primary behavioral study of wild bulls is attached.
