# Goldfish (*Carassius auratus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Goldfish
- **Scientific name:** *Carassius auratus* (Linnaeus, 1758)
- **Exact animal represented:** the goldfish as a species, represented by a healthy adult of the streamlined "common" body type: the form closest to the olive-brown wild Carassius carp of East Asian ponds and rivers that goldfish were bred from, and the form that survives and spreads when goldfish are released. Fancy breeds (fantail, oranda, bubble-eye and others) are captive-only varieties with poorer swimming and are not the canonical animal.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult common-type goldfish of **20 cm total length** and about **0.1 kg**. The length is ADW's average and sits at the top of Wikipedia's 12–22 cm typical wild range; the mass comes from FishBase's length-weight relationship for the species (W = 0.01148 × L^3.04, giving about 104 g at 20 cm).
- **Sex choice:** females tend to be rounder-bodied and males slimmer (ADW); sizes overlap and neither sex fights. The specimen is not sexed.
- **Population notes:** wild-caught fish are olive brown, slate olive or silvery; cultured forms run through gold, red, white and black (FishBase). Released goldfish revert toward duller colours within a few generations (ADW) and can grow much larger than tank fish.

- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 1.2 m/s**, the measured fast-start maximum. It comes from small fish; a 20 cm adult is probably faster in absolute terms, but no measurement was found.
- **Confidence:** Moderate for the measurement, low as an adult maximum.

- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 10.0**, a conservative typical value; long-lived pond fish are well documented, but ADW's 20–30-year wild range has no stated study.
- **Confidence:** Low-moderate.

### Mouth and bite
- The goldfish is a suction-feeding omnivore with a small, barbel-less mouth (FishBase) that takes plankton, invertebrates, plants and detritus. No bite-force measurement exists.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.**

### Defensive structure
- Cycloid scales (25–31 along the lateral line) and serrated bony spines at the front of the dorsal and anal fins (FishBase; ADW). Schooling and a chemical alarm substance released from damaged skin warn the group (ADW).

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The goldfish has essentially no weapon. It suction-feeds on midge and mosquito larvae, small crustaceans, pondweeds, worms and detritus (ADW) and is not aggressive toward other goldfish (ADW). In a fight it cannot injure anything larger than a tadpole or an insect larva.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Like most bony fish, the goldfish escapes with a C-start: the giant Mauthner neurons fire and bend the body into a C with very short latency, launching it away from a threat. The goldfish is the classic laboratory model for this reflex; sound clicks reliably trigger short-latency escapes, and cooling slows the response (Preuss and Faber 2003). Common and comet goldfish reach about 1.2 m/s in these bursts, while short-bodied fancy breeds have more drag, roll and yaw and waste energy when swimming (Blake et al. 2009). For sustained swimming it is a weak performer (Fu et al. 2013).

### Endurance
Radio-tracked goldfish in Australia's Murray River moved about 0.30 km per day on average; one travelled 231.3 km in a year and another 5.4 km in a single day (Beatty et al. 2017, via ADW). Common and comet goldfish have fatigue times similar to crucian carp (Blake et al. 2009). The goldfish's real endurance feat is metabolic: it can survive long periods without oxygen, and several months of low oxygen in cold water (see Special features).

### Senses
Goldfish have four cone types and see red, green, blue and ultraviolet light (Wikipedia; ADW). Weberian ossicles link the swim bladder to the inner ear, giving them sensitive hearing up to about 2–3 kHz (ADW); removing the tripus bones on both sides raises hearing thresholds by up to 33 dB (Ladich and Wysocki 2003). A lateral line senses ripples and vibrations, and chemical cues detect food and predators (ADW).

### Intelligence and tactics
Goldfish learn and remember well for a fish. Wikipedia reports a memory span of at least three months and the ability to distinguish shapes, colours and sounds. They copy novel feeding behaviour from other goldfish (ADW). Most strikingly, goldfish trained to steer a wheeled "Fish Operated Vehicle" drove their water tank across a room toward a visual target on land (Givon et al. 2022), showing that their navigation skills work even in a completely foreign setting.

### Sociality and hunting
Social schooling fish with no dominance hierarchy and no territories (ADW). They forage widely over vegetation and sandy bottoms and eat amphibian eggs and their own eggs.

### Fighting and aggression
Essentially none. Spawning involves chasing and bumping, not fighting.

### Environmental strengths
- Still, weedy, oxygen-poor ponds, ditches and canals, where its anoxia and ammonia tolerance let it outlast other fish.
- Cold water: tolerates 0–41 °C (FishBase) and overwinters under ice.
- Low salinity up to about 15–17 ppt.

### Weaknesses
- No weapon and no meaningful bite.
- Small, soft-bodied fish with only scales and fin spines for protection.
- Poor sustained swimmer.
- Eaten by herons, otters, snakes, bullfrogs, cats, dogs and many other predators (ADW).

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** insect larvae, zooplankton and eggs; any contest decided by surviving bad water rather than fighting.
- **Mixed:** other small non-predatory fish, where schooling and escapes decide the outcome.
- **Bad:** every predatory fish, wading bird, snake or mammal that can reach it.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Ethanol Engine** — without oxygen, goldfish (and crucian carp) switch to a pathway that turns pyruvate into ethanol, using a pyruvate decarboxylase like that of brewer's yeast, the first described in a vertebrate (Fagernes et al. 2017). With ethanol as the end-product they survive prolonged oxygen loss, tolerating several months of low oxygen at 2 °C (ADW), in water where most fish die within minutes.
2. **Mauthner Escape** — giant Mauthner neurons trigger a short-latency, C-shaped escape bend in response to a sudden sound or looming shadow, launching the fish away at up to about 1.2 m/s in small individuals.

### Unique traits
1. **Four-Colour Eyes** — goldfish have red, green, blue and ultraviolet cones and are among the most studied colour-vision systems in fish.
2. **Millennium Pet** — selectively bred for colour in imperial China for more than 1,000 years; by the Song dynasty, gold and red fish were kept in dedicated ponds.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Native to East Asia, including China and Japan (FishBase), in rivers, lakes, ponds and ditches with stagnant or slow-flowing, often eutrophic and weedy water (FishBase; ADW). Introduced almost worldwide, with established populations in North America, Europe, Australia, South Africa and elsewhere, where it can harm native ecosystems (FishBase; Wikipedia).

### Diet and ecology
Opportunistic omnivore. One Quebec pond study found vegetation made up 45% of the diet, midge and mosquito larvae 21%, and small crustaceans 4%, with worms, detritus and amphibian eggs also taken (ADW). Goldfish lack a stomach and produce a lot of waste (Wikipedia). Predators include herons, river otters, water snakes, bullfrogs, cats, dogs, coyotes, bears and mountain lions (ADW).

### Social structure
Schooling and gregarious, without hierarchies or territories. Injured fish release an alarm substance that puts the school on watch, and goldfish copy each other's feeding choices (ADW).

### Reproduction and life history
Spawning is triggered by warming water above about 10 °C in spring. Females release pheromones, males chase and bump them, and eggs stick to submerged plants or flooded meadow grass. Females spawn about 2,000–4,000 eggs at a time and may spawn several times per season; there is no parental care and adults eat eggs (ADW; FishBase). Maturity comes at 3–4 years in the wild (ADW).

### Conservation
Least Concern on the IUCN Red List (assessed 2010, via FishBase). Outside its native range it is a potential pest and an invasive species in parts of North America and Australia (FishBase; Wikipedia).

### Special features
- Ethanol-producing metabolism that allows long survival without oxygen, and months of low oxygen in cold water.
- High ammonia tolerance: 96-hour lethal concentrations about 5–6 times those of rainbow trout (Wilkie et al. 2011).
- Weberian ossicles linking the swim bladder to the ear for sensitive hearing.
- Four cone types including ultraviolet.
- Mauthner-cell escape reflex, the textbook model for fish startle responses.

### Human interaction
One of the world's most popular ornamental fish and a major laboratory species for vision, hearing and neuroscience (FishBase; Wikipedia). Goldfish kept in bowls suffer from low oxygen and high ammonia; single-tailed forms can exceed 30 cm and need large, well-filtered tanks or ponds (Wikipedia). Released pets become invasive and grow far larger than in tanks.

### Genuine fun facts
- Goldfish make alcohol: without oxygen they turn sugar into ethanol instead of lactic acid.
- Researchers taught goldfish to drive a wheeled tank across a room toward a target.
- Goldfish see ultraviolet light as well as red, green and blue.
- A released goldfish caught in a South Carolina lake in 2020 weighed about 4.1 kg.
- The longest-lived goldfish on record reached 43 years in captivity (ADW).
- Goldfish memory lasts at least three months, not three seconds.
- One radio-tagged goldfish in Australia's Murray River travelled 231 km in a year.

### Concise site-ready summary
The goldfish is a small East Asian carp bred for colour for more than a thousand years. It has no weapon and swims poorly, but it can see ultraviolet light, hear well, learn to navigate, and survive long spells without oxygen by turning sugar into alcohol.

### Rich narrative profile
Behind the bowl-and-castle image, the goldfish is a tough wild carp. Wild goldfish are olive-brown fish of East Asian ponds and ditches, where the water can turn warm, stagnant and oxygen-poor in summer and freeze over in winter. Goldfish and crucian carp cope with a metabolic trick described in no other vertebrate: when oxygen runs out, they convert pyruvate to ethanol as their metabolic end-product, and in cold water they can endure months of very low oxygen that would kill most fish within minutes.

The goldfish is also one of science's favourite fish. Its four-cone eyes see into the ultraviolet, bony Weberian ossicles give it sensitive hearing, and its Mauthner neurons have made its lightning-fast startle escape a textbook case. It learns and remembers for months, copies other goldfish, and has even learned to steer a wheeled tank toward a target on land.

None of that makes it a fighter. A healthy 20 cm goldfish weighs around 100 grams, has a toothless suction mouth for larvae and pondweed, and relies on scales, fin spines, schooling and a quick escape. Herons, otters, snakes and almost any predatory fish can eat it. Its best matchups are the ones decided by outlasting bad water, not by trading blows.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** ADW's "average 2 kg" contradicts its own 20 cm average length; FishBase's length-weight relationship gives about 0.1 kg at 20 cm and is used.
- **Lifespan:** ADW's 20–30-year wild range is not traced to a study; Wikipedia and ADW captive figures suggest 5–10+ years for typical pets; 10 years is a conservative middle value.
- **Speed:** 1.2 m/s was measured in 5 cm fish; adult absolute speed is unmeasured.
- **Records:** 2.3–4.1 kg fish are released pets in rich lakes, not typical animals.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Piranha (1.2 kg; A37 D20 Ag82 St55 I59):** twelve times heavier with serrated shearing teeth; far above the goldfish in Attack and Agility. The goldfish sits lower in Intelligence (48.0) because its cognition, while well studied, lacks the piranha's coordinated predatory targeting.
- **Versus Pufferfish (1.5 kg; A23 D56 Sp97) and Lionfish (0.5 kg; A25 D38 Sp87):** both carry a real toxin or venom system that punishes attackers; the goldfish has none, so Defense (9.0) and Special (58.0) stay far below.
- **Versus Betta Fish (0.002 kg, this batch):** fifty times heavier but far less aggressive; slightly higher Attack only because of mass, and higher Intelligence and Stamina.
- **Versus Axolotl (0.2 kg; A2.8 D4.5 Sp74):** similar harmlessness; the axolotl's regeneration outranks the goldfish's anoxia survival for Special.
- **Anti-inflation check:** anoxia and ammonia tolerance are counted once in Special/Abilities and lightly in Toughness, not in Defense or Stamina.
