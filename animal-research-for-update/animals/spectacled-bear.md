# Spectacled Bear / Andean Bear (*Tremarctos ornatus*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** Spectacled Bear
- **Accepted taxon:** *Tremarctos ornatus* (F. G. Cuvier, 1825), Ursidae
- **Living/extinct:** Living
- **Canonical fighter:** healthy prime **adult male, 115.0 kg**
- **Why male:** this species is strongly sexually dimorphic. Males are materially larger and more robust than females.
- **Population note:** size varies geographically and with condition. Upper-end 175-200+ kg males are not treated as representative.

The live schema values were treated only as legacy placeholders.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
**115.0 kg** is recommended for the canonical adult male. Major syntheses place males broadly around 100-200 kg and females around 35-82 kg. This preserves strong dimorphism without normalizing an exceptional male.

### Length
Recommended **165.0 cm head-body length**. The modern Mammalian Species synthesis reports about 119-174 cm for the species. Some secondary accounts extend to 200 cm. The canonical value stays inside the stronger range and represents a substantial adult male.

### Height
Recommended **80.0 cm shoulder height**. Institutional and compiled morphometric sources broadly place the species around 60-90 cm at the shoulder, with males toward the upper part of the range. Direct primary shoulder-height datasets are limited, so confidence is moderate.

### Speed
Recommended **0.0 unresolved**. Popular claims near 25 mph exist, but I did not find a sufficiently strong species-specific instrumented maximum. Daily travel cannot be converted into sprint speed.

### Lifespan
Recommended **20.0 years** as a representative wild estimate. ADW gives about 20 years for wild bears and a captive record of 36.7 years. Smithsonian states wild longevity is poorly known and human-care animals often reach their late twenties or early thirties.

### Bite force
Recommended bite_force_psi: **0.0 unresolved**. The 2021 Mammalian Species account reports **607.52 ± 143.77 N at the canine tip** from biomechanical work. This is force, not pressure. It is not converted to PSI because defensible contact area is unavailable.

### Weapons and structure
The skull is wide and heavy with thick bony walls and high bone density. The species has blade-shaped canines, enlarged molars and a relatively large zygomaticomandibularis. Forelimbs are robust and carry long curved claws used in climbing and tearing tough vegetation. No robust representative adult-male claw-length measurement was found, so no fabricated weapon dimension is proposed.

### Defenses
There is no armor. Defense comes from ursid body mass, dense coarse fur, robust skeleton, strong neck and forequarters, thick skull construction, claws, bite deterrence and vertical escape.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary weapons and offense
The close-range system is forelimb/claw control plus biting. A 115 kg male can grapple, rake or swat with powerful forequarters and curved claws, then bite at clinch distance. The measured/modelled canine-tip force is meaningful but moderate for a bear. This is not a pursuit predator, so the same musculature used to climb and tear fibrous plants should not be mistaken for specialized macropredatory killing performance.

### Defense and toughness
A prime male has enough mass and skeletal robustness to absorb attacks from many smaller opponents. Dense fur gives minor superficial protection. Dangerous counterweapons deter close engagement. Against larger bears, big cats or megafauna, however, 115 kg remains a clear limitation.

### Locomotion and agility
Andean bears are unusually arboreal for their size. Smithsonian and bear authorities document frequent climbing and construction of stick platforms for feeding/resting. Anatomical work on the ankle describes structures consistent with strong inversion during climbing. This supports above-average three-dimensional maneuverability for a 115 kg mammal.

On open ground, it should not be scored like a cursorial cat. Agility comes from climbing, balance and short-range repositioning, not an unsupported sprint number.

### Endurance
The species is active year-round and does not hibernate. Bear-specialist summaries report mean daily movement around 800 m in one study, maxima over 6 km, and daily movements up to 3-4 km in another. This supports solid general endurance, not extreme pursuit stamina.

### Senses
ADW identifies olfaction as the dominant communication/perception channel. Vision and hearing are useful but no extreme specialist sense comparable to owl hearing or cetacean echolocation is documented.

### Intelligence and tactics
Flexible foraging, climbing, platform construction, food-location memory and habitat switching support moderate-to-strong mammalian problem solving. The bear exploits fruits, bromeliads, palms, crops, carrion and occasional animal prey, but evidence does not justify great-ape or corvid-level cognition.

### Fighting and predation behavior
Adults are mostly solitary and generally avoid unnecessary confrontation. They are not specialized macropredators. Animal protein is opportunistic and livestock attacks occur, but plant matter can comprise up to about 90% of diet. Ferocity should therefore remain below similarly sized dedicated predators.

### Environmental pros
- Steep Andean forest, trees and broken terrain amplify climbing and balance.
- Dense vegetation provides disengagement routes.
- Curved claws provide traction and close-range utility.
- Broad dietary flexibility supports year-round activity.

### Environmental cons
- Open flat terrain removes much of the vertical-mobility advantage.
- No documented aquatic specialization.
- Larger terrestrial specialists can force direct exchanges the bear would prefer to avoid.

### Major weaknesses
- Moderate size by bear standards.
- No armor, venom, chemical weapon or exceptional verified sprint system.
- Bite is not exceptional among bears.
- Mostly herbivorous ecology means less routine killing practice than large felids.
- Climbing skill must not be double-counted as raw power.

### Matchup archetypes
**Favored:** small and many medium terrestrial opponents forced into claw-and-bite range; opponents unable to climb; fragile attackers lacking mass.

**Unfavored:** substantially larger bears, large felids with superior predatory weapon delivery, heavily armored reptiles, megafauna and aquatic specialists in deep water.

**Terrain-dependent:** trees and steep forest improve escape and repositioning; open terrain removes much of that advantage.

## 6. Six headline ratings
- **Attack 62.0:** serious claw-and-bite damage from a 115 kg ursid, but below sloth bear, big cats and heavyweight bears.
- **Defense 55.0:** mass, robust skeleton, dense coat and dangerous counters, but no armor.
- **Agility 64.0:** exceptional climbing for its size; deliberately not based on a speed claim.
- **Stamina 62.0:** year-round activity and sustained mountain movement, but not a pursuit specialist.
- **Intelligence 61.0:** flexible foraging, platform building and habitat use support solid mammalian cognition.
- **Special 58.0:** arboreal ability at bear scale and specialized tough-vegetation jaw mechanics matter, but there is no rare biochemical weapon.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities
1. **Canopy Grappler** - powerful forelimbs, curved claws, ankle mobility and strong climbing behavior let a large male reposition vertically and exploit steep substrates.
2. **Fiber-Crusher Jaws** - a wide dense skull, enlarged molars and relatively large zygomaticomandibularis support tough-plant processing and a stout close-range bite, grounded in the 607.52 N canine-tip force rather than fabricated PSI.

## 8. Exactly two unique traits
1. **Andean Platform Builder** - the bear breaks and arranges branches into arboreal feeding/resting platforms.
2. **Spectacle Signature** - cream facial, throat and chest markings vary strongly among individuals; some bears lack a complete eye-ring pattern.

## 9. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The Andean bear is the only living bear native to South America. The 2021 Mammalian Species synthesis places it across the tropical Andes of Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia and northern Argentina. It uses dry scrub, coastal dry forest, montane humid forest, cloud forest, elfin forest, oak forest, páramo and high-altitude grassland. Reported elevations extend roughly from 200 to 4,170 m.

### Diet and ecology
It is among the most plant-oriented bears. The International Association for Bear Research and Management summarizes plant matter at up to about 90% of diet, with bromeliads and palms especially important and fruit used seasonally. It also consumes invertebrates, small vertebrates, carrion and occasional livestock. Smithsonian lists fruits, bromeliads, berries, grasses, bulbs, cactus flowers and small animals.

### Social structure and space use
Adults are predominantly solitary except females with cubs and temporary mating associations. Aggregations can occur at concentrated food sources. Home ranges overlap. Published estimates vary greatly by site and method, including male ranges around 6.6-7.4 km² in Bolivia and much larger values in Ecuador. These should not be collapsed into one universal home-range value.

### Reproduction and life history
Females commonly raise one or two cubs. Delayed implantation contributes to variable gestation timing. The species does not normally hibernate.

### Conservation
The modern species synthesis records the Andean bear as **Vulnerable**, with a decreasing population. Major threats are habitat loss and fragmentation, illegal killing and human-bear conflict. The species is listed in CITES Appendix I.

### Human interaction
The bear is generally shy, but its size and weapons demand respect. Agricultural expansion brings bears into maize fields and livestock areas, where retaliatory killing is a major conservation problem. Occasional livestock attacks should not be generalized into routine predatory behavior.

### Adaptations
- Strong curved claws and forequarters for climbing and tearing vegetation.
- Ankle anatomy suited to strong inversion during climbing.
- Dense coarse fur for montane environments.
- Robust skull and grinding/cutting dentition for tough plant material.
- Flexible diet across heterogeneous Andean habitats.

### Genuine fun facts
- It is the only living bear species native to South America.
- It is the sole living species of the genus *Tremarctos*.
- Facial markings can help distinguish individuals.
- Some individuals do not have complete "spectacles."
- Bears build branch platforms in trees for feeding and resting.
- The species occurs above 4,000 m.
- It does not normally hibernate.
- Paddington Bear is famously described as coming from Peru and is associated with this South American bear.

### Concise site-ready summary
The spectacled bear is a strongly dimorphic Andean ursid whose prime males average around 115 kg. Curved claws, a robust skull and plant-processing jaws make it dangerous at close range, while unusually strong climbing ability lets it exploit steep forest and trees better than most bears. Its ABS profile emphasizes balanced ursid strength and terrain mobility rather than exaggerated predator ferocity.

### Narrative profile
A spectacled bear is best understood as a mountain-climbing generalist, not a miniature brown bear and not a giant herbivore. Its combat package begins with enough mass to dominate many medium animals, then adds the leverage and grip of bear forequarters. The same curved claws used to climb trunks and dismantle bromeliads can rake or hold an opponent. A stout skull and broad chewing apparatus provide a credible bite, but the available biomechanical value is force in newtons, not PSI.

Terrain changes the matchup. On open ground, a 115 kg male has no verified exceptional sprint speed. In steep forest or trees, it becomes harder to pursue and can use climbing routes unavailable to many similarly sized opponents. This vertical competence raises Agility, while Attack remains well below dedicated large-prey killers.

The mostly vegetarian diet also matters for calibration. Powerful jaws do not automatically imply high predatory Attack. Jaw anatomy is substantially shaped by fibrous plant foods. Likewise, shy behavior is not weakness: a cornered bear remains dangerous, but normal ecology does not justify inflated Ferocity.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** sources agree on extreme dimorphism but differ on upper limits. 115 kg represents an average-sized adult male, not a record.
- **Length:** peer-reviewed synthesis gives 119-174 cm, while weaker sources extend to 200 cm. Canonical 165 cm stays within the stronger range.
- **Lifespan:** 20 years is a common wild estimate, but wild longevity is incompletely known.
- **Speed:** popular 25 mph claims were not accepted.
- **Bite:** 607.52 ± 143.77 N is retained as force; legacy 100 PSI is rejected.

## 12. Cross-animal normalization
- **Sloth Bear, 120 kg:** similar mass, but sloth bear has stronger defensive-aggression evidence and specialized long claws. Spectacled Bear stays below Sloth Bear Attack 65.0 and Defense 57.0, while slightly higher in climbing maneuverability.
- **Siberian Tiger, 190 kg:** tiger remains far superior in predatory weapon delivery and absolute Attack.
- **Snow Leopard, 50 kg:** bear has much greater Raw Power and Defense, but lower Agility and predatory efficiency.
- **Serval, 14 kg:** bear dominates absolute power/durability despite serval agility.
- **Skunk, 4.5 kg:** bear dominates physical combat, while skunk remains far higher in Special due to chemical spray.
- **Snapping Turtle, 12 kg:** bear has greater overall Attack and mobility; turtle retains structural defense but not bear-level power.
- **Compression check:** 115 kg bear remains far above tiny vertebrates/arthropods but below large bears, bovids and megafauna.
- **Proportional-strength leakage:** climbing raises Maneuverability and Abilities, not Raw Power by itself.
- **Speed/agility:** Agility is based on climbing and balance, not unsupported sprint speed.
- **Double-counting:** jaw anatomy supports Weaponry/Attack and one ability, not Defense and Special simultaneously.

## 13. Future field proposals
1. High-rate GPS/IMU studies of healthy adult males to establish burst acceleration, turning and true maximum ground speed.
2. Validated bite-force measurement across sex/size classes with tooth contact geometry before any PSI estimate.
3. Standardized male shoulder-height and head-body measurements across Andean populations.
4. Quantify adult claw curvature, exposed length and substrate traction.
5. Compare climbing performance with black and sun bears using standardized substrate trials.
6. Expand wild longevity estimates through long-term photo-ID, genetic and mark-recapture datasets.
