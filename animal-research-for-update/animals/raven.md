# Raven

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** Raven
- **Scientific name:** *Corvus corax* Linnaeus, 1758, Common Raven.
- **Status:** living; IUCN/NatureServe treatment reported by Alaska Department of Fish and Game is Least Concern / G5.
- **Exact animal represented:** Common Raven, not American Crow or another *Corvus* species.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime **adult male, 1.3 kg**, representing a large northern bird without using an exceptional maximum. Alaska Department of Fish and Game gives 1.2 kg and 63 cm as a species profile value; historical measured Alaskan adult males include roughly 1.36 to 1.59 kg in a small sample. ADW gives 0.689 to 1.625 kg and up to 69 cm across the species.
- **Dimorphism and population variation:** sexes look similar, but females may be smaller. Body size varies geographically, with northern/cold-region birds generally larger. Because the male is modestly larger and territorial, the canonical fighter is male. This is not a record-sized raven.

## 3. Weapons, defenses and combat mechanics
### Weapons
The raven's main weapon is its large, strong bill, backed by strong feet and claws used for perching, handling and close grappling. The bill can peck, jab, pull, tear carrion and manipulate objects. Ravens also prey on eggs, nestlings, small vertebrates and invertebrates. Reports from Protection Island document digging into auklet burrows, carrying chicks, attacking an adult auklet and feeding on a freshly killed adult. These observations show genuine predatory competence but do not justify raptor-level weapon scores.

### Offensive mechanics
A raven's safest attack pattern is mobile harassment: approach from the air, peck or grab opportunistically, disengage and reposition. Against small prey it can pin or seize with bill and feet. Against a larger opponent it relies on repeated angles, distraction and escape rather than force-on-force control. Its roughly 1.3 kg mass places a hard ceiling on absolute damage potential.

### Defenses
There is no armor, thick hide or major passive protection. Defense comes from flight, vigilance, rapid disengagement and intelligent risk assessment. ADW notes that adult ravens are rarely observed being preyed upon and vigorously chase predators from nests, but large raptors and owls remain credible threats.

### Locomotion and agility
Ravens fly, soar, glide, roll and maneuver with a long wedge-shaped tail and broad wings. Ground locomotion is competent but not the battle advantage. Their ability to change altitude and attack angle gives them very high maneuverability against terrestrial opponents, while aerial specialists such as falcons still exceed them in high-speed pursuit.

### Endurance
GPS tracking of 10 nonbreeding ravens in the Eastern Alps recorded movements up to 40 km/h, more than 160 km in a day and more than 11,000 km over 20 months. Alaska wildlife guidance also notes nonbreeders may travel 30-40 miles daily between roosts and feeding areas. These are strong evidence for sustained ranging capacity, though not proof of continuous maximum-intensity fighting.

### Senses
Vision is the principal long-range battle sense, supplemented by hearing and sophisticated visual attention to competitors. Experimental ravens follow gaze and can reposition to follow a human's line of sight around an obstacle. Their senses score highly for visually mediated tactical awareness, but no extraordinary nocturnal, olfactory or infrared system is claimed.

### Intelligence and tactics
Raven cognition is one of the strongest evidence bases in the roster. In a *Science* experiment, ravens planned for tool use and bartering with delays up to 17 hours, showed self-control and considered future temporal distance. In cooperative experiments, untrained ravens spontaneously solved a loose-string cooperation task, with success strongly related to social tolerance. Caching experiments show they change cache placement and timing when competitors are present. Pilferers alter behavior according to whether the nearby dominant individual is the cache owner, and one experimental case documented diversion of a dominant competitor toward unrewarded locations. These results justify elite Intelligence and Tactics without inflating physical Attack.

### Fighting, predation and social behavior
Breeding pairs defend territories. Nonbreeders can gather at rich food sources and communal roosts. Ravens mob or chase threats, compete intensely at carcasses, steal food, cache resources and pilfer caches. Cooperation is conditional rather than automatic. The individual ABS fighter receives cognitive flexibility, not a fictional flock multiplier.

## 4. Environmental strengths, weaknesses and matchups
**Strengths:** open airspace, cliffs, forest edges, mountains, tundra, coastlines, farms and human-modified landscapes all provide useful vantage points or food opportunities. Flight and intelligence make terrain exploitation unusually flexible.

**Weaknesses:** extremely low absolute mass, no armor, no venom, no raptorial killing talons, and a bill that is dangerous only at close range. If seized by a much larger predator, durability is poor. Confined spaces remove much of its aerial advantage.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** tiny terrestrial animals, vulnerable eggs/nestlings, small prey it can repeatedly peck or seize, and slow ground opponents unable to contest the air.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized birds where maneuverability, bill access and tactics matter.
- **Bad:** falcons/eagles/large owls with superior killing feet, agile arboreal predators, and virtually all medium-to-large mammals if forced into direct contact.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities
1. **Cache Counterintelligence**: ravens alter caching and pilfering behavior according to observers and competitors, delay approaches when the cache owner is present, and can conceal or redirect behavior. In resource-based encounters this supports deception, memory and opponent-specific decision making.
2. **Three-Dimensional Harrier**: controlled flight lets a raven approach, peck, disengage and re-enter from changing elevations and angles, making it difficult for slow terrestrial opponents to force contact.

## 8. Exactly two unique traits
1. **Future Planner**: controlled experiments show ravens can save tools or barter tokens for future use after substantial delays and can reject an immediate lesser reward for a better later outcome.
2. **Wedge-Tail Flight Control**: the long graduated wedge-shaped tail is a distinctive common-raven feature that supports precise aerial control and separates it morphologically from many crow species.

## 9. Ecology and expanded profile
### Habitat and range
The Common Raven occupies much of the Northern Hemisphere across tundra, mountains, forest edges, coasts, deserts, agricultural land and cities. Alaska Department of Fish and Game describes it as a year-round resident throughout most of Alaska and notes broad Northern Hemisphere distribution.

### Diet and ecology
Ravens are opportunistic omnivores and scavengers. ADW lists carrion, arthropods, amphibians, reptiles, small mammals, birds, eggs, grains, acorns, fruits and buds. They contribute to carrion removal but can also prey on conservation-sensitive eggs and young. USGS documented repeated food theft from a Golden Eagle nest.

### Social structure
Breeding birds commonly occur as territorial pairs. Nonbreeders aggregate at concentrated food sources and communal roosts. Social relationships matter: experimental cooperation depends strongly on tolerance and previous reward outcomes.

### Reproduction and life history
ADW reports annual breeding, usually 3-7 eggs, incubation around 20-25 days, fledging at roughly 5-7 weeks and sexual maturity near three years. Females incubate, while both parents provision and defend young.

### Conservation and human interaction
The species is widespread and currently treated as Least Concern/G5. Human food sources can increase raven abundance locally. This adaptability can create conflict where ravens prey on threatened wildlife or exploit refuse and livestock-associated resources.

### Major adaptations
- large robust bill for a passerine
- powerful, highly controlled flight with wedge-shaped tail
- scatter-hoarding and spatial memory
- advanced social cognition and competitor tracking
- flexible omnivory/scavenging
- broad climatic and habitat tolerance

### Genuine fun facts
- Common Ravens are among the largest passerines.
- GPS-tagged nonbreeders traveled more than 160 km in a single day in one Eastern Alps study.
- Experimental ravens planned for tool use and bartering as far as 17 hours ahead.
- Ravens spontaneously cooperated in a loose-string task without training.
- They change caching tactics when competitors can watch them and routinely pilfer observed caches.
- A USGS-supported study documented ravens repeatedly stealing prey from a Golden Eagle nest.
- Young ravens engage heavily in object play, and 2025-2026 research continues to investigate how play and early social environment shape their development.

### Concise site summary
The Common Raven is a roughly 1.3 kg aerial generalist whose physical weapons are modest but whose flight control, endurance and cognition are exceptional. It wins by seeing problems early, changing tactics, exploiting terrain and refusing bad contact rather than overpowering larger animals.

### Rich narrative profile
A raven is an important test of ABS's absolute-scale discipline. It is one of the most cognitively impressive animals in the roster, but that does not make a 1.3 kg bird physically powerful. Its bill can tear carrion, raid nests and injure small prey, yet a medium mammal or raptor with specialized killing weapons has an enormous force and durability advantage. Attack therefore stays low.

What the raven does possess is options. It can move through three-dimensional space, cover enormous distances, inspect a threat from above, wait, cache resources, remember social context and alter its behavior around particular competitors. Experimental work goes beyond anecdotes: ravens plan for future tool use and barter, cooperate conditionally, alter caches under observation and selectively change pilfering behavior around cache owners. This gives the raven one of the roster's strongest Intelligence/Tactics profiles.

The resulting matchup identity is evasive and opportunistic. Against something small, the raven can become a predator. Against something slow and terrestrial, it can harass while avoiding contact. Against an eagle, owl, cat or other opponent able to seize it, the physical mismatch becomes decisive. ABS should preserve both truths at once: elite cognition and aerial control, low absolute fighting power.

## 12. Confidence, conflicts and cross-animal normalization
- **Mass/length:** high confidence in broad range; medium-high confidence in 1.3 kg northern male canonical choice because geographic variation is substantial.
- **Speed:** medium confidence. 11.1 m/s is directly derived from 40 km/h GPS movement, but it is not a maximum-flight-performance experiment.
- **Lifespan:** medium confidence. 21 years is an agency longevity value, not a population mean. Wild and captive longevity sources differ substantially.
- **Bite PSI:** deliberately unresolved at 0.0. No force-to-pressure invention.
- **Intelligence:** very high confidence that the species belongs near the roster's cognitive elite. Multiple independent controlled paradigms support planning, cooperation, gaze processing and cache strategy.
- **Attack normalization:** 17.0 keeps Raven far below Raccoon (30.0), Quoll (32.0), Jaguar (68.0) and giant constrictors despite impressive cognition. This prevents intelligence from leaking into raw damage.
- **Agility normalization:** 89.0 is high because three-dimensional maneuvering is genuinely exceptional, not because of top speed. It sits around elite mobile specialists while remaining conceptually separate from falcon-style velocity.
- **Stamina normalization:** 87.0 is supported by direct long-distance movement evidence and is comparable to other high-endurance flyers, but below Pronghorn's 96.0 terrestrial endurance anchor.
- **Intelligence normalization:** 95.0 exceeds Raccoon's 76.0 and Jaguar's 70.0 because the raven has unusually rich experimental evidence for future planning and social strategy. This is not copied from the live site's 94.0 placeholder; it is independently supported.
- **Special normalization:** 67.0 remains moderate-high rather than elite because advanced cognition is already represented in Intelligence and ordinary flight is represented in Agility. The score captures the matchup effect of cache/social counter-strategy and aerial access without triple-counting them.
