# Kudu — Greater Kudu (*Tragelaphus strepsiceros*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** Kudu
- **Resolved taxon:** Greater kudu, *Tragelaphus strepsiceros* (Pallas, 1766), family Bovidae, tribe Tragelaphini.
- **Living/extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy fully mature **adult male bull**, represented at **230.0 kg**, **145.0 cm shoulder height**, and **220.0 cm head-body length**, with fully developed spiral horns.
- **Why male:** sexual dimorphism is major and combat-relevant. Museum and zoo references place males roughly 190–300+ kg while females are commonly 120–210 kg. Only males carry the long spiral horns used in dominance contests.
- **Population/taxonomic variation:** range-wide sources disagree on subspecies treatment. Recent field work follows the broad *T. strepsiceros* arrangement used by IUCN/SSC rather than splitting regional forms. Body size, coat tone and horn development vary geographically and with age. The 230 kg baseline is representative rather than a 300–315 kg upper-end bull.

### Defensive structures
Greater kudu have no true armor. Their defenses are body size, long legs, vigilance, camouflage, evasive bounding through cover, and the bull's horns as a frontal deterrent. A 230 kg bull can absorb more trauma than a small antelope, but hide and fur should not be scored as armor.

## 4. Combat biology
### Weapons and offensive mechanics
The bull's paired spiral horns are the central weapon. Mature males interlock horns and shove during dominance contests. Potawatomi Zoo describes fights between similar-sized males in which horns are locked and the animals push until dominance is established; ADW likewise notes wild fighting primarily between similarly sized kudu. The long spirals provide reach, hooking and leverage, while a 200+ kg body and muscular neck supply the force.

The geometry has a downside: horns can become entangled. Rarely, two bulls lock so completely that they cannot separate. The horns are therefore powerful control and impact structures, but not simple forward-pointing spears.

### Defense and toughness
Absolute mass gives the bull meaningful durability against small and medium attackers, but it lacks armor. Lions, spotted hyenas and African wild dogs can prey on kudu. Large adult bulls are more difficult targets than cows or calves, yet a predator that reaches the flank, throat or hindquarters can bypass the frontal horn threat.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Greater kudu are tall, long-legged woodland antelope. Their strongest evasive locomotion is not certified straight-line speed but bounding through broken vegetation and clearing obstacles. Safari West reports leaps up to roughly 2.5 m when startled. Their narrow build and long legs help negotiate bush, but the same height and limb length reduce low-center-of-gravity turning compared with cats or smaller antelope.

### Endurance and recovery
They range through dry woodland and can obtain water from food, but available behavioral sources emphasize concealment and obstacle-clearing escape rather than prolonged cursorial pursuit. Stamina is therefore moderate-strong, not elite. No unusual regenerative or rapid recovery mechanism is documented.

### Senses
Large mobile ears are a conspicuous adaptation. MNHN emphasizes hearing, and Safari West describes extremely sensitive ears. Vision and olfaction also support predator detection. The striped coat and freeze response exploit broken woodland light, combining sensory vigilance with concealment rather than creating a supernatural camouflage effect.

### Intelligence and tactics
Kudu use assessment and ritualization in male competition. Dominance is often settled by posture and lateral display rather than maximal combat, while evenly matched bulls may escalate to horn locking. This supports moderate tactical assessment. It does not justify primate/corvid-level Intelligence.

### Fighting, predation and predator defense
Kudu are herbivores and do not hunt. Male aggression is concentrated in reproductive/dominance contexts. Predator defense centers on early detection, freezing/camouflage, alarm barking, sudden bounding escape, obstacle clearance, and, when forced, the bull's horns. ADW describes them as generally non-aggressive, with wild fights mainly between matched males.

### Environmental strengths
- **Woodland/thicket:** strongest overall environment because stripes, stillness, hearing and obstacle-clearing escape work together.
- **Broken/hilly ground:** long legs and jumping help reposition and break pursuit.
- **Open flat ground:** mass and horns remain useful, but concealment and obstacle escape lose value.
- **Confined space:** long horns can become awkward and reduce turning options.
- **Deep water/unstable footing:** removes the terrestrial leverage on which horn combat depends.

### Major weaknesses
- No armor and no carnivore-style killing bite or claws.
- Long horns require useful head orientation and room to deploy.
- Tall build is less compact and grappling-resistant than a similarly massive cat or hyena.
- Much predator defense is avoidance rather than standing combat.
- Horn entanglement is a real failure mode in conspecific fights.
- Reported speed numbers are inconsistent and should not be used to inflate Agility.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Favored:** smaller unarmored terrestrial opponents forced into a frontal approach.
- **Competitive:** similarly sized ungulates where horn reach and body mass control the engagement.
- **Unfavored:** large grappling carnivores that can reach the flank, armored opponents, and substantially heavier bovids/megafauna.
- **Terrain swing:** dense woodland improves escape and ambush detection, while tight enclosed spaces can make the long horns cumbersome.

## 6. Proposed six headline ABS ratings
- **Attack: 58.0** — a 230 kg bull with 1.2 m-class spiral horns has serious reach, leverage and impact potential, but lacks the more lethal tissue-damaging toolkit of large predators or the mass of heavyweight bovids.
- **Defense: 43.0** — body mass, vigilance and horns help, but there is no armor and large carnivores can kill kudu.
- **Agility: 72.0** — impressive obstacle-clearing and woodland bounding for its size, deliberately separated from disputed maximum-speed claims.
- **Stamina: 60.0** — solid dry-country mobility and sustained daily activity, but not an elite pursuit specialist.
- **Intelligence: 49.0** — ritualized assessment and flexible predator avoidance support moderate tactical cognition without evidence for exceptional problem solving.
- **Special: 61.0** — the spiral-horn fighting system plus concealment/escape specialization creates meaningful matchup effects without exotic biochemical weaponry.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and exactly two unique traits
### Special abilities
1. **Spiral-Horn Lock** — paired corkscrew horns let a mature bull hook, bind, shove and redirect a rival's head during frontal contests, turning horn geometry into leverage as well as impact.
2. **Brush-Break Bound** — long legs and powerful bounding allow rapid obstacle clearance through broken woodland, helping the kudu disengage from pursuers that must slow or detour.

### Unique traits
1. **Grey-Ghost Freeze** — vertical white stripes, woodland shade and a tendency to stand still when alarmed can break up the outline before the animal commits to flight.
2. **Ears Before Eyes** — oversized mobile ears provide strong acoustic vigilance, allowing a concealed kudu to detect danger while minimizing unnecessary movement.

## 8. Expanded profile
### Habitat and range
Greater kudu occupy eastern and southern Africa in woodland savanna, bushland, thickets, open forest, hills and wooded drainage systems. Columbus Zoo and MNHN both emphasize woodland/savanna habitat; current conservation references treat the species as widespread enough for Least Concern status.

### Diet and ecology
They are selective herbivores consuming leaves, herbs, vines, flowers, fruit and some new grass. ADW notes that they can persist in waterless regions and may use plant moisture during dry periods. Their tall browsing height lets them exploit vegetation layers unavailable to many smaller antelope.

### Social structure
Females and offspring form small herds, commonly around 6–10 animals, while mature bulls are often solitary or occur in loose bachelor groups. Males join female groups primarily around breeding. This social system matters for vigilance but does not multiply a canonical individual's combat score.

### Reproduction and life history
Gestation is about 7–9 months, generally producing one calf. Calves are hidden in vegetation early in life. San Diego Zoo notes that male spiral-horned antelope become solitary around four to five years as they mature, while horn development continues for years. Full fighting phenotype therefore arrives well after basic sexual maturity.

### Conservation
The Greater Kudu is currently listed as **Least Concern** in modern conservation summaries. Local populations nevertheless face hunting and habitat conversion. Horns are prized as trophies and have also been used culturally as instruments and containers.

### Adaptations
- Extreme male-biased horn development for display and combat.
- Long legs and narrow build for moving through woodland and clearing obstacles.
- Large mobile ears for acoustic vigilance.
- Striped coat and freeze behavior for concealment in broken light.
- Ability to use diverse browse and tolerate seasonally dry conditions.

### Human interaction
Greater kudu are hunted for meat and horns, and they are prominent in wildlife tourism and managed zoological collections. Hunting pressure and agricultural habitat conversion can be locally important even though the species remains Least Concern overall.

### Fun facts
- Male horns can reach about **1.8 m** along the curve in institutional accounts.
- Only males carry the spectacular spiral horns.
- Horns may make roughly **2 to 2.5 turns** when fully developed.
- Alarmed kudu can clear obstacles around **2.5 m** high according to Safari West.
- Rarely, fighting bulls can become fatally locked together by their intertwined horns.
- Greater kudu use a loud bark as an alarm call.
- A mature bull can be more than twice the mass of a smaller adult cow depending on population and individual.

### Concise site summary
The Greater Kudu is a massive woodland antelope whose mature bulls combine a roughly 230 kg body with extraordinarily long spiral horns. Those horns are built for locking, shoving and controlling rival males, while huge ears, striped camouflage and powerful obstacle-clearing bounds help the animal avoid predators in dense African bush. Its attack is formidable among antelope, but lack of armor and a primarily evasive anti-predator strategy keep it below large carnivores and heavyweight bovids in all-around combat.

### Narrative profile
A mature Greater Kudu bull is a reach fighter built around leverage. The horns rise from a muscular neck, twist outward and upward, and can exceed a metre along their curve. Against another bull they are not simply stabbing points. The spirals catch, bind and redirect the opponent while both animals drive forward with hundreds of kilograms of body mass. Much of the time the contest ends before maximal violence: size, posture and lateral display can settle rank, while evenly matched animals escalate into horn locking and shoving.

Outside the rut, the kudu's survival strategy changes completely. It is a woodland escape specialist. Large ears sample the bush for danger, stripes break the torso into bands of shadow and light, and the animal may freeze before exploding into long-legged bounds over obstacles. This is why its ABS profile should not be reduced to a questionable internet sprint number. Its real agility is the ability to place a very large body through broken cover and clear barriers while under pressure. At the same time, the kudu remains unarmored prey for lions, hyenas and wild dogs. Horn reach is dangerous, but it does not erase the vulnerabilities of a tall herbivore when a grappling predator reaches the flank.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Speed:** institutional and tertiary claims range from roughly 60–97 km/h, with no strong measured maximum recovered. `speed_mps` is therefore 0.0 rather than selecting a convenient claim.
- **Mass:** sources variously give bull maxima around 250, 300, 315 or 700 lb. These are compatible with geographic/individual variation and differing use of maxima. The canonical 230 kg bull is intentionally representative, not record-sized.
- **Horn length:** institutional sources range from roughly 1.2 m typical mature length to 1.8 m maximum. The report separates representative mature horns from maxima.
- **Lifespan:** wild averages/medians are much lower than captive maxima. The 10-year canonical field is a conservative wild benchmark, not a claim that individuals cannot live longer.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes
- **Versus Ibex (100 kg, Attack 51.0):** Kudu is more than twice the canonical mass and has longer horns, so Attack 58.0 and Raw Power 55.0 should exceed Ibex. Ibex retains slightly better terrain-generalized Agility on extreme rock because cliff footing is its deeper specialization.
- **Versus Impala (65 kg, Attack 43.0 / Agility 91.0):** Kudu is vastly more powerful and better armed in absolute combat, but Impala remains much more agile as a lighter escape specialist. This prevents size from leaking into Agility.
- **Versus Kangaroo (80 kg, Attack 52.0):** Kudu's greater mass and long horns justify higher Attack, while Kangaroo's elastic hopping system and locomotor endurance justify higher Agility/Stamina.
- **Versus Jaguar (100 kg, Attack 68.0):** despite greater mass, Kudu stays below Jaguar in Attack because the cat has a dedicated killing bite, claws, grappling control and predatory targeting. Body size alone is not Attack.
- **Versus Hyena (75 kg, Attack 61.0 / Stamina 83.0):** Kudu has more mass and reach but a less lethal finishing toolkit and far less pursuit endurance. Its Attack therefore remains slightly lower and Stamina substantially lower.
- No score is based on pound-for-pound performance, disputed sprint claims, or repeated counting of the same horn adaptation.
