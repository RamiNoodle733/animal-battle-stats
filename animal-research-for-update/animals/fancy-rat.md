# Fancy Rat (*Rattus norvegicus domestica*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Fancy rat (pet rat, domestic brown rat)
- **Scientific name:** *Rattus norvegicus* (Berkenhout, 1769), domestic form *Rattus norvegicus domestica*
- **Exact animal represented:** the domesticated brown (Norway) rat kept as a pet, the same subspecies as the laboratory rat. Wild brown-rat biology is included because the fancy rat is the same species and keeps its body plan and senses; the black rat (*Rattus rattus*) is a different species and is excluded.
- **Living / extinct:** Living (domestic form of a wild species).
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult male (buck) of about **0.5 kg**, a typical pet male rather than an obese or record animal.
- **Sex choice:** males are clearly larger. The Merck Veterinary Manual gives adult females **350–450 g** and adult males **450–650 g**.
- **Population variation:** wild brown rats weigh 140–500 g, and breeding wild rats commonly weigh under 300 g; individuals near 800 g are essentially domestic animals, and the heaviest live brown rat on record weighed 822 g. Domestic rats have smaller, sharper faces and smaller brains, hearts and adrenal glands than wild rats, and are much tamer.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The rat's weapons are sharp incisors backed by a strong bite for its size, plus small claws for gripping. Wild brown rats kill poultry, game birds and island wildlife and are omnivorous predators of small animals. Rats fight each other by boxing upright, biting and wrestling. Domestic rats fight differently from wild ones: wild rats almost always flee a lost fight, while caged rats spend long periods in belly-up or boxing positions. Bites can transmit rat-bite fever to people, and an early fancy-rat breeder recorded nearly dying from infected bites.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Rats climb well, including slim round metal poles to reach bird feeders, swim on the surface and underwater, dig burrows and move confidently through dark, complex tunnel and sewer networks. Domestic rats are less capable diggers and swimmers than wild rats.

### Endurance
Wild rats make long nightly foraging trips, and ADW describes them as excellent swimmers. Endurance is good for a small mammal but not exceptional.

### Senses
Acute hearing extending well into ultrasound (a behavioral audiogram was measured from 250 Hz to 70 kHz, identical in hooded and albino rats), a very highly developed sense of smell, and sensitive whiskers. Vision is poor, about 20/600 in pigmented rats and worse in pink-eyed albinos.

### Intelligence and tactics
Rats are among the most studied learners in science. They remember routes through complex sewer and burrow systems, learn mazes quickly, and show social learning, as when colonies on Italy's Po River learned to dive for mussels. Domestic rats learn faster than wild ones and have lower resting stress-hormone levels. A 2007 study suggested metacognition in rats, although later analysis argued simpler learning could explain the results. Animal trainers rate them among the easiest animals to train.

### Sociality
Brown rats live in large hierarchical groups in burrows, cellars and sewers. Young rats play-fight by chasing, tumbling and boxing, and tickled rats produce 50 kHz "laughter" chirps.

### Fighting and aggression
Dominance hierarchies are enforced by boxing and biting. Pet rats are generally gentle with people, but males may fight each other and any rat bites when frightened or hurt.

### Predator defense
Hiding, flight through burrows and cover, and group vigilance. A study of a New York City rat colony found feral cats killed only three rats and stalked them 20 times in five months, while city rats often weigh over 300 g, heavy enough to deter most cats.

### Environmental strengths
- Cluttered, three-dimensional human environments with pipes, walls and tunnels.
- Darkness, where smell, hearing and whiskers matter more than vision.
- Water crossings, where it swims well.

### Weaknesses
- Tiny absolute mass and reach.
- No armor.
- Poor vision.
- Domestic rats have lost some of the wild rat's digging and swimming ability and fear.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** mice, insects, small birds and eggs, small lizards and animals well under its weight.
- **Mixed:** house cats and small mustelids, where a large rat's bite and weight make it a costly target.
- **Bad:** ferrets, terriers, foxes, owls and hawks, snakes, and every medium or large predator.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Urban Infiltrator** — climbs slim metal poles, swims on the surface and underwater, and remembers routes through dark sewer and burrow networks, so it can reach, or escape through, places most predators cannot follow.
2. **Ultrasonic Channel** — hears well into ultrasound and communicates with calls too high for human ears, including the 50 kHz chirps rats make when played with or tickled; in a matchup this mainly aids awareness, not damage.

### Unique traits
1. **Tame Twin of the City Rat** — the fancy rat is the same species as the wild brown rat but is tamer, learns faster, has lower stress-hormone levels and smaller organs, and fights by boxing rather than fleeing.
2. **Too Big for Most Cats** — city brown rats often exceed 300 g, and a New York study recorded feral cats killing only three rats in five months, which is why cats make poor rat control.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Fancy rats live in homes worldwide. Their wild counterpart, the brown rat, lives almost everywhere people do, in cities, sewers, farms, docks, riverbanks and on islands, often near water.

### Diet and ecology
Omnivorous: grain, seeds, fruit, vegetables, meat, insects, eggs and human food waste. Wild brown rats disperse seeds and aerate soil with their burrows, but they also kill poultry and game birds and have driven many island species toward extinction. They carry diseases and parasites that affect people and livestock.

### Social structure
Large hierarchical colonies. Dominant rats outcompete subordinates for food and mates; young rats play-fight constantly. Pet rats are best kept in same-sex groups.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeding is rapid and can happen year-round; a female can raise successive litters without changing her food intake, and can extend pregnancy by over two weeks when food is short. Domestic rats mature and breed earlier than wild ones. Pet rats live about two to three years; most wild rats die within a year.

### Conservation
**Domesticated** as the fancy rat. The wild brown rat is Least Concern and is itself one of the world's most damaging invasive mammals.

### Special features
- Continuously growing incisors.
- Hearing extending well into ultrasound.
- Very highly developed sense of smell.
- Long, sensitive whiskers used for navigation in darkness.
- Strong climbing and swimming ability.

### Human interaction
The fancy rat began with the rat-catchers of 18th- and 19th-century Europe, who trapped rats for the blood sport of rat-baiting; organized rat shows started in England in 1901, and the same species became the laboratory rat. Today fancy rats are bred in many colors, coat types and ear shapes (such as "Dumbo" rats). Trainers regard them as one of the easiest animals to train. Wild rats remain major pests and disease carriers.

### Genuine fun facts
- Rats make ultrasonic 50 kHz chirps when tickled, which researchers compare to laughter.
- Researchers bred rats for high and low "tickle laughter" in just four generations.
- Brown rats on Italy's Po River learned from each other to dive for mussels.
- The heaviest live brown rat on record weighed 822 g.
- In a New York City study, feral cats killed only three rats in five months of filming.
- Pet rats see poorly, about 20/600 vision in pigmented rats, but hear far into ultrasound.
- Organized rat showing began in 1901, when Mary Douglas brought her pet rats to a National Mouse Club show in Aylesbury, England.

### Concise site-ready summary
The fancy rat is the tame, domestic form of the brown rat: a 0.5 kg climber, swimmer and problem-solver with sharp incisors, keen ears and a strong bite for its size. Clever and resilient, it is a real threat to mice and small birds but a lightweight on the absolute scale, with no armor and little reach.

### Rich narrative profile
The fancy rat is the brown rat that learned to live on people's shoulders. It shares its body plan with the wild city rat: a compact, long-tailed rodent with sharp, ever-growing incisors, a keen nose, whiskers that map the dark, and hearing that extends far into ultrasound. It climbs slim poles, swims well and remembers complex routes, and it is one of the most studied learners in science.

In a fight, a rat bites fast and hard for its size and grips with its claws, and wild rats kill poultry, eggs and small wildlife. Domestication has softened its temper: pet rats are tame, box and wrestle instead of fleeing, and rarely bite people unless frightened. On the absolute ABS scale, however, even a large 0.5 kg male has very little power or protection. It scores low in Attack and Defense but high in Agility and Intelligence.

Its wild counterpart shows the species' resilience. Brown rats thrive in cities worldwide, often grow too big for cats to tackle, and have spread to islands where they threaten native animals. The pet version keeps the same senses and intelligence in a gentler package.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** wild rats are much lighter than pet males; canonical 0.5 kg represents the pet male.
- **Length:** tail length is given only as "slightly shorter" than the body; canonical 25.0 cm is head-body only.
- **Bite:** no absolute measurement; viral pressure figures are rejected.
- **Metacognition:** reported in 2007 but disputed; not used to inflate Intelligence.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Naked Mole Rat (0.057 kg; A14 D24 Ag72 I76 Sp96):** the mole-rat keeps a fractionally higher Attack because its bite is directly measured and exceptional, and far higher Special for anoxia tolerance. The rat is heavier and more agile (78.0 vs 72.0) and close in Intelligence (72.0 vs 76.0).
- **Versus Ferret (1.5 kg; A17 D10 Ag89 I67):** the ferret is three times heavier and a specialist rodent hunter, so it stays above in Attack, Defense and Agility; the rat edges it in Intelligence.
- **Versus Hedgehog (1.0 kg; A11 D43):** the rat bites harder and is far more agile and intelligent, but the spined hedgehog keeps much higher Defense.
- **Versus House Cat (5 kg; A24 D16):** a cat is ten times heavier; the rat stays far below in Attack and Defense, reflecting that most cats avoid large rats but a committed cat still wins.
- **Anti-inflation check:** intelligence is high but capped below corvids and primates; the rat's reputation as a pest is not scored as combat power.
