# African Elephant Research Overhaul

## Research status

- **ABS roster name:** African Elephant
- **Canonical taxon for this profile:** African savanna elephant / African bush elephant, *Loxodonta africana*
- **Living/extinct:** Living
- **Canonical combat specimen:** Healthy mature adult bull, using a representative large male rather than a record-sized individual
- **Research confidence:** High for taxonomy, mass, shoulder height, ecology, social behavior, tusk biology, and broad locomotor performance; moderate for a single canonical body length and combat stamina; low for any precise bite-force PSI because elephants are not meaningfully bite-based combatants and no defensible species-standard PSI value was found

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

The ABS entry represents *Loxodonta africana*, the African savanna/bush elephant, not the smaller African forest elephant *Loxodonta cyclotis*. Canonical combat specimen is a healthy mature bull because males are materially larger and more heavily armed. Adult-male references span roughly 4,500–6,100 kg and about 3.2–4.0 m shoulder height. Historical growth work estimated mean maximum male mass around 5,450 kg and asymptotic male shoulder heights around 3.0–3.17 m in two Ugandan populations, demonstrating substantial geographic and methodological variation. The canonical animal is not a rare super-tusker.

## 2. Physical measurements

- **Mass:** recommended **5,500.0 kg**, within authoritative adult-male ranges and close to classic population growth estimates.
- **Shoulder height:** **360.0 cm**, a large mature bull without using a record specimen.
- **Length:** **660.0 cm**, within institutional 6.0–7.25 m descriptions; conventions vary around trunk/tail inclusion.
- **Speed:** **6.7 m/s** (~24 km/h). Treat as a conservative natural-history maximum, not a precision sprint trial.
- **Lifespan:** **65.0 years** as a representative long-lived wild value; literature commonly places upper life around 60–70 years.
- **Bite force:** **0.0 PSI** in the ABS field because no defensible species-standard bite-pressure measurement was located and biting is not the principal weapon.
- **Tusks:** continuously growing upper incisors. Extreme tusks can be several metres long, but representative contemporary tusks are smaller and poaching has measurably reduced tusk size in some populations. A 2015 peer-reviewed study found mean tusk length declines of about 21% in contemporary males of comparable stature in the studied population.
- **Skin:** up to roughly 30 mm over much of the body in ADW descriptions. Thick skin and enormous body depth confer protection but do not constitute armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
Tusks provide penetrating reach, leverage, shoving and stabbing potential. The multi-tonne head, shoulders and body provide extraordinary absolute force, while the feet can inflict crushing trauma. The trunk is a dexterous manipulator and sensory organ that can push, pull and reposition at close range, but it should not be fictionalized as the main killing weapon. Bull contests demonstrate real same-size combat mechanics involving assessment, pushing, tusk contact and escalation.

### Defense and durability
The elephant's primary defense is scale: huge body depth, robust skeleton, thick skin, columnar limbs and a dangerous frontal weapon system. It lacks a shell or heavy dermal armor. Eyes, trunk, mouth, joints and lower limbs remain vulnerable.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Elephants move surprisingly quickly for their mass and can traverse uneven terrain, but acceleration, fine turning and evasive repositioning remain limited compared with smaller terrestrial animals. Top speed must not be mistaken for agility.

### Endurance
Savanna elephants sustain long periods of walking and foraging in hot environments. Their huge thermal load prevents treating maximum-output combat endurance as unlimited. Stamina is high, not a cursorial ceiling.

### Senses and intelligence
Olfaction, low-frequency hearing, tactile trunk sensing, memory, individual recognition, learning and complex communication are major strengths. Social cognition is sophisticated, although adult bulls often range independently or in looser male associations than female family groups.

### Hunting, fighting and predator defense
The species is herbivorous. Combat capability comes from intraspecific competition and threat defense rather than hunting. Prime adult bulls have essentially no routine nonhuman predator, while calves and compromised animals face more risk. Musth intensifies male competitive behavior, but the canonical profile should not assume permanent peak-musth aggression.

### Environmental strengths
Open savanna and woodland provide room for mass-backed movement and excellent sensory awareness. Elephants can travel long distances and swim. Vegetation and terrain can be physically manipulated.

### Environmental limitations
Tight spaces reduce charge lanes; deep water removes terrestrial footing advantages; extreme slopes and fragile terrain constrain a multi-tonne body; aerial opponents cannot be pursued in three dimensions.

### Weaknesses
Exposed eyes/trunk, relatively low fine agility, heat load at maximal exertion, vulnerability of joints/feet, and difficulty precisely attacking tiny evasive targets.

### Matchup archetypes
Strong against nearly all much-smaller terrestrial animals and close-range predators that must accept frontal contact. Less favorable against substantially larger giants, specialized giant predators of comparable mass, inaccessible aerial opponents, or aquatic giants in deep water.

1. **Tusker Drive** — Long ivory incisors combine with multi-tonne forward force for stabbing, levering and displacement.
2. **Infrasonic Awareness** — Exceptional low-frequency hearing, chemical sensing and trunk-based sampling improve detection and assessment over large distances.

1. **Living Land Giant** — The largest living terrestrial animal combines huge body depth with a weaponized head and feet.
2. **Muscular Hydrostat** — The trunk provides unusually precise grasping, smelling and tactile manipulation without bones.

## 8. Expanded profile content

### Habitat and range
Savannas, grasslands, woodlands, scrub and semi-arid habitats across sub-Saharan Africa. The species remains broadly distributed but fragmented and pressured by habitat loss and poaching.

### Diet and ecology
Herbivorous browser/grazer consuming grasses, leaves, bark, roots, fruits and woody vegetation. Elephants are major ecosystem engineers, opening vegetation, dispersing seeds, digging for water and physically restructuring habitat.

### Social structure
Females and young form matriarchal family societies with rich long-term relationships. Mature males increasingly live independently or in male associations and interact with female groups during reproductive periods.

### Life history
Gestation is about 22 months, among the longest known in mammals. Reproduction is slow and calves receive prolonged maternal care. Bulls continue growing for decades.

### Conservation
The African savanna elephant is listed **Endangered** by IUCN. Major threats include ivory poaching, habitat conversion and human-elephant conflict.

### Adaptations
Large heat-dissipating ears, highly dexterous trunk, continuously growing tusks, huge grinding molars replaced sequentially, thick skin, padded feet, sophisticated acoustic/chemical communication and remarkable memory.

### Human interaction
Elephants are culturally and ecologically important but can be dangerous around settlements and crops. Ivory demand has caused intense selective pressure and population losses. Conflict mitigation and landscape connectivity are central conservation issues.

### Strong fun facts
- It is the largest living land animal.
- A bull can exceed five tonnes without being a record specimen.
- Tusks are incisors, not canine teeth.
- The trunk combines the nose and upper lip into an extraordinarily dexterous muscular hydrostat.
- Poaching has measurably shifted tusk size in studied populations.
- Elephant communication includes very low-frequency calls that can travel long distances.
- Pregnancy lasts roughly 22 months.

### Concise site-ready summary
The African savanna elephant is the living terrestrial heavyweight: a multi-tonne bull armed with tusks, crushing mass and exceptional sensory intelligence. It is difficult to disable and devastating at close range, but its immense size limits fine agility and it lacks exotic mechanisms such as venom or armor.

### Detailed narrative profile
A prime African savanna elephant bull wins terrestrial matchups primarily through scale that remains biologically usable. The animal is not merely heavy. Its tusks project a penetrating weapon system in front of a massive head and shoulder complex, its feet support and can weaponize several tonnes of body mass, and its trunk adds tactile and olfactory precision. The same body that makes the elephant difficult to injure also limits tight evasive movement, so the profile should not inflate Agility merely because elephants can move quickly. Cognitively, elephants bring memory, assessment and sophisticated communication that distinguish them from similarly massive herbivores. Their battle profile is therefore an unusual combination of extreme terrestrial force and defense, high intelligence and senses, moderate agility, and no magical invulnerability.

### Confidence and caveats
Mass/height and taxon are high confidence. Length and a single maximum speed are moderate because measurement conventions and field reporting vary. Bite PSI intentionally remains absent/0.0. Tusk size varies strongly with sex, age, genetics, geography and poaching history, so record tusks must never be canonicalized.

## 10. Cross-animal calibration notes

African Elephant remains far above Bison (900 kg; Attack 72.0, Defense 68.0) in absolute raw power, durability and attack reach. It should also sit far above Bighorn Sheep and Bactrian Camel in those physical dimensions. Its Agility stays well below Bald Eagle, Barn Owl, small carnivores and mountain specialists despite respectable speed. Intelligence should remain among the roster's upper tier but subject to later normalization against great apes, cetaceans, corvids and parrots. Attack 92.0 and Defense 94.0 are provisional terrestrial-megafauna anchors and must later be checked against rhinoceroses, hippopotamus, large whales and extinct giants.
