# Markhor (*Capra falconeri*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Markhor (screw-horned goat)
- **Scientific name:** *Capra falconeri* (Wagner, 1839)
- **Exact animal represented:** the wild markhor of the mountains of Pakistan, Afghanistan, India, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan, across its recognized subspecies (such as Astor, Kashmir, Bukharan and Suleiman markhor). Domestic goats are not represented.
- **Living / extinct:** Living.
- **Status:** Living; Pakistan's national animal.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult male (buck) of about **100 kg** and **100 cm** at the shoulder, with fully developed corkscrew horns of about **120 cm** along the curve. A representative prime male, not a trophy-record animal.
- **Sex choice:** males weigh about 80–110 kg and females only about 32–50 kg, so males are roughly twice as heavy. Only males carry the huge spiral horns, and males are the sex that fights during the rut.
- **Population variation:** horn shape varies between subspecies, from open flaring spirals to tight straight corkscrews. Body size and horn length also vary with age; the number of turns grows as a male ages.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The markhor's weapons are the male's long corkscrew horns, backed by a 100 kg body. During the rut, rival males lunge at each other, lock horns and shove and twist, each trying to push the other off balance on steep ground. The horns are built for this wrestling rather than stabbing, but a lunge or horn sweep can still injure a predator or rival. Hooves give strong, precise footing for pushing on slopes.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Markhor live on steep, rocky slopes and cliffs, with a strong preference for steep terrain; in Chitral Gol National Park they were found at a mean slope of about 33 degrees. They move with sure-footed jumps and balance on narrow ledges, and they are widely reported to climb into trees to browse. On flat ground they are ordinary runners; their escape is vertical.

### Endurance
Markhor range over a wide altitude band, from scrub-covered foothills to high mountain slopes, and move up and down the slopes with the seasons while browsing and grazing for long hours. They are well suited to steep, high terrain, though no measured endurance data were found.

### Senses
Sharp eyesight and a keen sense of smell help markhor spot snow leopards and wolves from a distance and retreat to cliffs before an attack.

### Intelligence and tactics
Behavior is typical of wild goats: herd vigilance, retreat into steep cliffs, and dominance contests among males. No evidence of unusual problem solving.

### Sociality and predator defense
Females and young form herds; adult males live alone or in bachelor groups for much of the year and join the females for the rut in late autumn and early winter. The main natural predators are snow leopards and wolves. The best defense is terrain: few predators can follow a markhor onto a cliff face.

### Fighting and aggression
Rut fights are intense but ritualized, decided by pushing and balance. Markhor avoid predators rather than confront them, turning to fight only when cornered.

### Environmental strengths
- Steep cliffs and rocky slopes, where footing and balance decide any encounter.
- Mountain forest of oak, pine and juniper, where it can browse and hide.
- Cold, high terrain that tires heavier lowland animals.

### Weaknesses
- Horns are wrestling tools, less suited to stabbing than a mountain goat's short horns.
- No armor; light compared with large bovids.
- Ordinary speed on open, flat ground.
- Prefers retreat to confrontation.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** predators that cannot follow it onto cliffs; similar-sized herbivores on steep ground.
- **Mixed:** snow leopards and other agile mountain predators that can stalk it on slopes.
- **Bad:** large predators on flat ground, heavy bovids, bears and any opponent that can pin it away from cliffs.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Corkscrew Horn Lock** — rival males lunge, lock their spiral horns and shove and twist to throw each other off balance; the same horns and neck strength can be turned on a predator that corners a buck.
2. **Cliff Refuge Escape** — sharp eyes and smell spot snow leopards and wolves early, and the markhor retreats onto steep cliffs and ledges where few predators can follow.

### Unique traits
1. **Spiral-Horned Giant** — mature males carry tightly twisted horns about 80–165 cm long, among the longest horns of any wild goat, with more turns as the animal ages.
2. **Hunt-Funded Comeback** — community-run trophy hunting programs in Pakistan and community conservancies in Tajikistan helped markhor numbers recover enough for the IUCN to move the species to a lower threat category in 2015.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Mountains of Central and South Asia: the Hindu Kush, Karakoram and western Himalaya, and ranges in Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan. Markhor use steep, rocky slopes and cliffs with scrub and open forest of oak, pine and juniper, and tend to avoid the highest ground. In Chitral Gol National Park they occurred at a mean elevation of about 2,800 m.

### Diet and ecology
Markhor both graze and browse, eating grasses and herbs as well as leaves and twigs of shrubs and trees such as oak and juniper, especially in forested areas. They are an important prey species for snow leopards and wolves.

### Social structure
Females and young live in herds; adult males are mostly solitary or in small male groups outside the rut. During the rut, which peaks from about November to January, males join the herds and compete for females.

### Reproduction and life history
Females give birth to their kids months after the winter rut. Males keep growing their horns throughout life, and the number of horn turns rises with age.

### Conservation
Near Threatened on the IUCN Red List since 2015, when improved numbers led to its move down from Endangered; some subspecies and regional populations remain in more serious danger. Pakistan's population has been estimated at over 5,000, with the largest single population in Chitral Gol National Park (estimated at about 2,278 animals in 2021). Tajikistan surveys counted about 1,018 markhor in 2012 and later monitoring showed recovery. Community-based trophy hunting programs, in which most permit revenue goes to local villages that protect the herds, are credited with much of this recovery. Remaining threats include poaching, habitat loss, competition with livestock and disease.

### Special features
- Corkscrew horns up to about 165 cm in males.
- Long ruff of hair on the throat and chest in mature males.
- Sure-footed hooves for cliffs and narrow ledges.
- Mixed grazing and browsing diet across seasons.

### Human interaction
The markhor is Pakistan's national animal. Carefully limited trophy hunting, with high permit fees shared with local communities, is a well-known example of hunting revenue funding conservation. Poaching and livestock competition remain problems in parts of the range.

### Concise site-ready summary
The markhor is a 100 kg wild goat of the Himalaya and Hindu Kush, famous for males' corkscrew horns that can pass 1.5 m. It is a cliff specialist that escapes predators by climbing where they cannot follow, and fights rivals by locking and twisting horns, but it is no heavyweight on the absolute scale.

### Rich narrative profile
A mature markhor buck is one of the most striking animals in the mountains: a big, grizzled goat with a long ruff of hair spilling down its chest and a pair of horns twisted like corkscrews, sometimes more than a metre and a half long. It lives on steep slopes and cliffs from northern Pakistan and Afghanistan into Central Asia, moving between high summer pastures and lower winter slopes and browsing oak, juniper and shrubs when grass is scarce.

Its life centres on terrain. Sharp eyesight and smell let it spot snow leopards and wolves at a distance, and its response is to climb onto rock faces where few predators can follow. In the rut, bucks come down to the female herds and fight by lunging, locking their horns and twisting, each trying to shove the other off balance on the slope. Those contests look dramatic, but they are wrestling matches rather than stabbing duels.

For ABS, the markhor sits beside the Alpine ibex in size and Attack, with slightly higher Agility for its cliff and slope skills, and below the Mountain Goat, which remains the roster's cliff-agility benchmark. Its horns earn a strong Weaponry score, but its mass and lack of armor keep its Attack and Defense firmly in the middle band. Its recovery story, from Endangered to Near Threatened on the back of community conservation, is one of the more hopeful in the mountains.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Conservation status:** some summaries still describe the species as Endangered, reflecting the pre-2015 listing or subspecies-level concern; Near Threatened is used as the current species listing.
- **Shoulder height:** the 65–104 cm male range is wide; 100 cm reflects a mature buck.
- **Tree climbing:** widely reported in popular sources but not verified in a peer-reviewed study in this pass; it is mentioned but not used to raise scores.
- **Speed:** no measured value found.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Ibex (100 kg; A51 D43 Ag71 St66 I51 Sp59):** the same mass class, so Size, Raw Power and Defense match closely. The markhor gets one point more Attack for longer, heavier horns and slightly higher Agility (75.0 vs 71.0) and Special (61.0 vs 59.0) for its documented preference for very steep slopes.
- **Versus Mountain Goat (110 kg; A51 D42 Ag88 Sp78):** the mountain goat remains the roster's cliff-agility benchmark and keeps much higher Agility and Special; its short dagger horns are better stabbing weapons, but the markhor's longer horns and wrestling style put Attack about level.
- **Versus Bighorn Sheep (110 kg; A58 D48):** the bighorn keeps higher Attack and Defense because its massive curled horns and reinforced skull are built for high-speed head-on impacts.
- **Versus Snow Leopard (50 kg; A61):** a main natural predator; the snow leopard keeps higher Attack as a dedicated killer, while the markhor relies on terrain.
- **Anti-inflation check:** spectacular horns earn Weaponry, not an Attack jump above the bighorn.
