# Goliath Tigerfish (*Hydrocynus goliath*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Goliath tigerfish (giant tigerfish)
- **Scientific name:** *Hydrocynus goliath* Boulenger, 1898
- **Exact animal represented:** the goliath tigerfish of the Congo River basin and Lake Tanganyika, the largest of the African tigerfishes. Not the smaller African tigerfish (*Hydrocynus vittatus*), which is used here only where noted as a comparison species within the same genus.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy large adult, **25 kg** and **110 cm** long, sex unspecified.
- **Sex choice:** no reliable data on size differences between the sexes were found in this run, so no sex is assumed.
- **Population notes:** FishBase gives a maximum length of 133 cm and a maximum published weight of 50 kg. Angler and popular accounts claim fish of 150 cm, 70 kg and even 2 m, but these are not scientifically verified. Genetic work in the region (2011) suggests there may be more tigerfish species in the Congo basin than traditionally recognised, so "goliath tigerfish" records may mix forms.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The goliath tigerfish's weapon is a mouth full of 32 large, interlocking, dagger-like teeth, each up to about 2.5 cm, exposed along the jaw edges. They are built to seize and slice fish, and popular accounts describe tigerfish removing chunks from larger fish. The fish is a strict piscivore, eating smaller fish, including smaller tigerfish and cichlids such as tilapia. Unlike a piranha, it does not rely on a shoal; unlike a barracuda, it is deep-bodied and powerful in strong currents.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Goliath tigerfish live in fast, highly oxygenated channels of the Congo River and in open waters of Lake Tanganyika. Holding position and striking in turbulent water requires strong, controlled swimming and quick turns. The related African tigerfish is famous for its speed: in South Africa's Schroda Dam, O'Brien et al. (2014) filmed tigerfish launching out of the water to catch barn swallows in flight, the first confirmed record of a freshwater fish catching flying birds. That is strong evidence of explosive, precisely aimed strikes in the genus, though it has not been documented for the goliath tigerfish itself.

### Endurance
Radio-telemetry of the African tigerfish shows fairly local movements: in the Incomati River system, 41 tagged fish had a mean longest distance travelled of 730 m and home ranges averaging about 48,800 m²; in the Upper Zambezi, fish moved on average about 1.4 km between surveys. Tigerfish are strong swimmers in fast water but not long-distance travellers.

### Senses
Tigerfish are visual predators of open, flowing water with the usual fish lateral line and smell. No species-specific sensory measurements were found.

### Intelligence and tactics
Evidence of tactics comes mostly from the genus: the bird-catching behavior of African tigerfish required fish to track swallows skimming the surface and time a leap. The goliath tigerfish is a capable ambush-and-strike predator, but there is no evidence of complex problem solving.

### Hunting behavior
Fast strikes on fish in currents and eddies. Young fish start on plankton, move to small fish and then to large fish as adults.

### Intraspecific fighting and predator defense
Tigerfish eat smaller tigerfish; no ritual fighting is documented. Large adults probably have few predators apart from crocodiles and people.

### Aggression
Reputedly very aggressive, with claims of attacks on swimmers in the Congo. These claims come from popular sources and were not confirmed in the scientific literature during this research.

### Environmental strengths
- Fast, turbulent, well-oxygenated river water.
- Open water where visual strikes are effective.
- Fights against soft-bodied fish and similar prey that its teeth can slice.

### Weaknesses
- No armor; ordinary fish scales.
- Modest mass on the full roster.
- Needs well-oxygenated water.
- Useless out of water.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** smaller fish, soft-bodied opponents, animals that enter its fast-water habitat without protection.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized predatory fish such as barracuda.
- **Bad:** crocodilians, large armored reptiles, big mammals and anything much heavier.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Dagger-Tooth Slash** — 32 large, interlocking teeth, each up to about 2.5 cm and exposed along the jaw, seize and slice prey. Against soft-bodied opponents it can remove chunks of flesh in a single strike.
2. **Whitewater Strike** — it hunts in fast, turbulent river channels, holding position and lunging at prey swept by the current. In a matchup it keeps full striking power in water that tires or unbalances many opponents.

### Unique traits
1. **Five-Day Tooth Reload** — in tigerfish, a whole quadrant of teeth is shed and replaced at once over about 3–5 days, so broken weapons are quickly renewed.
2. **Giant of the Tigerfishes** — the largest tigerfish species, reaching up to 133 cm and 50 kg according to FishBase, and the top fish predator of the Congo's fast waters.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The goliath tigerfish lives in the Congo River basin, including the Lualaba River and Lake Upemba, and in Lake Tanganyika. It prefers highly oxygenated, fast-flowing water in deep river channels and open lake waters.

### Diet and ecology
Adults are strict fish-eaters, taking smaller fish including tilapia and smaller tigerfish. Juveniles eat plankton before switching to small fish. It is one of the top predators of the Congo's rivers.

### Social structure
Little is known about the social life of wild goliath tigerfish; no stable groups or cooperative hunting are documented for large adults.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeding is poorly studied. Reports suggest young fish grow slowly at first. No age-validated growth study was found.

### Conservation
The goliath tigerfish is listed as **Least Concern** on the IUCN Red List (2010 assessment). Local threats may include fishing pressure and river development, but its conservation is poorly studied.

### Special features
- 32 large, interlocking, dagger-like teeth up to about 2.5 cm.
- Whole-quadrant tooth replacement over a few days (documented in the genus).
- Deep, powerful body suited to fast river channels.
- Largest species of tigerfish.

### Human interaction
The goliath tigerfish is a prized and difficult sport fish and is eaten locally. It is kept in some large public aquariums. Stories of attacks on swimmers are common in popular media but were not confirmed in scientific sources during this research.

### Genuine fun facts
- Its 32 dagger-like teeth are each up to about 2.5 cm long.
- Tigerfish replace a whole set of teeth at once in just a few days.
- A related tigerfish was filmed leaping from the water to catch swallows in flight, the first confirmed case of a freshwater fish catching flying birds.
- FishBase lists a maximum length of 133 cm and a maximum published weight of 50 kg.
- It lives only in the Congo River basin and Lake Tanganyika.
- Adults eat smaller tigerfish as well as other fish.

### Concise site-ready summary
The goliath tigerfish is the Congo River's dagger-toothed hunter: a powerful 25 kg fish armed with 32 large, interlocking teeth that it replaces in whole sets within days. It strikes hard and fast in turbulent water, but with ordinary scales and modest mass it is outclassed by crocodiles, big sharks and other heavyweights.

### Rich narrative profile
The goliath tigerfish is the biggest and most heavily armed of the African tigerfishes. Its jaws carry 32 large teeth that sit along the outside of the mouth like a row of daggers, meshing together when the mouth closes. When teeth break or wear, the fish sheds a whole set and grows a new one in a matter of days, so its weapons are almost always sharp.

It hunts in some of the most demanding freshwater on Earth, the fast, churning channels of the Congo River, where it holds position in the current and strikes at fish swept past. Its smaller relative, the African tigerfish, has been filmed leaping out of the water to snatch swallows in flight, a sign of how fast and precisely these fish can attack.

In a battle, the goliath tigerfish is a sharp but lightly built attacker. Against another fish of its size, its teeth and fast-water power make it very dangerous. It has no armor, however, and at around 25 kg it cannot absorb the bite of a crocodile or large shark. Many of the frightening stories about it come from popular media rather than science; the real animal is impressive enough without them.

- **Human attacks:** popular claims only; not used to raise Attack or Ferocity.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Barracuda (17 kg; A48 D28 Ag70 St64 I51 Sp52):** the closest roster match: a slicing-tooth predatory fish of similar size. The tigerfish is somewhat heavier with larger teeth, so Attack (52.0 vs 48.0), Defense (30.0 vs 28.0), Agility (72.0 vs 70.0) and Special (56.0 vs 52.0) sit slightly higher. Stamina is lower (58.0 vs 64.0) because barracuda tagging shows longer travel.
- **Versus Piranha (1.2 kg; A37 D20 Ag82 St55 I59 Sp67):** the piranha has a finer shearing bite and group behavior but is twenty times lighter. The tigerfish is well above in Attack and Defense, while the piranha keeps higher Agility, Intelligence and Special for its shoaling and acoustic signals.
- **Versus Moray Eel (25 kg; A49 D32):** similar mass; the tigerfish's larger teeth and open-water strikes put Attack slightly above, Defense slightly below.
- **Versus Bull Shark (111 kg; A72 D52) and Nile Crocodile (400 kg; A89 D80):** both are far heavier and armed with stronger bites; the tigerfish is far below them, as it should be for a 25 kg fish.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the goliath tigerfish's fearsome reputation and human-attack stories were not allowed to lift Attack or Ferocity beyond what its size and teeth support.
