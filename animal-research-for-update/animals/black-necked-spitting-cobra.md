# Black-necked Spitting Cobra (*Naja nigricollis*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Black-necked Spitting Cobra
- **Scientific name:** *Naja nigricollis* Reinhardt, 1843, family Elapidae
- **Exact animal represented:** the black-necked spitting cobra of western, central and eastern sub-Saharan Africa. The former subspecies now treated as the separate species *Naja nigricincta* (zebra and black spitting cobras) and the Mozambique spitting cobra *N. mossambica* are not used here; older southern African records under this name refer to those species.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult, **1.5 kg** (estimate) and **150 cm** total length, sex unspecified. No reliable size difference between the sexes was verified in this pass.
- **Population notes:** adults generally measure 1.2–2.2 m, varying with region. Color varies widely: black or grey with a broad black neck band and reddish belly, yellowish-brown or coppery without neck bands, reddish-brown or olive. Venom profiles differ between Nigerian regions (2026 studies).

### Bite, fangs and venom
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No bite-pressure measurement; the weapons are venom by bite and by spraying.
- No reliable fang-length measurement was located.
- **Yield:** average **200–350 mg** dry venom per bite (Minton 1974, via Wikipedia).
- **Potency:** mouse LD50 about **2 mg/kg** subcutaneous and **1.15 mg/kg** intravenous.
- **Composition and action:** mainly cytotoxic three-finger toxins with phospholipase A2s and some neurotoxic and cardiotoxic components. Low-molecular-mass cytotoxins, helped by PLA2s, reproduce the skin necrosis seen after bites (Rivel et al. 2016). Spitting cobra venoms have extra PLA2 that boosts the cytotoxins' activation of pain nerves (Kazandjian et al. 2021). Bites cause severe pain, swelling, blistering and tissue death; untreated mortality is about 5–10% (under 1% in some endemic regions), with death, when it occurs, from paralysis of breathing.
- **Spitting:** the venom stream is released in about 50 ms, often at a target more than 1 m away. Venom in the eyes causes intense pain, corneal erosions and possible permanent blindness if not washed out.

### Defensive structure
- Smooth scales; no armor. The spray of venom is its main defense.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary and secondary weapons
Its first weapon is a ranged one: it can spray venom from its fangs at an attacker's face, causing blinding pain. Its second is the bite, which delivers a large dose of cytotoxic venom that destroys tissue, with some neurotoxic effect.

### Offensive mechanics
Against prey, mostly small rodents, it bites like other cobras. Against a threat, it raises its body, spreads its hood and spits. Experiments show spitting cobras track a moving target and anticipate its next movement, rapidly oscillating the head to direct the stream (Westhoff et al. 2010). The spray pattern is matched to the size of the target regardless of distance (Berthé et al. 2009), and the snake aims at the center of the nearest body part, which is usually the face, so at least one eye is usually hit (Berthé et al. 2013).

### Defensive features and durability
A roughly 1.5 kg cobra with no armor. The spit genuinely prevents many attacks by blinding or distracting the attacker before contact, which is why its Defense sits slightly above that of non-spitting cobras of similar size.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
An active forager that may travel fair distances and is an excellent climber, sometimes arboreal. Good close-range control when rearing and striking.

### Endurance
Active by day or night depending on season, location and temperature. Ectothermic, so not built for long, high-output effort.

### Senses
Vision good enough to track moving targets and aim at faces, plus normal elapid chemoreception.

### Intelligence and tactics
The target-tracking and anticipation seen in spitting cobras involve a level of neural processing not previously attributed to snakes (Westhoff et al. 2010). It spits, then may flee or bite if pressed. Its flexible day or night activity and use of farmland also show behavioral range.

### Sociality and hunting
Solitary. Feeds mainly on small rodents, also birds, fish, lizards, eggs and other snakes.

### Fighting and aggression
Wild animals are nervous and quick to spit and bite at slight provocation; captive-bred animals are calmer.

### Environmental strengths
- Savanna, semi-desert, moist savanna, farmland and cleared forest.
- Open ground where it can see an attacker's face coming.
- Villages and towns with rodents.

### Weaknesses
- No armor and a light body.
- Bite lethality is low compared with many cobras; the venom mostly destroys tissue.
- Spitting deters but does not kill; a determined or protected attacker can close in.
- Preyed on by snake eagles and other snakes.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** mammals and birds that attack face-first, especially at close to mid range; rodents and small prey.
- **Mixed:** other venomous snakes, mongooses (which can still be blinded).
- **Bad:** snake eagles and raptors attacking from above, armored animals whose eyes are protected, and large animals that can trample it despite being sprayed.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Aimed Venom Spray** — it sprays venom from its fangs toward an attacker's face, releasing the stream in about 50 ms, tracking and anticipating the target's movement, and matching the spray to the target's size. Venom in the eyes causes intense pain and can cause permanent blindness.
2. **Flesh-Melting Bite** — its bite delivers 200–350 mg of venom dominated by cytotoxins that destroy skin and muscle, with phospholipase A2s that boost pain and some neurotoxic effect.

### Unique traits
1. **Face-Finding Aim** — tests with artificial targets show it spits most at oval or round shapes and aims at the center of the nearest body part, usually the face, so at least one eye is usually hit.
2. **Day-or-Night Cobra** — it shifts between daytime and night-time activity with season, location and temperature, and in Nigeria has spread from rainforest edges into farmland and drier areas.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The black-necked spitting cobra is widespread across sub-Saharan Africa from Senegal and Mauritania east to Ethiopia and Somalia and south to Angola, Zambia and Tanzania, entering southern Africa only in northern Namibia. It is absent from the Congo Basin rainforest. It prefers savanna and semi-desert but also lives in moist savanna, cleared former forest, coastal scrub and dry grassland up to 1,800 m, often near water. It shelters in termite mounds, rodent holes and especially hollow tree trunks.

### Diet and ecology
It eats mainly small rodents, plus birds, fish, lizards, eggs and other snakes. Snake eagles, including the short-toed snake eagle, and other snakes prey on it.

### Social structure
Solitary.

### Reproduction and life history
Egg-laying. Mating runs from about September to December. Females lay about 10–15 eggs (range 8–22), which hatch after 60–70 days at 28–30 °C. Hatchlings are 20–25 cm long and independent.

### Conservation
Listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List (as reported by Wikipedia). It is common and widespread, and in southern Nigeria it has done well where rainforest has been replaced by farmland and plantations.

### Special features
- Fangs that can spray venom as well as inject it.
- Target-tracking and face-aiming spit.
- Cytotoxic venom with pain-enhancing PLA2s.
- Hood display with a black neck band in many populations.
- Good climbing and flexible day or night activity.

### Human interaction
Because it is common around villages and towns, it often meets people. Bites cause pain, swelling, blistering and tissue death; untreated mortality is about 5–10% and under 1% in some endemic regions. Venom spat into the eyes causes painful venom ophthalmia that can lead to corneal damage and blindness without prompt washing. In 1944 the ophthalmic surgeon Harold Ridley described a Ghanaian labourer spat on from about four or five feet away; the eye recovered in about a week. It is one of the main causes of snakebite in Nigeria, alongside the carpet viper and puff adder.

### Genuine fun facts
- It can spray venom at an attacker's eyes from more than a metre away.
- A spit stream is released in about 50 thousandths of a second.
- It tracks where an attacker's face is about to move and aims there.
- It spits most at round or oval shapes, like faces.
- Its venom has extra enzymes that make its cytotoxins more painful.
- Eye-surgery pioneer Harold Ridley wrote about a spitting-cobra eye injury in 1944.
- Some individuals are black with a red belly, others coppery brown without a neck band.

### Concise site-ready summary
The black-necked spitting cobra is an African cobra that defends itself by spraying venom at an attacker's eyes, tracking its target and aiming at the face. Its bite delivers a large dose of tissue-destroying venom. At about 1.5 m it is light and unarmored, but its blinding spit makes it one of the hardest snakes to attack.

### Rich narrative profile
The black-necked spitting cobra fights at a distance. When threatened it rears up, spreads its hood and sprays venom from its fangs toward the attacker's face. The stream leaves in about 50 milliseconds, and laboratory studies show that spitting cobras follow a moving target and anticipate where it will go, rapidly swinging the head to spread venom over the face. The snake matches its spray to the size of the target and aims at the center of the nearest body part, which is usually the face, so at least one eye is usually hit.

Venom in the eyes causes intense burning pain and can cause permanent blindness if it is not washed out. The bite adds another layer: 200–350 mg of venom dominated by cytotoxins that destroy skin and muscle, with enzymes that make the pain worse. It kills far less often than many cobras, with untreated mortality around 5–10%, but it can leave lasting tissue damage.

For battle ratings, the spit gives this cobra a genuinely unique ranged defense and the roster's first ranged venom attack, earning a very high Special. Its body is still that of a 1.5 kg snake, with no armor and little strength, and a bird of prey striking from above or an opponent with protected eyes can overcome it.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** no reliable field mass; AnAge's 8.8 kg is rejected and 1.5 kg is a labeled estimate.
- **Spitting range:** Wikipedia's "over 7 m" is unsourced and not accepted; peer-reviewed work describes targets often more than 1 m away, and Ridley's case was about 1.2–1.5 m.
- **Tracking study species:** Westhoff et al. (2010) studied spitting cobras in general; the spray-matching study explicitly included *N. nigricollis*.
- **Mortality:** 5–10% untreated is an older general figure and varies by region.
- **Lifespan:** 23.2 years is a captive maximum.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Indian Cobra (2 kg):** similar length and build. The Indian cobra's neurotoxic bite is more lethal, so Attack is close (57 vs 56). The spitting cobra gets higher Defense, Tactics, Intelligence and Special because aimed spitting stops attacks at range and involves target tracking.
- **Versus King Cobra (8 kg):** the king cobra is far larger with a much bigger neurotoxic dose and more complex behavior, so it keeps higher Attack, Stamina, Size and Intelligence. The spitting cobra's ranged spray gives it a slightly higher Special and similar Defense.
- **Versus Black Mamba (1.6 kg):** similar mass. The mamba's fast neurotoxic venom and speed keep its Attack and Agility higher; the spitting cobra gets higher Defense and Intelligence from its ranged deterrent and aiming behavior, and a slightly higher Special.
- **Versus Bombardier Beetle:** both spray a defensive chemical at attackers, but the cobra's spray is aimed at the eyes and backed by a dangerous bite and a vastly larger body, so all its physical ratings stay far higher.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the spit is counted in Special and, modestly, in Defense because it genuinely prevents contact; it is not counted again as extra Attack beyond a small amount, since it rarely kills.
