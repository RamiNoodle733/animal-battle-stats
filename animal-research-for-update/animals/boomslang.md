# Boomslang (*Dispholidus typus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Boomslang ("tree snake" in Afrikaans and Dutch)
- **Scientific name:** *Dispholidus typus* (Smith, 1828), family Colubridae, subfamily Colubrinae
- **Exact animal represented:** the boomslang of sub-Saharan Africa. The related *D. pembae* and *D. punctatus* are separate species and are not used here.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult, **0.3 kg** and **140 cm** total length, sex unspecified. The sexes differ in color (most males green, females brown) but no reliable size difference was verified, so no sex-based modifier is applied.
- **Population notes:** color varies strongly by region; in the Cape provinces many males are black above with green, yellow or orange sides. Juveniles are grey or brown with a huge emerald-green eye and take on adult colors at about 1 m long.

## 2. Physical measurements

- AnAge (Myhrvold et al. 2015 amniote database) lists an adult weight of **3,765 g**, which is not accepted: it is more than seven times the heaviest field figure and implausible for a slender 1.2–1.6 m tree snake.
- **Canonical:** **0.3 kg**, the reported average.
- **Confidence:** Moderate-low (single secondary figure, but internally consistent with the snake's build).

### Height
- **Canonical `height_cm`: 0.0** (standing height does not apply to a snake).

### Length
- Wikipedia: adults average **100–160 cm** total length; some exceed 183 cm.
- ADW: average adult **1.2–1.5 m**.
- Hatchlings 29–38 cm.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 140.0 cm** total length.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Speed
- Moves swiftly through branches; no measured speed exists.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0** (no reliable measurement).

### Lifespan
- ADW: estimated about **8 years in the wild**, based on captive observations; slightly longer in captivity.
- AnAge: maximum recorded longevity **8.7 years in captivity** (small sample).
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 8.0.**
- **Confidence:** Low.

### Bite, fangs and venom
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No bite-pressure measurement; the weapon is venom from rear fangs.
- **Fangs:** three very large grooved fangs sit below each eye, behind seven or eight small front teeth. As a rear-fanged snake it must bite and hold, working venom into the wound. It can open its jaws to about 170°.
- **Yield:** about **1.6–8 mg** of venom per adult.
- **Potency:** mouse LD50 about **0.07–0.1 mg/kg** intravenous, but **12.5 mg/kg** subcutaneous and 1.3–1.8 mg/kg intraperitoneal. Given the small yields and serious human cases, only 2–3 mg has been suggested as potentially lethal to an adult person.
- **Composition:** seven toxin families, dominated by metalloproteinases, plus three-finger toxins, phospholipase A2s, CRISPs, serine proteinases and C-type lectins (Pla et al. 2017). The metalloproteinases activate prothrombin (factor II) and factor X, and possibly factor IX.
- **Action:** a consumption coagulopathy that causes widespread tiny clots and then uncontrolled bleeding into muscles, brain and other organs. Symptoms can take many hours to appear. A specific monovalent antivenom is made by South African Vaccine Producers; the usual dose is two 10 ml vials.

### Defensive structure
- Narrow, oblique, strongly keeled scales on a slender, slightly compressed body. No armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary and secondary weapons
The primary weapon is a bite with three large grooved fangs on each side at the back of the upper jaw, delivering a small amount of an extremely potent clotting venom. The very wide 170° gape lets it bring these rear fangs into play. The secondary assets are sharp vision and speed in the branches, which it uses both to catch prey and to escape.

### Offensive mechanics
It must bite and hold to inject venom well, which suits small prey such as chameleons and nestling birds. Against a large opponent the risk is delayed: the venom sets off widespread clotting and bleeding over hours, so a bitten animal may keep fighting at full strength for a long time.

### Defensive features and durability
Light and fragile: a 300 g snake with no armor. When cornered it inflates its neck and adopts an S-shaped striking pose, but it prefers to flee through the branches.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Highly arboreal and an excellent climber. Its long, slender body lets it move quickly from branch to branch. It also comes down to the ground to hunt or bask.

### Endurance
An active daytime hunter. Ectothermic; in cool weather it becomes inactive for short periods, often curled inside a weaver bird's nest.

### Senses
Its eyes are exceptionally large, and it has excellent eyesight. It often sways its head from side to side to judge objects in front of it.

### Intelligence and tactics
A visual stalker that glides slowly through branches, holds still in cover and strikes without warning. It is very reluctant to bite people and retreats when pursued by anything too large to eat.

### Sociality and hunting
Solitary; it even eats other boomslangs. Diet: chameleons and other tree lizards, frogs, nestling birds and eggs, occasional small mammals and other snakes.

### Fighting and aggression
Timid. Bites to people almost always happen when someone tries to catch, handle or kill it. The African Snakebite Institute describes it as extremely reluctant to bite.

### Environmental strengths
- Trees and shrubs in savanna, karoo scrub and lowland forest.
- Daylight, when its vision works best.
- Hunting small arboreal prey that it can hold after biting.

### Weaknesses
- Very light, fragile body.
- Rear fangs need a firm, held bite.
- Tiny venom doses and slow onset.
- Much weaker on open ground than in trees.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** chameleons, tree lizards, small birds and other small prey that can be held after a bite.
- **Mixed:** similar-sized snakes.
- **Bad:** raptors (including snake eagles), larger carnivores, anything armored, and opponents too big to hold that can kill it long before the venom takes effect.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Silent Clot Venom** — metalloproteinases in its venom activate prothrombin and factor X, causing widespread tiny clots and then uncontrolled bleeding. Symptoms can take many hours to appear, which misled the herpetologist Karl Schmidt into thinking his 1957 bite was harmless; he died about a day later.
2. **170-Degree Rear-Fang Bite** — it can open its jaws to about 170°, bringing three large grooved fangs on each side into contact, then holds on to work the venom in.

### Unique traits
1. **Emerald-Eyed Sharpshooter** — its eyes are exceptionally large, and it sways its head from side to side to judge distance, giving it some of the best vision of any snake.
2. **Two-Tone Tree Snake** — most males are bright green and females brown, and it changes from a grey juvenile with a huge green eye into adult colors at around 1 m long.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The boomslang lives across sub-Saharan Africa, from Senegal and The Gambia through West, Central and East Africa to southern Africa, with dense populations in Angola, Botswana, Eswatini, Malawi, Mozambique, Namibia, South Africa, Zambia and Zimbabwe. It uses karoo scrub, savanna, lowland forest and grassland, living mainly in shrubs and trees and avoiding very dry desert.

### Diet and ecology
Chameleons and other tree lizards are its main prey, along with frogs, nestling birds and eggs, small mammals and other snakes, including boomslangs. It hunts by day, stalking through branches.

### Social structure
Solitary; conspecifics may be eaten.

### Reproduction and life history
Egg-laying. Mating is recorded in December and January; females lay about 8–14 leathery eggs (up to 27) in a hollow tree trunk or rotting log. Incubation averages about three months. Hatchlings are 29–38 cm and become dangerously venomous at around 45 cm.

### Conservation
Listed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List (as reported by Wikipedia). It is widespread across much of sub-Saharan Africa.

### Special features
- Three large grooved rear fangs on each side of the upper jaw.
- 170° gape.
- Exceptionally large eyes and excellent vision.
- Slender, fast, highly arboreal body.
- Strong color difference between males and females.

### Human interaction
Bites are rare and almost always happen when people handle or try to kill the snake. Karl Patterson Schmidt, a leading American herpetologist, died in 1957 after a bite from a juvenile boomslang he doubted could deliver a fatal dose, and kept notes on his symptoms nearly to the end. D. S. Chapman recorded eight serious boomslang envenomings between 1919 and 1962, two fatal. Many hospitals do not stock the monovalent antivenom because bites are so rare. Dogs are also bitten occasionally.

### Genuine fun facts
- Its name means "tree snake" in Afrikaans and Dutch.
- It can open its mouth to about 170°.
- Juveniles have a huge emerald-green eye compared with their head.
- Karl Schmidt kept a diary of his symptoms after being bitten, almost until he died the next day.
- Its venom was once so underrated that many experts thought rear-fanged snakes could not kill people.
- It sometimes spends cool spells curled up inside a weaver bird's nest.
- Most males are green and females brown, so a pair can look like two different species.

### Concise site-ready summary
The boomslang is a slender, sharp-eyed African tree snake whose small doses of rear-fang venom cause deadly bleeding hours after a bite. It is fast and precise in the branches but timid and fragile, weighing only about 300 g, so in a fight its venom is too slow to protect it.

### Rich narrative profile
The boomslang looks like a harmless green tree snake, and for a long time experts treated it like one. Its fangs sit at the back of its mouth, and it produces only a few milligrams of venom. Then, in 1957, the herpetologist Karl Schmidt was bitten by a juvenile and died about a day later, after writing down his symptoms almost to the end. The venom, rich in enzymes that switch on the body's own clotting factors, causes widespread tiny clots and then uncontrolled bleeding, but only after hours of little warning.

In life the boomslang is a hunter of chameleons, tree lizards and nestling birds. It stalks slowly through branches using its exceptionally large eyes, swaying its head to judge distance, then bites and holds so its rear fangs can work. Its jaws open to about 170°. When threatened by something too large to eat, it usually slips away through the canopy; if cornered, it inflates its neck and takes an S-shaped strike pose.

For battle ratings, the boomslang's venom is dangerous but works on the wrong timescale for a fight: an opponent could keep fighting for hours before bleeding begins. Its body is tiny and fragile, and it is very reluctant to bite. Its genuine strengths are tree agility, vision and a venom system unlike that of any front-fanged snake.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** AnAge's 3.77 kg conflicts with field-based 175–510 g and is rejected.
- **Venom potency:** mouse LD50 changes more than 100-fold with injection route (0.07 IV vs 12.5 mg/kg SC); humans appear more sensitive than mice.
- **Temperature-dependent sex:** ADW states offspring sex depends on temperature; this was not confirmed elsewhere and is not used.
- **Lifespan:** based on limited captive records.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Black Mamba (1.6 kg):** the mamba is about five times heavier with front fangs and a fast-acting neurotoxic venom, so it keeps far higher Attack, Weaponry and Special. The boomslang is nearly as agile, especially in trees, and has better eyesight but a much lower Ferocity.
- **Versus Eastern Brown Snake (1 kg):** both venoms trigger runaway clotting, but the brown snake's front fangs deliver it fast, so the brown snake gets much higher Attack. The boomslang gets slightly higher Agility in trees and higher Senses.
- **Versus Saw-scaled Viper (0.15 kg):** similar tiny size class. The viper's front fangs and readiness to strike give it higher Attack and Ferocity; the boomslang is more agile, more perceptive and has more stamina.
- **Versus Gila Monster (1.1 kg):** both must hold on to work venom in. The boomslang's venom is far more dangerous, so its Attack is higher, but its tiny, fragile body gives it lower Defense.
- **Anti-inflation check:** famous human deaths are not counted as extra Attack; slow onset and tiny doses keep the boomslang's fight value low despite a lethal venom.
