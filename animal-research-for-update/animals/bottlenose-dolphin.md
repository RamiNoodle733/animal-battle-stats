# Bottlenose Dolphin

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Common bottlenose dolphin / bottlenose dolphin
- **Scientific name:** *Tursiops truncatus* (Montagu, 1821)
- **Exact ABS taxon:** Common bottlenose dolphin, not Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphin (*Tursiops aduncus*) or Burrunan dolphin.
- **Living/extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical specimen:** Healthy mature coastal adult male, using the exceptionally well-studied Sarasota Bay population as a practical representative rather than an offshore maximum-size individual.
- **Canonical body mass:** **260.0 kg**. Sarasota Dolphin Research Program reports mature Sarasota males averaging about 260 kg versus about 190 kg for adult females.
- **Canonical total length:** **275.0 cm**. Sarasota males reach roughly 9 ft (274 cm), while NOAA gives a much broader species range of 6-13 ft because body size varies strongly by population/ecotype.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Population-dependent, but adult males can be larger/heavier. Sarasota mature males average about 26% heavier than females.
- **Population/ecotype caveat:** Coastal and offshore forms differ substantially in size and morphology. NOAA gives 300-1,400 lb and 6-13 ft species-wide, so a 260 kg coastal male is deliberately representative rather than a record-sized offshore dolphin.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
NOAA Fisheries gives a species-wide range of **300-1,400 lb (136-635 kg)**. Sarasota long-term research gives approximately **260 kg** for mature males and **190 kg** for mature females in Sarasota Bay. The canonical **260.0 kg** value therefore represents a measured/studied coastal adult male without importing the offshore upper extreme.

### Length
NOAA gives **6-13 ft (1.8-4.0 m)** across the species. Sarasota reports Florida males around **9 ft (2.74 m)** and females around 8 ft. Canonical recommendation: **275.0 cm** total length.

### Height
Cetacean standing height is biologically inapplicable. `height_cm` should remain **0.0** rather than inventing a dorsal-fin or body-depth proxy.

### Speed
Institutional sources often report cruising or near-surface speeds rather than a controlled species maximum. A UNCW/NOAA-hosted fact sheet reports cruising at **1.4-3.1 m/s**, while Chesapeake Bay Program reports ordinary swimming around 2-4 mph and nearly 18 mph (~8.0 m/s) at faster speeds. Because these are not a clean instrumented maximum-speed trial, this profile conservatively recommends **8.0 m/s** as a documented fast-swimming natural-history value, with only moderate confidence. It must not be presented as a physiological absolute maximum.

### Lifespan
NOAA Fisheries reports bottlenose dolphins can live at least **40 years**, with some females reaching **60+ years**. ADW and Ocean Conservation Society similarly distinguish male longevity around 40-45 years from females over 50. For the canonical male, **42.5 years** is a defensible representative longevity field rather than using the female maximum.

### Teeth and bite force
NPS reports **18-26 pairs of robust conical teeth in each jaw**. These teeth primarily seize slippery prey rather than chew it. No reliable species-specific maximum bite pressure in PSI was established in this run, so `bite_force_psi` remains **0.0**. This means unresolved, not a literal absence of bite force.

### Defensive structures
No armor, shell or dermal weapon. Defense comes from a streamlined muscular body, substantial mass, thick skin/blubber, mobility, social support and the ability to maneuver in three dimensions. Shark scars and predation demonstrate that these defenses are not armor-equivalent.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary and secondary weapons
The primary direct weapon is the robust toothed rostrum/jaw system, with roughly 18-26 pairs of conical teeth per jaw. Teeth are optimized for gripping fish rather than slicing flesh like a shark's serrated teeth. Secondary offensive tools include high-speed body checks, ramming with the rostrum/head region, tail-fluke strikes and coordinated harassment. Sarasota researchers document **fishwhacking**, in which dolphins strike fleeing fish with the flukes hard enough to launch prey from the water.

### Offensive mechanics
A 260 kg dolphin can accelerate and redirect substantial body mass in water. Its best attacks are dynamic rather than stationary: close rapidly, ram/body-check, bite/grasp, disengage, and reposition. Against small aquatic opponents, speed plus mass is formidable. Against heavily armored animals or very large sharks/cetaceans, its conical teeth and moderate body mass impose a ceiling on raw damage.

### Defensive adaptations and durability
Blubber and a robust fusiform body provide insulation and some tissue depth, but neither should be scored as armor. Survival is primarily active: detect danger early, maneuver rapidly, exploit three-dimensional water space, coordinate socially, and avoid a predator's weapon arc. Sharks are documented predators, so Defense must remain below animals with much greater mass or true armor.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Bottlenose dolphins are highly maneuverable swimmers. Pectoral flippers steer, flukes generate propulsion, and the dorsal fin stabilizes. They can accelerate, brake, roll, leap and change depth rapidly. This supports very high aquatic Agility even though the canonical 8.0 m/s factual speed field is not an extreme roster-wide top-speed anchor.

### Endurance and respiration
They are air-breathing mammals capable of repeated sustained swimming and foraging bouts. Chesapeake Bay Program notes routine surfacing around one to two times per minute and submergence beyond four minutes in some cases; other references report dives of several minutes. This supports strong sustained aquatic performance but not the transoceanic endurance ceiling represented by wandering albatross or the extreme scale of large migratory whales.

### Senses
**Echolocation is the standout.** A 2022 JASA experiment measured approximately **1.5° horizontal angular resolution** in bottlenose dolphin biosonar and about **6.7 cm spatial-separation discrimination at 4 m** in a related task. Vision and passive hearing complement active sonar. This combination supports an elite Senses score and a very high Special rating.

### Intelligence, learning and tactics
Bottlenose dolphins show advanced learning, flexible foraging and sophisticated social cognition. Shark Bay bottlenose dolphins use marine sponges as foraging tools, a socially transmitted specialization. A 2022 PNAS study found male bottlenose dolphins form large multilevel alliance networks in which intergroup cooperation improves access to contested resources. Intelligence is therefore scored among the roster's highest nonhuman values, while avoiding an unsupported claim that a single cognition metric proves universal superiority over every ape, corvid or elephant.

### Hunting behavior
Diet is primarily fish, squid and crustaceans. Hunting can be solitary or cooperative and varies by population. Documented strategies include fishwhacking, coordinated pursuit, prey herding and locally learned specializations. Aerial behavior rises during feeding in small hunting groups, although one study noted that its exact causal role in prey capture remained unresolved.

### Intraspecific fighting
Male competition can be intense. Alliance formation, coercive herding of females, tooth-rake scars and aggressive encounters demonstrate that the species has a real conflict repertoire. However, the canonical animal is not assumed to receive permanent pack/alliance assistance in a one-on-one ABS matchup.

### Predator defense
Large sharks are important natural predators. Dolphins use detection, evasive swimming, group defense and potentially ramming/harassment. Their intelligence and sonar can reduce surprise underwater, but neither makes them invulnerable to ambush or a large shark's bite.

### Aggression / ferocity
Bottlenose dolphins are not harmless despite their public image. They can be forceful in intraspecific conflict and aggressive interspecific encounters. Ferocity should be moderate-high, but not equated with constant aggression or predatory specialization against large vertebrates.

### Environmental strengths
- Open/coastal water with room to accelerate and turn
- Murky or dark water where echolocation remains useful
- Complex prey environments where learning and flexible tactics matter
- Social contexts where coordination can be exploited

### Environmental limitations
- Completely dependent on water for locomotion and body support
- Must surface to breathe
- No armor against penetrating bites
- Echolocation performance can be complicated by acoustic clutter/noise
- A solitary ABS specimen does not automatically gain alliance partners

### Major weaknesses
1. Conical gripping teeth are less destructive than serrated shark dentition.
2. No true armor.
3. Must breathe air.
4. Much smaller than orcas, sperm whales and giant baleen whales.
5. Aquatic specialization makes terrestrial combat effectively impossible.
6. Intelligence and sonar improve positioning but do not substitute for raw force against a much larger opponent.

### Matchup archetypes
**Strong against:** smaller aquatic vertebrates, opponents vulnerable to repeated high-mobility strikes, and visually dependent aquatic animals in poor visibility.

**Less favorable against:** large sharks with devastating cutting bites, armored aquatic animals, orcas and other much larger odontocetes, and giant animals that cannot be meaningfully disabled by moderate conical teeth/body strikes.

## 7. Abilities and traits

### Proposed special abilities, exactly 2
1. **Biosonar Lock** - Broadband echolocation actively interrogates underwater targets. Experimental work demonstrates fine angular and spatial discrimination, allowing the dolphin to track and classify objects even when vision is degraded.
2. **Adaptive Huntcraft** - Bottlenose dolphins learn and deploy locally specialized foraging techniques, including fishwhacking and, in Shark Bay populations, sponge-assisted foraging. The matchup value is behavioral flexibility rather than a magical damage bonus.

### Proposed unique traits, exactly 2
1. **Alliance Mind** - Male bottlenose dolphins can maintain multilevel cooperative relationships among unrelated individuals, evidence of unusually sophisticated social cognition. Solo ABS scoring credits the cognitive flexibility, not extra allied bodies.
2. **Three-Axis Athlete** - The fusiform body, powerful flukes and steering flippers enable rapid turns, rolls, depth changes and repeated repositioning in a fully three-dimensional arena.

## 8. Expanded profile content

### Habitat and geographic range
NOAA describes *T. truncatus* as globally distributed through temperate and tropical coastal and offshore waters. Coastal animals use bays, estuaries, sounds and continental-shelf habitats; offshore ecotypes occupy deeper oceanic waters.

### Diet
Primarily fishes, squid and crustaceans. Teeth seize prey, which is commonly swallowed rather than chewed. Diet and technique vary strongly by region.

### Ecology
A highly adaptable mesopredator/top coastal predator depending on ecosystem. Bottlenose dolphins can influence schooling fish behavior and interact competitively or aggressively with other cetaceans. Population ecology varies greatly, so a Sarasota coastal male should not be treated as representative of every offshore population's dimensions or movement ecology.

### Social structure
The species uses **fission-fusion** social organization rather than a permanently fixed pod. Group composition changes frequently. Male alliance networks can persist across multiple social levels.

### Reproduction and life history
NOAA reports reproductive onset generally between 5 and 15 years depending on population, about 12 months gestation, nursing for roughly 20 months, and calves often remaining with mothers for 3-6 years. Females may reproduce into advanced age.

### Conservation status
The common bottlenose dolphin is globally treated as **Least Concern** by IUCN, but NOAA lists several U.S. stocks as depleted under the Marine Mammal Protection Act. Global species status therefore must not erase severe local-stock pressures.

### Major adaptations
- Active echolocation
- Streamlined fusiform body and powerful dorsoventral fluke propulsion
- Large brain and flexible learning
- Fission-fusion social cognition and alliance formation
- Conical prey-gripping dentition
- Breath-hold diving and voluntary respiratory control

### Human interaction
Bottlenose dolphins are heavily studied and commonly encountered near coasts. NOAA identifies entanglement/gear ingestion, contaminants, disease, harmful biotoxins, habitat alteration, vessel strikes, ocean noise, oil/energy development, and illegal feeding/harassment among important threats. Their familiarity around people should not be mistaken for domestication or harmlessness.

### Strong fun facts
- Sarasota mature males average about 26% heavier than adult females.
- Bottlenose dolphins live in changing fission-fusion societies rather than permanently fixed pods.
- Sarasota researchers documented fishwhacking, where a dolphin can strike a fish with its tail flukes and launch it into the air.
- Experimental biosonar work measured roughly 1.5° horizontal angular resolution.
- Shark Bay dolphins are famous for carrying marine sponges on their rostra during specialized foraging.
- Male Shark Bay dolphins form multilevel alliance networks unusually complex outside humans.
- NOAA reports some females living 60 years or more.
- Adults can carry dozens of robust conical teeth, but these primarily grip rather than chew prey.

### Concise site-ready summary
The common bottlenose dolphin is a 200-plus-kilogram aquatic tactician built around mobility, echolocation and behavioral flexibility. A mature male combines robust conical teeth, mass-backed ramming and elite three-dimensional maneuverability with one of the roster's strongest sensory and cognitive packages. It is not a shark-like cutting weapon or a whale-sized tank: its battle advantage comes from finding, reading and repeatedly out-positioning opponents rather than overpowering giant animals head-on.

### Detailed narrative profile
A bottlenose dolphin's familiar silhouette hides a highly specialized combat geometry. The body is a muscular hydrodynamic projectile: flukes generate thrust, flippers steer, the dorsal fin stabilizes, and the animal can redirect through a three-dimensional water column in ways terrestrial fighters cannot reproduce. At roughly 260 kg for the canonical Sarasota male, there is enough mass behind a body check or rostral strike to matter, while dozens of conical teeth can grip flesh or prey. Yet the dolphin is not a miniature orca. Its teeth are not shark-like saws, its body lacks armor, and its raw damage output should remain below the roster's major marine macropredators.

Its strongest advantage is information. Echolocation allows active interrogation of the underwater environment, and controlled experiments show fine spatial resolution. That sensory stream feeds an unusually flexible brain. Different bottlenose populations develop different foraging traditions, some use tools, and males in Shark Bay maintain layered alliances. In an ABS matchup this should not be translated into fictional foresight or free teammates. It does justify unusually strong perception, tactical adaptation and rapid behavioral switching.

This creates a clear profile: moderate direct Attack and Defense, elite aquatic Agility, strong Stamina, and top-tier Intelligence/Special. The dolphin is most dangerous when it has water, space and time to sense and reposition. Its advantages collapse on land and diminish against opponents so large, armored or lethally armed that superior positioning cannot create a viable damage path.

## 10. Cross-animal calibration notes

- **Versus Beluga Whale:** Bottlenose Dolphin is far smaller than the 1,500 kg canonical beluga, so Raw Power/Defense must remain lower. Its Maneuverability and tactical/social cognition can reasonably be higher. Both are echolocating odontocetes, so Bottlenose Special should not gain points merely for being more familiar.
- **Versus Blue Whale:** Blue Whale dominates Size/Defense and absolute physical scale. Bottlenose Dolphin should dominate maneuverability, tactical flexibility and active echolocation. This prevents body mass from becoming Intelligence or Special.
- **Versus Barracuda:** Dolphin has far greater mass, cognition and sensory flexibility, while barracuda retains specialized predatory jaws and burst ambush design. Dolphin Attack should not be inflated simply because it is smarter.
- **Versus Black Mamba / Black Widow:** Their Special scores are driven by venom systems. Dolphin Special 92.0 is a different axis, active biosonar, and should be revisited during final cross-domain normalization so sensory utility and lethal biochemical bypass are not treated as identical concepts.
- **Versus African Wild Dog:** Both show sophisticated cooperation, but dolphin Intelligence/Tactics can sit higher due to tool-associated traditions and multilevel alliances. African Wild Dog Stamina remains a terrestrial pursuit specialist and should not be directly equated with dolphin swimming endurance.
- **Watch item:** Intelligence 94.0 and Senses 96.0 are intentionally elite and require later normalization against chimpanzee, elephant, corvid, orca and sperm-whale research files.
