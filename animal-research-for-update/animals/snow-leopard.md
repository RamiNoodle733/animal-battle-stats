# Snow Leopard (*Panthera uncia*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** Snow Leopard.
- **Taxon:** *Panthera uncia* (Schreber, 1775), family Felidae.
- **Living/extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime **adult male**, **50.0 kg**, **60.0 cm shoulder height**, **115.0 cm head-body length**, plus an approximately **90.0 cm tail**. The ABS `length_cm` recommendation is **205.0 cm total nose-to-tail-tip**.
- **Dimorphism and variation:** males are somewhat larger. ADW gives adults broadly **25–75 kg**, with typical adults around **35–45 kg**; Southwick's Zoo gives males **99–121 lb (44.9–54.9 kg)** and females 77–88 lb. A 50 kg male is therefore a strong representative fighting-sex adult without using the 75 kg extreme.

## 2. Physical measurements and weapons
### Size
ADW reports head-body length roughly **100–130 cm**, tail **80–100 cm**, mass 25–75 kg, and very large front footpads around **90–100 × 70–80 mm**. PBS reports about **24 in / 61 cm shoulder height**, male mass 99–121 lb, body length 39–51 in and tail 31–39 in. Marwell gives 86–125 cm head-body and 80–105 cm tail. These sources support the 50 kg / 60 cm shoulder / 115 cm body / 90 cm tail canonical male.

### Speed and locomotion
No instrumented, species-specific maximum sprint speed was found in the stronger sources. The legacy 64.8 km/h figure is therefore rejected and `speed_mps` remains **0.0 unresolved**. This does not imply slowness. Snow leopards have long hind limbs, broad furred paws, exceptional balance, and institutional sources report horizontal leaps around **10–15 m**. Leap claims are useful locomotor evidence but are not converted into a top running speed.

### Lifespan
Multiple zoo references place wild lifespan around **10–12/13 years**, with animals in human care reaching roughly 20–22 years. Proposed representative `lifespan_years` is **12.0**.

### Bite and natural weapons
No defensible direct snow-leopard bite-pressure measurement was located. The site's legacy **300 PSI** is not retained. `bite_force_psi: 0.0` means unresolved, not no bite. Primary weapons are retractile felid claws, large canines/carnassials, powerful forelimbs for grappling, and a throat/nape killing bite. No force-to-PSI conversion is fabricated.

### Defensive structures
The snow leopard lacks armor. Defense comes from dense insulating fur, compact body form, strong musculoskeletal construction, camouflage, balance, terrain control and evasive mobility. Thick fur is thermal protection, not equivalent to hide armor.

## 4. Combat biology
### Offense and predation
Snow leopards are solitary ambush predators. They stalk from cover or elevation, accelerate into a short pursuit on steep slopes, grapple with the forelimbs and kill with a neck/throat bite. Snow Leopard Trust identifies blue sheep/bharal, Asiatic ibex and argali as key prey. Institutional sources report that snow leopards can kill prey around three times their own mass. The Trust reports a large kill roughly every **8–10 days**, with cats often remaining near a carcass for **3–4 days**.

### Intraspecific fighting and ferocity
Adults are mainly solitary and advertise presence through scent marks and scrapes. Physical conflict is possible but avoidance and spacing are important. The species should not inherit extreme Ferocity merely because it is a predator. Its combat behavior is better characterized as committed ambush and grapple when attacking prey, with risk avoidance when unnecessary.

### Locomotion and terrain mechanics
The cat's standout combat advantage is control on broken mountain terrain. Long hind limbs generate powerful jumps, broad paws distribute weight on snow, and the very long tail functions as a counterbalance during sharp changes of direction and precarious footing. This supports elite Agility without pretending that unverified sprint speed is measured fact.

### Endurance
GPS telemetry demonstrates large-scale movement and home ranges rather than sprint physiology. A 2025 Nepal study tracked one adult male and two subadult females across international mountain terrain; another 2025 telemetry analysis reported an adult male LoCoH home range around **312 km²** (MCP 1,032 km²). This supports substantial travel endurance, but does not justify endurance equal to cursorial specialists.

### Senses, intelligence and tactics
As a felid, the snow leopard combines strong low-light vision, hearing, olfaction and whisker/tactile input. Its tactical strengths are stalking, use of elevation, route selection, concealment and timing. There is evidence of flexible spatial behavior over huge ranges, but no basis for elevating cognition into ape/corvid/cetacean tiers.

### Environmental pros and cons
**Best:** steep cliffs, ridges, rocky gullies, snow and broken alpine terrain where camouflage, grip, jumping and balance matter. **Worse:** flat open ground against larger predators, where terrain-specific advantages shrink. Deep cold is physiologically manageable, but low oxygen is an adaptation context rather than a combat bonus by itself.

### Weaknesses and matchup archetypes
- Only ~50 kg canonically, so large cats, bears and megafauna have major absolute mass/power advantages.
- No armor, venom, horns or long-range weapon.
- Ambush and terrain advantage matter more than frontal attrition.
- Excellent against similarly sized or smaller animals vulnerable to a grapple-and-throat-bite sequence, especially in broken terrain.
- Poor against much larger armored, thick-skinned or crushing opponents that can survive the initial grapple and overpower it.

## 7. Abilities and traits
### Special abilities
1. **Alpine Ambush** — cryptic rosetted coat, stalking behavior and use of cliffs/ridges allow close approach and favorable first contact in broken mountain terrain.
2. **Cliffbound Leap** — long hind limbs, broad paws and tail-assisted balance enable unusually controlled leaps and rapid repositioning across steep, unstable surfaces.

### Unique traits
1. **Counterbalance Tail** — the exceptionally long, thick tail approaches body length and stabilizes the cat while also serving as a cold-weather wrap at rest.
2. **Snowshoe Paws** — unusually broad, furred paws spread load over snow and improve traction while reducing heat loss.

## 8. Expanded profile
### Habitat, range and ecology
Snow leopards occupy Central and South Asian mountain systems from the Himalayas and Tibetan Plateau through parts of Central Asia, Mongolia and southern Siberia. ADW places the broad elevational range around **900–5,500 m**, commonly 3,000–4,500 m, with lower winter movement in parts of the range. Preferred terrain includes cliffs, ridges, gullies, rocky outcrops, alpine meadow, shrubland and open conifer habitat rather than dense forest.

### Diet and social system
Principal wild prey are mountain ungulates, especially bharal, ibex and argali, supplemented by marmots, hares, pikas, rodents and birds. Livestock may also be taken. Adults are predominantly solitary outside mating and mother-young associations. Scent marking and scrapes mediate spacing and communication.

### Reproduction and life history
Females generally produce small litters after roughly three months gestation. Cubs remain dependent for an extended period while learning terrain use and hunting. Sexual maturity is reached at roughly 2–3 years in zoo summaries.

### Conservation and human interaction
The snow leopard is listed **Vulnerable** on the IUCN Red List. Major threats include prey depletion, retaliatory killing after livestock losses, poaching/illegal trade, habitat fragmentation and climate-related landscape change. Community livestock protection and prey conservation are therefore central to coexistence.

### Adaptations
Dense fur, small rounded ears, enlarged nasal passages, broad furred paws, long hind limbs, a compact body and a long bushy tail all suit cold, high-elevation terrain. Pale rosetted coloration provides disruptive camouflage against rock and snow.

### Genuine fun facts
- The tail can be nearly as long as the head-body length.
- Large paws function like natural snowshoes.
- Snow leopards cannot produce the classic full roar of lions and tigers, but communicate with growls, chuffs, moans and other calls.
- Institutional references report leaps around 10–15 m horizontally.
- A large prey animal may be consumed over several days, and large kills average roughly one every 8–10 days according to Snow Leopard Trust.
- Recent GPS work has documented individual home ranges extending across international borders.

### Concise site-ready summary
A 50 kg adult male snow leopard is a specialized mountain ambush cat built around claws, a throat bite, camouflage and extraordinary terrain control. Its broad paws, long hind limbs and counterbalancing tail make it exceptionally agile on cliffs and snow, but it lacks armor and gives up substantial absolute power to tigers, lions and bears.

### Narrative profile
The snow leopard's combat identity is positioning rather than brute-force supremacy. It is large enough to kill mountain ungulates substantially heavier than itself, but its real edge comes before contact. Broken rock, ledges and snow create routes that its broad paws, long hind limbs and tail can exploit with precision. Its coat suppresses visual detection, allowing the cat to turn terrain into an ambush weapon.

Once contact occurs, the mechanics are recognizably felid: forelimbs and claws establish control, the body rides or destabilizes prey, and the jaws seek the neck or throat. This is formidable at 50 kg, but ABS must preserve the large absolute gap to a 190 kg Amur tiger or much larger bear. The snow leopard should therefore score elite in Agility and high in terrain-dependent Special, while Attack, Defense and Raw Power remain constrained by body scale.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
Published size ranges vary because sources mix sexes, populations and body-vs-total length conventions. The canonical values explicitly separate head-body and tail before recombining for ABS total length. Wild longevity varies and is less certain than captive maximums. Leap distances are widely repeated by zoos but are not treated as instrumented maxima. No credible bite PSI or top sprint speed was found, so both remain unresolved rather than inheriting legacy catalogue values.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization
- **Versus Siberian Tiger:** snow leopard must be far lower in Raw Power, Attack, Defense and Toughness because the canonical tiger is ~190 kg versus 50 kg. Snow leopard can exceed it in cliff/snow Maneuverability and terrain-specific Special without implying greater overall combat power.
- **Versus Serval:** snow leopard is much heavier and more damaging, so Attack/Defense/Raw Power are materially higher. Serval remains extremely agile at smaller scale; snow leopard's 94.0 Agility reflects exceptional broken-terrain control rather than speed alone.
- **Versus Sloth Bear:** the 120 kg bear retains higher absolute Attack/Defense/Raw Power. Snow leopard dominates maneuverability and camouflage.
- **Anti-inflation check:** no score is boosted because the species is charismatic or an apex predator. Prey-relative feats do not erase body-mass gaps, leap claims do not become speed, dense fur does not become armor, and unsupported 300 PSI/64.8 km/h legacy values are rejected.
