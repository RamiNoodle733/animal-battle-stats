# Chameleon research report

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Roster name:** Chameleon
- **Proposed exact taxon:** Common / Mediterranean chameleon, *Chamaeleo chamaeleon* (Linnaeus, 1758), family Chamaeleonidae.
- **Living:** Yes.
- **Why this taxon:** The live profile currently identifies only family Chamaeleonidae, which is too broad for defensible species-level physical fields. This staging proposal resolves the generic roster label to the common chameleon, a well-studied representative species.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature female, non-gravid, approximately 45 g and 14 cm snout-vent length (SVL). Female selection is deliberate: ADW reports adult females 21-56 g versus males 17-37 g, so the larger sex is the appropriate absolute-combat representative. A separate Israeli field sample of 41 wild adults found no significant sex difference and a pooled 58.8 +/- 22.5 g, showing meaningful population/sample variation.
- **Dimorphism/variation:** sex differences are not uniform across populations. Reproductive condition can greatly inflate female mass, so gravid females are excluded from the canonical specimen.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- ADW: adult females 21-56 g; adult males 17-37 g; overall average 35 g.
- Western Andalusia reference data: adults 14-88 g excluding pregnant females.
- Israeli wild-caught sample (n=41): 58.8 +/- 22.5 g, with no significant male/female difference.
- **Canonical:** 0.045 kg. This is a representative non-gravid adult-female value, not a maximum.

### Length
- ADW reports adults 7.7-16 cm in the measurement used on its account, consistent with SVL-scale values.
- Israeli adults averaged 12.2 +/- 1.8 cm SVL.
- The tail is long and prehensile, roughly comparable to head-body length in chameleon morphology, so total length is substantially greater than SVL.
- **Canonical length_cm:** 28.0 cm total length, an editorial representative conversion for a roughly 14 cm SVL adult with a long intact tail. Confidence is moderate because published sources often mix SVL and total length.
- **height_cm:** 0.0. Standing height is not a stable or useful standardized field for this arboreal lizard.

### Speed
No strong instrumented species-specific terrestrial maximum was found. The live 36 km/h value is implausible for this slow, sit-and-wait arboreal species and is rejected. **speed_mps = 0.0 pending defensible measurement.**

### Lifespan
ADW reports a maximum captive lifespan of 3.6 years and limited wild longevity data. MoroccoHerps cites an estimated maximum of 5-7 years. Because the schema asks for a representative lifespan rather than a record, **lifespan_years = 3.6** is retained as the strongest explicitly reported species-specific value, with moderate confidence.

### Bite force and weapons
No reliable species-specific bite-force pressure measurement was found. **bite_force_psi = 0.0.** The mouth can bite during close conflict, but its major feeding weapon is the ballistic tongue rather than crushing jaws.

The tongue can extend to approximately body length and uses elastic-energy storage plus a sticky terminal pad to seize prey. Chameleon-family biomechanics demonstrates that ballistic tongue projection maintains unusually high performance across temperature changes: over a 10 C drop, projection velocity/power fell only 10-19%, while muscle-powered retraction declined by more than 42%.

### Defenses and structural adaptations
- Granular/scaly skin provides ordinary reptilian surface protection, not armor.
- Laterally compressed body and cryptic coloration reduce detection.
- Zygodactyl feet and a prehensile tail provide exceptional branch grip and stability.
- Color change is physiologically and socially important, but should not be treated as magical invisibility.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The chameleon's main specialized strike is its ballistic tongue. It is superb for insects and other small prey but is not a high-energy weapon against similarly sized robust vertebrates. Once contact becomes physical, the animal can gape and bite, but jaw damage remains limited by a body mass measured in tens of grams.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Common chameleons are deliberate arboreal climbers rather than sprinters. Zygodactyl feet clamp branches, the prehensile tail acts as an additional anchor, and slow controlled stepping gives excellent stability on narrow supports. This produces strong *positional control* in vegetation but poor open-ground escape speed. Agility therefore should be above its raw speed while remaining far below fast aerial or cursorial specialists.

### Endurance
The species is ectothermic and generally sedentary. It can maintain slow stalking, climbing, basking and territorial activity, but it is not adapted to prolonged high-output pursuit. Tongue projection is partially buffered against cooling by elastic recoil, which improves feeding performance without implying whole-body endurance.

### Senses
Vision is the standout system. Experiments on *C. chamaeleon* show simultaneous monocular tracking of two targets and context-dependent coordination: disconjugate scanning, binocular convergence/tracking for prey, and coordinated monocular tracking. This gives excellent visual surveillance and depth targeting without requiring the simplistic claim that the eyes are always fully independent.

### Intelligence and tactics
This is a sit-and-wait ambush hunter with visually guided target selection and territorial/social displays. It can coordinate scanning and prey fixation precisely, but there is no evidence for mammal-like planning or flexible tool use. Tactical score should reward sensory integration and ambush positioning without inflating general intelligence.

### Fighting and aggression
Common chameleons are solitary and territorial. Males can threaten rivals with color displays, body presentation and hissing, and mating-season studies document prolonged male mate guarding. Physical conflict can include biting, but avoidance/display is generally safer than sustained combat.

### Environmental strengths
- Dense shrubs and branches: excellent grip, concealment and 3D positioning.
- Moderate thermal variation: elastic tongue projection retains performance better than ordinary muscle-powered movements.
- Visually complex habitat: scanning eyes and cryptic body pattern are especially useful.

### Environmental weaknesses
- Open ground: very poor escape speed and little cover.
- Cold: whole-body locomotor performance remains ectotherm-limited even though tongue projection is comparatively buffered.
- Large opponents: tiny absolute mass, no armor, and limited damaging power.
- Aerial or highly mobile predators: slow whole-body repositioning makes detection especially dangerous.

### Matchup archetypes
**Best:** tiny arthropods and similarly minute prey vulnerable to tongue capture; opponents dependent on visual detection in foliage.

**Worst:** virtually any substantially larger vertebrate, fast ground predator, raptor, armored opponent, or animal too large for tongue capture. Its specializations improve prey acquisition and concealment, not absolute duel power.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Elastic Tongue Ballista** - elastic-energy storage launches a sticky prey-capture tongue rapidly and with relatively little performance loss across cooler temperatures, giving the chameleon a ranged capture mechanism against appropriately sized targets.
2. **Dual-Track Vision** - eyes can scan disconjugately and experimentally track separate targets monocularly, then converge into binocular prey tracking for precise strike guidance.

### Unique traits
1. **Five-Point Branch Grip** - zygodactyl feet plus the prehensile tail create exceptional anchoring and balance on narrow vegetation.
2. **Dynamic Chromatophore Signaling** - rapid color-state changes support communication, threat display, physiological state signaling and context-dependent concealment; this is not treated as perfect invisibility.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The common chameleon occurs around the Mediterranean and adjacent southwest Asian/north African regions, occupying scrub, woodland edges, orchards, gardens and other vegetated habitats. Adults are primarily arboreal.

### Diet and ecology
Primarily an insect/arthropod predator using sit-and-wait stalking and ballistic tongue capture. Its low-energy hunting strategy fits a slow-moving ectotherm: scan widely, approach cautiously, fix the prey binocularly, fire the tongue, then retract and swallow.

### Social structure
Mostly solitary and strongly territorial outside reproduction. Color displays, posture and threat behavior communicate state and reduce the need for costly physical fights.

### Reproduction and life history
Sexual maturity occurs at about one year. Southern Spanish work reports one clutch annually, with clutch size varying strongly with maternal size and environmental conditions. A study comparing wet and dry years reported 4-40 eggs and relative clutch mass around 60-70%, illustrating the unusually high reproductive burden on females.

### Conservation
Conservation status should be resolved against the current IUCN species assessment before production migration because regional populations and legal protections vary. This report does not invent a global status where a current authoritative record was not retrieved in-run.

### Adaptations
The species combines grasping feet, prehensile tail, laterally compressed body, visually controlled slow stalking, dynamic coloration, panoramic visual surveillance and an elastic projectile feeding system. These adaptations are highly coherent for arboreal ambush predation but do not translate into high absolute fighting power.

### Human interaction
Common chameleons are vulnerable to habitat alteration, road mortality and collection in parts of their range. Their slow movement makes road crossings especially hazardous. Captive longevity and health are sensitive to husbandry, thermal environment, hydration and reproductive burden.

### Genuine fun facts
- *C. chamaeleon* can simultaneously track two moving targets monocularly, but its eyes are coordinated by context rather than functioning as two completely independent cameras.
- The tongue's elastic launch mechanism loses far less performance in cooler conditions than the muscle-powered retraction phase.
- Adults are chiefly arboreal while juveniles can be more terrestrial.
- Female reproductive investment can be enormous: relative clutch mass around 60-70% was reported in southern Spain.
- The species reaches sexual maturity at roughly one year.

### Concise site summary
A tiny Mediterranean arboreal ambush specialist whose combat value comes from extraordinary vision, branch control, dynamic coloration and a ballistic sticky tongue. Against insect-sized prey it is a precision hunter; against most of the ABS roster its tens-of-grams body and lack of armor impose a severe absolute-power ceiling.

### Narrative profile
The common chameleon wins by controlling information and distance on a miniature scale. It moves slowly through vegetation, locking branches with split gripping feet and using its prehensile tail as an anchor while its eyes survey different portions of the scene. Once prey is selected, visual behavior changes: the eyes converge, the head stabilizes the target and an elastic mechanism launches the tongue before ordinary muscle contraction alone could match the strike's performance. This makes the animal far more capable than its walking speed suggests when the target is an insect.

That specialization must not be confused with general battle power. At roughly 45 g, a representative female has almost no mass-based protection against medium or large roster animals. The tongue is a capture tool, not a spear, and color change is not invisibility. Its best ABS categories are therefore Senses, Abilities, Special and vegetation-specific maneuverability, while Attack, Defense, Size and Raw Power remain close to the absolute roster floor.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- Adult mass differs substantially among sources/populations. Canonical 45 g is representative rather than a claim of a universal female mean.
- Length sources mix SVL and total length. Total `length_cm` is therefore moderate confidence.
- Lifespan evidence is sparse and differs between a documented captive maximum (3.6 y) and regional estimates (5-7 y).
- No defensible species-specific maximum running speed or bite PSI was found, so both remain unresolved instead of inheriting legacy placeholders.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization

- At 45 g, this animal must remain vastly below Bullfrog (0.5 kg), Capuchin Monkey (4 kg), Caracal (17 kg), Capybara (54 kg), Cassowary (65 kg) and Cape Buffalo (750 kg) in absolute Raw Power, Attack and physical Defense.
- Agility 65.0 is not a speed score. It rewards precise branch control and balance, while Speed 4.0 captures slow translation.
- Special 88.0 is intentionally high because ballistic prey capture, unusual visual coordination and dynamic coloration are rare mechanisms. This does not leak into Attack: the tongue is devastating only at tiny-prey scale.
- Intelligence 48.0 remains far below Capuchin Monkey 92.0. Sophisticated sensory control is not equivalent to generalized problem solving.
- Defense 8.0 prevents camouflage from being double-counted as armor or toughness.

**Normalization verdict:** the profile preserves the required tiny-vertebrate-to-megafauna gap while allowing chameleon-specific sensory and ballistic specializations to score strongly in the categories they genuinely affect.
