# Donkey (*Equus africanus asinus*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Common name:** Donkey / domestic ass
- **Taxon:** *Equus africanus asinus* (often also written *Equus asinus* in domestic-animal literature)
- **Status:** Living, domesticated equid descended from African wild ass ancestry.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature standard-size jack, **150.0 kg**. This is intentionally not a miniature and not a giant Mammoth/Poitou extreme.
- **Sex/dimorphism:** Sex effects vary among populations and breeds. A 205-adult Cameroon sample found no significant sex effect on measured dimensions and mean males 120.44 kg versus females 124.72 kg, while a 2026 Abruzzo breed study found larger males (292.4 kg mean) than females (242.5 kg). Breed/population variation overwhelms any simple universal sex rule. A mature jack is retained because intact males are the territorial/fighting sex.
- **Variation:** Domestic donkeys span extreme breed and management variation. The canonical specimen is a robust middle-sized working type, not a record individual.

## 2. Physical measurements
### Mass and dimensions
- Cameroon, 205 adults: mean live weight **122.11 kg**, withers **98.05 cm**, trunk length **101.37 cm**.
- Kabylie, Algeria, predominantly males: estimated mean weights differed by equation, **144.3 ± 23.9 kg** and **171.5 ± 28.8 kg**, illustrating method uncertainty.
- Abruzzo breed: males **292.4 ± 39.5 kg**, withers **129.1 ± 3.4 cm**; females **242.5 ± 54.3 kg**, withers **122.6 ± 6.4 cm**.
- ADW summarizes broad domestic/wild-ass shoulder height around **110-140 cm**, but domestic breed variation is substantial.
- **Canonical:** 150.0 kg, 110.0 cm at withers, 150.0 cm body length. These are representative working-donkey values, not maxima.

### Speed
Reliable instrumented species-specific maximum sprint evidence was not found. Popular 36-40 km/h claims are insufficient for a canonical physiological maximum. **Do not retain the legacy 36 km/h as verified.**

### Lifespan
Domestic donkeys commonly live for decades. ADW reports captive maximum values reaching roughly 47-50 years, while institutional husbandry sources commonly give about 25-40 years. A representative canonical **30.0 years** is used, explicitly not the longevity record.

### Bite force and weapons
- No defensible donkey-specific bite pressure in PSI was found. **`bite_force_psi = 0.0`** rather than preserving the legacy 100 PSI placeholder.
- Primary serious weapons are the **hind hooves**, delivered by powerful backward kicks. Forefeet can strike/stamp, and the incisors/canines can bite during conflict.
- Adult male equids can use biting, chasing, striking and kicking in territorial or reproductive disputes. No fabricated kick-force number is assigned because a directly applicable donkey measurement was not verified.
- Hooves are relatively small/narrow compared with horses, useful for sure-footed travel on rough ground.

### Defensive structures
No armor. Defense comes from 150 kg body mass, robust equid skeleton and musculature, mobility, threat detection, and dangerous counter-kicks rather than hide thickness.

## 4. Combat biology
### Weapons and offensive mechanics
A donkey's decisive close-range weapon is its hindquarter-driven kick. The long limb provides reach and a hard hoof concentrates impact. It can kick backward rapidly when a threat enters the rear arc. Bites, foreleg strikes and trampling/stamping are secondary options. A 150 kg jack therefore has far more absolute striking power than medium canids even though it lacks carnivore cutting weapons.

### Defense and toughness
The donkey is a substantial ungulate with a sturdy trunk and limbs, but it lacks horns, tusks, armor or thick specialized hide. Its best defense is spatial: face or angle away, keep a threat in kicking range, move over difficult terrain, and punish close pursuit. Large predators that survive or avoid the kick can exploit the neck, muzzle, abdomen and limbs.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Donkeys are adapted to dry, rocky landscapes and have relatively small, narrow hooves associated with sure footing. They are less specialized for extreme high-speed flight than horses, but deliberate footing and rapid close-range repositioning make them more agile than their old site score suggested. They remain less maneuverable than cougar-sized ambush predators or small canids.

### Endurance and water economy
Desert physiology is a major strength. In a peer-reviewed dehydration/rehydration study, donkeys endured **four days of water deprivation**; plasma volume fell 24%, and after drinking they restored baseline status within the first two hours. Another experiment found donkeys compensated accurately after 19 hours without water and had a higher thirst threshold than ponies. This supports high environmental endurance, but dehydration tolerance is not identical to continuous high-output fighting stamina.

### Senses
Large mobile ears support acoustic awareness, while equid vision gives broad surveillance. Smell and hearing are useful for social and environmental assessment. No exceptional sensory mechanism warrants elite roster placement.

### Intelligence and tactics
The stereotype of a cognitively poor or merely stubborn donkey is not supported. A 2024 controlled study of 12 miniature donkeys found object permanence, short-term memory of at least **90 seconds**, and performance on a detour task, broadly supporting equid-level cognition. In combat this translates to threat assessment and learned avoidance, not carnivore-style predatory tactics.

### Fighting and social behavior
Intact males can be territorial and defend areas against other males. Donkeys are social, but one individual's physical ratings are not multiplied by herd context. Their conflict repertoire includes threats, bites, chases and kicks.

### Environmental advantages
- Dry/arid and rocky terrain
- Long periods with restricted water
- Open terrain where approach can be detected
- Uneven footing that rewards sure-footed locomotion

### Environmental disadvantages
- Deep water and aquatic combat
- Confined spaces that prevent kick spacing
- Slippery terrain
- Sustained attacks by larger predators able to control the head/neck or evade the hindquarters

### Major weaknesses
No penetrating primary weapon, limited frontal reach compared with horned ungulates, vulnerable lower legs, no armor, and kick effectiveness depends heavily on angle and distance.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** small-to-medium terrestrial attackers that must enter hoof range; fragile opponents vulnerable to blunt trauma.
- **Bad:** large cats capable of explosive flank/neck attacks, heavily armored opponents, much larger ungulates, and aquatic specialists.

## 7. Abilities and traits
### Special abilities
1. **Hammerhoof Counter** - Powerful hindquarter-driven backward kicking makes the rear arc dangerous for predators that must close distance.
2. **Desert Rehydration** - Exceptional water conservation plus rapid restoration after dehydration supports function in arid, resource-poor matchups.

### Unique traits
1. **Rock-Step Hooves** - Relatively small/narrow hooves provide secure footing in rough, rocky terrain.
2. **Long-Ear Surveillance** - Large mobile ears contribute to acoustic monitoring and heat-management morphology characteristic of the donkey.

## 8. Expanded profile
### Habitat and range
Domestic donkeys occur worldwide in farms, pastoral systems, drylands and feral populations. Their ancestry and physiology are strongly associated with African arid environments.

### Diet and ecology
Herbivorous grazer/browser. Donkeys can exploit coarse, relatively low-quality forage and have long served as working animals in resource-limited landscapes.

### Social structure
Flexible social organization depending on resource distribution and management. Males may be territorial; domestic management can create pairs, groups or solitary housing.

### Reproduction and life history
Gestation is about a year and usually produces one foal. Sexual maturity occurs well before full adult longevity. Long life and low reproductive rate contrast with many smaller roster animals.

### Conservation
The domestic donkey itself is not treated as a wild conservation unit in the same way as its critically endangered African wild-ass ancestor. Feral populations can also create management and ecological conflicts where introduced.

### Major adaptations
Efficient water conservation, rapid rehydration, arid-climate tolerance, rough-terrain footing, large ears, and durable herbivore digestive strategy.

### Human interaction
Donkeys have been used for transport, traction, guarding and agriculture for millennia. Archaeological evidence from Abydos shows prime adult male asses with skeletal loading patterns associated with early transport use, demonstrating the deep history of working adaptation under domestication.

### Fun facts
- Experimental donkeys can remember a hidden object's location for at least 90 seconds.
- After four days without water, studied donkeys restored baseline hydration status within roughly two hours after drinking.
- A Cameroon study of 205 adults found strong population variation and no simple universal male-larger pattern.
- Donkey hooves are narrower/smaller than typical horse hooves, fitting their rough-terrain ancestry.
- Domestic longevity can exceed 40 years, although that is not representative of every working population.

### Concise site summary
A tough, sure-footed desert equid whose dangerous hind-hoof counter, substantial body mass and exceptional water economy make it much more formidable than its placid reputation suggests.

### Narrative profile
The donkey is not a miniature horse with worse stats. It is an arid-adapted equid built around caution, footing, resource efficiency and a punishing close-range counterattack. A representative jack gives away speed and specialized weaponry to many predators, but forcing entry into its rear arc risks a hard hoof strike backed by a 150 kg body. Its physiology is especially distinctive: controlled studies show impressive dehydration tolerance and rapid rehydration. Those adaptations justify high Stamina and meaningful Special scores without pretending they are armor or raw attack. Cognitively, donkeys demonstrate memory and problem-solving consistent with capable equids. Their battle identity is therefore a durable, terrain-aware counterfighter rather than a passive livestock animal.

### Future field proposals
- Instrument maximum sprint speed in healthy standard adult jacks over standardized footing.
- Measure peak hind-hoof impact force and impulse with force plates or instrumented targets.
- Quantify bite force in adult jacks without converting force to PSI unless contact area is measured.
- Compare maneuverability and fatigue curves among donkey, horse and zebra under standardized protocols.
- Expand cognition testing beyond miniature breeds and small sample sizes.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
The largest numerical conflict is body size. Domestic donkey breeds and regional populations differ dramatically, from roughly 120 kg Sahelian means to nearly 300 kg male Abruzzo means. The canonical 150 kg specimen is a deliberate representative working type, not a claim of a global mean. Maximum speed and bite PSI remain unknown rather than inheriting legacy placeholders. Lifespan varies heavily with husbandry.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization
- At 150 kg, Donkey must have clearly greater **Raw Power** and blunt-impact potential than the 15-18 kg Coyote/Dingo/Dhole controls, even though those canids have superior killing dentition and predatory tactics.
- Donkey Attack **48.0** sits below Cougar **55.0** because the cougar combines 70 kg mass with specialized claws, bite and ambush delivery, while preserving the donkey's much larger body and dangerous kick.
- Donkey Agility **58.0** stays far below Cougar 92.0 and medium canids around 82-84. It does not confuse locomotor speed with close-control agility.
- Donkey Stamina **77.0** is below Dhole 86.0 pursuit specialization but above Coyote 74.0 on the strength of exceptional arid-resource persistence. This is close enough to warrant future normalization when standardized exercise data are available.
- Intelligence **61.0** remains far below Crow 96.0 and below the researched canids in the mid/high 70s. The cognition experiments justify more than a low livestock stereotype, not elite cognition.
- No proportional-strength leakage or fabricated PSI is used.
