# Rhinoceros Research Overhaul

## Research status
- **ABS roster name:** Rhinoceros
- **Canonical taxon:** Southern white rhinoceros, *Ceratotherium simum simum*
- **Living/extinct:** Living
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime mature adult male, representative rather than record-sized
- **Research confidence:** high for taxon, body scale, horn biology, social ecology and locomotor order of magnitude; moderate for a single representative lifespan and maximum speed; low for any bite-pressure claim

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
The generic roster entry **Rhinoceros** is resolved here to the **southern white rhinoceros (*Ceratotherium simum simum*)**. This avoids duplicating the separately researched Black Rhinoceros entry (*Diceros bicornis*) and gives the generic entry the largest living rhinoceros species commonly represented by abundant southern populations.

ABS should use a **healthy mature adult male**. Males are larger than females and territorial adult bulls are the principal same-sex fighting class. The canonical animal is set at **2,300.0 kg**, representative of a mature bull rather than a record individual. ADW gives a broad adult species range of 1,000-3,600 kg and identifies males as larger; San Diego Zoo gives white rhinos up to about 2,300 kg in its comparative rhino account. Geographic/subspecies variation matters: the northern form is smaller and functionally nearly extinct, so it is not used as the canonical fighter.

## 2. Physical measurements and variation
### Mass
- ADW: adults approximately **1,000-3,600 kg**, with males larger.
- San Diego Zoo: white rhino up to about **2,300 kg** in its species comparison.
- SeaWorld: broad **600-2,260 kg** range, females smaller.
- **Canonical `weight_kg`: 2300.0.** This represents a large but ordinary mature southern bull and stays well below exceptional multi-tonne claims.

### Shoulder height
Institutional summaries generally place white rhinos around **1.7-1.9 m** at the shoulder. ADW reports an unusually broad 1.71-2.85 m figure, which conflicts with the tighter institutional range and is not used to set the representative value. **Canonical `height_cm`: 180.0**.

### Length
ADW reports mean head-body length around **3.35-3.77 m** with a separate 0.57-0.77 m tail; SeaWorld gives **3.25-4.25 m** length. **Canonical `length_cm`: 380.0** for representative head-body length. Tail is documented separately rather than silently folded into the field.

### Speed and locomotion
Toronto Zoo reports a short gallop around **40 km/h** and a high-stepping trot around **24 km/h** that can be maintained for at least 5 km. This is much better evidence for combat-relevant locomotion than generic internet claims of 50-64 km/h. **Canonical `speed_mps`: 11.1** (40 km/h converted), explicitly a short-burst natural-history value, not an instrumented race-track maximum.

### Lifespan
SeaWorld gives **25-45 years**; San Diego Zoo gives about **37 years average**. **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 40.0**, a rounded representative adult-life value within those institutional estimates rather than a longevity record.

### Bite force
No defensible species-specific standardized bite-pressure measurement was found. White rhinos are specialized grazers with no incisors or canines and use their horns/body mass, not a predatory bite, as the primary combat system. **`bite_force_psi: 0.0`** means reliable PSI is unavailable, not literal absence of jaw force.

### Horns
White rhinos have two keratin horns. Toronto Zoo reports the front horn averaging about **90 cm** and reaching 150 cm, while ADW reports a recorded longer horn of 166 cm and rear horn up to 55 cm. Other references give lower front-horn averages, so horn size is variable and affected by wear, age and sex. Females can carry longer/thinner horns, while the male's battle advantage comes more from greater mass and territorial fighting experience. For combat description, use **~60-90 cm as a representative adult front-horn band**, not a record horn as canonical.

### Hide and structural defense
ADW reports dense, tough skin with roughly **1 mm epidermis and 18 mm dermis** on average. This is substantial biological protection backed by a massive skeleton, broad chest, thick neck and deep muscle, but it is not rigid plate armor. Wallowing mud assists thermoregulation and ectoparasite/sun management and should not be scored as armor.

## 4. Combat biology
### Weapons and offensive mechanics
The primary weapon is the long anterior keratin horn, with the shorter posterior horn as secondary structure. A white rhino can thrust, hook, butt, shove and charge. The critical fact is not horn length alone: a pointed horn is driven by roughly 2.3 tonnes of body mass, a robust skull and enormous neck/shoulder musculature. Even a glancing body impact can be dangerous before the horn penetrates.

White rhinos are grazers rather than biting predators. Their broad square lips and cheek teeth are feeding adaptations, so Attack should be based on charge momentum, horn delivery and close pushing rather than fabricated bite numbers.

### Defense and durability
The canonical bull combines extreme terrestrial mass, thick dermis, deep muscle and robust bones. Healthy adults have essentially no routine natural predator pressure. Soft targets remain, especially eyes, mouth, ears, abdomen and distal limbs, and the hide can be penetrated by sufficiently powerful weapons. Defense therefore belongs among the roster's strongest living terrestrial mammals but below elephant-scale protection and body depth.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
A 2.3-tonne animal capable of a short 40 km/h gallop is remarkably mobile. White rhinos can accelerate, turn and use short charges effectively, but inertia limits fine evasiveness. The documented 24 km/h trot maintained for at least 5 km is important evidence that the species is not merely a few-second sprinter. Speed and Agility remain separate.

### Endurance and thermoregulation
Routine grazing, travel to water, territory patrol and the documented sustained trot support solid endurance. However, high-output fighting and galloping produce major heat load in a huge animal. Wallowing, shade and activity timing help thermoregulation. Stamina should be strong but not comparable to migration specialists such as pronghorn or reindeer.

### Senses
White rhinos rely heavily on **smell and hearing**. ADW and experimental cognition work describe relatively poor vision alongside strong olfactory communication. Wild experiments show southern white rhinos can discriminate both **sex and familiarity** from conspecific dung odors. Ears pivot independently, improving auditory sampling.

### Intelligence and tactics
The species maintains territories, recognizes social/chemical information, uses communal middens, distinguishes conspecific identity classes from scent, and has a varied vocal repertoire. These are meaningful cognitive and communication abilities, but there is no evidence for elephant, great-ape, cetacean or corvid-level flexible problem solving. Combat remains comparatively direct: assess, display, horn-butt, push, charge and disengage.

### Intraspecific fighting
Adult bulls are primarily solitary and territorial. Peer-reviewed work describes territorial males and agonistic Grunt/Hiss calls; ADW notes fights among bulls and confrontation around receptive females. Serious fights are not constant, but the horn is genuinely used in adult conflict rather than being a decorative structure.

### Predator defense
A prime adult male is a prohibitive target for normal African predators. Calves can be vulnerable, but juvenile predation should not reduce the canonical adult's Defense. The combination of horn reach, mass and burst acceleration makes frontal engagement exceptionally dangerous.

### Environmental strengths
- Open savanna and grassland provide charge lanes and normal footing.
- Dry firm ground lets mass and horn reach work together.
- Familiar territories and scent-marking networks improve spatial information.
- Wallowing and access to water help manage heat and skin condition.

### Environmental limitations
- Deep water removes much of the terrestrial charge advantage.
- Steep, highly technical terrain favors smaller mountain specialists.
- Dense obstacles shorten charge lanes.
- Extreme heat penalizes prolonged high-output combat.
- Relatively poor visual resolution can make precise tracking of small fast targets harder.

### Major weaknesses
- No shell or rigid full-body armor.
- Eyes and other soft tissues remain vulnerable.
- Huge inertia limits fine evasive changes of direction.
- Limited manipulation and attack-angle variety compared with elephants or grappling predators.
- Direct horn-first fighting style can be predictable.
- Cannot meaningfully contest aerial or deep-aquatic opponents.

### Matchup archetypes
**Strong against:** most terrestrial carnivores, lightly armored medium/large mammals, and opponents forced into frontal close combat without equivalent mass or penetrating reach.

**Difficult against:** elephants and the very largest megafauna, large hippos in water/very close quarters, giant extinct taxa, and rare special-mechanism opponents able to bypass bulk while avoiding the charge.

## 7. Abilities and traits
### Proposed special abilities, exactly two
1. **Two-Tonne Horn Charge** - a long anterior keratin horn concentrates a short rush from roughly 2.3 tonnes into a penetrating, hooking or lifting contact point, with the second horn adding close-range structure.
2. **Scent-Midden Intelligence** - communal dung sites and urine marking form an olfactory information network; experiments show white rhinos discriminate sex and familiarity from dung, helping territorial awareness and opponent assessment.

### Proposed unique traits, exactly two
1. **Square-Lipped Grazer** - the exceptionally broad, flexible grazing mouth specializes the species for cropping short grasses and distinguishes it ecologically from the hook-lipped black rhino.
2. **Megafaunal Trotter** - despite multi-tonne mass, Toronto Zoo reports a 24 km/h trot sustainable for at least 5 km and a short 40 km/h gallop, an unusual mobility profile for such a large land mammal.

## 8. Expanded profile content
### Habitat and range
Southern white rhinos occupy open savannas and grasslands in southern and parts of eastern Africa, primarily in managed and protected populations. Their ecology depends on productive short-grass grazing, access to water, shade and wallows.

### Diet and ecological role
They are specialized bulk grazers. The broad square mouth crops short grasses, and their large-scale grazing can modify grass structure, pathways and nutrient distribution. Dung middens also concentrate chemical and nutrient information in the landscape.

### Social structure
Southern white rhinos are the most social living rhinoceroses. Adult bulls are usually solitary and territorial, sometimes tolerating subordinate satellite males. Females can form temporary associations with other females and subadults, and mother-offspring bonds are prolonged. Peer-reviewed acoustic work identifies roughly 10-11 call types and individually distinctive calls.

### Reproduction and life history
ADW reports gestation around **530-550 days**, one calf, weaning around one year and independence around 2-2.5 years. Females breed slowly, roughly every 2.5-3 years. Males mature later and territorial status matters strongly for reproductive success.

### Conservation
The southern white rhinoceros remains conservation-dependent and heavily affected by horn poaching. The northern white rhinoceros has been reduced to only a tiny nonbreeding remnant, reinforcing why the southern subspecies is the representative living population for this generic roster slot. Conservation status and population estimates can change and should be refreshed at migration time from IUCN/recognized rhino authorities.

### Major adaptations
- Multi-tonne grazing body plan with enormous neck/shoulder musculature
- Two keratin nasal horns
- Thick dermis and robust skeleton
- Broad square grazing lip
- Strong olfaction and mobile ears
- Communal middens and urine-based territorial signaling
- Wallowing behavior for heat, sun and ectoparasite management

### Human interaction
White rhinos are major conservation flagships and ecotourism animals, but poaching for horn remains a severe threat. Rhino horn is keratin. Wild rhinos require substantial viewing distance because a short charge from a multi-tonne animal can be lethal even without deliberate predation.

### Genuine fun facts
- White rhinos are among the largest living land mammals, exceeded in mass mainly by elephants.
- Their name refers to the broad or wide mouth tradition, not white skin; the animals are gray.
- They have no incisors or canines, relying on high-crowned cheek teeth for grazing.
- Adult bulls use communal dung middens and may kick dung to amplify scent dispersal.
- Experiments show they can distinguish the sex and familiarity of other rhinos from dung odor.
- Their vocal repertoire includes grunts, hisses, pants and snorts, with some call types individually distinctive.
- Their horns are keratin structures rather than bony horns attached to the skull.
- A multi-tonne white rhino can still gallop briefly around 40 km/h according to Toronto Zoo.

### Concise site-ready summary
The white rhinoceros is a roughly 2.3-tonne territorial megaherbivore whose combat system is brutally simple: thick-bodied durability, a long keratin horn and enough short-range speed to drive that horn with enormous momentum. Strong smell, hearing and scent-based social recognition compensate for relatively poor vision.

### Rich narrative profile
The generic ABS Rhinoceros slot is best represented by the southern white rhinoceros, leaving the smaller, browsing black rhinoceros as its own distinct entry. A mature bull is among the heaviest living terrestrial fighters in the roster. Its front horn supplies reach and penetration, but the decisive multiplier is the animal behind it: roughly 2.3 tonnes of bone, muscle and body depth moving at a short gallop near 40 km/h.

That scale should not be mistaken for clumsiness. White rhinos can trot rapidly for kilometers, patrol territories and pivot into short charges. Their fine evasiveness is limited by inertia, but they do not need cat-like agility to dominate a frontal ground exchange. Defense likewise comes from bulk and tough tissue rather than fantasy armor. The hide is thick and the body deep, yet vulnerable eyes, limbs and soft regions remain real targets.

Their information system is quieter but biologically important. Adult bulls mark territories with urine and dung; communal middens carry social information, and experiments demonstrate recognition of sex and familiarity through odor. Vocal studies likewise show a richer social system than the stereotype of a solitary, unintelligent tank. ABS therefore gives the species competent Senses and Intelligence while reserving its elite scores for the categories actually supported by physics: Raw Power, Weaponry, Attack, Toughness and Defense.

## 11. Confidence, conflicts and cross-animal normalization
- **Mass:** high confidence that a mature southern bull belongs near 2.3 tonnes; broad all-adult ranges include females, regional variation and occasional exceptional animals.
- **Height/length:** moderate-high confidence; institutional ranges cluster tightly enough for 180 cm shoulder and 380 cm head-body recommendations.
- **Speed:** moderate confidence. Toronto Zoo provides useful 40 km/h gallop and 24 km/h sustained-trot values, but these are natural-history observations rather than a controlled maximum-speed experiment.
- **Bite PSI:** unresolved and deliberately 0.0. No force-to-PSI fabrication is permitted.
- **Horn length:** variable. Record female horns must not be used as a representative male weapon dimension.
- **Black Rhinoceros comparison:** the existing 1,200 kg black-rhino control is Attack 82.0 / Defense 76.0 / Agility 59.0. The 2,300 kg white bull is therefore raised to Attack 86.0 and Defense 82.0 for its much greater absolute mass, while Agility is slightly lower at 56.0 because of inertia. This avoids the inverted comparison that would result from treating black-rhino aggression as equivalent to almost double the body mass.
- **Elephant comparison:** white rhino remains below adult African Elephant in absolute Raw Power, Attack and Defense because elephants are several tonnes heavier and have greater reach/manipulation.
- **Hippo comparison:** both are multi-tonne tanks, but the rhino's horn/charge and the hippo's enormous gape/canines are mechanically different weapon systems; neither should inherit the other's bite/horn metrics.
- **Pronghorn/Reindeer comparison:** 40 km/h burst speed does not make the rhino highly agile or endurance-elite. Its Agility 56.0 and Stamina 73.0 remain far below specialized runners/migrators.
- **Special:** kept moderate because horn, mass and hide are already represented in Attack/Defense. Scent communication is distinctive but not a venom/electricity-like bypass mechanism.
