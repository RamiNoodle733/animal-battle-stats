# Gaboon Viper (*Bitis gabonica*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Gaboon Viper / Gaboon Adder
- **Scientific name:** *Bitis gabonica* (Duméril, Bibron & Duméril, 1854)
- **Site taxon:** *Bitis gabonica*, matching the current roster schema.
- **Living/extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature **female**, 7.0 kg and 130.0 cm total length. Females are the larger/heavier sex, so a representative adult female is the appropriate fighting specimen. This is deliberately below exceptional historical giants.
- **Taxonomic caution:** older literature and web summaries often fold western Gaboon vipers into *B. gabonica*. The West African form is now generally treated as *Bitis rhinoceros*. Record lengths from Sierra Leone and some extreme mass claims therefore cannot automatically be assigned to *B. gabonica* sensu stricto.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass and dimensions
Animal Diversity Web reports a 7-10 kg adult mass range, an average length near 1.2 m, and females as larger. Smithsonian describes the species as a very heavy viper and documents 5 cm fangs. Other southern-African references give an average around 90 cm and maxima around 1.2 m in that part of the range, showing substantial geographic/source variation. Older monographic summaries commonly give roughly 125-155 cm for adults, but some historical maxima are taxonomically contaminated by *B. rhinoceros*.

**Canonical recommendation:** 7.0 kg and 130.0 cm total length for a robust but non-record adult female.

### Natural weapons
- **Fangs:** up to about **5.0 cm**, the longest documented among venomous snakes in major zoo/natural-history references.
- **Venom apparatus:** very large venom glands and exceptionally high potential venom yield. Historical extraction literature reports several millilitres and very large dry yields, but electrically stimulated extraction maxima must not be treated as the amount delivered in every defensive or predatory bite.
- **Bite mechanics:** solenoglyphous hinged fangs fold against the palate and rotate forward during a strike. Smithsonian notes that unlike many strike-and-release snakes, Gaboon vipers can hold prey until it dies.

### Defenses
No armor or shell. Defense comes from stout body construction, cryptic disruptive coloration, immobility, warning hisses, a fast close-range strike, and the deterrent value of severe envenomation. These are not equivalent to high trauma resistance.

## 4. Combat biology

### Offense and weapons
The Gaboon viper is an ambush specialist. Its practical offensive sequence is concealment, very short-range explosive strike, deep fang penetration, venom injection, and often retention of prey. The long fangs give unusually deep delivery. Large venom reserves increase the potential dose, but venom quantity is actively variable, and a dry or reduced-dose bite is possible.

The venom is medically dangerous and predominantly cytotoxic/hemotoxic in clinical effect, with severe local tissue injury, swelling, coagulopathy and systemic complications possible. This is a matchup-changing biochemical weapon, not a reason to inflate raw muscular power.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Routine locomotion is slow and deliberate. This snake is not a pursuit predator. Its head and anterior body, however, can accelerate very rapidly over strike distance. Agility must therefore distinguish **strike reflex** from whole-body repositioning. In open terrain it is poor at sustained pursuit or repeated rapid direction changes compared with fast terrestrial vertebrates.

### Endurance
Energy economy is excellent for an ambush ectotherm, but low metabolic expenditure is not equivalent to high sustained combat output. A Gaboon viper can wait motionless for long periods, yet it is not adapted to prolonged chase or high-output fighting. Stamina is therefore below the roster's endurance specialists.

### Senses
Like other advanced snakes it combines chemoreception through tongue-flick/Jacobson's organ with vision and vibration sensitivity. Its broad head and ambush ecology support precise close-range prey localization. Claims of exceptional binocular vision are not used as a scored fact without stronger primary evidence.

### Tactics and behavior
Predation is patient and position-dependent rather than cognitively elaborate. The animal chooses concealment and waits for prey. Smithsonian characterizes it as placid and reluctant to bite humans, typically escalating through hissing/body elevation before striking when harassed. That lowers ferocity relative to highly confrontational species but does not reduce weapon lethality.

### Fighting and predator defense
The principal defense is avoidance through camouflage, then warning and a venomous strike if contact continues. It lacks grappling limbs, armor, high-speed escape, or prolonged wrestling ability. A successful bite can radically alter a matchup, but opponents able to attack from beyond strike range, exploit open terrain, or incapacitate the head rapidly are problematic.

### Environmental strengths
- Forest floor, leaf litter, dappled shade: camouflage is exceptionally effective.
- Confined close range: long fangs and venom delivery are maximally relevant.
- Warm humid habitats: normal physiological context.

### Environmental weaknesses
- Open high-visibility ground reduces the value of crypsis.
- Cold conditions suppress ectothermic performance.
- Long pursuit strongly disadvantages its locomotor design.
- Very large opponents may remain mechanically dangerous after a bite because venom is not instantaneous.

### Matchup archetypes
**Strong against:** similarly sized or moderately larger soft-bodied terrestrial animals that enter strike range and lack venom resistance; inattentive attackers vulnerable to ambush.

**Weak against:** armored/shelled opponents; very large animals able to inflict immediate crushing trauma; long-range or highly mobile attackers that can avoid the strike zone; specialized snake predators with relevant resistance/handling behavior.

## 7. Abilities and traits

### Special abilities
1. **Deep-Venom Drive** - solenoglyphous fangs reaching about 5 cm permit unusually deep venom injection, backed by very large venom reserves.
2. **Leaf-Litter Vanish** - geometric disruptive coloration and prolonged stillness can erase the snake's outline against forest-floor debris, enabling close ambushes.

### Unique traits
1. **Longest Venomous Fangs** - major institutional references place Gaboon-viper fangs at roughly 5 cm, an exceptional delivery apparatus among venomous snakes.
2. **Hold-and-Envenom Ambush** - Smithsonian reports that this species commonly holds prey after striking rather than immediately releasing it, enabling continued venom delivery and physical control of small prey.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
*B. gabonica* inhabits humid forest and forest-associated habitats in central/eastern portions of sub-Saharan Africa. Taxonomic separation of western *B. rhinoceros* means older 'Gaboon viper' range statements should be handled cautiously.

### Diet and ecology
A sit-and-wait predator of small to medium mammals and birds. Regional references also report prey extending to hares and other vertebrates. Its ecological role includes rodent control.

### Social structure
Predominantly solitary outside reproductive interactions. There is no basis for the live catalogue's generic 'Social' label.

### Reproduction and life history
Viviparous. Smithsonian reports females can produce 50-60 live young, illustrating unusually large litters for a snake. Lifespan is about 20 years in institutional references.

### Conservation
Recent IUCN-linked references list *B. gabonica* as **Vulnerable**, with habitat loss an important concern. Older sources may still describe it as common or use outdated assessments.

### Adaptations
Its broad leaf-like head, disruptive geometric pattern, heavy body, large venom glands and long rotating fangs form a coherent ambush system. The species invests in concealment and a decisive close-range strike rather than pursuit.

### Human interaction
Bites are uncommon because the species is generally placid and cryptic, but envenomation is a medical emergency. Low encounter/bite frequency must not be confused with low venom hazard.

### Fun facts
- Its fangs can reach about 5 cm, longer than those of any other venomous snake cited by Smithsonian.
- It can remain so still and cryptic in leaf litter that people may step close before noticing it.
- Smithsonian notes that it often holds prey after the strike instead of immediately releasing it.
- Females can produce extraordinarily large litters, with Smithsonian reporting 50-60 young.
- Historical giant 'Gaboon viper' records require taxonomic checking because the western form is now *Bitis rhinoceros*.

### Concise site summary
A massive forest-floor ambush viper whose extraordinary camouflage, 5 cm hinged fangs and high-capacity venom system make close-range contact dangerous, despite slow locomotion and a generally placid temperament.

### Rich narrative profile
The Gaboon viper is built around one tactical problem: letting prey come close enough that speed over distance becomes irrelevant. Its patterned body dissolves into leaf litter, its heavy frame remains motionless with little energetic cost, and its broad head houses an extreme venom-delivery system. Once prey crosses the strike envelope, the apparent sluggishness disappears. The anterior body launches, very long hinged fangs penetrate deeply, and venom is delivered through a system capable of holding a large reserve. Smithsonian's account that the snake may hold prey until death reinforces how different this is from a chase predator.

That specialization also defines its limitations. A seven-kilogram snake cannot trade blunt-force trauma with large mammals, and scales are not armor. Its excellent short-range strike does not translate into high travel speed or prolonged combat stamina. ABS therefore treats the Gaboon viper as a low-to-moderate physical combatant carrying a near-ceiling special weapon, rather than inflating every category because the venom is frightening.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Black Mamba:** Gaboon viper should be slower and less agile, with a heavier body, longer fangs and greater emphasis on venom volume/deep delivery. Special can remain near elite without copying mamba speed or aggression.
- **Versus Emperor Scorpion:** both possess venom, but the Gaboon viper's absolute mass, delivery depth and clinically dangerous injected dose justify much higher Attack while the scorpion remains tiny on absolute scaling.
- **Versus Electric Eel:** both are specialists whose unusual mechanism drives Special. Electric Eel's electricity is immediate and can affect targets without fang penetration; Gaboon venom requires a successful bite and time to act, so the categories must not be treated as equivalent mechanisms.
- **Versus Cougar/Elk:** despite lethal venom, the viper's mechanical power, durability and sustained mobility remain far below large mammals. Attack is not allowed to become a proxy for hypothetical eventual venom lethality.
- **Inflation check:** Weaponry 89.0 and Special 97.0 are intentionally the outliers. Raw Power 18.0, Defense 24.0 and Stamina 35.0 preserve the huge absolute gaps required by CALIBRATION.md.
