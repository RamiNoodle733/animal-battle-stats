# Sun Conure (*Aratinga solstitialis*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Sun Conure (also sun parakeet or yellow conure)
- **Scientific name:** *Aratinga solstitialis* (Linnaeus, 1758)
- **Exact animal represented:** the living South American parakeet *Aratinga solstitialis* of the Guiana Shield (northern Roraima in Brazil, southern Guyana, and possibly southern Suriname and French Guiana), in its wild form. Pet sun conures are the same species and are covered where relevant. The similar sulphur-breasted parakeet (*A. pintoi*) of the lower Amazon is a separate species and is not included.
- **Status:** Living; Endangered (IUCN); CITES Appendix II.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult male, **0.12 kg**, **30.0 cm** total length. Males are described as slightly heavier and more strongly built, with a longer tail and a larger head and bill.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** the sexes look alike and need DNA tests to be told apart reliably (Wancham et al. 2024). ADW notes that females tend to have shorter tails and lighter, slenderer bodies with smaller heads and beaks.
- **Population notes:** juveniles are mostly green and gain full yellow and orange plumage at about 18 months to two years. A red-factor colour form exists only in captivity.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The hooked bill is the only weapon. Parrots of this size bite far harder than other birds of similar mass; a related 85 g parakeet in the same genus measured about 54 N (Harrison et al. 2025). A sun conure can deliver a painful, bleeding bite to a hand, and hand-reared birds can be aggressive towards strangers (Wikipedia). But this is a 120 g bird that eats fruit, seeds, flowers and some insects, and its bite is a defensive tool, not a way to kill.

### Offensive mechanics
Bites are delivered from a perch or while climbing. The feet can hold food like hands, and the bill is used as an extra support when moving through trees (Wikipedia). Males defend the nest aggressively against potential predators.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Fast, direct flier and nimble climber. Flocks move between fruiting trees and palm groves, and birds climb through branches using feet and bill. Its small size and quick flight support high Agility.

### Endurance
Sun conures are reported to travel many miles in a single day between forest patches, crossing open savanna mainly when moving between them (Wikipedia). No tracking study was located.

### Senses
Normal parrot vision and hearing. Flocks are quiet while feeding but very loud in flight, and a separated bird's high-pitched screams carry for hundreds of metres.

### Intelligence and tactics
Curious and social; in captivity it learns tricks readily and needs constant mental stimulation. Its speech mimicry is moderate compared with larger parrots. No controlled cognition study of this species was located, so Intelligence is set below the tested large parrots.

### Social behavior and aggression
Very social, usually in flocks of about 15–30. Pairs are monogamous. Active nests have been seen with several adults and chicks of different ages, suggesting cooperative breeding (Wikipedia, citing field reports from Guyana).

### Predator defense
Flocking, vigilance, loud calls and flight. Feeding flocks near Karasabai in Guyana blend in against the red and yellow flowers of monkey-brush plants while they eat.

### Environmental strengths
- Edges of humid foothill forest, palm groves and savanna woodland.
- Open air between forest patches.

### Weaknesses
- About 120 g in absolute mass.
- No armor, fragile flying body.
- Small bill with no measured force.
- Heavily trapped; wild numbers are now very small.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** insects and very small animals.
- **Mixed:** other small parrots and songbirds.
- **Bad:** hawks, falcons, snakes, cats and anything larger that can catch it.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Hookbill Nip** — a short, deep hooked bill gives a sharp, painful bite far stronger than a songbird of the same size could manage, used to defend the nest and deter handlers.
2. **Flock Siren** — flocks fly in with loud screeching calls, and a separated bird's high-pitched screams carry for hundreds of metres, keeping the flock together and warning of danger.

### Unique traits
1. **Golden Plumage** — adults are golden-yellow with orange face and belly, but juveniles are mostly green and take up to two years to colour up; feeding flocks blend in against red and yellow flowers.
2. **Cooperative Nesters** — wild nests have been found holding several adults and chicks of different ages, a sign that sun conures may raise young cooperatively.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
The sun conure lives in a small area of north-eastern South America: northern Roraima in Brazil, southern Guyana, and possibly southern Suriname and French Guiana, with unconfirmed reports from south-eastern Venezuela. It is found mostly below 1,200 m at the edges of humid foothill forest on the Guiana Shield, in palm groves and fruiting trees, crossing open savanna mainly when moving between forest patches. Birds once reported along the Amazon are now known to be the sulphur-breasted parakeet.

### Diet and ecology
It eats fruit, flowers, berries, blossoms, seeds, nuts and insects, including red cactus fruit, *Malpighia* berries and legume pods (WPT; Wikipedia). Flocks near Karasabai often feed on monkey-brush flowers. It sometimes raids crops.

### Social structure
Very social, usually in flocks of about 15–30 birds, larger where fruit is plentiful (WPT). Birds rest, feed each other, preen and bathe together through the day. Pairs are monogamous.

### Reproduction and life history
Nests are in tree cavities, including hollows in *Mauritia flexuosa* palms. Clutches are 3–4 eggs, incubated by the female for about 23–27 days, with both parents feeding the chicks. Young fledge at 7–8 weeks and reach sexual maturity at about 2 years. Nests with several adults suggest cooperative breeding.

### Conservation
The sun conure is listed as **Endangered** by the IUCN and on CITES Appendix II. WPT gives a world population of about 1,000–2,499 mature birds and decreasing. Trapping for the pet trade caused the collapse: more than 20,000 birds were reportedly exported to Singapore alone in 2005–2006 (Wikipedia, citing BirdLife), and numbers in Roraima fell by 50–79% over three generations. Guyana and Suriname are the only Neotropical countries that still allow capture and trade of native parrots (Romero-Vidal et al. 2022). In its Guyana stronghold, easing trapping pressure has allowed early signs of recovery.

### Special features
- Golden-yellow and orange adult plumage; green juveniles.
- Very loud calls for a small bird.
- Uses the bill as a climbing aid and the feet like hands.
- Possible cooperative breeding.

### Human interaction
The sun conure is one of the most popular pet parrots, and far more now live in homes than in the wild. It is loud, curious and affectionate with familiar people but can be territorial with visitors. Import bans on wild birds by the United States (1992), Australia (1995) and the European Union (2005) have shifted most pet supply to captive breeding, but poaching of wild birds continues.

### Genuine fun facts
- Far more sun conures now live as pets than in the wild, where fewer than 2,500 mature birds remain.
- Young sun conures are mostly green and take up to two years to turn gold.
- A lost sun conure's screams can carry for hundreds of metres to find its flock.
- Feeding flocks almost disappear against red and yellow monkey-brush flowers.
- More than 20,000 were reportedly exported to Singapore in just 2005–2006.
- Wild nests sometimes hold several adults and chicks of different ages.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Sun Conure is a 120 g golden parakeet from the edge of the Guiana Shield forests, loud, fast and highly social. Its hooked bill gives a sharp bite for its size and it flies and climbs nimbly, but it has no armor and little mass. It is now Endangered in the wild because of trapping for the pet trade.

### Rich narrative profile
The sun conure is a bright yellow and orange parakeet that lives in flocks along forest edges and palm groves in northern Brazil and Guyana. Flocks are quiet and hard to see while feeding, sometimes blending into red and yellow flowers, but explode into screeching as they take off. Birds climb through branches using the bill as a hook, hold food in their feet, and fly fast and straight between patches of forest, sometimes covering many miles in a day.

Its beauty nearly destroyed it. Tens of thousands were exported for the pet trade, and the wild population has fallen to perhaps 1,000–2,500 mature birds, while captive-bred sun conures fill homes around the world. Wild behavior is still poorly studied: nests with several adults and chicks of different ages hint at cooperative breeding, but much of its ecology is unknown.

In an absolute matchup the sun conure is a small, light bird whose best defenses are flight, its flock and loud alarm calls. Its bite is strong for its size and painful to a person, but against anything larger that can catch it, it has no protection and very little power.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** ADW 100–123 g versus WPT 120–130 g; 0.12 kg fits both.
- **Lifespan:** all figures come from captivity or unclear sources; canonical 25 years is low confidence.
- **Loudness:** a figure of over 120 decibels appears on Wikipedia from a hobby website and is not used.
- **Wild behavior:** poorly studied, partly because of past confusion with the sulphur-breasted parakeet.
- **Bite:** no species measurement; the related 53.6 N value is context only.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Magpie (0.23 kg; A15.0 D12.0 Ag84.0 St61.0 I91.0 Sp58.0):** the magpie is about twice the mass with a bill and claws used on prey, so Attack stays above. The magpie's experimental memory evidence keeps it far above on Intelligence.
- **Versus Cockatoo (0.8 kg; A21.0 D13.0 I94.0 Sp76.0) and Macaw (1.2 kg; A24.0 I86.0):** much smaller and untested in cognition studies, so every physical and cognitive rating sits below except Agility, which is similar.
- **Versus Rosy-faced Lovebird (0.055 kg; A8.0 D8.0):** about twice the mass with a larger bill, so slightly higher Attack and Defense.
- **Versus Hummingbird (0.0038 kg; A5.0 Ag98.0 St82.0):** far heavier with a real bite, so higher Attack; the hummingbird's hover control and migration keep it well above in Agility and Stamina.
- **Anti-inflation check:** a strong bite for its size is not scored as absolute power; flight does not raise Defense.
