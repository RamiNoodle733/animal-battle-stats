# Hedgehog research profile: Western European Hedgehog (*Erinaceus europaeus*)

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Roster label:** Hedgehog
- **Live schema taxon:** Erinaceinae, a subfamily-level label that is too broad for defensible species measurements.
- **Staging resolution:** **Western European hedgehog / European hedgehog, *Erinaceus europaeus* (Linnaeus, 1758)**. This is a staging taxonomic resolution only and does not edit production data.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature **male**, **1.0 kg** and **25.0 cm head-body length**. Males are slightly larger on average, but seasonal mass variation is greater than sexual dimorphism.
- **Dimorphism and variation:** ADW identifies males as slightly larger; Vincent Wildlife Trust gives both sexes about 20–30 cm and 0.5–1.0 kg, while broader institutional sources report individuals up to about 2 kg. Body mass changes strongly with season and hibernation preparation, so a 1.0 kg adult male is more representative than a 2 kg upper extreme.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
ADW reports 0.8–1.2 kg for *E. europaeus*. Vincent Wildlife Trust gives 0.5–1.0 kg for both sexes, and the Natural History Museum notes animals can reach 2 kg. A canonical **1.0 kg** adult male avoids using an autumn maximum or record specimen.

### Length and height
ADW gives head-body length up to 265 mm and a tail around 20 mm; Vincent Wildlife Trust gives 20–30 cm. Canonical `length_cm` is **25.0 cm**. No standardized shoulder-height series was found, so `height_cm` remains **0.0** rather than inventing a value.

### Speed and locomotion
ADW reports routine travel around **110–220 yards/hour**, which describes foraging pace rather than top speed. The British Wildlife Centre states hedgehogs can sprint at **6 mph (2.68 m/s)**. Because this is a zoological-institution value rather than an instrumented maximum, **2.7 m/s** is retained with moderate-low confidence and clearly separated from agility.

### Lifespan
A 2023 peer-reviewed Danish study aged 388 dead hedgehogs. Mean age at death was **1.8 years**, with male life expectancy at birth **2.6 years**, female **2.1 years**, and observed ages up to **16 years**. Natural History Museum summarizes average wild lifespan as 2–3 years. Canonical `lifespan_years` is **2.5**, representing normal wild longevity rather than maximum potential.

### Bite and dentition
The permanent dental formula is 3/2, 1/1, 2/3, 3/3. Hedgehogs can bite small prey and use teeth during mating, but no defensible species-specific bite pressure measurement was found. `bite_force_psi` is **0.0**. No force-to-PSI conversion is fabricated.

### Spines and defensive apparatus
ADW reports about **5,000 spines** on an adult. The spines are keratinous, hollow and longitudinally grooved. Each is associated with musculature for erection. A specialized sheet of muscle, the panniculus carnosus, enables the animal to curl into a tight ball while erecting the spines and shielding the face, limbs and vulnerable underside. ADW gives individual spines at roughly **0.75–1 inch (1.9–2.5 cm)**.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offensive mechanics
The hedgehog has small insectivore teeth, jaws and digging claws. These are adequate for invertebrate prey and close defensive nipping but provide little absolute stopping power against medium or large roster animals. The spines are primarily defensive rather than active projectile or stabbing weapons.

### Defense and durability
The defining defense is the muscular curl. When threatened, the hedgehog retracts the vulnerable head and limbs and presents thousands of erected keratin spines. This is highly effective against many similarly scaled predators and makes a clean bite difficult, but it is not rigid armor. Large predators, crushing force, specialized badger predation, vehicles and attacks to an uncurled underside remain serious vulnerabilities.

### Locomotion and agility
European hedgehogs walk and forage extensively at night, can climb and swim, and can sprint briefly. They are not fast-turning pursuit predators. Curling is a defensive commitment that sacrifices mobility while deployed, so their excellent passive defense should not inflate Agility.

### Endurance and hibernation physiology
Hedgehogs are capable nightly foragers with sizable home ranges. They also undergo seasonal hibernation supported by fat reserves. Hibernation is metabolic economy, not evidence of sustained high-output combat endurance. Danish telemetry shows strong seasonal movement and home-range differences, especially expanded male ranging during breeding.

### Senses
Foraging relies strongly on smell and hearing, with vision useful but not exceptional. The elongated mobile snout supports close-range prey search in litter and vegetation. Sensory competence is meaningful for nocturnal navigation but not a rare roster-level special mechanism.

### Tactics and cognition
The species is primarily solitary and non-territorial. The main defensive decision is simple but effective: flee or curl when threatened. Males expand their range during mating season, and courtship can last hours. There is no evidence supporting high tactical cognition relative to corvids, primates or social carnivores.

### Fighting, predation and predator defense
European hedgehogs consume mainly invertebrates. A 2026 metabarcoding study of 209 hedgehogs found slugs/snails in **92%**, earthworms in **54%**, beetles in **54%**, Lepidoptera in **48%**, and Orthoptera in **54%**. ADW describes the species as solitary and non-territorial, with aggressive encounters uncommon. Woodland-hedgehog literature notes occasional male breeding-season aggression that is usually brief. Their combat biology is therefore defensive rather than ferocious.

### Environmental strengths
- Dense ground cover, hedgerows, gardens and woodland edges provide concealment and forage.
- Night conditions favor its sensory ecology and reduce exposure.
- Tight spaces can protect flanks and limit access by larger predators.

### Environmental limitations and weaknesses
- Open ground exposes a slow animal to vehicles and large predators.
- Curling prevents active repositioning while the defense is fully deployed.
- The face, belly and limbs are vulnerable when uncurled.
- Spines do not stop crushing, trampling or sufficiently powerful specialized attacks.
- Seasonal body condition and hibernation strongly affect available energy.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** small attackers that cannot penetrate the spine barrier or safely reach the underside.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized predators able to probe, flip or wait out the curl.
- **Bad:** badger-like specialists, large crushing predators, heavy hoofed animals and opponents capable of manipulating or overturning the animal.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and exactly two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Spineball Lock** — the panniculus carnosus contracts the body into a tight defensive sphere while thousands of spines erect, hiding the face, limbs and belly and making safe biting difficult for many attackers.
2. **Quill Erection** — roughly 5,000 independently rooted keratin spines can be raised together, converting the dorsal surface into a dense passive deterrent without treating the spines as projectile weapons.

### Unique traits
1. **Nocturnal Hedge-Runner** — strong smell/hearing, low-profile movement and nightly roaming suit cluttered hedgerows, gardens and field margins.
2. **Seasonal Hibernator** — major seasonal fat storage and metabolic suppression permit overwintering when prey availability falls, but do not count as combat regeneration.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and geographic range
The species occupies temperate Europe and extends eastward into parts of western/central Eurasia, with introduced populations in New Zealand. It favors hedgerows, field margins, gardens, parks, scrub and woodland edges and can occur from sea level to high elevations. Human-modified landscapes can provide excellent forage and nesting cover when connectivity remains intact.

### Diet and ecology
European hedgehogs are opportunistic ground-foraging omnivores dominated by invertebrate prey. The 2026 European metabarcoding study provides unusually strong modern diet evidence: gastropods were nearly ubiquitous, while earthworms, beetles, Lepidoptera and Orthoptera were also frequent. Anthropogenic food occurred in 29% of sampled individuals, showing substantial interaction with human landscapes.

### Social structure and life history
Adults are mainly solitary and non-territorial. Males roam more widely during breeding season. Gestation is roughly five weeks, litters commonly contain several young, and the mother provides care alone. Juveniles must accumulate sufficient mass before their first winter.

### Conservation
The IUCN moved *E. europaeus* from Least Concern to **Near Threatened** on **28 October 2024**. IUCN reported declines in more than half the countries in its range, with national estimates of roughly 16–33% over ten years in several areas. Agricultural intensification, habitat degradation/fragmentation, roads and urban development are major pressures.

### Adaptations
- Thousands of keratin spines with muscular erection.
- Panniculus-carnosus-driven defensive curling.
- Strong olfactory and auditory ground-foraging system.
- Digging claws and plantigrade feet.
- Hibernation and seasonal fat accumulation.
- Ability to swim and climb despite a low, compact body.

### Human interaction
European hedgehogs frequently use gardens and suburban greenspace. Roads are a major mortality source. A Danish age study found males more likely than females to die in traffic, consistent with their broader seasonal ranging. Pesticides, rodenticides, habitat fragmentation and prey decline are additional conservation concerns.

### Genuine fun facts
- An adult typically carries around **5,000 spines**, each a modified keratinous hair.
- The spines are hollow and longitudinally grooved, reducing weight while retaining stiffness.
- Hedgehogs can swim and climb as well as walk through dense ground vegetation.
- The oldest scientifically confirmed individual in the 2023 Danish sample reached **16 years**, despite population life expectancy being only a few years.
- Male home ranges expand during breeding season as males search for mates.
- A 2026 DNA study detected slugs/snails in **92%** of sampled European hedgehogs.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Western European Hedgehog is a small nocturnal mammal whose battle value comes almost entirely from defense. About 5,000 keratin spines and a specialized muscular curl can turn the animal into a compact spine-covered ball, frustrating many similarly sized attackers. Its bite, speed and raw power are modest, so large or specialized predators remain overwhelming threats.

### Rich narrative profile
A hedgehog is not a miniature porcupine and should not be scored like one. Its spines do not launch, and its offensive tools are ordinary small-mammal teeth and claws. What makes it unusual is the integration of thousands of spines with a body-wide muscular defense. When the animal curls, vulnerable anatomy disappears behind a dense field of erected points. Against an attacker that depends on placing a bite around a small prey animal, this can transform an apparently easy target into an awkward and painful problem.

That defense has strict limits. The hedgehog weighs around a kilogram, carries no rigid shell and has little ability to absorb heavy crushing trauma. Curling also exchanges mobility for protection. A sufficiently large opponent can manipulate, crush or wait out the animal, and specialist predators such as badgers can overcome the defense. ABS therefore gives the hedgehog a much higher Protection and Special profile than Attack or Raw Power without letting proportional defensive success inflate its absolute durability.

Its ecology reinforces that interpretation. The European hedgehog is a nocturnal roaming forager, not a pursuit hunter or territorial fighter. It searches ground litter for invertebrates, travels across fragmented landscapes, and relies on avoidance and curling when danger arrives. The result is a classic defensive specialist: difficult for the right small opponent to attack efficiently, but physically outclassed once its one major defensive system is bypassed.

## 11. Confidence, conflicts and cross-animal normalization

### Confidence and conflicts
- **Mass:** high confidence that ~1.0 kg is representative, but seasonal variation is large and 2 kg is an upper extreme rather than normal baseline.
- **Length:** institutional ranges vary. 25 cm is a defensible representative adult value.
- **Speed:** 2.7 m/s is retained only as a moderate-low-confidence institutional sprint figure. It must not be treated as instrumented physiology.
- **Lifespan:** 2.5 years represents ordinary wild life expectancy. The 16-year maximum demonstrates potential longevity but is not canonical.
- **Bite:** no credible PSI value found; 0.0 means unsupported, not literally no bite.
- **Taxonomy:** production's Erinaceinae label is broader than this staging species resolution and should be explicitly migrated only after taxonomy review.

### Cross-animal normalization
- **Versus Armadillo:** hedgehog Protection must stay well below rigid osteoderm armor. Spines deter bites but do not provide equivalent crush resistance.
- **Versus Porcupine:** hedgehog spines are shorter and its body far smaller; it must not inherit porcupine-level absolute weaponry or defense.
- **Versus Gecko:** hedgehog is physically larger and more protected, while Tokay Gecko remains dramatically superior in three-dimensional maneuverability and adhesive Special.
- **Versus Harpy Eagle / Gray Wolf:** the hedgehog's curl can complicate attack placement but cannot justify comparable Attack, Toughness or total combat capability.
- **Anti-leakage check:** 5,000 spines raise Protection and Special, but are not counted again as high Raw Power, Ferocity or Attack. Hibernation supports ecological endurance but is not scored as combat regeneration.
