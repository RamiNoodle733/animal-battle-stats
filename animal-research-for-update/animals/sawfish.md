# Sawfish

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Roster name:** Sawfish
- **Resolved taxon:** Largetooth sawfish / freshwater sawfish, ***Pristis pristis*** (Linnaeus, 1758), family Pristidae.
- **Why this resolution:** the live profile identifies only family `Pristidae`, which is too broad for species-level physical fields. This staging report resolves the generic roster entry to *P. pristis*, the largest and most broadly euryhaline living sawfish, rather than averaging five biologically distinct species.
- **Status:** living; Critically Endangered globally, and ESA-listed as endangered throughout its foreign range.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy large mature **adult female, 300.0 kg and 500.0 cm total length**. This is deliberately below published maxima of 500-600+ kg and 610-705 cm. Females are used conservatively because the species' largest adults include very large females and female life-history data are strongest, but evidence for a large, consistent adult sex-size gap is not strong enough to claim a precise dimorphism multiplier.
- **Population variation:** current taxonomy combines populations formerly called *P. microdon* and *P. perotteti* with *P. pristis*. Life-history information comes mainly from northern Australia and the Lake Nicaragua/Rio San Juan system. Regional differences in rostral tooth counts and reproductive periodicity are documented.

## 3. Weapons, defenses and combat mechanics

### Primary weapon: the rostral saw
The rostrum is a long, flattened extension of the skull edged by 14-24 large rostral teeth on each side. It is both weapon and sensory platform. NOAA documents lateral slashing through fish schools to impale or stun prey. Computational-fluid-dynamics work on sawfish rostra found negligible turbulent flow during lateral swipes, supporting a stealthy strike that is less likely to warn teleost prey through their lateral-line system before contact.

The saw is formidable but not a chainsaw: the teeth do not rotate, and damage depends on lateral acceleration, contact geometry and repeated swipes. The weapon is long and exposed, so confined spaces or an opponent that gets past the rostral arc reduce its leverage.

### Electrosensory targeting
The rostrum contains electroreceptive organs that detect weak bioelectric fields produced by other animals. This is especially useful in murky estuaries, rivers and near-bottom habitats where vision is limited. Electroreception is treated as a targeting/sensory advantage, not supernatural detection.

### Offensive mechanics
A sawfish can sweep the rostrum laterally through prey, producing cuts, impacts and stuns before ingesting disabled animals with the ventral mouth. Against a hypothetical similarly scaled opponent, its best geometry is frontal or oblique spacing where the long saw can intercept before body contact. A 300 kg, 5 m adult has substantial whole-body momentum behind the weapon, but no direct strike-force measurement was found, so no invented newton value is assigned.

### Defense and toughness
The largetooth sawfish is a very large cartilaginous fish/ray with a broad head-disc, stout trunk and dermal denticles. Size alone gives meaningful resistance to small attackers, but there is no shell, osteoderm armor or mammalian thick-hide equivalent. The long rostrum can deter approach, but deterrence is not double-counted as physical Protection.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Sawfish are benthic-oriented rays with shark-like propulsion. Adults use estuarine and coastal waters while juveniles can move hundreds of kilometers into river systems. The body is built for controlled swimming and bottom-oriented maneuvering rather than high-speed pelagic pursuit. The long rostrum increases reach but also expands turning clearance.

### Endurance
The species is capable of major habitat transitions between freshwater, estuarine and marine systems and adults can occupy broad coastal spaces. This supports solid sustained locomotor capacity, but there is no evidence for tuna-like continuous high-output cruising or billfish sprint physiology. Stamina is therefore moderate-strong rather than elite.

### Senses
Electroreception is the standout. Vision, mechanoreception/lateral-line input, smell and other ordinary elasmobranch senses contribute, but the report does not inflate all sensory modalities simply because electroreception is exceptional.

### Intelligence and tactics
No evidence supports mammal/corvid-level cognition. Combat-relevant sophistication comes from prey localization, positioning, rostral strike timing and flexible use of freshwater, estuarine and marine habitat. These are effective predatory behaviors but not evidence of complex abstract problem solving.

### Fighting, predation and defense behavior
Sawfish primarily feed on fish and also consume crustaceans and molluscs. The rostrum is used in prey capture and can be used defensively if the animal is threatened or startled. Sawfish are not generally aggressive toward humans, which argues against inflating Ferocity merely because the weapon looks dangerous.

### Environmental strengths
- murky estuaries and rivers where electroreception offsets poor visibility
- shallow coastal water with room for lateral saw sweeps
- freshwater-to-marine salinity transitions that exclude many strictly marine or freshwater opponents
- bottom-oriented encounters where the flattened body and dorsal eyes/spiracles suit the geometry

### Environmental weaknesses
- land or prolonged stranding is catastrophic
- cramped structure can interfere with the long rostrum's swing arc
- gillnets and entangling structures exploit the saw's projections, a major real-world vulnerability
- no heavy armor against comparably massive macropredators
- slow life history and low reproductive output are conservation weaknesses, not direct combat weaknesses

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** smaller unarmored aquatic vertebrates approaching within the saw's lateral arc; visually dependent prey in turbid water.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized sharks/rays where first contact, turning room and whether the opponent gets inside rostral reach matter greatly.
- **Bad:** substantially larger macropredators with powerful bites and enough mass to survive or bypass a saw strike; terrestrial matchups; restrictive structure that prevents sweeping.

## 6. Exactly two special abilities
1. **Electrosensory Saw** - ampullary electroreceptors distributed around the rostrum detect prey-generated electric fields, allowing targeting in turbid water and near the substrate when vision is poor.
2. **Silent Lateral Slash** - the toothed rostrum is swept side-to-side to stun, injure or impale prey; CFD research indicates sawfish rostra generate little turbulent disturbance during these swipes, reducing hydrodynamic warning to prey.

## 7. Exactly two unique traits
1. **River-to-Sea Giant Ray** - *P. pristis* uses freshwater river systems heavily when young and shifts toward estuarine/coastal marine habitat as it matures, an unusual euryhaline life history for a giant ray.
2. **Twenty-Plus-Foot Potential** - the species can reach at least 705 cm total length, making it one of the largest living rays, although the canonical ABS specimen is deliberately smaller than this maximum.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Historically *P. pristis* had an enormous tropical and subtropical distribution across Atlantic, eastern Pacific and Indo-West Pacific systems. It inhabits shallow bays, estuaries, coastal waters and rivers. Northern Australia remains a major stronghold, while many former populations are extirpated or extremely rare.

### Diet and ecology
Diet is primarily fish with crustaceans and molluscs also consumed. The saw combines prey detection and prey manipulation. Juveniles use shallow freshwater habitats as nurseries, where large marine predators are reduced; older animals expand into estuarine and coastal marine environments.

### Social structure
Evidence points to a mostly solitary animal rather than a coordinated social hunter. Aggregation can occur where habitat or resources concentrate individuals, but no pack-style combat bonus is assigned.

### Reproduction and life history
The species is aplacental viviparous. The best modern synthesis reports birth at 72-90 cm, maturity at roughly 280-300 cm for males and about 300 cm for females, age at maturity 8-10 years, litter size 1-20, and longevity around 30-36 years. Reproductive periodicity may differ geographically, with evidence for biennial reproduction in Lake Nicaragua and annual reproduction in Australia. This uncertainty is retained rather than flattened into one universal schedule.

### Conservation
The largetooth sawfish is Critically Endangered and protected under CITES Appendix I. NOAA lists it as endangered under the U.S. Endangered Species Act throughout its foreign range. Major threats include gillnet/trawl bycatch, deliberate retention, habitat loss and river alteration. Its saw makes entanglement particularly dangerous.

### Adaptations
- long rostrum carrying paired rows of rostral teeth
- electroreception concentrated around the rostral/head region
- spiracles permitting respiration while resting near the bottom
- euryhaline physiology spanning fresh, brackish and marine water
- broad pectoral disc and shark-like caudal propulsion
- countershaded brown/olive dorsal and pale ventral coloration

### Human interaction
Sawfish generally pose little danger unless threatened or mishandled, but the rostrum can seriously injure people at close range. Historically they were killed as bycatch, for fins and rostra, and because fishers feared gear damage or injury. Modern handling should prioritize protected-species release protocols.

### Genuine fun facts
- Sawfish are rays, not sharks, despite their shark-like body shape.
- A newborn already has a fully developed saw, temporarily covered by a soft sheath that protects the mother during birth.
- Juveniles can live far upriver in freshwater, while adults increasingly use coastal marine habitat.
- The species can reach at least 7.05 m total length.
- Its rostral tooth count varies geographically and between sexes.
- The saw is also a sensory antenna packed with electroreceptive capability.
- CFD work suggests the rostrum creates surprisingly little turbulence during prey-directed swipes.
- The modern species concept combines forms once treated as *P. microdon*, *P. perotteti* and *P. pristis*.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Largetooth Sawfish is a giant tropical ray armed with a metre-scale toothed rostrum that doubles as an electroreceptive prey detector. A representative 5 m adult combines hundreds of kilograms of body mass with long-range lateral slashes, excellent close-range sensing and the unusual ability to move between rivers, estuaries and the sea.

### Rich narrative profile
The sawfish's battle identity is distance control. Before another animal reaches its head or body, it must cross the arc of a long rigid rostrum carrying rows of hard lateral teeth. That weapon is not merely passive. Sawfish actively swing it through prey, and fluid-dynamics research indicates the rostrum can move with little hydrodynamic warning. At the same time, electroreceptors help reveal living targets even when mud, darkness or turbidity make vision unreliable.

That combination justifies high Attack and especially high Special, but not invulnerability. The animal has no heavy shell and no crocodilian crushing bite. Its 5 m body and long saw require room to turn and sweep, and the projections that make the weapon effective also make the species disastrously susceptible to fishing nets. ABS therefore treats it as a large aquatic specialist with elite sensory weaponry, not as generic marine megafauna.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
1. **Longevity:** modern life-history synthesis supports 30-36 y, while NOAA summarizes study-dependent 36-80 y and older growth modeling can yield ~80 y. Canonical is 36.0 y with the conflict retained.
2. **Maximum mass:** 500-600 kg and ~590 kg estimates refer to very large animals. They are not used as representative adult mass. The 300 kg canonical mass is a conservative large-adult modeling control with only moderate confidence because population-level adult mass distributions are poor.
3. **Rostrum proportions:** proportional length changes with age and sources use different rostral measurements. No false exact canonical rostrum length is assigned.
4. **Speed:** no strong species-specific maximum was found; legacy 36 km/h is rejected.
5. **Bite PSI:** no defensible measurement was found; the mouth is not the main combat weapon.
6. **Diet mechanics:** older accounts sometimes describe bottom stirring. CFD evidence argues against the rostrum functioning primarily by stirring substrate; the stronger supported combat/feeding mechanism is lateral prey manipulation/slashing.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes
- **Sailfish:** Sawfish Attack is much higher because a 300 kg adult has far greater absolute mass and a rigid toothed weapon, while Sailfish remains substantially more agile and speed-specialized.
- **Saltwater Crocodile:** the 500 kg crocodile retains clearly higher Attack and Defense due to directly measured 16,414 N bite force, osteoderm-supported body protection and grappling/crushing mechanics. Sawfish wins Special/sensory reach, not frontal durability.
- **Great White Shark:** a large great white should remain above Sawfish in concentrated bite lethality, pursuit performance and macropredatory finishing ability. Sawfish's advantage is stand-off rostral reach and electroreceptive weapon integration.
- **Manta Ray:** Sawfish should score much higher in Attack/Weaponry because its rostrum is a dedicated damaging tool; manta locomotor grace does not imply comparable combat lethality.
- **Piranha:** Sawfish absolutely dwarfs individual piranha damage capacity despite the latter's impressive bite relative to body size. No proportional-strength leakage is allowed.
- **Rhinoceros:** both are large weapon-bearing animals, but the 2,300 kg white-rhino control remains decisively above in Raw Power/Defense. Sawfish Special is far higher because electroreception plus the saw is a rarer mechanism.
