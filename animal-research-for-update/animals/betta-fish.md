# Betta Fish (*Betta splendens*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Betta fish (Siamese fighting fish, plakat)
- **Scientific name:** *Betta splendens* Regan, 1910
- **Exact animal represented:** the Siamese fighting fish as a species, represented by an adult male of the short-finned wild type: the green-brown fish of shallow marshes, floodplains and rice paddies in Thailand and neighbouring countries that the ornamental and fighting strains were bred from. Long-finned show bettas and short-finned "plakat" fighting strains are domestic forms of the same species and are discussed where they differ.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult wild-type male, **6.0 cm total length** and about **2 g (0.002 kg)**. FishBase gives a maximum of 6.5 cm TL and Wikipedia a usual 6–8 cm (often including long domestic fins); the mass is estimated from FishBase's body-shape length-weight relationship (W = 0.00977 × L^2.97, about 2.0 g at 6 cm) because no weighed wild sample was found.
- **Sex choice:** males are the fighting sex, with longer fins and stronger colour; they defend territories and bubble nests (Wikipedia; ADW). Females of fighting strains are also more aggressive than wild-type females (Ramos and Gonçalves 2019).
- **Population notes:** laboratory-reared eight-month-old wild-type males from Chiang Rai averaged 3.42 cm standard length, fighter-strain males 3.78 cm (Ramos and Gonçalves 2019). Wild fish are dull green, brown and grey and show strong colour only when agitated (Wikipedia).

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The betta's only weapon is its small mouth. Male fights consist of display, then ramming and biting at the opponent's fins, flanks and scales; confined fish without an escape route can kill each other (Wikipedia). In the wild it feeds on zooplankton, small crustaceans, mosquito and other insect larvae, and insects that fall onto the water surface (FishBase; Wikipedia). Against anything larger than another small fish, the bite is meaningless.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
A small, upturned-mouth surface feeder (ADW) that moves through dense vegetation and leaf litter. Wild-type and fighter males with short fins are quick and agile at close quarters; fighter males swim more and make frequent fast strikes at rivals, while wild-type males display mostly at close range (Ramos and Gonçalves 2019). Long-finned show bettas are slowed by their fins.

### Endurance
Wild bettas usually spar for only a few minutes before one retreats; ADW reports wild fish rarely fight more than 15 minutes, whereas cultivated fighting fish are considered poor if they fight for less than an hour. Aggression is metabolically expensive: losers show larger drops in muscle glycogen (Haller 1992), and displaying males meet their raised oxygen demand by taking more air at the surface rather than using their gills more (Alton et al. 2013).

### Senses
Good vision for colour displays, a lateral line, and surface-feeding orientation. Males assess each other through display, and during a fight the brain gene-expression profiles of the two opponents become synchronized in a pair-specific way (Vu et al. 2021).

### Intelligence and tactics
Contests are ritualized: males flare their gill covers, spread their fins and posture before escalating, and winners and losers change behaviour and brain gene expression after the outcome (Vu et al. 2021). The mirror test reliably triggers the same aggressive displays as a live rival (Ramos and Gonçalves 2019). This is sophisticated innate assessment, not flexible problem solving.

### Sociality and hunting
Solitary and territorial. Wild bettas live at a low density of about 1.7 fish per square metre; males coexist by claiming dense patches of plants as territory (Wikipedia). They hunt small invertebrates at and near the surface.

### Fighting and aggression
Aggression is the species' defining trait. Males defend territories and bubble nests; courtship is rough enough that females often end up with torn fins and missing scales (ADW). For centuries, breeders in Southeast Asia have bred the winners of staged fights, and fighter-strain males are more aggressive than wild-type males in every measured behaviour; even fighter-strain females are more aggressive (Ramos and Gonçalves 2019).

### Environmental strengths
- Shallow, hot, oxygen-poor water where air breathing gives it a large advantage (Wikipedia; FishBase).
- Dense vegetation and floating leaves that provide cover and territory boundaries.
- Drought: during the dry season many can survive in moist cavities in the mud until rain returns, though not total drying (ADW).

### Weaknesses
- About 2 g; negligible absolute power and durability.
- Needs access to surface air; displaying males must interrupt displays to breathe.
- Long domestic fins reduce agility.
- Aggression drives it into costly fights with its own kind.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** mosquito larvae, zooplankton and other tiny invertebrates; rival fish of similar size in foul, oxygen-poor water.
- **Mixed:** similar-sized small fish, where persistence and air breathing decide the contest.
- **Bad:** any larger fish, bird, frog, snake or mammal.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Labyrinth Lung** — an accessory labyrinth organ lets the betta gulp air at the surface, so it thrives in hot, shallow, oxygen-starved paddies; during fights it meets its extra oxygen demand by taking more air rather than working its gills harder.
2. **Flare Display** — males flare their gill covers, spread their fins and intensify colour before escalating to ramming and biting, letting rivals size each other up; in a matchup this can deter an evenly matched rival without contact.

### Unique traits
1. **Bred to Fight** — domesticated in Thailand at least 1,000 years ago and bred for staged fights for centuries; fighter strains are more aggressive than wild fish in every measured behaviour.
2. **Bubble-Nest Father** — the male builds a floating nest of mucus-coated air bubbles, catches sinking eggs in his mouth, places them in the nest and guards the eggs and fry alone.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Native to mainland Southeast Asia, centred on the central plain of Thailand from the Mae Khlong to Chao Phraya basins, the eastern Cardamom Mountains of Cambodia and the Isthmus of Kra, with records from Laos, Vietnam, Myanmar and Malaysia (Wikipedia); FishBase lists the Mekong basin. It lives in shallow, heavily vegetated standing waters: marshes, floodplains, canals, rice paddies and slow parts of rivers (FishBase; Wikipedia). Air temperatures there range from about 15 to 40 °C and water pH from 6.9 to 8.2 (Wikipedia).

### Diet and ecology
Carnivorous surface and mid-water feeder on zooplankton, small crustaceans, mosquito and other insect larvae, and fallen insects (FishBase; Wikipedia). It helps control mosquito larvae in paddies. Introduced populations can threaten native fish, frogs and other wetland animals (Wikipedia).

### Social structure
Solitary and territorial. Males space themselves among dense plants; females and fry use vegetation for protection (Wikipedia).

### Reproduction and life history
The male builds a bubble nest at the surface, often anchored to plants. After a courtship "dance", he wraps around the female, fertilizes eggs as she releases a few at a time, catches the sinking eggs in his mouth and places them in the nest, then drives the female away and guards the brood. Eggs hatch in 24–48 hours; fry depend on their gills until the labyrinth organ develops at three to six weeks (ADW; Wikipedia).

### Conservation
Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List (Wikipedia; assessment by Vidthayanon), threatened by agricultural and chemical runoff, pharmaceutical pollution and conversion of wetlands, including to oil-palm plantations. Captive bettas are extremely abundant, and ornamental strains have hybridized with wild populations of related species (Kwon et al. 2022).

### Special features
- Labyrinth organ for breathing atmospheric air.
- Ritualized flaring display before contact.
- Male-built bubble nests and paternal egg care.
- Can survive the dry season in moist mud cavities, but not complete drying (ADW).
- Enormous colour and fin variety in domestic strains, controlled by a few genes of large effect (Kwon et al. 2022).

### Human interaction
Among the most popular and varied pet fish in the world and the national aquatic animal of Thailand, which remains the main breeder and exporter (Wikipedia). Fish fighting for wagers has a long history in Thailand and Malaysia, and FishBase discourages keeping males in very small containers.

### Genuine fun facts
- Genetic analysis suggests bettas were domesticated at least 1,000 years ago, among the earliest of any fish.
- The Thai name *pla kat* means "biting fish".
- Wild bettas rarely fight for more than 15 minutes, but a bred fighting fish was judged poor if it quit within an hour.
- During a fight, the brain gene activity of two rival males synchronizes.
- A betta breathes air: it gulps oxygen at the surface with its labyrinth organ.
- The father builds a bubble nest and catches falling eggs in his mouth.
- A mirror is enough to make a male betta flare and display.

### Concise site-ready summary
The betta, or Siamese fighting fish, is a 2-gram paddy fish that breathes air and fights with a ferocity bred into it for centuries. It flares, rams and bites rival males, but on the absolute scale it is among the smallest and weakest fighters on the roster.

### Rich narrative profile
In the shallow, sun-baked rice paddies and marshes of Thailand, the water can be warm, stagnant and nearly empty of oxygen. The wild betta, a small, dull green-brown fish, thrives there because it can breathe air through its labyrinth organ. Males stake out patches of dense plants, build floating bubble nests, and chase off any rival male that comes near.

Their contests are ritualized. Two males flare their gill covers, spread their fins and flush with colour before escalating into ramming and biting at fins and scales. In the wild, most fights end within minutes when one fish backs away. People in Thailand and Malaysia began collecting these fish centuries ago and breeding the winners of staged fights, producing domestic fighting strains that attack faster and keep going for an hour or more, as well as the flowing, jewel-coloured show bettas sold today.

For all its reputation, the betta is a 2-gram fish with a tiny mouth. Its ferocity is real and scores near the top of the roster, but its Attack and Defense sit near the floor. Its genuine edge is survival: in foul, oxygen-poor water, where rivals suffocate, the betta simply rises to the surface and takes a breath.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Length:** FishBase's 6.5 cm maximum versus Wikipedia's 6–8 cm and ADW's 7.5 cm average, which likely include long domestic fins.
- **Mass:** no weighed sample; 2 g is a length-weight estimate.
- **Lifespan:** only a 2-year captive average and maximum were found; longer pet lifespans are commonly claimed but unverified here.
- **Wild versus domestic behaviour:** fighting strains are far more aggressive and persistent than wild fish; ratings use the wild-type male with domestic differences noted.
- **Speed:** unmeasured.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Piranha (1.2 kg; A37 D20 Ag82):** six hundred times heavier with shearing teeth; far above in every physical rating. The betta keeps a much higher Ferocity substat (80.0 vs 55.0).
- **Versus Goldfish (0.1 kg, this batch):** fifty times lighter and far more aggressive; slightly lower Attack because of mass, higher Agility at close range, lower Intelligence.
- **Versus Black Widow (1 g; A24 D2) and Africanized Honey Bee (A7):** similar or smaller mass, but venom lifts their Attack; the betta has no venom and stays at 2.5.
- **Versus Mantis Shrimp (0.08 kg; A34 f78):** similar ferocity, but the mantis shrimp's club is a real weapon; the betta's Ferocity (80.0) sits slightly above it while its Attack stays near the floor.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the "fighting fish" name is expressed in Ferocity, not in Attack, Defense or Special.
