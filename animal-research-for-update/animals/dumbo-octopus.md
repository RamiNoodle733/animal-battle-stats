# Dumbo Octopus (*Grimpoteuthis* spp.) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Dumbo Octopus
- **Scientific name:** *Grimpoteuthis* spp. (family Grimpoteuthidae)
- **Exact animal represented:** Finned octopuses in the genus Grimpoteuthis. The common name can also be used loosely for other finned octopuses; this entry does not include them.
- **Living / extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** The profile uses a measured 36.5 g, 21.3 cm Grimpoteuthis feitiana specimen as its size reference; its maturity and sex were not reported. The battle ratings describe a small, healthy adult form.
- **Population notes:** Size and depth vary substantially among species. The photographed octopus is identified to genus.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
A collected Grimpoteuthis feitiana specimen weighed 36.5 g. Use the measured specimen as the small-bodied reference; there is no reliable genus-wide adult average.

### Height
Height is not a useful standard measure for a swimming octopus.

### Length
The same specimen measured 212.6 mm total length and 43.6 mm mantle length. A different species, Grimpoteuthis challengeri, has recorded total lengths up to 370 mm. The catalogue length includes the arms.

### Speed
Fin swimming, takeoff and crawling are documented. No reliable top speed was found.

### Lifespan
No directly verified adult lifespan was found for the represented form.

### Bite and venom
A small beak handles food. No reliable bite pressure or evidence for a venom-based fighting ability was found.

### Defensive structure
The body is soft. The internal structure that supports the fins is not external armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The arm web encloses small prey at close range. Suckers retain food while the fingerlike cirri between them help move it toward the mouth. This is effective for catching small invertebrates and offers little leverage against a much heavier animal.

### Defense and maneuverability
Paired fins provide control during swimming. Takeoff from the bottom and fin swimming give it an escape route into open water. The web is flexible tissue, so it does not provide much protection from a direct strike.

### Endurance, senses and tactics
Slow movement near the seafloor, deliberate prey enclosure and the ability to move up into the water column are better supported than sustained pursuit or complex fighting tactics. Suckers and cirri help handle food at close range; there is no measured long-range sensory advantage.

### Weaknesses
- Soft tissues and little external protection.
- Short effective reach and very low absolute power.
- Closely tied to deep water; few options if pinned by a stronger animal.

### Good matchups
- Very small bottom-dwelling invertebrates it can enclose under the arm web.

### Bad matchups
- Larger fish and fast cephalopods that can strike from outside the arm web.
- Hard-shelled animals too large to enclose.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Fin Glide** — The paired fins propel and steer the octopus as it leaves the seafloor or moves through the water above it.
2. **Web Enclosure** — The arms spread their connecting web over small prey, then draw it in toward the mouth.

### Unique traits
1. **Cirri Rows** — Small, fingerlike projections line the arms between suckers and help guide food inward.
2. **Deep Seafloor Dweller** — Many sightings are near the bottom in cold, dark water. Grimpoteuthis has been filmed in the Java Trench at nearly 7,000 m.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Dumbo octopuses live in deep waters in multiple ocean basins, often at or just above the bottom. The 1,240 m record for the measured specimen and a sighting at 6,957 m are from different animals. The latter is an exceptional record rather than a usual depth.

### Diet and ecology
Bottom-dwelling finned octopuses commonly eat amphipods, marine worms and other small invertebrates. Observations of Grimpoteuthis show the arm web and cirri being used to gather and handle small prey.

### Social structure
Most field observations describe individuals. There is no reliable evidence of regular group hunting or a day and night activity schedule for the represented form.

### Reproduction and life history
A Grimpoteuthis hatchling was recorded emerging from an egg case on a deep-sea octocoral. Its fins, arms and suckers were already developed enough for movement and handling small prey.

### Conservation
Grimpoteuthis covers multiple species. There is no single genus-wide Red List category. Rare encounters make abundance and population trends difficult to assess.

### Special features
Two fins project from the mantle, one on each side. The arms are joined by a flexible web, with suckers and short cirri along the inner surfaces.

### Human interaction
Deep-sea cameras and occasional scientific collections provide most direct observations. The animal usually lives far below normal diving depths.

### Genuine fun facts
- The ear-shaped parts are swimming fins.
- Its eight arms are linked by a flexible web.
- Cirri sit between the suckers along the arms.
- It can swim clear of the seafloor using its fins.
- A dumbo octopus was filmed in the Java Trench at 6,957 m.
- One filmed hatchling already had functioning fins and well-developed suckers.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Dumbo Octopus steers with two ear-shaped fins in the dark waters near the seafloor. Its eight arms spread a flexible web over small prey, then guide the food toward its beak.

### Rich narrative profile
Near the seafloor, a dumbo octopus can rise into open water and steer with the fins on either side of its mantle. The fins work with the flexible web between its arms, letting the octopus hold and change posture without having to chase prey at speed.

The same web can spread over a patch of small prey. On the inner surface of the arms, suckers and the little projections called cirri help move the food toward the beak. Small crustaceans and marine worms fit this way of feeding.

The name Dumbo Octopus covers multiple species in Grimpoteuthis. One small measured specimen from a western Pacific seamount was just over 21 cm long. An octopus filmed far deeper in the Java Trench was identified to the same genus, but it should not be treated as the same species or size.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Blue-Ringed Octopus:** Both are small and soft. Dumbo stays far below in Attack and Special because it has no documented comparable venom, and it has fewer documented near-reef escape options.
- **Octopus (Giant Pacific):** Dumbo stays below its reach, strength, maneuverability and evidence for flexible problem solving.
- **Vampire Squid:** Dumbo has a more direct prey-enclosing move. The vampire squid gets the stronger defensive special because of its light display, without gaining a high physical damage score.
