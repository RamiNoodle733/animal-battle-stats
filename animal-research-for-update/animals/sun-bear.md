# Sun Bear (*Helarctos malayanus*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Sun bear (Malayan sun bear, honey bear)
- **Scientific name:** *Helarctos malayanus* (Raffles, 1821)
- **Exact animal represented:** the sun bear of South and Southeast Asian tropical forests, the only species in the genus *Helarctos* and the smallest living bear. The site photo shows the Bornean form, *H. m. euryspilus*, at Sepilok in Sabah.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy prime adult male, **50.0 kg**, about **70 cm** at the shoulder and **130 cm** head-body, representing a mainland or Sumatran animal rather than the lighter Bornean form.
- **Sex choice:** males are the larger sex, by roughly 10–20% according to San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance and Wikipedia, and by about one-third according to the International Association for Bear Research and Management (IBA).
- **Population variation:** published adult ranges run from about 23–65 kg (SDZWA, ADW, Wikipedia) to 30–90 kg (IBA, covering all populations). IBA notes the Bornean subspecies is about 40% lighter than mainland and Sumatran bears, so the photographed Sepilok bear is smaller than the canonical specimen. A 50 kg male is the same mass as the animal used in the published voluntary bite-force work.

- Wikipedia: captive lifespan generally over 20 years; one bear lived 34 years.

- **Confidence:** Low.

### Bite and dentition
- Van der Meijden et al. (2023, *Journal of Experimental Biology*) measured voluntary maximum bite force in two Malayan sun bears using reward-dispensing bite plates. Wikipedia, citing this work, reports that a **50 kg sun bear bites with a maximum force of about 1,907–2,021 N at the rear molars**.
- Wikipedia also notes very large canines and a bite force quotient that is high for the body size, possibly linked to tearing into tropical hardwood for insects and honey.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** The measurement is force, not pressure. No tooth contact area is available, so it is not converted to PSI.

### Claws and tongue
- SDZWA: claws about **10 cm (4 in)** long; sickle-shaped and heavy on the forefeet (Wikipedia).
- IBA: claws comparable in size to those of a brown bear.
- IBA: the longest tongue of any bear, stated as reaching up to **46 cm**; this is a maximum figure without a cited primary measurement and is treated with caution.

### Defensive structure
- Short, dense black coat and notably loose skin. SDZWA states that if a predator latches on, a sun bear can turn inside its loose skin and bite back. No armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The sun bear packs a strong weapon set into a small bear. Its bite is unusually strong for its size, with about 2,000 N measured at the molars in a 50 kg animal, and its canines are large. Its forepaws carry heavy, sickle-shaped claws around 10 cm long that it uses to rip open hardwood trunks and termite mounds. In a fight, it combines bites with claw swipes and grappling. It is not a specialised large-prey killer: vertebrates such as birds, rodents, lizards and the occasional deer are a minor part of the diet.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
It is the most arboreal of the bears. Inward-turned front feet, hairless soles, a flattened chest and powerful forelimbs let it climb well and rest in trees 2–7 m above the ground. On the ground it walks with a pigeon-toed gait and has no evidence of high running speed. Maneuverability is excellent in trees and moderate on the ground.

### Endurance
Sun bears do not hibernate and forage year-round across tropical forest. Home-range and daily-movement data exist from radio-tracking in Borneo, but no endurance-specific measurements were found. Stamina is rated as ordinary for a medium mammal.

### Senses
Foraging relies heavily on smell to find insect nests and honey inside trees. Detailed sensory thresholds were not located, so the score is moderate.

### Intelligence and tactics
Taylor et al. (2019) showed that group-housed sun bears, normally solitary, mimic the exact facial expressions of playmates and produce open-mouth faces mainly when the other bear is watching. Wikipedia reports a captive bear that watched sugar being locked in a cupboard and later opened the lock with a claw. Together these support good social cognition and problem solving for a carnivoran, below the great apes.

### Fighting and aggression
Sun bears usually avoid people and do not attack unless provoked, injured or with cubs, and mothers defend cubs aggressively. Aggressive bears bark, growl and roar. Tigers are the main predator; leopards and dholes are recorded only occasionally (Naing et al. 2020). Wikipedia reports one tiger–sun bear fight that ended with both animals dead, and a female sun bear swallowed by a large reticulated python.

### Predator defense
Climbing is the first escape. When caught, the loose skin, strong bite and claws make it a costly target: SDZWA describes it twisting inside its skin to bite an attacker.

### Environmental strengths and limitations
- Dense tropical forest with large trees favors climbing escape and ambush from above.
- Open ground removes its climbing advantage and exposes its short legs.
- Degraded or logged forest is avoided.

### Weaknesses
- Small mass for a bear; outweighed by most large predators.
- No evidence of speed on the ground.
- No armor; defense depends on skin, coat and fighting back.
- Primarily an insect and fruit eater, not a trained large-prey killer.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** medium carnivores and herbivores of 20–50 kg that cannot follow it into trees or cannot match its bite and claws at close range.
- **Mixed:** similar-mass predators such as leopards or wolves, where grappling and bite placement decide.
- **Bad:** tigers, large bears, big constrictors and any heavy megafauna.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Loose-Skin Counterbite** — the skin is loose enough that a bear gripped by a predator can twist around inside it and bite back, turning a hold into a close-range counterattack.
2. **Hardwood Ripper** — 10 cm sickle claws and a bite of about 2,000 N at the molars let it tear open living tree trunks and termite mounds, the same tools it swings in a fight.

### Unique traits
1. **Smallest Bear** — the lightest living bear species at around 25–65 kg in most accounts, and the most arboreal, often resting and sunbathing in trees 2–7 m up.
2. **Longest Bear Tongue** — the longest tongue of any bear, used to lick insects, larvae and honey out of narrow cavities.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Tropical evergreen and lowland forests of South and Southeast Asia, from north-eastern India and Bangladesh through mainland Southeast Asia to Sumatra and Borneo. Mangroves are used near preferred forest. The species avoids heavily logged forest and areas near settlements.

### Diet and ecology
An omnivore with a broad diet: ants, bees and bee larvae, beetles, termites, honey, fruit and seeds, plus occasional birds, rodents, lizards, deer and scavenged tiger kills. It tears open hollow trees for bees' nests and breaks termite mounds, licking and sucking out the contents while holding pieces with its forepaws. Several bears may gather at large fruiting trees.

### Social structure
Solitary in the wild apart from mothers with cubs, though group-housed rescued bears form complex play relationships and mimic one another's facial expressions.

### Reproduction and life history
Breeding occurs year-round. Litters are one or two cubs of about 325 g, born in tree cavities. Cubs stay with the mother for about two to three years; sexual maturity comes at two to four years (Wikipedia; SDZWA gives three to five).

### Conservation
Listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List and on CITES Appendix I. IBA reports a range-wide decline of more than 30% in 30 years, with populations in Bangladesh and China close to local extinction. Deforestation, forest fires and hunting for the pet and bear-part trade are the main threats; human–bear conflict is a smaller one.

### Special features
- Sickle-shaped foreclaws about 10 cm long.
- Very large canines and a strong bite for its body size.
- Loose skin that allows turning within a predator's grip.
- Inward-turned front feet and hairless soles for climbing.
- Exceptionally long tongue.
- Pale chest patch on a short black coat.

### Human interaction
Crop damage rose after the 1997–98 El Niño drought and fires in Borneo reduced forest fruit; farmers were most hostile when bears killed old coconut trees by climbing and feeding in them, and metal sheeting on trunks deterred bears for at least a few years (Fredriksson 2005). Cubs were often taken as pets because of their timid nature, and bear parts are still found for sale in traditional medicine shops in Sabah and Sarawak.

### Genuine fun facts
- It is the smallest bear species, yet its claws are comparable in size to a brown bear's.
- A 50 kg sun bear was measured biting with about **2,000 N** at the molars using a reward-driven bite plate.
- Sun bears copy the exact facial expressions of their playmates, a precision previously reported mainly in humans and gorillas.
- A captive sun bear watched sugar being locked away and later opened the lock with a claw.
- One recorded fight between a tiger and a sun bear ended with both animals dead.
- The Malay and Indonesian name *beruang madu* means "honey bear".

### Concise site-ready summary
The smallest bear, but one of the fiercest for its size: the sun bear carries 10 cm sickle claws, a bite of about 2,000 N at the molars and loose skin that lets it twist round and bite anything that grabs it. It climbs better than any other bear, though at around 50 kg it is badly outmatched by tigers and big bears.

### Rich narrative profile
The sun bear looks like a compact, short-coated black bear with an oversized set of tools. Most adults weigh 25 to 65 kg, less than half the size of the black, sloth and spectacled bears on the roster, yet its claws are about 10 cm long and its bite is strong enough for its size that researchers have linked it to ripping open living hardwood trunks for bees and larvae. In a close fight those tools make it a costly opponent for any medium-sized predator.

Its defense is built around getting up a tree first and fighting back second. It is the most arboreal bear, climbing with inward-turned forefeet and bare soles and resting in branches several metres above the ground. When something does get hold of it, the loose skin gives it room to twist and bite, and zoo staff describe exactly that response. Tigers are its main natural enemy, and one recorded tiger–sun bear clash killed both animals, a sign of how expensive it can be to finish one off.

The sun bear is also smarter than its size suggests. Rescued bears housed together mimic one another's facial expressions with remarkable precision, and a captive bear once opened a locked cupboard with a claw. Its limits are real, though: it is slow and short-legged on open ground, it has no armor, and at around 50 kg it is simply outweighed by the big cats and larger bears that can overpower it.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** 23–65 kg (zoo and encyclopedic) versus 30–90 kg (IBA, all populations). 50 kg is a prime male in the commonly cited range; heavier mainland males exist.
- **Dimorphism:** 10–20% (SDZWA, Wikipedia) versus about one-third (IBA).
- **Bite:** the ~2,000 N figure comes from Wikipedia's summary of the 2023 study; the paper's full text could not be opened here. Force is not converted to PSI.
- **Tongue length:** IBA's "up to 46 cm" has no primary measurement attached and may be a stretched maximum.
- **Lifespan:** only captive values exist.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Black Bear (120 kg; A56 D49 Ag70), Sloth Bear (120 kg; A65 D57) and Spectacled Bear (115 kg; A62 D55):** all are more than twice the sun bear's mass, so they stay above it in Attack (50.0), Defense (46.0) and raw power. The sun bear's strong bite and long claws keep it within about 6–15 points rather than far below. Agility (60.0) sits below the black bear, which has a documented running speed, and near the spectacled bear.
- **Versus Honey Badger (14 kg; A42 D45 Sp80):** both use loose skin to fight back from a grip, but the sun bear is more than three times heavier, so it gets higher Attack and Defense and a lower Special, because the honey badger's toolkit is more extreme.
- **Versus Gray Wolf (50 kg; A55 D41) and Leopard (60 kg; A60 D43):** similar mass. The dedicated predators keep equal or higher Attack; the sun bear gets slightly higher Defense for its skin and bear frame.
- **Anti-inflation check:** the old live placeholders (Attack 76.3, Defense 12.4) are not used. A 50 kg bear should not out-attack tigers or larger bears, and it clearly has more physical protection than a 12.4 Defense implies.
