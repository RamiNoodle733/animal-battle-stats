# Brazilian Wandering Spider (*Phoneutria nigriventer*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Brazilian Wandering Spider (also "armed spider", Portuguese *armadeira*, and "banana spider" in trade contexts)
- **Scientific name:** *Phoneutria nigriventer* (Keyserling, 1891), family Ctenidae
- **Exact animal represented:** the single species *P. nigriventer*, the best-studied and medically most important member of the genus *Phoneutria*. Other *Phoneutria* species (for example *P. boliviensis*, *P. fera*, *P. keyserlingi*) are used only as clearly labelled proxies where species data are missing.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult female, **3.5 cm body length** (prosoma plus opisthosoma), roughly **13–15 cm leg span**, estimated **about 4 g** live mass.
- **Sex choice:** females are the larger sex and carry the stronger weapon. Herzig et al. (2002) found adult females produced nearly three times the venom yield of equal-sized males (1079 µg versus 296 µg), and female venom was more lethal in mice (LD50 about 0.63 mg/kg versus 1.57 mg/kg for male venom). The female is therefore both larger and more heavily armed.
- **Population notes:** genus body length spans roughly 17–48 mm across species and sexes. Adult female *P. nigriventer* can reach about 42 mm body length. Venom composition also changes as spiders mature (Herzig et al. 2004), so juveniles are not representative of the adult weapon.

### Defensive structure
- Ordinary araneomorph exoskeleton; no urticating hairs, heavy armour or shell. The abdomen is soft. Defence relies on a conspicuous threat posture, readiness to bite, nocturnal habits and hiding by day.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary weapons
The main weapon is a pair of fangs delivering a complex neurotoxic venom. *P. nigriventer* venom contains several toxin fractions (PhTx1–PhTx4) with different targets. PhTx3 is a broad-spectrum calcium-channel blocker that inhibits glutamate release, and the Tx2 fraction includes Tx2-5 and Tx2-6, which cause priapism through a nitric-oxide pathway and can kill mice. PhTx1, PhTx2 and PhTx3 all act on mammals. This multi-target cocktail is the reason the spider is medically important and why it rates highly in Special.

### Offensive mechanics
Wandering spiders do not build capture webs. They roam at night over the ground and low vegetation, seize prey with the front legs and pedipalps and bite. The pedipalp tibiae and tarsi carry dense scopulae (hair brushes) in both sexes, which help grip prey. In the congener *P. boliviensis* prey can also be wrapped in silk after capture. Prey reported in general sources includes insects, other spiders and small vertebrates such as frogs.

### Delivered effect on large animals
Most human bites are mild. In a Brazilian clinico-epidemiological series, **89.8%** of cases were mild, **8.5%** moderate and **0.5%** severe; pain (92.1%) and swelling (33.1%) dominated. Severe envenomation (tachycardia, vomiting, priapism, shock, pulmonary oedema) is uncommon and concentrated in children. The venom is dangerous, but the delivered dose from one bite is small and often limited, which caps real Attack against large opponents.

### Defensive features and durability
The famous threat display lifts the body almost upright, raises the first two leg pairs high to show the banded undersides, and sways side to side with the hind legs cocked. This is an honest warning backed by a ready bite. Physical protection is minimal: a 4 g spider is crushed or pierced by any vertebrate-scale strike.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Long legs, a cursorial hunting style and scopulate feet allow quick starts, climbing on leaves and bark, and fast repositioning at close range. The spider is not a laterigrade crevice specialist like the huntsman, and no running speed has been measured, so Agility is high but below the roster's arthropod agility leaders.

### Endurance
Nightly foraging bouts and roaming are sustained at low power output. No physiology data show exceptional aerobic performance.

### Senses
Eight eyes serve short-range vision, while leg hairs and slit organs detect vibration and air movement. These are well suited to nocturnal close-range hunting. No species-specific sensory thresholds were located.

### Intelligence and tactics
Behaviour is effective but largely stereotyped: roam, detect, seize, bite, sometimes wrap; when threatened, escalate from retreat to display to bite. This supports modest tactical ratings.

### Intraspecific fighting and predator defense
Courtship and mating are the main intraspecific encounters; cannibalism risk exists but no quantified contest data were found. Against predators the sequence is hiding by day, then deimatic display and biting when cornered.

### Aggression
Frequently described as a nervous, readily defensive spider. Clinical data show most bites happen indoors and by day, when people disturb hidden spiders, which reflects hiding behaviour more than unprovoked attack.

### Environmental strengths
- Forest floor and understory at night, where tactile and vibration senses dominate.
- Human structures and plantations: over half of recorded bites in one series happened inside houses.

### Weaknesses
- Tiny absolute mass and negligible raw force.
- Soft abdomen and thin exoskeleton.
- Small venom dose per bite; many bites cause only local pain.
- Must make fang contact to do any damage.

### Good matchup archetypes
- Insects, other spiders and small frogs or lizards within its prey range.
- Small, thin-skinned animals that must engage at close range.

### Bad matchup archetypes
- Any mammal, bird or reptile large enough to crush it before venom acts.
- Armoured arthropods and animals with protective hair, scales or shells that block fang penetration.
- Fast aerial predators that strike from above.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Armadeira Threat Stance** — the spider rears up, raises its front two leg pairs to flash the banded undersides and sways while keeping its fangs forward. The display often ends an encounter without contact, and if it does not, the spider is already in striking position.
2. **Multi-Channel Neurotoxin** — the venom mixes toxin fractions that hit calcium and sodium channels and nitric-oxide pathways (PhTx3, Tx2-5, Tx2-6). One bite can cause intense pain and, in rare severe cases, cardiovascular and respiratory effects.

### Unique traits
1. **Webless Night Roamer** — instead of waiting in a web, it hunts by walking the forest floor and foliage at night and hides in crevices, leaves or buildings by day. That habit also brings it into houses and cargo.
2. **Female-Weighted Arsenal** — adult females yield about three times more venom than equal-sized males, and their venom is more lethal in laboratory tests. The larger sex also carries the stronger weapon.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
*Phoneutria* is a Central and South American genus, and *P. nigriventer* is a Brazilian species of forest and forest-edge habitats that also lives around plantations, gardens and houses. In one Brazilian clinical series, **54.5%** of bites happened inside homes and **76.5%** during daytime, when hidden spiders were disturbed. Commercially shipped bananas occasionally carry *Phoneutria*, but UF/IFAS notes that the most potent species, *P. nigriventer* and *P. keyserlingi*, are rarely exported because most of Brazil's banana crop is consumed domestically.

### Diet and ecology
A nocturnal generalist predator of insects and other arthropods, also taking small vertebrates such as frogs when available. Its own predators were not quantified in the sources reviewed; nocturnal activity and daytime hiding are its main ways of avoiding them.

### Social structure
Solitary. Adults meet mainly for courtship and mating, and there is no cooperative hunting or group defence.

### Reproduction and life history
Females produce silk egg sacs. In the congener *P. boliviensis*, females laid up to four sacs, **430–1300** spiderlings emerged after **28–34 days**, and spiders matured after **14–17 molts** at **300–465 days**. Venom composition shifts during development, so adult venom differs from juvenile venom.

### Conservation
No IUCN Red List assessment of *P. nigriventer* was found in this research pass, so its status is treated as Not Evaluated. It remains common in parts of its range, including human-modified landscapes.

### Special features
- Complex neurotoxic venom with calcium- and sodium-channel-active fractions.
- Dense prolateral scopulae on the pedipalps for gripping prey.
- Conspicuous banded leg undersides shown during the rearing threat display.
- Webless, nocturnal, roaming hunting mode.
- Venom composition that changes with age and differs between the sexes.

### Human interaction
Brazil records many *Phoneutria* bites, most of them mild and mainly painful; only about 0.5–1% become severe, mostly in children, who need hospital care. Bites mainly hit feet and hands (40.9% and 34.3% in one series), which fits spiders hidden around the home being disturbed. The venom is also a research source: the peptide Phα1β is being studied as a non-opioid analgesic.

### Genuine fun facts
- The genus name *Phoneutria* comes from Greek for "murderess".
- In one Brazilian case series, **89.8%** of bites were mild and only **0.5%** were severe.
- Adult females produce nearly **three times** the venom of equal-sized males.
- The toxin Tx2-6 causes priapism in laboratory mice, which is why the species turns up in toxin-mechanism research.
- Over half the bites in one clinical study happened indoors, usually when a hiding spider was disturbed.
- A venom peptide, Phα1β, is being tested as a painkiller.
- A congener needs about **14–17 molts** to reach adulthood.

### Concise site-ready summary
A fast, webless night hunter from Brazil's forests, the Brazilian wandering spider backs its dramatic rearing threat display with one of the most medically significant spider venoms. The venom is a multi-toxin nerve cocktail, but a 4 g body, a small per-bite dose and a soft exoskeleton keep its absolute Attack and Defense low on the full roster.

### Rich narrative profile
The Brazilian wandering spider earns its reputation from chemistry, not size. An adult female has a body about the length of a thumb joint and legs spanning a hand's width, and she weighs only a few grams. Her advantage is a venom that combines several nerve-targeting toxins: one fraction blocks calcium channels and neurotransmitter release, and others act on sodium channels and nitric-oxide signalling, producing intense pain and, in rare severe cases, cardiovascular collapse and pulmonary oedema.

Its fighting style follows from its lifestyle. Instead of waiting in a web, it walks the forest floor and foliage at night, finds prey through touch and vibration, grips it with brush-tipped pedipalps and bites. When something large threatens it, it rears up, lifts its banded front legs and sways with its fangs ready. The display is a warning, and the spider does not bluff.

On an absolute roster these strengths have hard limits. Clinical records show that nine in ten human bites are mild, so the dose from one bite is usually small. A bird, mammal or reptile that strikes first can crush the spider before any venom matters, and armour, scales or dense fur can stop the fangs entirely. The result is a profile with low Attack and very low Defense but high Agility and a near-elite Special: dangerous at close range and fragile at every range.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** no weighed series found. The 4 g canonical value is a labelled scaling estimate.
- **Length:** sources mix genus-wide and species ranges; 3.5 cm is a representative adult female, not the 42–48 mm maximum.
- **Lifespan:** based on a congener's maturation timing; low confidence.
- **Toxicity versus danger:** mouse LD50 and dramatic toxin effects must be read alongside clinical data showing most human bites are mild.
- **Bite force:** no PSI exists and none is proposed.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Black Widow (Attack 24.0, Defense 2.0, Agility 48.0, Special 92.0):** the wandering spider is several times heavier, hunts actively rather than from a web, and delivers roughly a milligram of venom, so it sits slightly above in Attack (27.0) and much higher in Agility (72.0). Special 93.0 is close to the widow's because both carry potent neurotoxic venom whose effect on large animals is usually limited by dose.
- **Versus Huntsman Spider (Attack 10.0, Agility 86.0, Special 61.0):** the huntsman keeps the arachnid agility lead thanks to laterigrade crevice movement and extreme span; the wandering spider's much more dangerous venom puts it far above in Attack and Special.
- **Versus Goliath Birdeater (Attack 16.0, Defense 12.0, Special 90.0):** the Goliath is roughly 35 times heavier with urticating setae, so it keeps the higher Defense, Size and Raw Power. The wandering spider's venom is far more medically significant, which justifies its higher Attack and slightly higher Special.
- **Versus Deathstalker Scorpion (Attack 19.0, Special 96.0) and Bullet Ant (Special 96.0):** these venom specialists stay slightly above in Special. The wandering spider's Attack is higher because of its larger body and higher venom yield, not greater raw power.
- **Anti-inflation check:** Raw Power 2.0, Protection 3.5 and Defense 5.0 keep the vast gap to any vertebrate predator. Venom notoriety drives Weaponry and Special only.
