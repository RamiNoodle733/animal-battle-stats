# Vampire Squid (*Vampyroteuthis infernalis*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Vampire Squid
- **Scientific name:** *Vampyroteuthis infernalis* (family Vampyroteuthidae)
- **Exact animal represented:** The living deep-water cephalopod Vampyroteuthis infernalis, order Vampyromorpha.
- **Living / extinct:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** Healthy adult, roughly 25 cm long and 0.10 kg. Length and mass are references from separate field specimens. Sex is not fixed for the profile.
- **Population notes:** A 100 g mature male and 138 g mature female are documented, and a larger female measured 398 g in the same study. These examples do not establish a species average.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
The selected mass is 100 g, the measured weight of one mature male with a 114 mm mantle. Other measured adults can be heavier; the catalogue does not use the largest specimen.

### Height
No useful standard body height for an open-water swimmer.

### Length
A photographed specimen from the San Clemente Basin measured 25 cm total length. MBARI lists 30 cm as the maximum total length. The catalogue length includes the arms and excludes extended feeding filaments.

### Speed
Adults swim mainly by flapping their fins and can make short escape movements. Published work uses an estimated swimming speed for locomotion calculations; it is not a verified maximum.

### Lifespan
Repeated spawning is documented. A verified typical lifespan in years is not established by the sources used here.

### Bite and venom
It has a beak for feeding. No measured bite pressure or evidence for a venom-based attack was found.

### Defensive structure
Soft body and a foldable web between the arms. The web can cover the mantle during a threat display, but it is not armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
Its small beak processes food that reaches the mouth. The long, thin filaments are used to collect particles and small plankton. They are not striking or grappling weapons.

### Defense and maneuverability
When disturbed it can turn the webbed arms over its mantle and show the rows of soft cirri. The tips of the arms can flash or release glowing particles, which can draw attention away while it moves off. Its fins provide control in open water. Neither behavior gives it the toughness to trade direct hits.

### Endurance, senses and tactics
Low metabolic demand and efficient oxygen transport support slow activity in oxygen-poor water. That is an advantage during quiet movement rather than repeated high-power bursts. Large eyes and light-sensitive structures are useful in dim water. Evidence supports distinct defensive responses, but not advanced problem solving or coordinated fighting.

### Weaknesses
- Soft body and little ability to withstand a direct grab.
- No documented high-power attack.
- Glowing distractions are more conspicuous and useful in dark water.

### Good matchups
- In a dark-water encounter, it can break contact with some visually guided pursuers.

### Bad matchups
- Persistent larger hunters that can find it after the light display.
- Close grapples with larger squid or fish.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Glowing Particle Screen** — The arm tips can release a cloud of tiny glowing particles that can distract a pursuer while the squid moves away.
2. **Web Fold** — The webbed arms curl back over the mantle, changing the outline and exposing rows of soft cirri.

### Unique traits
1. **Marine Snow Collector** — Two thin, retractable filaments gather sinking particles. The arms sweep the collected food toward the mouth.
2. **Low-Oxygen Drifter** — Low energy use and blood that binds oxygen efficiently support slow activity where dissolved oxygen is scarce.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Vampire squid occur in tropical and temperate oceans around the world. In Monterey Canyon, researchers recorded them chiefly in midwater between 600 and 900 m, within a layer with little dissolved oxygen. The depth of that layer varies with location.

### Diet and ecology
The squid collects marine snow: sinking bits of organic matter such as plankton remains and discarded mucus structures. Small zooplankton also occur in its diet. Food adheres to the filaments and is carried to the mouth in mucus with help from the cirri.

### Social structure
Field observations mainly describe individuals feeding or drifting in open water. Coordinated group behavior and a fixed day and night schedule are not documented.

### Reproduction and life history
Ovary samples indicate that females can lay eggs in multiple batches and have resting periods between them. This does not establish a typical adult lifespan in years.

### Conservation
Broad geographic records exist, but sightings and collected specimens alone do not establish a global population trend.

### Special features
Eight webbed arms surround the mouth. Adults swim with a pair of fins and can produce light at the tips of the arms. The soft projections inside the web are cirri.

### Human interaction
Researchers study this animal using remotely operated vehicles, collected specimens and short-term observations at sea. It usually lives far below normal diving depths.

### Genuine fun facts
- Its main food is sinking marine snow.
- Two retractable filaments can gather food without a chase.
- Its arms can fold over the mantle like a covering.
- The arms can flash blue and release glowing particles.
- It uses light for distraction rather than squirting ink.
- Females can lay several batches of eggs with rests between them.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Vampire Squid drifts in deep, low-oxygen water and gathers marine snow on two retractable filaments. When disturbed, it folds its webbed arms over its body and can scatter glowing particles as it escapes.

### Rich narrative profile
A vampire squid can stay suspended in deep water with a thin filament extended into the current. Sinking food particles stick to that filament. The squid draws it across the arms, where mucus and the small cirri help gather the particles into a meal.

The dark water also gives it room to escape. The squid can fold its arms back over the mantle so that its outline changes. A bright flash from the arm tips or a cloud of glowing particles can occupy a pursuer while the animal slips away.

Much of this routine takes place where oxygen is scarce. Efficient oxygen transport and a low rate of energy use suit a life spent drifting and collecting food. Those strengths matter more for survival than speed or the ability to overpower other animals.

- **Humboldt Squid:** It ranks far below a heavy, active predator in power, speed and weaponry. Its score for Special reflects light and web displays rather than damage.
