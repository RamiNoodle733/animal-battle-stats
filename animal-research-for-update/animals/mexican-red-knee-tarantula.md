# Mexican Red-knee Tarantula (*Brachypelma hamorii*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Mexican red-knee tarantula (Mexican redknee, Mexican orangeknee)
- **Scientific name:** *Brachypelma hamorii* Tesmoingt, Cleton & Verdez, 1997
- **Exact animal represented:** the red-kneed tarantula of the Pacific slope of western Mexico (Colima, Jalisco and Michoacán), which is the species behind most pet-trade "Mexican red-knees". For decades it was confused with the very similar *B. smithi* from south of the Balsas River basin; Mendoza and Francke clarified the split in 2017, and many older sources on "*B. smithi*" actually describe *B. hamorii* (Wikipedia; Fukushima et al. 2019). Where only *B. smithi* data exist, they are used and flagged.
- **Status:** Living.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult female, body length about **5.3 cm** and leg span about **13 cm**, with an estimated mass of **15 g (0.015 kg)**. Seven measured females had body lengths of 52–54 mm (Wikipedia, from Mendoza and Francke); ADW gives 12.7–14 cm overall for the red-knee.
- **Sex choice:** females are heavier-bodied, live far longer and hold burrows; males have slightly shorter bodies but longer legs (fourth leg 75 mm in the type male versus 67 mm in a female) and wander in the mating season (Wikipedia). The female is the representative fighting animal.
- **Population notes:** females vary more in carapace colour and pattern than males. The species is fragmented by roads and farmland, and one Colima subpopulation supplies most trafficked specimens (Fukushima et al. 2019).

- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 0.0** (no reliable measurement).

### Lifespan
- Wikipedia (*B. hamorii*): females up to **30 years**; males about **5 years**.
- ADW (*B. smithi*): females typically **25–30 years**, males rarely more than 10. Males mature at about 4 years, females at 6–7.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 25.0**, for the female specimen.
- **Confidence:** Moderate; long-term captive records dominate.

### Fangs and venom
- Paired chelicerae with hollow, downward-striking fangs connected to venom glands (ADW; Wikipedia). The venom contains insecticidal peptides and other proteins (Corzo et al. 2008; Kaiser et al. 1994) but is only slightly venomous to humans, although the large fangs can cause painful puncture wounds and secondary infection (Wikipedia).
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No bite measurement exists.

### Urticating setae and defensive structure
- *Brachypelma* carries type I urticating setae on the top of the abdomen (Kaderka et al. 2019), which it kicks off with its hind legs when disturbed rather than biting (Wikipedia). The barbed setae work into skin and eyes; a review of 25 published eye injuries from tarantula hairs identified two cases from Mexican red-knees (Jalink and Wisse 2021).
- The exoskeleton gives basic arthropod protection; the abdomen is soft and vulnerable. Deep burrows give refuge.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The red-knee waits at night near its burrow entrance and rushes out to seize passing ground-dwelling arthropods, other arachnids, myriapods and occasionally small vertebrates such as frogs and mice (Fukushima et al. 2019; ADW; Wikipedia). It holds prey with its front legs and palps, drives in its fangs, injects venom, and carries the meal back into the burrow, later wrapping the remains in silk (ADW). Against vertebrates larger than a mouse, its venom is weak and its reach tiny.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
A heavy-bodied terrestrial spider with a soft abdomen. Two claws on each foot help it climb slippery surfaces (ADW), but it is not a runner or jumper. Its useful movement is the short ambush rush and retreat into the burrow.

### Endurance
Built for low-energy waiting rather than exertion. Females spend most of their lives in their burrows, moult about once a year and can go without food for long periods around moulting (Wikipedia; Fukushima et al. 2019). Sustained high-output activity is not part of its biology.

### Senses
Eight eyes but poor vision. Hairs on the legs sense vibration, and sensory organs on the palps and feet detect smell, taste and touch (ADW). Prey is detected when it crosses the silk-lined area near the burrow.

### Intelligence and tactics
Behaviour is largely fixed: ambush, threat posture, setae kicking, and burrow sealing. Its threat sequence is to rear up and display the fangs, then kick urticating hairs, and bite only as a last resort (ADW; Wikipedia).

### Sociality and hunting
Solitary. Adults are often aggressive toward other spiders that intrude (ADW). Mating happens near the female's burrow in the late rainy and early dry season (August to January), when mature males wander in the open searching for females (Fukushima et al. 2019).

### Fighting and aggression
Considered extremely docile by keepers (Wikipedia). Defensive rather than aggressive toward large animals, relying on hairs and retreat. Females may attack males after mating (ADW).

### Environmental strengths
- Seasonally dry thorn and deciduous forest, scrub and rocky slopes of western Mexico, from sea level to about 2,100 m (Fukushima et al. 2019; ADW).
- Burrows under logs, rocks and tree roots that shelter it from predators and heat and serve as ambush points.

### Weaknesses
- Tiny absolute mass; negligible raw power.
- Soft abdomen and thin exoskeleton; highly vulnerable to crushing.
- Poor vision and slow movement.
- Mild venom against vertebrates; its hairs deter mammals but not wasps.
- Tarantula hawk wasps (*Pepsis*) lure it from its burrow, paralyse it and lay eggs on it (ADW).

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** insects, other arthropods and small frogs or mice that blunder near its burrow.
- **Mixed:** similar-sized arthropods and small lizards, where the ambush and fangs decide it.
- **Bad:** coatis, birds, snakes and other vertebrate predators too large to subdue, and tarantula hawk wasps.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Barbed Hair Barrage** — when threatened, the red-knee kicks type I urticating setae off its abdomen with its hind legs; the barbed hairs work into skin, nose and eyes, and have caused documented eye injuries in people. Against a mammal predator this can end a fight before contact.
2. **Burrow Ambush Strike** — it waits at the burrow mouth at night, senses prey through leg hairs and silk, rushes out to pin it with front legs and palps, strikes with downward fangs and drags the prey underground.

### Unique traits
1. **Thirty-Year Spider** — females can live 25–30 years, maturing only at 6–7 years, while males rarely pass 10 and often live about 5.
2. **Regrowing Legs** — like all tarantulas it replaces its whole exoskeleton, including fangs, at each moult, and lost legs regrow gradually over successive moults.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Endemic to Mexico on the Pacific slope of the Sierra Madre Occidental and Sierra Madre del Sur, mainly Colima, north-western Michoacán and southern Jalisco (Wikipedia; Fukushima et al. 2019). It lives in subtropical dry forest, mostly thorn and deciduous secondary forest, from the coastal lowlands into the foothills up to about 2,100 m, preferring undisturbed shaded areas and places near seasonal watercourses (Fukushima et al. 2019). ADW describes red-knee habitat as dry scrubland, desert, dry thorn forest and tropical deciduous forest, often at the base of thorny plants such as cacti.

### Diet and ecology
Nocturnal sit-and-wait predator of ground-dwelling insects, arachnids and myriapods, and occasionally small vertebrates such as frogs and mice (Fukushima et al. 2019; ADW; Wikipedia). It sucks out the liquefied body contents and wraps the remains in silk. Predators include white-nosed coatis (Wikipedia), birds and lizards (ADW), and *Pepsis* tarantula hawk wasps (ADW).

### Social structure
Solitary. Each female keeps her own burrow; males leave their burrows to search for females in the mating season, when roads are a significant cause of male deaths (Fukushima et al. 2019).

### Reproduction and life history
Mating follows the male's final moult. The male hooks the female's fangs open with spurs on his front legs, transfers sperm with his palps, then retreats quickly because females can turn on males (ADW). The female lays about 200–400 eggs on a silk mat in the dry winter months, wraps them into an egg sac and guards it; young emerge about two months later and disperse before the early summer rains (ADW; Fukushima et al. 2019). Growth is slow: males mature at about 4 years, females at 6–7.

### Conservation
Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List (Fukushima et al. 2019 assessment, per Wikipedia), with an estimated extent of occurrence of about 13,000 km² and an area of occupancy of about 8,450 km², both declining through urbanisation, agriculture and roads (Fukushima et al. 2019). Listed on CITES Appendix II since 1985 (then under the name *B. smithi*). Illegal collection continues, especially around Colima, while legal captive breeding in Mexican management units supplies part of the pet trade (Fukushima et al. 2019). No protected area covers its range.

### Special features
- Type I urticating setae on the abdomen, kicked off in defense.
- Downward-striking fangs with insecticidal venom peptides.
- Deep burrows with a molting chamber and a feeding chamber, sealed with silk when molting or brooding.
- Regrowth of lost legs across moults.
- Exceptional female longevity of 25–30 years.

### Human interaction
One of the most popular pet tarantulas because of its bright orange-banded legs and calm temperament (Wikipedia). Its hairs are the main risk to keepers, especially to the eyes (Jalink and Wisse 2021). Some local people kill the spiders with pesticides or by pouring gasoline into burrows (Wikipedia), and demand for adults drives illegal collection.

### Genuine fun facts
- For decades, pet "Mexican red-knees" sold as *Brachypelma smithi* were mostly a different species, *B. hamorii*; the mix-up was only settled in 2017.
- Female red-knees can live 25–30 years, while males often live about five.
- Its burrow tunnel is about three times the spider's leg span long and leads to a molting chamber and a separate feeding chamber.
- It flicks barbed hairs at attackers instead of biting, and those hairs have caused eye injuries in people.
- A tarantula even replaces its fangs when it moults, and lost legs regrow over several moults.
- The male uses hooks on his front legs to hold the female's fangs open during mating.
- Tarantula hawk wasps can lure it out of its burrow, paralyse it and use it as a living nursery for their young.

### Concise site-ready summary
The Mexican red-knee tarantula is a calm, long-lived burrow ambusher of western Mexico's dry forests, famous as a pet for its orange-banded legs. Its fangs and mild venom handle insects and mice, and its barbed defensive hairs deter mammals, but at around 15 grams it is fragile and weak on the absolute scale.

### Rich narrative profile
On the dry, thorny hillsides of Colima and Michoacán, the Mexican red-knee tarantula spends most of its life underground. A female digs or remodels a burrow beneath a log, rock or tree root, with a tunnel leading to a molting chamber and a deeper room where she rests and eats. At dusk she waits near the entrance, feeling for the vibrations of beetles, crickets or the occasional mouse, then rushes out, pins the prey, bites, and drags it below.

Her defenses are layered. Threatened, she rears up to show her fangs, then turns and kicks a cloud of barbed hairs from her abdomen, hairs that irritate skin and can injure the eyes of mammals, people included. Only as a last resort does she bite, and her venom, though deadly to insects, is mild to people. She grows slowly, moulting once a year and regrowing lost legs, and can live for 25 to 30 years, while males mature, wander in search of mates, and usually die within a few years.

In battle terms, the red-knee is a small specialist. Its fangs, venom and hairs give it a high Special rating for its size, but it weighs perhaps 15 grams, moves slowly, and has a soft, easily crushed abdomen. It wins against insects and small prey at its burrow and loses to almost any vertebrate predator large enough to shrug off a few hairs.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Taxonomy:** many sources before 2017 describe "*B. smithi*" but may refer to *B. hamorii*; ADW data are used with that caveat.
- **Mass:** no published mass; 15 g is an allometric estimate with low confidence.
- **Length:** body length (5.2–5.4 cm) versus overall/leg span (12.7–14 cm); the roster convention for tarantulas uses leg span.
- **Lifespan:** female figures (25–30 years) come mostly from captivity; wild survival is unknown.
- **Venom:** biochemical studies of *B. smithi* venom are cited at title level only; potency to vertebrates is described as mild.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Goliath Birdeater (150 g; A16 D12 Ag61 St35 I31 Sp90):** ten times heavier with larger fangs and audible stridulation; it stays above the red-knee in every headline rating. The red-knee's Special (78.0) keeps the shared setae-plus-venom toolkit but drops for the smaller body and no stridulation.
- **Versus Huntsman Spider (A10 D7 Ag86 Sp61):** the huntsman is a fast, flat cursorial hunter, so its Agility is far higher; the red-knee's urticating hairs give it higher Defense and Special.
- **Versus Black Widow (1 g; A24 D2 I28 Sp92):** the widow's neurotoxic venom is far more dangerous to vertebrates, so its Attack is higher despite tiny size; Intelligence ties at 28.0 as both run largely fixed programmes.
- **Versus Tarantula Hawk (A32.6):** its natural enemy has a much higher Attack; the wasp's sting paralyses tarantulas.
- **Anti-inflation check:** long female lifespan is kept out of Stamina; urticating hairs count toward Defense and Special but not Attack.
