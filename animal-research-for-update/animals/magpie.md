# Magpie

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen
- **Resolved taxon:** Eurasian Magpie / Common Magpie, *Pica pica* (Linnaeus, 1758), family Corvidae; living.
- **Canonical specimen:** healthy fully mature **adult male**, **0.23 kg** and **48.0 cm total length**. Male is used because males average slightly larger although plumage is essentially alike.
- **Dimorphism:** published identification ranges place males around 185-268 g and females around 160-240 g; sex is difficult to assign safely from plumage alone. The difference is modest, so the male baseline does not justify a large combat-stat gap.
- **Taxonomic caution:** the roster's generic “Magpie” is resolved here to Eurasian Magpie *Pica pica*. North American Black-billed Magpie *Pica hudsonia*, Oriental Magpie *P. serica*, Asir Magpie *P. asirensis* and other magpies are not substituted.
- **Population variation:** a wide Eurasian distribution and multiple populations/subspecies produce some size variation. Canonical values represent a mid-large adult male rather than the maximum of a particular population.

## 3. Combat biology
### Weapons and offensive mechanics
The bill is the primary weapon: a straight, robust corvid bill suited to probing, tearing, pecking, scavenging and handling varied foods. Feet and claws can grip branches and assist close contact, but they are not raptorial talons. Eurasian magpies take insects and other invertebrates, eggs and nestlings, carrion and some small vertebrate prey. Against another small bird, repeated pecks, bites and pursuit can matter; across the full ABS roster, a 230 g bird has very low absolute damage output.

### Defense and durability
Feathers provide insulation and limited superficial protection, not meaningful armor. Hollow/pneumatized avian construction and very low mass make direct contact with larger predators dangerous. Defense is therefore based mainly on vigilance, flight, mobbing, social alarm and intelligent avoidance rather than absorbing damage.

### Locomotion and agility
Magpies walk and hop confidently on the ground and fly between ground, shrubs and trees. Experimental work on free descending *P. pica* showed the alula contributes to steep descent and body-orientation changes near high angle of attack. That supports strong low-speed aerial control. The bird's long tail also supplies a large control surface. No maximum-speed claim is needed to justify high maneuverability.

### Endurance
This is an active resident corvid that forages widely within territories and can sustain repeated ground/air transitions. Breeding pairs defend territories throughout the year, while nonbreeders may flock. Evidence supports good routine avian endurance but not the extreme sustained-flight physiology of migratory seabirds or coursing mammals.

### Senses
Vision and hearing are the principal battle-relevant senses. Magpies detect predators and conspecific territorial signals, and recent playback experiments show they discriminate temporal structure in territorial chatter calls. No extraordinary modality such as echolocation, electroreception or infrared sensing is assigned.

### Intelligence, learning and tactics
This is the profile's standout category. Peer-reviewed experiments show *P. pica* can remember **what, where and when** it cached food, learn local retrieval cues, and develop advanced object permanence. Prior, Schwarz and Güntürkün (2008) reported mirror-induced mark-directed behavior consistent with self-recognition, although mirror-test interpretation should not be inflated into a blanket claim of human-like self-awareness. The strongest combat-relevant conclusion is flexible memory, learning, recognition and response selection.

### Fighting, predation and predator defense
Breeding pairs defend territories year-round. Chatter calls are used during conspecific territorial disputes and predator encounters. Playback experiments show pair-level call timing changes receiver approach and response, demonstrating that territorial communication carries behaviorally relevant information. Magpies also mob predators. Against small nest competitors they can be a serious threat; against raptors and mammalian predators their strategy is warning, harassment and disengagement rather than trading blows.

### Environmental strengths and limitations
**Best:** woodland edges, farmland, parks, suburban mosaics and other environments combining open ground with trees/perches.

**Strengths:** three-dimensional escape, excellent visual surveillance, rapid switching between ground and air, memory, food caching, pair coordination, alarm/mobbing behavior.

**Weaknesses:** tiny absolute mass, no armor, no venom, no raptorial talons, modest bill reach, vulnerable if seized, and no evidence for exceptional maximum flight speed.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Favorable:** very small terrestrial invertebrates and tiny animals that cannot reliably reach an airborne/perched bird.
- **Mixed:** similarly sized generalist birds where maneuverability, bill use and cognition can influence engagement.
- **Unfavorable:** raptors, cats, mustelids and essentially all medium/large roster animals once contact is secured.

## 5. Proposed six headline ABS ratings
- **Attack 15.0:** bill and claws can injure tiny prey or birds, but absolute damage is capped by 0.23 kg body mass.
- **Defense 12.0:** structurally vulnerable; avoidance is mostly represented in Agility rather than double-counted here.
- **Agility 84.0:** excellent three-dimensional repositioning and low-speed control.
- **Stamina 61.0:** solid daily activity and territorial persistence without specialist migration/endurance evidence.
- **Intelligence 91.0:** species-specific experimental evidence for episodic-like what-where-when memory, cache retrieval learning, object permanence and sophisticated social responses.
- **Special 58.0:** cognitive flexibility plus flight and coordinated alarm/mobbing can alter small-scale matchups, but no venom, electricity, armor or comparable rare physical mechanism exists.

## 6. Exactly two special abilities
1. **Cache-Memory Map** - experimental *P. pica* can integrate what food was cached, where it was hidden and when it was cached, supporting unusually flexible spatial/episodic-like memory.
2. **Chatter Alarm Network** - territorial and predator-context chatter calls carry behaviorally meaningful timing/duration information and can coordinate rapid warning and approach responses.

## 7. Exactly two unique traits
1. **Alula-Controlled Descent** - experimental free-flight work shows the alula contributes to steep descent and body reorientation at low airspeed/high angle of attack, supporting precise landing and evasive control.
2. **Long-Tail Rudder** - the exceptionally long graduated tail forms a major portion of total length and provides a large control surface during flight and landing.

## 8. Expanded profile
### Habitat and geographic range
Eurasian magpies occupy open and semi-open country with trees: farmland, hedgerows, woodland margins, parks, gardens and suburbs across much of Europe and temperate Asia. Dense closed forest and completely treeless terrain are generally less suitable. The species is primarily resident over much of its range.

### Diet and ecology
An opportunistic omnivore, it eats insects and other invertebrates, seeds, fruit/acorns, carrion and human-associated food, and also takes eggs, nestlings and small vertebrates. It scatter-hoards food. A 2021 Journal of Ecology study used radio tracking and cameras to show Eurasian magpies caching holm-oak acorns, demonstrating an ecological role in seed dispersal as well as predation/scavenging.

### Social structure and life history
Breeding pairs can defend territories throughout the year. Nonbreeders flock, and even territorial pairs may temporarily join winter feeding groups. Vines' 18-month field study found higher feeding rates in flocks than for birds alone or in pairs, while aggression increased when birds fed closer together and at richer patches. Nests are substantial domed stick structures. Sexes look similar, with males averaging somewhat larger.

### Conservation
The widespread Eurasian Magpie is generally treated as Least Concern. Local control occurs where it is regarded as an agricultural or nest-predation nuisance, but broad adaptability to human-modified landscapes has helped it persist in many cities and farming regions.

### Major adaptations
Key adaptations are behavioral rather than brute-force: flexible omnivory, food caching, strong spatial memory, sophisticated object representation, rapid learning, social/territorial signaling and maneuverable flight. These traits make the bird highly adaptable without turning its small body into a powerful fighter.

### Human interaction
Magpies are conspicuous urban/suburban corvids and are embedded in European and Asian folklore. Their reputation for indiscriminately stealing shiny objects should not be treated as established biological fact. They can exploit anthropogenic foods and nest in developed landscapes, bringing them into both conflict and familiarity with people.

### Fun facts
- The long tail makes up roughly half of the bird's total apparent length.
- *P. pica* has experimentally demonstrated what-where-when memory for cached foods.
- Magpies can learn to use local visual cues to recover caches.
- A famous 2008 experiment reported mirror-directed mark behavior consistent with self-recognition.
- Breeding pairs may defend territories all year rather than only around the nest.
- Chatter calls are used both against territorial intruders and in predator contexts.
- Experimental flight work on magpies has directly investigated the aerodynamic function of the alula.

### Concise site summary
The Eurasian Magpie is a ~230 g long-tailed corvid whose combat value comes from maneuverable flight and exceptional cognition rather than force. It remembers complex cache information, interprets social/alarm signals and aggressively defends territory, but its tiny body, lack of armor and modest natural weapons keep absolute Attack and Defense low.

### Narrative profile
A magpie is a tactical lightweight. On open ground it walks and hops while scanning continuously; at the first change in risk it can take to the air, use trees and structures as vertical cover, and re-engage from a different angle. The long tail and alula-assisted low-speed control make landings and steep transitions precise. Against an insect or tiny vertebrate, the bill is a legitimate weapon. Against a cat, eagle or even a much larger generalist bird, the correct strategy is rarely a direct force contest.

Its exceptional rating belongs in cognition. Controlled studies show that magpies can remember what they cached, where and when, develop sophisticated object permanence and learn retrieval cues. Territorial pairs also respond to details in each other's and rivals' chatter sequences. ABS therefore gives Intelligence an elite score while keeping Raw Power near the floor. This is exactly the kind of animal where pound-for-pound admiration must not leak into Attack or Defense.

## 11. Confidence, conflicts and cross-animal normalization
- **Taxon:** high confidence for roster resolution to *Pica pica*, while acknowledging that “magpie” is not globally species-specific.
- **Mass/length:** high confidence. Male ranges overlap females substantially, so canonical male selection changes little.
- **Speed:** unresolved. `0.0` is preferable to adopting a generic 20-30 mph web figure without a strong species-specific measurement.
- **Lifespan:** moderate confidence. Longevity records above 20 years describe maxima; representative life expectancy is much shorter. Canonical 5.0 years is intentionally not the record.
- **Bite pressure:** unresolved; no PSI fabricated.
- **Macaw comparison:** Magpie is far smaller and has weaker weaponry, so Attack is lower. Magpie's species-specific memory/object-permanence evidence supports slightly higher Intelligence, while Scarlet Macaw remains stronger physically.
- **Crow/corvid control:** high cognition should not pull Attack, Defense or Raw Power upward. Final full-roster audit should compare all corvids for consistent cognition bands and avoid treating one mirror-test result as an automatic ceiling.
- **Small-bird control:** flight is concentrated in Maneuverability/Agility. It is not also counted as structural Defense or extreme Stamina.
- **Special control:** cognition and alarm coordination are unusual advantages but do not belong in venom/electricity-tier Special.
