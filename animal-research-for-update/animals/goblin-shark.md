# Goblin Shark (*Mitsukurina owstoni*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** Goblin shark
- **Scientific name:** *Mitsukurina owstoni* Jordan, 1898
- **Exact animal represented:** the single living goblin shark species, a pink, soft-bodied deep-sea shark with a long flat snout and jaws that shoot forward to catch prey. It is the only living member of the family Mitsukurinidae.
- **Living / extinct:** Living. It has been filmed alive in its deep habitat, on a seamount near Jarvis Island in 2019 and on the slope of the Tonga Trench in 2024; earlier live footage showed animals brought up near the surface.
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy mature adult, **380 cm** total length and **210 kg**, the weighed specimen that held the species weight record until 2022 and sits at the top of the usual 3–4 m adult range.
- **Sex choice:** sex is left unspecified. Mature males have measured 264–384 cm and mature females 335–372 cm (Florida Museum, citing Duffy et al. 2004). The largest known animals, a female estimated at 5.4–6.2 m caught in 2000 and a heavily pregnant 4.7 m, 800 kg female landed in Taiwan in 2023, suggest females grow larger, but data are too few to state the size difference.
- **Population notes:** fewer than a few hundred specimens are known, mostly from bycatch off Japan. Juveniles live shallower (100–350 m in submarine canyons off southern Japan) than adults.

### Lifespan
- Unknown. No age or growth study was found.
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 0.0**, meaning no reliable data.

### Bite and dentition
- Long, narrow, nail-like front teeth that are finely grooved lengthwise, and small flattened rear teeth for crushing. The upper jaw has 35–53 teeth and the lower 31–62 (Florida Museum).
- A 2023 three-dimensional lever analysis of lamniform shark jaws found the goblin shark has the least efficient jaws of the group because its jaw-closing muscles sit far back, giving low bite force at the narrow front teeth.
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** No absolute measurement exists.

### Defensive structure
- None to speak of. The body is slender and flabby; the soft, semi-translucent skin shows blood vessels through it (hence the pink colour) and carries small spine-like denticles.

## 4. Combat biology

### Weapons and offense
The goblin shark's weapon is its slingshot jaw. At rest the jaws sit tucked under the head. When it strikes, the lower jaw swings down and back to open a gape of up to about 116°, then elastic ligaments at the jaw joint release and both jaws fly forward. Nakaya et al. (2016) filmed the lower jaw reaching **3.1 m/s** and both jaws moving forward by 8.6–9.4% of the shark's total length, by far the fastest and farthest jaw protrusion known in sharks; Wikipedia summarises the range as 2.1–9.5 times more protrusible than other sharks. It also opens and closes its mouth a second time while pulling the jaws back, something not seen in other sharks.

The long nail-like teeth snag soft, slippery prey such as squid, fish and shrimps, while the rear teeth crush crabs. Bite force is low: its jaw muscles are the least mechanically efficient among lamniform sharks. The weapon is built for a fast snatch, not for cutting large animals.

### Defense and durability
Very poor. A flabby, low-density body with soft skin and small fins offers little protection, and there is no armor or spines. Its main defense is living in dark water at depths of hundreds of metres, where few large predators hunt. It may be eaten by blue sharks.

### Locomotion and maneuverability
Anatomy points to a sluggish swimmer: soft body, small fins and a tail with a weak lower lobe. Low-density flesh and a large oily liver make it neutrally buoyant, so it can drift toward prey with very little movement.

### Endurance
Unknown. Its low-effort drifting lifestyle suggests low energy use rather than high output.

### Senses
The long, soft snout is covered underneath with ampullae of Lorenzini that detect the weak electric fields of nearby animals. Oceana notes it likely relies more on smell, hearing and electroreception than on eyesight in the dark. The snout is too soft to dig with.

### Intelligence and tactics
Its tactic appears to be a single well-honed ambush: hang neutrally buoyant, detect prey electrically, drift close and snap the jaws out. Nothing suggests flexible problem solving.

### Hunting behavior
Feeds on bottom and midwater animals: blackbelly rosefish, squid, octopus, crabs and even ostracods have been recorded (Wikipedia, FishBase, Florida Museum). It is a ram feeder: it swims into prey with the mouth open rather than sucking it in (Nakaya et al. 2016).

### Intraspecific fighting and predator defense
Nothing is known about interactions between goblin sharks.

### Aggression
No known attacks on people. The Florida Museum notes that its large size could make it potentially dangerous, but it is almost never met alive.

### Environmental strengths
- Deep, dark water, usually 270–960 m, down to at least 1,300 m caught and about 2,000 m seen.
- Close-range ambush in darkness, where electroreception beats eyesight.

### Weaknesses
- Soft, flabby, unarmored body.
- Weak bite despite long teeth.
- Probably slow, with little acceleration.
- Rarely leaves the deep; shallow water is outside its normal range.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** small soft-bodied prey such as squid and fish that come within jaw range.
- **Mixed:** crabs and armored small animals, which the rear teeth can crush.
- **Bad:** almost any active predator of similar or larger size, which can outswim and outbite it.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Slingshot Jaws** — elastic ligaments fire both jaws forward at up to 3.1 m/s, reaching 8.6–9.4% of the shark's length beyond the head, the fastest and greatest jaw protrusion measured in any shark. Prey that thinks it is out of reach is not.
2. **Electric Snout Radar** — the long, flat snout is covered with ampullae of Lorenzini that sense the faint electric fields of hidden animals in total darkness.

### Unique traits
1. **Pink Ghost of the Slope** — soft, semi-translucent skin lets blood vessels show through, giving adults their pink colour; young goblin sharks are almost white.
2. **Neutral-Buoyancy Drifter** — low-density flesh and a large oily liver let it hang in the water and drift toward prey with very little movement.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
Goblin sharks have been found in scattered deep-water locations across the Atlantic, Pacific and Indian Oceans, over upper continental slopes, submarine canyons and seamounts. Most records come from 270–960 m, with catches to 1,300 m, a tooth in an undersea cable at 1,370 m and sightings to about 2,000 m. Juveniles use shallower canyons, occasionally coming as shallow as 40 m.

### Diet and ecology
A deep-water ambush predator of soft-bodied and bottom animals: rockfish such as the blackbelly rosefish, squid, pelagic octopus, crabs, shrimps and ostracods. It drifts close using neutral buoyancy and snatches prey with its projecting jaws.

### Social structure
Unknown; specimens are caught singly.

### Reproduction and life history
Almost nothing is known. Birth size is probably about 82 cm, the length of the smallest specimen. Males mature at about 2.6 m. A pregnant 4.7 m female was landed in Taiwan in 2023. Age, growth and lifespan have not been studied.

### Conservation
Listed as **Least Concern** on the IUCN Red List (2017) because of its wide range and low catch rate, despite its rarity. It is taken occasionally as bycatch in deep trawls, longlines and gillnets.

### Special features
- Jaws that shoot forward on elastic ligaments.
- Long, flattened snout covered in electroreceptors.
- Long, nail-like front teeth and flattened crushing rear teeth.
- Soft, semi-translucent pink skin.
- Low-density flesh and a large oily liver for neutral buoyancy.

### Human interaction
Almost never seen alive; most knowledge comes from specimens brought up by deep-sea fishing gear. The first scientific report of wild goblin sharks filmed in their deep habitat was published in 2026.

### Genuine fun facts
- Its jaws shoot forward faster and farther than those of any other shark measured.
- A goblin shark opens and closes its mouth twice during one strike, a pattern unseen in other sharks.
- A goblin shark tooth was once found stuck in an undersea cable 1,370 m down.
- Its pink colour comes from blood vessels showing through its skin.
- Early scientists described several "new species" by mistake because preserved goblin sharks had their jaws fixed at different positions.
- The first goblin sharks filmed in their natural deep habitat were seen near Jarvis Island in 2019 and in the Tonga Trench in 2024; earlier footage showed animals brought up to the surface.
- A heavily pregnant 4.7 m female landed in Taiwan in 2023 weighed about 800 kg.

### Concise site-ready summary
The goblin shark is a pink, soft-bodied deep-sea shark whose jaws shoot forward on elastic ligaments faster and farther than any other shark's. It senses prey electrically with its long snout and snatches it in the dark, but its flabby body, weak bite and slow swimming make it a poor fighter against active predators.

### Rich narrative profile
The goblin shark lives on the dark slopes and seamounts of the deep ocean, hundreds of metres down. A mature adult of about 3.8 m and 210 kg has a soft, pale pink body, small fins, and a long flat snout that sticks out over its mouth like a blade. That snout is not a weapon; it is a sensor, covered underneath with electroreceptors that pick up the faint electric signals of squid, fish and crabs hidden in the darkness.

When prey comes close, the goblin shark fires its jaws. Elastic ligaments that were held taut release, and both jaws shoot forward out of the head. Filmed strikes show the lower jaw moving at 3.1 m/s and the jaws reaching almost a tenth of the shark's body length forward, farther and faster than in any other shark. Its long, nail-like front teeth snag slippery prey, and its back teeth crush crabs.

Outside that strike, the goblin shark has little to fight with. Its jaw muscles are mechanically inefficient, its body is flabby and unarmored, and it almost certainly swims slowly, drifting on neutral buoyancy instead of chasing. In the deep, dark water it calls home, that is enough. Against an active hunter it would rely on the darkness and depth that keep it out of reach.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Mass:** only two weights are widely reported, and one is a pregnant giant; 210 kg is the best-supported adult figure.
- **Sex dimorphism:** female maturity size and typical adult size by sex are poorly known.
- **Speed, stamina and lifespan:** never measured; ratings rest on anatomy.
- **Bite:** only relative jaw efficiency is known; no absolute force.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Greenland Shark (400 kg; A55 D54 Ag12 St58 I38 Sp66):** another slow deep-water shark, but nearly twice as heavy with a seal-cutting roll bite and a thick body, so it stays above in Attack and Defense. The goblin shark's snatching jaw likely moves faster than anything the Greenland shark does, so its Agility is a little higher (25.0 vs 12.0), and its unique jaw mechanism earns a higher Special (80.0 vs 66.0).
- **Versus Anglerfish (77 cm female; A27 D16 Ag39 Sp76):** both are deep-sea ambushers with one specialised trick. The goblin shark is vastly larger, so Attack and Defense are higher. Special is slightly higher (80.0 vs 76.0): the slingshot jaw is at least as rare as the lure. Agility is lower because the anglerfish is small and the goblin shark's body is sluggish.
- **Versus Oceanic Whitetip Shark (150 kg; A71 D53 Ag70) and Thresher Shark (230 kg; A56 D48 Ag80):** similar masses, but both are strong active swimmers with firmer bodies, so both are well above the goblin shark in Attack, Defense and Agility.
- **Versus Great White Shark (900 kg; A88):** a fellow lamniform with efficient, cutting jaws; the goblin shark's jaws are the least efficient in the group, which is why its Attack is held at 46.0 despite long teeth.
- **Anti-inflation check:** its famous jaw is counted in Weaponry and Special, but Attack stays low because bite leverage is weak and the teeth suit soft prey. Stamina and Agility are held low because nothing has been measured and the anatomy is that of a slow drifter.
