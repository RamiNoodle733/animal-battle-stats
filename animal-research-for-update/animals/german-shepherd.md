# German Shepherd (*Canis familiaris*) research profile

## 1. Identity and canonical specimen

- **Common name:** German Shepherd (German Shepherd Dog, Deutscher Schäferhund; Alsatian in Britain)
- **Scientific name:** *Canis familiaris* Linnaeus, 1758 (domestic dog; German Shepherd Dog breed, FCI standard No. 166)
- **Exact animal represented:** the working-type German Shepherd Dog as used for herding, police and protection work, not show lines with extreme hind angulation and not the oversized "Shiloh Shepherd" offshoot.
- **Living / extinct:** Living (domestic breed).
- **Canonical combat specimen:** healthy adult male working dog, **35.0 kg**, **63.0 cm** at the withers.
- **Sex choice:** the FCI standard gives males 60–65 cm and 30–40 kg, females 55–60 cm and 22–32 kg. Male police dogs measured in a Finnish study had a median of **35.2 kg** and **63.0 cm**, which the canonical specimen follows.
- **Population notes:** the breed was founded in Germany from 1899 by Max von Stephanitz from traditional German herding dogs; the first registered dog was Horand von Grafrath. Working and show lines differ in build. The breed is one of the most common police and military dogs worldwide.

## 2. Physical measurements

### Mass
- FCI standard: males **30–40 kg**.
- AKC (tertiary): males **65–90 lb (29.5–40.8 kg)**.
- Finnish police dogs (Hyytiäinen et al. 2021): six male GSDs, median **35.2 kg** (IQR 5.2).
- **Canonical:** **35.0 kg**, the centre of the FCI range and the measured police-dog median.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Height
- FCI: males **60–65 cm**. Finnish police GSDs: median **63.0 cm**.
- **Canonical:** **63.0 cm**.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Length
- FCI: trunk length exceeds withers height by about **10–17%**.
- **Canonical `length_cm`: 71.0 cm**, a derived trunk length for the 63 cm male at about 13% longer than tall. This is **not** head-body length.
- **Confidence:** Moderate; derived from the standard's ratio, not measured.

### Speed
- Hyytiäinen et al. (2021) measured male police GSDs accelerating over **25 m** toward a helper: speeds of **35–42 km/h** (median 37 km/h), with median peak acceleration of 7.1 g across the study dogs.
- **Canonical `speed_mps`: 11.7 m/s** (42 km/h), the highest GSD speed measured in that study. Because the run-up was only 25 m, this is probably **below** the dog's true top speed.
- Popular claims of 48 km/h (30 mph) were not traced to a measurement.
- **Confidence:** Moderate for the measured value; low as a true maximum.

### Lifespan
- VetCompass UK primary-care records (O'Neill et al. 2017): median longevity **10.3 years** (IQR 8.0–12.1).
- Wikipedia summarizes other studies: 11.3 years (UK 2024), 10.16 years (UK 2022), 10 years (Italy 2024), 10.08 years (France 2015).
- **Canonical `lifespan_years`: 10.3**.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Bite force
- **Canonical `bite_force_psi`: 0.0.** The only functional measurement located is a force, not a pressure.
- Hyytiäinen et al. (2021) fitted a helper sleeve with three force sensors and measured male police dogs biting at the end of a 25 m run. The GSDs' median bite force was **360.4 N** (IQR 628.6 N), against **247.0 N** for Belgian Malinois; the authors concluded functional bite force was "lower than generally assumed". A sleeve bite is a working bite on padding, not a maximal crushing bite.
- The television programme *Dangerous Encounters* reported about **1,060 N (238 lbf)** for a GSD (via Wikipedia); no protocol was published and it is not used.
- Lindner et al. (1995) measured 13–1,394 N (mean 256 N) across 22 pet dogs; Ellis et al. (2008) 147–3,417 N in anesthetized dogs. None of these can be converted to PSI without a contact area.

### Coat and defensive structure
- Double coat with a dense undercoat and a straight, harsh outer coat (FCI). No armor.

## 4. Combat biology

### Primary weapon and offensive mechanics
The weapon is the canid bite. Police and protection dogs are trained to run in, take a full grip and hold. The Finnish sleeve study shows what that looks like in numbers: a sprint to about 37–42 km/h, a peak acceleration around 7 g, a deceleration spike over 11 g on impact, and a median bite of about 360 N on the sleeve. The impact is a large part of the effect, but the measured grip itself is far lower than internet figures suggest.

### Defense and toughness
A 35 kg dog with a dense double coat and no armor. It has no special trauma resistance. The breed also carries notable orthopaedic problems: the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals reports hip dysplasia in about 19% of evaluated GSDs, and a small Zurich study found degenerative spinal stenosis in 45% of police GSDs (both via Wikipedia).

### Locomotion and maneuverability
The FCI standard calls the GSD a trotter, built for a smooth, ground-covering trot. It accelerates hard over short distances, as the police-dog data show, and is agile enough for obstacle and bite work, but it is heavier and less nimble than wild canids and the Belgian Malinois.

### Endurance
The breed was made to herd sheep all day at the trot, and the FCI standard repeatedly links correct angulation to stamina. That supports good sustained working endurance, though no field endurance measurements were located.

### Senses
Excellent domestic-dog olfaction, used widely in tracking, detection and search-and-rescue. No breed-specific sensory thresholds were located, so the score follows proven working performance rather than invented numbers.

### Intelligence and tactics
German Shepherds learn complex tasks quickly and are used across police, military, guide and search work. In a fight this translates into controlled, trained bite work and responsiveness to a handler, not the cooperative hunting tactics of a wolf pack.

### Intraspecific fighting
Males can be dog-aggressive. VetCompass UK recorded aggression in 4.76% of GSDs under veterinary care, more often in males.

### Predator defense
Originally a herding dog that also protected flocks; today mostly a guard and police dog rather than a livestock guardian against wolves.

### Aggression and ferocity
Confident and protective; willing to engage on command and to defend its handler. A 2020 review (via Wikipedia) found the GSD responsible for the most hospital-treated bites among US purebred dogs between 1971 and 2018, which partly reflects how common the breed is.

### Environmental strengths
- Open ground and mixed terrain where it can accelerate and trot for long periods.
- Temperate to cold climates, helped by the double coat.

### Weaknesses
- No armor; a single bite weapon.
- Measured functional bite force is moderate.
- Common hip, elbow and spinal problems in the population.
- Far outmatched by bears, big cats and large ungulates.

### Matchup archetypes
- **Good:** coyotes, jackals, foxes and other light canids; unarmored medium mammals.
- **Mixed:** dingoes, African wild dogs and wolves, where the wild canid's mobility or mass decides it; heavier guardian breeds.
- **Bad:** bears, big cats, large ungulates, crocodilians, armored or venomous opponents.

## 7. Exactly two special abilities and two unique traits

### Special abilities
1. **Long Attack Strike** — a sprint to about 37–42 km/h ending in a full-mouth grip, with measured accelerations around 7 g and impact decelerations over 11 g. The collision and grip can knock an opponent of similar size off balance.
2. **Scent Tracker** — follows and finds targets by smell in police, military and search-and-rescue work. In a matchup this helps it locate and follow an opponent rather than add damage.

### Unique traits
1. **Tireless Trotter** — the FCI standard defines the GSD as a trotter built for a smooth, ground-covering gait and ties correct angulation directly to stamina.
2. **Von Stephanitz Working Dog** — founded in 1899 around Horand von Grafrath with a breed club that put working ability first; it became one of the world's most numerous and most widely used service breeds.

## 8. Expanded profile

### Habitat and range
A domestic breed kept worldwide in homes, farms, police and military kennels. It originated in Germany.

### Diet
Fed by its owners on commercial dog food, meat and household scraps.

### Ecology
No wild ecological role. Working roles include herding, police patrol, detection, search-and-rescue, military work and guiding people with visual impairments.

### Social structure
Bonds closely with its handler or family and is watchful toward strangers. Stanley Coren ranked it second among breeds most likely to bark as watchdogs (via Wikipedia).

### Reproduction and life history
Breeding is managed by breed clubs and owners. The male police GSDs measured in Finland had a median age of 58.5 months, about 4.9 years, showing that working dogs serve well into adulthood.

### Conservation
Not applicable as a wild species; it is a **Domesticated** breed. VetCompass data show GSD numbers declining in the UK, from 3.5% of the 2005 birth cohort to 2.2% in 2013.

### Special features
- Wedge-shaped head with erect ears; trunk about 10–17% longer than tall.
- Dense double coat with harsh outer hair.
- Smooth, ground-covering trot.
- Strong scent-work and trainability.
- Measured working bite about 360 N on a padded sleeve.

### Human interaction
The GSD is one of the most popular and most used working breeds. Its popularity also puts it near the top of hospital-treated bite statistics in the United States. Health concerns include hip and elbow dysplasia, degenerative spinal disease and a shorter than average life.

### Genuine fun facts
- Horand von Grafrath, bought at a dog show in 1899, was the first dog in the German Shepherd breed register.
- In Britain the breed was long called the Alsatian.
- In a Finnish study, police German Shepherds hit about 42 km/h within 25 m before biting.
- The same study found their median bite on a sensor sleeve was about 360 N, "lower than generally assumed".
- The FCI standard describes the German Shepherd as a trotter and warns that too much hind angulation reduces stamina.
- VetCompass UK recorded a median lifespan of 10.3 years for the breed.

### Concise site-ready summary
The German Shepherd is a 35 kg German herding and police dog built to trot all day and to finish a sprint with a hard grip. Measured police dogs reach about 42 km/h in 25 m, but their bite on a sensor sleeve is moderate, so it sits between wild dogs and heavy guardian breeds and is badly outmatched by large predators.

### Rich narrative profile
The German Shepherd was created in Germany from 1899, when cavalry officer Max von Stephanitz registered Horand von Grafrath as the first dog of a new breed meant to be the ideal working shepherd. A fit working male weighs about 35 kg and stands about 63 cm tall, and the breed standard describes it as a trotter whose build is judged by how well it holds up over a long working day.

Today it is best known as a police and military dog. Finnish researchers measured police German Shepherds sprinting 25 m and biting a sleeve fitted with force sensors. The dogs reached up to 42 km/h and hit with accelerations of around 7 g, but their median bite force was about 360 N, roughly the grip of an elderly man's hand. The shock of the collision matters as much as the jaws.

On the absolute scale that places it above dingoes and coyotes in attack but below the 50 kg Kangal, Rottweiler and Gray Wolf. Its trained control, stamina and nose make it a capable all-rounder, but it has no armor and a population prone to hip and spine problems, and it is far too small to stand up to bears, big cats or large hoofed animals.

### Conflicts and uncertainty
- **Speed:** 11.7 m/s is measured, but after only 25 m; true maximum is probably higher and unmeasured.
- **Bite:** the measured sleeve force (median 360 N) is a working bite on padding, not a maximum; `0.0` PSI means no pressure can be derived.
- **Length:** derived from the FCI ratio.
- **Weight:** AKC figures run heavier than the FCI range; the canonical value follows FCI and measured police dogs.

## 11. Cross-animal normalization notes

- **Versus Kangal (50 kg; A53 D44 Ag59 St68 I63 Sp47):** 15 kg lighter, so lower Attack and Defense; more agile, better stamina at the trot and higher Intelligence and Special for trained scent and bite work.
- **Versus Gray Wolf (50 kg; A55 D41 Ag66 St89 I78 Sp64):** the wolf is heavier, a proven large-prey killer and a far better endurance hunter; the GSD is lower in every category except that it ties Agility over short distances.
- **Versus Dingo (18 kg; A40 D24 Ag82 St81 I75):** almost twice the mass, so higher Attack and Defense; much lower Agility and lower Stamina and Intelligence than a wild hunter.
- **Versus Belgian Malinois (30 kg; A46 D32 Ag76 St75 I70 Sp54):** the GSD is heavier with a higher measured sleeve bite, so slightly higher Attack and Defense; the Malinois is more agile and driven.
- **Anti-inflation check:** no score uses television bite figures or popular speed claims; measured bite and speed data are used only as recorded.
